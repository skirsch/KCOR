# Objection 5: the null in each validation

The manuscript defines KCOR as a ratio of cumulative hazards after its model-based frailty adjustment. Anchored KCOR divides that ratio by its value at a reference week. Section 3.3.2 explicitly defines the quiet-window experiment's null by the identical baseline hazards. This is a valid reference for the stress test. [Manuscript §§2.1.1 and 3.3.2](../../documentation/preprint/paper.md)

| Validation | What is known | What the result can show |
|---|---|---|
| Clean synthetic negative control | Both groups have the same underlying baseline hazard. They differ in frailty, which can produce different observed mortality. | A KCOR near 1 supports recovery of the common baseline under the simulation model. |
| Quiet-window contamination | Both cohorts share the same underlying baseline. The simulator adds a hazard term to one cohort's observed death process during the quiet window and keeps the simulated baseline fixed. | A comparison with 1 measures how the reported KCOR responds to the injected disturbance relative to the known baseline. “KCOR above 1 under the baseline null” describes the reported statistic directly. |
| Cox comparison | The simulated individual-level intervention effect is null. The observed group hazards can differ because higher-risk people die at different rates across groups. | The results illustrate how observed hazards and depletion-adjusted cumulative hazards can answer different questions. Comparing both outputs with 1 does not establish which estimator better recovers a shared target. The shared-frailty curve in Figure 5 is explicitly an approximation. |

The simulator adds the disturbance to the hazard used to generate observed deaths and accumulates the underlying baseline separately. The simulated deaths feed both the frailty fit and the cumulative hazard used in the final calculation. A deviation from 1 therefore measures the response of the whole pipeline to the disturbance. Attributing that deviation specifically to the fitted frailty would require a comparison that holds frailty fixed. [Simulation code](../../test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py)

**Revised disposition.** The paper already defines a valid null for the quiet-window experiment. “Sign reversal” can be replaced with “departure above the baseline null.” The remaining substantive question is how the Cox comparison is framed. Section 2.11 already distinguishes the quantities estimated. The accuracy claims in §2.11.1 should distinguish departure from a hazard ratio of 1 for individuals at the same frailty from departure from KCOR's cumulative baseline target. If the manuscript wants to establish better recovery of a common cumulative target, a direct Gompertz–gamma fit using the same cohort counts and follow-up would provide a comparison on that scale.
