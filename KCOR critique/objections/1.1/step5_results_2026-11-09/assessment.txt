STEP 5: CONTAMINATION OF THE EARLY BASELINE-ESTIMATION WEEKS
Date: 2026-11-09 (America/Toronto)

Main finding
------------
Moving the same added-mortality profile five weeks earlier, into the weeks used
to estimate baseline mortality, can greatly amplify the corrected contrast in
this simulator. This occurs even though the later quiet-window observations and
the total added mortality are unchanged. Systematic fit residuals remain visible;
these are not established examples passing the full manuscript diagnostic rules.

Design
------
All week numbers below are rebased after the original two-week skip. The same
three profiles and strengths from Steps 3-4 are used, with the original cohort
frailty values (affected group 0.5, comparator 4), 50,000 initial people per group,
and a common 200-week follow-up.

                          Clean early baseline    Affected early baseline
Effect active             weeks 5-57              weeks 0-52
Baseline-estimation       weeks 0-3               weeks 0-3
Anchor                    week 4                  week 4
Quiet window              weeks 58-110            weeks 58-110

Each profile's 53 weekly added-hazard values are shifted, not rescaled. The new
case includes harm in the early baseline and anchor, but no harm in the quiet
window. This specifically tests the early estimate of baseline mortality; it
does not add an epidemic or a new persistent effect shape.

The following are numerically verified to match between placements:
- integrated added hazard;
- final observed cumulative hazard and total expected additional deaths;
- observed hazards, survivor counts, and deaths throughout the fixed quiet window;
- the final raw reference contrast that retains the additive effect while removing
  the simulator's baseline frailty depletion.

Consequently, the contrast changes cannot be attributed to more total harm or
different mortality data within the quiet window. The early information used to
set the baseline changes, which changes the fitted frailty and the inversion.

Results before anchoring, at common final follow-up

Profile       Reference   KCOR clean baseline   KCOR affected baseline
Fading        1.073202       1.082516                2.155671
Constant      1.049735       1.057600                1.349050
Combined      1.122937       1.135423                2.612933

The no-effect result remains 1.004895, versus a reference of 1.
These are cumulative-hazard contrasts, not death-count ratios or causal estimates
from real data. Extra expected deaths compared with the SAME affected group
without the extra hazard remain about 350, 238, and 586 for the three profiles,
respectively, identical between placements.

Mechanism visible in the fitted parameters
------------------------------------------
With a clean early baseline, fitted affected-group frailty is 0.492, close to its
generating value of 0.5. When early baseline weeks contain harm:

Profile       Fitted baseline / clean baseline    Fitted frailty
Fading                    1.833                       11.60
Constant                  1.219                        4.75
Combined                  2.051                       13.11

The fitter interprets the high early mortality as a higher underlying baseline.
It then uses much larger frailty to explain the later, lower quiet-window hazard.
The resulting inversion applies a substantially larger correction. This is an
amplification of the contrast in these cases, rather than the attenuation observed
when the early baseline was clean but the later fit window was contaminated.

Anchoring is handled with its own matching reference
----------------------------------------------------
Because the early case also contaminates the anchor, its correctly anchored
reference differs from the clean-baseline placement. It must not be compared
with an unanchored reference or assumed to be above one just because harm exists.

Profile       Reference anchored    KCOR anchored (affected baseline)
Fading             0.592480                    1.159029
Constant           0.861497                    1.097569
Combined           0.553206                    1.245519

The large raw discrepancy already exists before anchoring. Anchoring therefore
does not explain it away. The anchored disagreement is also present when the
same reference convention is applied to both quantities.

Diagnostic evidence
-------------------
All nominal fits return status 'ok'. However, residual patterns are systematic:

Profile       Pooled relative RMSE    First quiet residual    Last quiet residual
Fading                8.51%                  -16.87%                  +13.15%
Constant              3.30%                   -6.45%                   +5.01%
Combined              9.63%                  -19.13%                  +14.91%

Residual = observed minus predicted hazard, divided by observed hazard for the
last two columns. The fitted model starts above the observed hazard and ends
below it. Pooled RMSE includes quiet and baseline anchor fitting points; quiet-
only RMSE is slightly higher and is retained in baseline_results.csv.

Moving each quiet-window boundary by +/-4 weeks still leaves the entire quiet
window uncontaminated. Across these nine perturbations, final raw KCOR ranges:
  Fading:   2.077581 to 2.245909
  Constant: 1.333086 to 1.366788
  Combined: 2.496333 to 2.749335

The effect is not cured by these small boundary shifts. Those ranges are
descriptive; no new pass/fail tolerance is invented. Smooth-data residual
patterns are visible, but statistical detection rates with random deaths have
not been evaluated. Do not claim that the full diagnostic procedure accepts
these examples merely because the fitter returns 'ok'.

Scope of the evidence
---------------------
This is a controlled demonstration of sensitivity to early baseline contamination
at the inherited effect strengths. The initial added hazards are about 90%, 22%,
and 112% of the saved initial mortality scale. It is not a claim that those
strengths are plausible in the real application, nor that its early weeks were
contaminated. The same discrete generator and fixed baseline estimation procedure
are retained. No production code, configurations, original outputs, or earlier
audit artifacts were changed.

For objection 1, we now have two different failure mechanisms to keep distinct:
1. With a clean early baseline, overlap with the later window can understate the
   specified added-effect contrast.
2. If early baseline estimation is affected, the fitted correction can instead
   amplify that contrast substantially, even with an uncontaminated later window.

Both tested mechanisms have diagnostic warning patterns in expected-count data.
Neither establishes a hidden failure passing the complete manuscript procedure.
