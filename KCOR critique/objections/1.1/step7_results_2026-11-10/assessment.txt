STEP 7: ADDITIONAL DIAGNOSTICS ON THE STEP 6 RANDOM DRAWS
Date: 2026-11-10 (America/Toronto)

Main finding
------------
Adding window, skipped-week, mortality-trend, and corrected-curve checks removes
more affected-baseline runs, but does not reliably identify accurate results.
The combined exploratory rule accepts 110/200 affected-baseline runs (55%).
Their median raw contrast is 1.309151 versus the reference 1.049735, about 25%
too high. Of those accepted runs, 87/110 (79.1%) differ by more than 10% from
the reference. The same combined rule rejects 60/200 no-effect runs (30%).

These results concern the specific numerical checks below. They do not establish
acceptance by the complete manuscript procedure or real-data plausibility.

What was done
-------------
Retain Step 6's 200 separate calibration draws and 200 paired evaluation draws
per case, at 50,000 initial people per group. Regenerate their original binomial
death counts with the same seeds and source functions. Reproduce all 600 nominal
evaluation endpoints and parameters, plus both fitted frailty parameters in
each of the 200 calibration draws. Hold each realized dataset fixed across fits.

Additional diagnostics:
1. Independently shift each quiet-window boundary by -4, 0, or +4 weeks, giving
   eight alternatives to the nominal window. Measure the largest absolute
   relative change in the final raw contrast.
2. Skip zero or four initial weeks instead of two, with the physical quiet
   window fixed. Measure the largest absolute relative contrast change.
3. Change the fitted Gompertz growth rate by -5% or +5%, holding the generated
   data fixed. Measure the largest absolute relative contrast change.
4. Fit a straight line to each nominal corrected cumulative-hazard curve versus
   Gompertz-transformed time within the quiet window. Use the worse of the two
   values of 1 minus R-squared. This is a specified shape metric, not proof of
   valid correction; it also differs from ordinary-calendar-time linearity.

The script uses the production fitter and inversion directly so that changing
the skipped weeks changes both the effective hazard and its time coordinate.
All 10,400 saved case/variant records have eligible fits. Comparator fits are
shared across evaluation cases within each repetition.

Before replaying evaluation draws, freeze each diagnostic's cutoff at the higher
empirical 95th percentile of its 200 no-effect calibration values:
  Window sensitivity:      0.043963824842217614 (about 4.40% contrast change)
  Skipped-week sensitivity:0.3227947085754006   (about 32.28% contrast change)
  Growth-rate sensitivity: 0.008838900033899533 (about 0.884% contrast change)
  Curve nonlinearity:      0.001812386317579142 (R-squared at least 0.998188)

Each added check is combined with Step 6's frozen residual rule. The final
intersection requires all four added checks and that residual rule. There is
no claim of a 5% overall false-alarm rate: separately calibrated cutoffs do not
provide that guarantee when combined. The combined rule accepts 155/200
calibration draws and 140/200 independent no-effect evaluation draws.
Individual evaluation acceptance also need not equal the calibration target.

Of all 200 affected-baseline draws, 87 (43.5%) both pass every implemented check
and have more than 10% error. The corresponding no-effect fraction is 35.0%.
The estimates remain imprecise without contamination, so large individual
errors cannot all be attributed to contamination.

The combined acceptance-rate Wilson 95% simulation intervals are 63.3-75.9%
for no effect and 48.1-61.7% for affected baseline. These intervals condition on
the calibrated cutoffs and do not include cutoff-estimation uncertainty.

Interpretation
--------------
The extra checks provide some discrimination: they reject 45% of affected-
baseline runs versus 30% of no-effect runs. They do not ensure recovery of the
specified contrast among runs that remain. The accepted affected-baseline
median falls from 1.345 to 1.309, but stays substantially above 1.050.

No individual added check provides a clean separation in this experiment.
Sensitivity to skipping early weeks is itself substantial in no-effect draws.
Near-zero fitted frailty, reported descriptively for either cohort, appears in
74/200 no-effect, 75/200 clean-baseline, and 13/200 affected-baseline runs. It
would not automatically identify this mechanism. No new near-zero rejection
threshold was introduced.

Scope and qualifications
------------------------
All week labels in the profiles are rebased after the original two-week skip.
The clean-baseline harm lasts weeks 5-57, affected-baseline harm weeks 0-52,
and the nominal quiet window weeks 58-110. Moving its start four weeks earlier
includes four harm weeks in the clean-baseline case, but no harm weeks in the
affected-baseline case. Thus clean-baseline window sensitivity can include a
response to actual added hazard entering the fitting window.

Skip changes also change the included follow-up and matching reference. Their
contrast changes are stability measures, not errors against the nominal target.
All accuracy summaries use the nominal raw contrast and its original matching
reference, before anchoring. These are not death-count ratios.

The boundary shifts follow the previously audited diagnostic specification;
skip zero/four and growth-rate +/-5% remain exploratory choices from Step 4.
One quiet window cannot test omission of a window or coherence across windows.
The optional epidemic module is inactive. This is not a complete numerical
implementation of all manuscript requirements, which lack complete cutoffs.
The alternative outcomes were already known from Step 6; this stage is an
exploratory diagnostic extension, not new independent validation.

Remaining bounded work
----------------------
Two planned steps remain for this investigation:
1. Repeat these already fixed rules on fresh independent simulations at the
   same cohort size and the same three cases, without tuning the cutoffs.
2. Write the conclusion for objection 1, distinguishing demonstrated sensitivity,
   diagnostic limitations, and unresolved applicability to the actual data.
Completion means a defensible assessment, not a promise to eliminate the
objection. No additional mechanism or larger scenario campaign is planned here.
