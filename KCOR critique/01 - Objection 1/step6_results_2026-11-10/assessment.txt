STEP 6: DO RANDOM DEATHS HIDE THE EARLY-BASELINE WARNING?
Date: 2026-11-10 (America/Toronto)

Finding
-------
At 50,000 initial people per group, this exploratory residual check does not
reliably detect the previously demonstrated distortion from early-baseline
contamination. It accepts 199/200 affected-baseline runs. Their median raw
contrast is 1.345, compared with the specified reference of 1.050.

This is evidence against relying on this particular residual check as a
sufficient safeguard. It is not a test of the complete manuscript diagnostic
procedure, and does not establish that the simulated harm occurred or was
plausible in the actual application.

Design
------
Reuse the original binomial death generator and production fitting/inversion
functions. Retain the preceding experiment's 200-week follow-up, two-week skip,
frailty values 0.5 and 4, and quiet window at rebased weeks 58-110.
Compare three cases, with 200 paired evaluation repetitions each:
  No effect: no added hazard.
  Clean baseline: added weekly hazard 0.0001 during weeks 5-57.
  Affected baseline: the same added hazard during weeks 0-52.
Baseline estimation uses weeks 0-3. Both harm profiles end before the quiet
window. They have the same integrated added hazard and raw reference contrast.
The comparator draws/fits are identical within each repetition; affected-group
cases share random seeds, but do not have identical realized death counts.

Use 200 separate no-effect simulations to calibrate a residual check. Its score
combines quiet-week residual scatter, average discrepancy, and time trend,
standardized using binomial death-count variance. Take the worst component
across both cohorts. Require fitting success and absence of specified fitter
warning flags as well. The exact rule is recorded in design.json and the script.

The 95th empirical percentile (higher order statistic) gives a frozen cutoff
of 31.009394979566853. Calibration accepts 191/200. Evaluation uses a separate
seed branch; the cutoff is not adjusted after seeing evaluation results.
The independent no-effect evaluation accepts 188/200, a 94% acceptance rate.

These are simulation distribution percentiles, not confidence intervals for
an individual estimate. Even the no-effect case is imprecise at this cohort
size under this fitting setup; large individual errors are not exclusively
caused by contamination.

The paired comparison helps separate the additional timing effect:
affected-baseline estimates exceed clean-baseline estimates in 198/200 pairs.
The median within-pair increase is 0.265591 contrast units, with 5th-95th
percentiles 0.079957 to 0.353484. This uses all pairs, before diagnostic selection.

Interpretation and limits
-------------------------
The visible residual pattern in expected-count data from Step 5 does not imply
that this check detects it reliably in finite samples. Here the contaminated
case passes more often than the clean cases. Diagnostic acceptance therefore
does not rank these cases by accuracy. Why its residual-score distribution has
a smaller upper tail requires further analysis; this run does not establish
that mechanism.

The reference removes baseline frailty depletion while retaining the added
hazard. It is a stated mechanistic comparison, not a death-count ratio or an
unqualified claim about KCOR's target under arbitrary model violations.
All headline comparisons use raw final contrasts, before anchoring.

This residual check is exploratory and calibrated for this particular synthetic
null and cohort size. The study does not implement per-run window, skip, or
gamma sensitivity, nor a formal near-zero-frailty rejection rule. It cannot
establish acceptance by the complete manuscript procedure. Summary Wilson
intervals measure simulation-rate uncertainty conditional on the frozen cutoff;
they do not include uncertainty in calibrating the cutoff.

Only one inherited constant-effect strength was tested. The initial addition is
about 22% of the underlying initial mortality scale. No real-data plausibility
assessment or general robustness claim follows from this constructed example.
Objection 1 remains open. A sensible next bounded step is to determine whether
the additional stated diagnostics distinguish these accepted, distorted runs
from the accepted no-effect runs, with criteria specified before evaluating
further simulations.
