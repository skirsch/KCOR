OBJECTION 1: FINAL ASSESSMENT OF THE CONTAMINATION INVESTIGATION
Date: 2026-11-10 (America/Toronto)

Decision
--------
The planned investigation is complete. Objection 1 is narrowed and supported
by concrete evidence, but it is not rebutted.

KCOR's correction is sensitive to when added mortality occurs. In the tested
mechanism, harm during the later fitting window can reduce the recovered
contrast, while harm during the early baseline-estimation weeks can amplify
it, even when the later quiet window is uncontaminated. Some smooth-data
failures have clear diagnostic warnings. With random deaths, however, our
specified residual, stability, and shape checks often accept materially
distorted contrasts. That finding reproduces on fresh independent simulations.

This supports conditional interpretation and narrower diagnostic claims. It
does not prove that the actual application is biased, that these harm patterns
occurred or are plausible there, or that the full manuscript procedure would
accept the same examples.

What objection 1 means
----------------------
The concern is not whether a frailty parameter can be fitted under the assumed
model, or whether the inversion formula is correct within that model. It is
whether the fitted correction represents the selection mechanism it is intended
to remove, rather than a genuine change in mortality that should remain in the
comparison. A good fit alone cannot establish the origin of the change.

This assessment addresses that central concern in KCOR_Independent_Review_EN.md,
item 1, and KCOR_Review_EN.md, section 2. It also touches the related validation
and target-definition concerns. It does not resolve the separate objection
about the optional epidemic-wave correction or the bootstrap.

The question we actually tested
------------------------------
We reused the original quiet-window contamination generator and production
fitting/inversion functions. Added hazard acts outside the generator's frailty
depletion denominator. It changes mortality without selectively targeting the
frailest individuals through that added component. We varied timing while
holding the added-hazard profile fixed within each matched comparison.

For an explicit benchmark, we removed the baseline depletion denominator while
retaining the added mortality. If B is accumulated baseline hazard and G is
accumulated added hazard after the same initial skip, the reference is:

                         reference = (B + G) / B

An anchored comparison divides both KCOR and this reference by their respective
values at the same anchor. This benchmark tests a specified meaning of removing
selection while retaining added mortality. It is not a death-count ratio, an
externally established causal effect, or an automatic target guaranteed by
KCOR's descriptive definition under arbitrary model violations.

What the original experiment did and did not show
------------------------------------------------
The original experiment anchored at the first contaminated week. For its
smallest fading effect, final KCOR was 1.112093 before anchoring and 0.941326
after anchoring. But the matching reference was also below one after anchoring:
0.950218. Thus a final anchored value below one did not, by itself, show that
harm had been erased or turned into benefit.

For all 24 nonzero original settings with smooth expected counts, whole-ratio
errors against the matching raw and anchored references stayed below 1% over
the tested post-skip trajectories. This is a favorable, bounded result. It
does not imply equally small error relative to the added increment: for the
smallest constant effect, the change in KCOR relative to its paired no-effect
run recovered 81.9% of the reference's added contrast.

Conclusion: the original test was not a demonstrated hidden reversal of harm.
Its interpretation needed the correct target and anchoring convention. These
corrections are part of the final assessment, not superseded by later failures.
[Evidence: Steps 1-2.]

What the timing extensions established
-------------------------------------
In the 200-week extensions, all following week numbers are after the original
two-week skip. Baseline estimation uses weeks 0-3, the anchor is week 4, and
the later fitting window is weeks 58-110. The initial cohort size is 50,000
per group. The constant-effect example adds weekly hazard 0.0001 for 53 weeks.

Effect placement                   Raw reference   Smooth-data KCOR
Weeks 5-57: after baseline,
  before the later fitting window       1.049735        1.057600
Weeks 58-110: during later fitting      1.049735        1.029471
Weeks 111-163: after later fitting      1.049735        1.057600
Weeks 0-52: including early baseline    1.049735        1.349050

The first three placements share a clean early baseline and anchor. Their
comparison isolates overlap with the later fitting window. In the constant
case, the during-window change relative to the no-effect result retains 49.4%
of the reference added contrast. This is not a percentage of excess deaths
detected. Harm is attenuated on that scale, not completely erased or reversed.

Moving the same effect from weeks 5-57 to weeks 0-52 contaminates the early
baseline estimate. The later quiet-window hazards, expected survivors, and
expected deaths remain identical in the smooth-data comparison. The fitter
then attributes higher early mortality to a higher baseline and fits more
frailty to explain the subsequent lower hazard. The correction becomes much
larger. Because the raw contrast is already distorted, anchoring cannot explain
away this result. The anchor is also affected and needs its own matching target.

The fading and combined profiles show the same qualitative timing mechanisms.
These findings are conditional on the retained discrete generator, including
its small no-effect discrepancy. The smooth no-effect KCOR is 1.004895, not
exactly one. The mechanism does not model treatment-induced changes in frailty
or selective epidemic mortality; no conclusion about those mechanisms follows.
[Evidence: Steps 3 and 5.]

What the diagnostic audit changed
---------------------------------
Fitter status 'ok' is not equivalent to passing the manuscript's diagnostics.
The smooth cases overlapping the later window have strong, systematic fitting
residuals and warrant flagging under the qualitative residual requirements
audited in Step 4. They are not examples of failure passing the full protocol.
Smooth early-baseline contamination also leaves residual patterns.

The audited manuscript requirements do not supply a complete numerical
acceptance classifier. We therefore introduced explicitly exploratory rules:
a residual check, quiet-window boundary shifts, skipped-week sensitivity,
fitted mortality-growth sensitivity, and corrected-curve linearity in
Gompertz-transformed time. Cutoffs were calibrated on separate no-effect draws.
They were fixed before evaluation and unchanged for the fresh validation.

These rules do not cover every manuscript requirement. There is one quiet
window, so omission of a window and coherence across windows cannot be tested.
The optional epidemic module is inactive. Some perturbation magnitudes and all
numerical cutoffs are choices of this investigation, not official universal
manuscript tolerances. No overall 5% false-alarm rate was promised for their
intersection. Moving the window earlier also reaches four harm weeks in the
clean-baseline case; changing skipped weeks changes included follow-up.
[Evidence: Steps 4, 6 and 7.]

Fresh independent validation: the strongest finite-sample evidence
----------------------------------------------------------------
Step 8 used 200 new paired repetitions of each case, under the same generator,
sample size, and fixed rules. The following values concern nominal raw
contrasts among runs passing all implemented checks:

Case                Reference  Passed all checks  Median estimate  >10% error*
No added harm       1.000000     151/200 (75.5%)       1.047899       66/151
Harm after baseline 1.049735     130/200 (65.0%)       1.103584       53/130
Harm in baseline   1.049735     114/200 (57.0%)       1.309078       94/114

*Absolute relative error in the raw contrast versus its matching reference.
The 10% reporting threshold is exploratory, not a manuscript acceptance rule.

For harm in the baseline weeks, the passing median is 24.7% above its reference.
Of accepted runs, 82.5% have more than 10% error; 47% of all affected-baseline
runs both pass and have that much error. The earlier evaluation gave 55%
acceptance and a passing median of 1.309151, closely matching the fresh result.

The checks reject 43% of affected-baseline runs, but also reject 24.5% of no-harm
runs. They provide some discrimination, not assurance that accepted results
are accurate. Individual estimates are also noisy without contamination:
43.7% of accepted no-harm runs differ by more than 10% from their reference.
That uncertainty must not be misattributed entirely to contamination.

The paired timing comparison supports an additional distortion: before any
diagnostic selection, moving harm into baseline weeks raises the estimate in
199/200 fresh pairs. The median within-pair increase is 0.264373 contrast units.
The cases share comparator draws and random streams within repetitions; they
are 200 paired repetitions, not 600 independent comparisons.

The fresh affected-baseline acceptance rate has a Wilson 95% simulation interval
of 50.1-63.7%; the accepted-large-error rate has interval 74.4-88.3%. These
intervals condition on the fixed cutoffs and do not include their calibration
uncertainty. No interval-coverage or clinical-effect validation was performed.
[Evidence: Steps 6-8; headline results from Step 8.]

Disposition of the objection
----------------------------
1. The original anchored reversal interpretation is not supported and should
   not be used as evidence against KCOR.
2. Sensitivity to contamination of estimation periods is demonstrated for the
   tested mechanism. It can attenuate or amplify the specified contrast.
3. Reliable automatic detection is not established. Our implemented checks
   accept many distorted results, including on fresh simulations. Neither the
   smooth failures nor these exploratory passes establish a failure passing
   every requirement of the complete manuscript procedure.
4. Applicability to the actual study remains unresolved. The smallest constant
   addition is about 22% of the initial baseline hazard scale and lasts 53 weeks;
   the fading and combined additions are larger at onset. No empirical evidence
   was used to label those profiles plausible or implausible in the application.
   The experiment cannot attribute the added hazard to treatment versus an
   environmental cause; it specifies its mathematical placement, not its origin.

I would therefore retain objection 1 as a limitation of interpretation and
validation, with the more precise evidence and qualifications above. I would
not claim that KCOR is generally invalid, that every accepted result is wrong,
or that the actual result has a quantified bias based on these simulations.

Recommended manuscript response
-------------------------------
The justified response is to narrow the claims and report this evidence:
- State that interpretation depends on assumptions about both the early
  baseline-estimation weeks and the later quiet fitting windows.
- Replace assurances of automatic detection or conservative behavior with the
  conditional statement that diagnostics can reveal some violations but do not
  establish that the inferred correction represents only pre-existing selection.
- Define the reference quantity under each simulated violation, with the same
  anchoring convention as the estimator. Report raw and anchored quantities
  separately and distinguish whole-ratio error from recovery of an added effect.
- Report acceptance together with error among accepted runs and rejection of
  no-effect controls. Do not treat fitting success as full diagnostic acceptance.
- Support any claim about real-data robustness with application-specific
  evidence for the estimation periods and competing mechanisms. This requirement
  remains outstanding; it is not supplied by our constructed profiles.

Suggested wording for a response, not inserted into the manuscript:

  We agree that fitting the assumed frailty model does not identify the origin
  of a time-varying mortality pattern. In extensions of the contamination
  experiment, an explicit benchmark retained added mortality while removing
  baseline depletion. Overlap with later fitting attenuated the contrast,
  whereas contamination of early baseline estimation amplified it. Some
  smooth-data cases had clear residual warnings. With random deaths, a fixed
  exploratory set of residual, stability, and shape checks nevertheless
  accepted many materially distorted contrasts, and this persisted in fresh
  simulations. These checks are not the complete manuscript protocol, and the
  scenarios have not been established as plausible in the actual application.
  The findings support conditional interpretation and do not support a general
  guarantee that diagnostic acceptance establishes valid selection removal.
