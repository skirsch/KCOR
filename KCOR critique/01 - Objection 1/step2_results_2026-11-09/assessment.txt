The expected reference
----------------------
The existing generator specifies weekly population hazard

  h_j = b_j / (1 + theta * Bpre_j) + g_j,

where b_j is baseline mortality, Bpre_j accumulates previous baseline increments,
and g_j is the added mortality, independent of the frailty-depletion denominator.

For this assessment, selection neutralization means removing that denominator
while retaining the added component. Equivalently, evaluate the same generating
schedule with theta=0 in BOTH groups. The treated reference hazard is b_j+g_j;
the control reference hazard is b_j. This gives an explicit reference under which
the added mortality is supposed to remain in the contrast.

Accumulate B=sum(b_j) and G=sum(g_j) from the same post-skip origin as KCOR:

  reference(t) = (B(t) + G(t)) / B(t)
  anchored_reference(t) = reference(t) / reference(anchor).

This is a chosen mechanistic benchmark for retaining added mortality while
removing the generator's baseline selection. It is NOT a death-risk ratio, a
death-count ratio, an externally established causal effect, or a claim that
KCOR's descriptive definition alone guarantees recovery outside its model.
Simply substituting the true theta into gamma inversion of the entire
contaminated cumulative hazard would not define this reference.

The correct anchored fading reference is itself below one. Thus the direction
of the anchored result is not a failure for this benchmark. KCOR is close to the

relative error in the whole raw ratio ranges from -0.776% to +0.382%; final
relative error in the anchored ratio ranges from -0.978% to +0.112%.
Over all post-skip time points and these settings, the absolute relative errors
also remain below 1% in both versions. This descriptive result is not a
prespecified acceptance threshold and does not establish general accuracy.

Why whole-ratio error is not the only metric
-------------------------------------------
The fading example's expected raw excess above one is 0.120206, whereas KCOR's is
0.112093: 93.25% of that excess. The raw no-contamination KCOR is already 1.006380,
rather than exactly 1. Subtracting that no-contamination result, the effect-induced
change in KCOR is 0.105712, or 87.94% of the reference added contrast.

For the smallest constant case, the corresponding figures are 89.72% using the
excess above one and 81.91% using the paired no-contamination comparison.
For the smallest combined case, they are 96.28% and 93.12%.

These percentages concern an increment in the specified cumulative-hazard
contrast. They do NOT mean that those percentages of excess deaths are detected,
or that the biological treatment hazard has been estimated to that accuracy.
The paired metric accounts for the algorithm's existing null discrepancy; both
metrics are retained so the choice is visible.

Interpretation for objection 1
------------------------------
The existing smooth-data scenarios do not demonstrate that KCOR eliminates the
added harmful effect or reverses its direction on the unanchored reference scale.
Much of the earlier alarming interpretation arose from the wrong reference for
the anchored quantity. There is still a measurable discrepancy, especially when
expressed relative to the added contrast rather than the whole ratio.

The status 'ok' and fitted-parameter behavior from Step 1 have not been redefined
as proof that the manuscript's diagnostics passed. Parameter accuracy and output
accuracy are distinct: fitted theta can be wrong while this particular contrast
is close to the chosen benchmark.

Limits
------
- The original low-frailty affected group, clean baseline-estimation weeks, and
  original timing are retained. Other contamination placements are untested here.
- These are deterministic expected-count calculations, not uncertainty or
  coverage results from the 520 saved random repetitions.
- The original discrete generator approximation is retained. The no-effect
  discrepancy is reported explicitly rather than silently normalized away.
- No plausibility judgment has been made about the tested hazard strengths.
- This benchmark is explicit and scientifically interpretable, but does not
  settle all possible intended targets under model violations.
