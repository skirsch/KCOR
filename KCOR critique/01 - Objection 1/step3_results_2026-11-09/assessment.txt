Controlled design
-----------------
All scenarios have 50,000 initial people per group, 200 weeks of follow-up, the
same baseline mortality schedule, and the original frailty values (0.5 for the
affected group and 4 for its comparator). Baseline level estimation uses the
first four post-skip weeks. The anchor is post-skip week 4; it is uncontaminated.

All following week numbers are rebased after the original two-week skip:

  Baseline-estimation weeks: 0-3
  Anchor:                    4
  Fixed frailty-fit window:  58-110
  Effect before estimation: 5-57
  Effect during estimation: 58-110
  Effect after estimation:  111-163

The longer common follow-up and middle fitting window allow all three placements
to be tested without contaminating the baseline/anchor. This is a new matched
comparison; differences from Step 2 cannot all be attributed to effect timing.
Within this step, only placement changes for a given profile.

Each effect lasts 53 weeks. Its complete weekly added-hazard vector is identical
across positions, merely shifted in time. Profiles are the smallest existing
fading epsilon=0.0005, constant epsilon=0.0001, and their combination. The fading
profile retains the original exp(-0.05*4) onset scaling rather than accidentally
changing its peak when shifting it.

The defined reference remains (B+G)/B: remove baseline frailty depletion while
retaining additive mortality. All placements have the same final B and G, hence
the same final reference. The final additional expected deaths are also equal
across the three placements, within each profile. The reference's anchor is
exactly one because no effect has begun there.

Why timing changes the result
-----------------------------
When the effect is outside the fitting window, the estimated affected-group
frailty stays near its clean-data value (0.492 versus generating value 0.5).
When it overlaps the fitting window, the estimate reaches essentially zero.
The extra mortality changes the fitted correction, and the resulting final
contrast is smaller. Holding the clean-data fit fixed gives the before/after
final result even in the overlapping case; this isolates the refitting effect.

Before and after have identical final values here because the fit sees the same
uncontaminated weekly hazards and inversion depends on accumulated hazard. The
additive component does not selectively target frail individuals. This equality
should not be generalized to frailty-dependent treatment effects or epidemics.

Diagnostics and interpretation
------------------------------
All these fits report status 'ok'. However, relative root-mean-square fit errors
for during-window cases are about 0.219, 0.141, and 0.303, respectively; the code's
configured bad-fit threshold is 100000. Thus an 'ok' return here is not evidence
of a close fit or of passing every diagnostic described in the manuscript.

The experiment establishes a timing-dependent distortion for these defined
mechanisms and settings. Harm remains visible; it is not completely erased or
reversed. This supports a bounded concern about effects overlapping estimation,
but does not establish that the real study has the same problem or that its
full diagnostic protocol would accept these scenarios.

The inherited effect peaks are about 90% (fading), 22% (constant), and 112%
(combined) of the saved initial baseline hazard scale. No empirical plausibility
judgment is made. No uncertainty, interval coverage, or finite-sample detection
rate is established by these expected-count calculations.
