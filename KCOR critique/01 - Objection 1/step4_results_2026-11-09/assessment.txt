STEP 4: MANUSCRIPT DIAGNOSTICS VERSUS THE TESTED OVERLAP CASES
Date: 2026-11-09 (America/Toronto)

Main conclusion
---------------
The overlapping-effect cases from Step 3 show distortion, but they do NOT
demonstrate a wrong result that passes the full manuscript diagnostic procedure.
They have conspicuous residual patterns of the kind the manuscript says should
signal model inadequacy. Under a faithful qualitative application of those
requirements, these cases warrant flagging rather than normal interpretation.

The current numerical fitter nevertheless returns 'ok'. That is a mismatch
between this software status and the manuscript's broader diagnostic standard,
not proof that the whole standard has been passed. No production code was changed.

Manuscript version and requirements
-----------------------------------
Source: the retained 78-page KCOR critique/KCOR.pdf, identified by its SHA-256 in
manifest.json, rather than a newer online manuscript or the current editable text.
Relevant complete pages were extracted; pages 14, 58, 59 and 68 were rendered and
visually checked, including table continuations and equations.

Pages 7-8, sections 2.1.2-2.1.3:
Interpretation requires stable parameters, suitable normalized shape, and absence
of systematic residual structure. If no candidate window meets the criteria,
results should not be reported as interpretable contrasts.

Page 14, section 2.6.2:
Requires small, unstructured hazard residuals, post-normalization shape checks,
parameter/window stability, and consideration of near-zero frailty estimates as
a weak-identification warning.

Pages 58-59, Tables S2-S3:
Lists the checks and an all-required-diagnostics rule. Failure should lead to
non-identification and withheld interpretation. Optional epidemic-module checks
apply only when that module is used.

Page 68, sections S5.2-S5.3:
Residuals should be approximately random around zero, without strong patterns or
autocorrelation. Quiet-window boundaries should be perturbed by +/-4 weeks.
Window omission, skip-week sensitivity, and modest Gompertz-slope sensitivity
are also described.

These sections do not provide a complete set of numerical pass/fail tolerances
for 'small', 'modest', or 'approximately linear'. Accordingly, this audit does
not invent cutoffs or assign a calibrated universal diagnostic-pass label.

Observed checks
---------------
                                   Fading       Constant     Combined
Pooled relative hazard RMSE          21.86%        14.13%        30.35%
Positive residual weeks             53/53         53/53         53/53
First quiet-week relative residual  44.55%        15.70%        50.08%
Last quiet-week relative residual    2.85%        13.56%        17.53%
Fitted theta                         ~0            ~0            ~0
Fitter's reported status              ok            ok            ok

A positive residual means the fitted hazard is LOWER than the observed simulated
hazard. This is a persistent mismatch, not random scatter about zero. The no-
effect control and clean-window cases have pooled relative error about 0.0109%.

The quoted RMSE is the square root of the mean squared residual divided by the
observed hazard, over the pooled fit set (quiet window plus early anchor weeks),
matching the fitter's definition. The 53/53 counts and endpoints concern only
the quiet window. These are smooth expected-count data, so no stochastic
significance test or false-acceptance probability is claimed.

Near-zero theta is a warning under the manuscript's own wording. Scientifically,
a boundary estimate alone does not establish non-identifiability; that warning
is supplementary to the direct and substantial lack of fit here.

Which checks can look reassuring despite the distortion?
--------------------------------------------------------
Window boundaries were independently moved by -4, 0, or +4 weeks (nine fits per
case). The affected group's frailty remained essentially zero. Final raw KCOR:

  Fading:   1.052922 to 1.053231
  Constant: 1.029327 to 1.029629
  Combined: 1.102931 to 1.103254

The estimator is therefore stable under these perturbations even though its
fit is inadequate. Stability is not a substitute for residual review.

Quiet-window cumulative-hazard R-squared against ordinary time was approximately
0.99644, 0.99994 and 0.99733. All would exceed 0.995 if that value were used as a
standalone rule. This is NOT a full diagnostic-pass conclusion. The high values
illustrate why a nearly straight cumulative curve alone is insufficient.

The manuscript's baseline is Gompertz, whose cumulative hazard is not exactly
linear in ordinary time. We also computed linearity against Gompertz-transformed
time rather than silently declaring the ordinary-time check a universal test.
Corresponding R-squared values were approximately 0.99502, 0.99999 and 0.99609.

Additional exploratory sensitivity
----------------------------------
Following the types of checks on page 68, we also fitted the SAME physical death
schedule using skip=0 or 4 (instead of 2) and gamma +/-5%. Those amounts were
chosen for this audit; the paper does not prescribe them. The fitting windows
remain at the same physical weeks when skip changes. No model-generation
parameters or effect schedules were changed.

These checks preserved positive final contrasts and near-zero affected-group
frailty for overlap cases, while altering the numerical contrast. Their ranges
are saved in perturbation_ranges.csv. They are sensitivity measurements, not
formal passes under invented tolerances.

Checks that cannot be established here
-------------------------------------
- There is only one quiet window. Leave-one-window-out and cross-window offset
  coherence cannot be assessed meaningfully without changing the design.
- Optional epidemic correction is inactive, so its identification checks do
  not apply.
- No empirical external information is used to declare these windows quiet.
- Random-data diagnostic acceptance rates and interval coverage remain untested.

What the code enforces
----------------------
Current code/KCOR.py:
- fit_theta0_gompertz reads bad_fit_relrmse_threshold at line 1160.
- The supplied configuration resolves it to the default 100000 (1e5), as shown
  by get_theta_degenerate_fit_config around line 2220.
- The rejection/fallback comparison is around line 1698. Errors of 0.14-0.30
  do not exceed this threshold.
- The identifiability flag around line 1639 is based on few fitting points or
  a small model cumulative-hazard span. It does not itself flag the near-zero
  lower boundary in these cases. The bound check around line 1653 is an UPPER-
  bound check. The workbook status mapping around line 7098 relies on these
  reported flags and fitting success.
- We did not execute the full workbook path or claim to audit every external
  analysis procedure. This scope is the fitter used in our experiment and the
  located status/diagnostic code.

A separate empirical-window scan does contain a pass classifier:
test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py.
It uses at least 30 points, R-squared >=0.995, theta<100, and a cumulative-hazard
RMSE cutoff at the 75th percentile of that scan's eligible fits (lines 12-15 and
243-257). It uses its own constant-baseline cumulative-hazard fitting function,
not the current Gompertz fitter used here. Those dataset-relative thresholds
cannot be imported as a universal diagnostic gate for this experiment. We have
not claimed that these cases pass that separate scan's full classifier.

Conclusion for objection 1
--------------------------
Our current examples establish that overlap can distort the fitted correction.
They also show visible evidence of inadequacy that the manuscript explicitly
asks analysts to inspect. Thus they support a need to enforce/document the
diagnostic procedure, but do not yet establish that a plausible effect can
materially distort the result while satisfying that procedure.

The general mathematical ambiguity remains, but these particular cases are not
evidence of hidden failure under all of the paper's stated checks. Whether the
signals would be reliably detected with finite random data is a separate question.
