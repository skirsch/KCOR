# Original intent of the KCOR revisions

Reviewed the preserved manuscript and method, earlier specifications and experiment plans, the current manuscript and method, and the recorded revision checks. A written convention can be part of the original design even when a later objection gives a reason to change it.

## Restored experiment design

**2.8 · Quiet-window contamination.** The original plan starts contamination in rebased week 4 and specifies `skip_weeks + 4` as the normalization index. The first four accumulating weeks supply the uncontaminated baseline-rate estimate. The fifth accumulating week is both the first contaminated fitting week and the normalization week. Section 3.3.2 again uses that design in its main results, figures and table. The fourth-week workbook comparison remains a sensitivity analysis using the same fitted curves. The general single-reference-week formula in §2.8 still applies. The archived grid supplies the primary means, and the revision adds pointwise 95 percent Monte Carlo intervals. [Original plan](../../.cursor/plans/quiet-window_contamination_experiment_f3a4b7c2.plan.md#L43) · [Anchor comparison](../objections/2.8/README.md)

## Intent and disposition by objection

| Item | Original intent and scope of the revision |
|---|---|
| **1.1 · Diagnostic claims** | The original method aims to detect some violations and make the adjusted contrast conditional on the working model. The edits retain fit, residual and stability diagnostics and qualify claims that contamination must be detected or that the adjustment must be conservative. The broad identification objection had already been withdrawn. [Original scope and assessment](../objections/1.1/KCOR_Objection_1_Corrected_Framing.md) |
| **1.2 · Optional wave module** | The original purpose was to adjust wave contributions after ordinary inversion while preserving continuity through wave exit. The original §2.7.3 also explicitly preserves the ordinary post-wave increments via a constant offset. The approved conditional continuation changes that rule to account for the surviving distribution after an $\alpha\ne1$ wave. It keeps the proportional-wave identity and uses the in-wave exit exposures. The opening-moment fix corrects the moment used at entry and leaves the later in-wave approximation open. [Original equation](baseline/documentation/preprint/paper.md#273-application-of-the-nph-correction-once-alpha-is-specified) · [Post-wave checks](../objections/1.2/verification.md) · [Opening moment](../objections/1.2/entry-moment/README.md) |
| **1.3 · Real-data intervals and first-window fitting** | The original paper explicitly specifies aggregate contiguous-block resampling. The author approved adopting the available person bootstrap, which keeps each sampled person's follow-up and refits each draw. Independent sample clocks and diagnostics support that purpose. The original paper and v7.5 specification prescribe a joint first-window fit, while an earlier build plan explicitly prescribes a fixed early-week rate. The joint seed is selected for the revised analyses and remains pending lead approval as the production default. Its final refit still includes early anchor weeks outside the paper's quiet-only description. Full production coverage is open. | **1.4 · Validation controls and Table 8** | Relabeling Czech age groups removes the vaccination contrast from the age-shift exercise while retaining real differences in mortality. The exercise checks the method under composition differences. The revision keeps the historical estimates and recovered intervals and adds the joint-fit replay on the archived population. It preserves the original distinction between pre-existing selection and treatment-induced frailty and states what evidence could distinguish them. Interpretation of the 2022 flags, intervals and forced exponent remains deferred. The edits distinguish the conditional treatment hazard ratio from the adjusted cumulative target, identify the supplied variances, and show intervals for the seven reproduced endpoints. The author’s proposed analytic derivation remains a separate proposal. | **1.6 and 2.2 · Gamma inversion, weekly fitting and diagnostics** | The original target is the underlying cumulative hazard under the fitted Gompertz–gamma model. Exact weekly inversion and integrated weekly predictions use the same model at the week boundaries. The scaling and identification edits retain the fixed Gompertz slope and distinguish rescaling observed hazards from rescaling each individual's baseline. Shape diagnostics refer to the fitted Gompertz curve and the supplement preserves the observation of approximate straightness in the historical Czech plot. The weekly wave-reference follow-up uses the same integrated-step convention in the optional module. | **2.3 · End of a gap** | The original specification says excess accumulates during the wave and persists once it ends. The revision reads both reconstructed and expected totals at quiet-window re-entry, after the last gap week has contributed. This fixes the boundary for the stated offset. The rule for responding to the resulting offset is the separate 3.1 question. [Original offset](../../documentation/specs/KCORv7/KCORv7.5.md#L280) · [Boundary check](../objections/2.3/verification.md) |
| **2.4 · First wave week** | The original plan explicitly anchors the optional reference at the first bin **inside** the wave, and §2.7.3 sets it equal to that week's accumulated observed value. The approved revision instead starts at the preceding boundary and includes a fitted baseline increment for the first week. This changes a documented convention so that deaths in the first modeled week can contribute to estimated wave excess. The earlier explanation should have identified the original choice explicitly. [Original plan](../../.cursor/plans/nph_manuscript_punch66_ff4e8d5f.plan.md#L56) · [Boundary evidence](../objections/2.4/verification.md) |
| **2.5 · Quiet-window scan** | The original study tests fit behavior across nearby quiet-window placements and asks whether failures are visible. The approved replay preserves the workbook, six groups, 13 windows per group and enrollment clock. It changes the fitter and diagnostic criteria and uses all attempts in the denominator. The 78 passes apply to the revised criteria. The manuscript reports the spread of the estimates across windows. [Original SI](baseline/documentation/preprint/supplement.md#s54-quiet-window-robustness-scan) · [Paired scan](../objections/2.5/verification.md) |
| **2.6 and 3.3 · Positive controls** | The original paper aims to show that the fitted pipeline responds to injected harm and benefit. Its wording suggests an individual-baseline intervention and its historical values are reproducible as raw ratios. The current experiment retains the generator's cohort-hazard multiplier, the counts and effect dates, and replays them with the joint fitter for Table 9. This verifies direction for those injections and the manuscript identifies the retrospective fit. An individual-level intervention would be a different experiment. The command originally used to produce the manuscript table remains unknown. | **2.7 · Contamination threshold** | The original plan asks whether additive hazard changes frailty estimates and can produce KCOR above the equal-baseline target. It also seeks a threshold. The extended sweep uses the planned anchor and original fitter. Reporting both the zero-addition result and the differences from it retains that question while exposing the upward departure already present at zero. The threshold claim about the Czech data and the low-variance estimation problem remain separate. | **2.9 and 2.10 · Fitting groups and worked examples** | The original bootstrap distinguishes resampling strata from fitted cohorts. The revision states which groups were actually fit and pooled in Table 8. The Czech figure was chosen to illustrate curvature reduction. The revision keeps that purpose and discloses the observed endpoint and curvature criteria. It distinguishes the configurations used in the worked example from prespecification in the general method. | **3.1 · Gap fallback** | The original code and specification deliberately rejected negative first-pass offsets as a safeguard, and the later insufficient-signal policy set applied frailty to zero. The five noiseless counterexamples establish that a **true zero** can trigger this due to numerical error, despite a correct positive frailty estimate. The approved code continues the fit for **all nonpositive** offsets, including materially negative ones. The check does not establish when a material deficit warrants rejecting the fit. The original concern and the scope of the revised rule both need to be visible when reviewing this decision. | **3.2 · Before the first quiet window** | The current method uses the first available quiet interval. The alternative accounting for survival to that interval is a deferred sensitivity experiment. | **Supporting evidence edits** | The old multiwave recovery test deliberately uses expected deaths and an exact early rate. The pilot coverage plan explicitly uses one fixed reference realization, and the older figure and grid use earlier procedures. The edits describe those historical choices and restrict what the results establish. The audit restored 2.8 and recorded the other differences. Prior decisions with the largest effects on method behavior are the wave-entry boundary, the handling of materially negative gap offsets, and the pending production fitter default. The original and both completed revision checkpoints remain selectable.
