**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound under the stated model. The main problem is how the manuscript decides that observed mortality curvature measures frailty, then uses that decision to interpret the adjusted curves. A good fit can occur when the inferred frailty is wrong, even with unlimited data. There are also two directly checkable inconsistencies between the equations and the reported workflow. Both can be fixed and tested. [Combined manuscript, pp. 10–14](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.pdf)

### 1. The identification proposition uses the wrong criterion

Let \(h\) be the observed cohort hazard. On a quiet interval under the paper’s exact Gompertz–gamma model, differentiation gives

\[
h'(t)=\gamma h(t)-\theta h(t)^2,\qquad
\theta=\frac{\gamma h(t)-h'(t)}{h(t)^2}.
\]

This is a positive result for KCOR. With a known, correct \(\gamma\) and an exactly observed positive hazard trajectory, the model identifies \(\theta\). But the proposition equates nonzero curvature of cumulative hazard with positive frailty. Two counterexamples follow immediately. With \(\theta=0\) and \(\gamma>0\), the Gompertz cumulative hazard is curved. With \(k=\gamma/\theta\) and \(\theta>0\), the observed hazard is constant and its cumulative hazard is perfectly straight. The post-inversion diagnostic has a related problem. The model predicts a Gompertz cumulative curve in quiet weeks. Testing for a straight line in event time can penalize a correct fit. [Baseline and proposition, pp. 10–12](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323) · [Linearity diagnostic, p. 14](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:458)

**Fix.** Prove the narrower identification result above, and assess practical precision with a parameter profile. Check deviations from the fitted Gompertz curve, or plot normalized cumulative hazard against \((e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\), where linearity is expected within each quiet segment. Outside the assumed baseline family, every candidate \(\theta\ge0\) gives a baseline \(H_{0,\theta}=(e^{\theta H_{\rm obs}}-1)/\theta\) that reproduces the same observed survival, taking the limit at zero. No diagnostic using only those same observations can distinguish mechanisms that generate identical data. The claims that misspecification will reveal itself or lead to conservative results should be withdrawn. [Inversion, p. 10](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Conservativeness claims, p. 38](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1023)

### 2. The multiplicative-invariance claim fails as written

The manuscript explicitly considers multiplying the **observed** hazard by \(c\). Write \(G(t)=(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). Then
\[
c\,\frac{k e^{\gamma t}}{1+\theta kG(t)}
=\frac{(ck)e^{\gamma t}}{1+(\theta/c)(ck)G(t)}.
\]
The fitted variance changes from \(\theta\) to \(\theta/c\), even when both cohorts receive exactly the same multiplier. Under this ideal rescaling the two reconstructed cumulative hazards both scale by \(c\), so **the KCOR ratio can be invariant while the inferred frailty variances change**. A multiplier on the *individual baseline hazard before depletion* is a different operation. It changes \(k\) and the subsequent depletion path while leaving the generating frailty variance fixed. [Effect-scale claim, p. 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345)

**Fix.** State both operations explicitly and specify which one each simulation injects. Report sensitivity to the numerical value of \(\gamma\). From the identity above, holding the observed local hazard and slope fixed, a slope error \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied \(\theta\) by \(\delta\gamma/h\). This matters especially in low-mortality cohorts. The supplement lists the slope only as “Fixed implementation value.” [Slope and cancellation claim, p. 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Supplementary defaults, Table S5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:167)

### 3. The weekly fitting algorithm and the principal figure need an arithmetic audit

- **Two different inversions are used.** For a weekly observed integrated hazard \(x\), the exact gamma update multiplies \(1+\theta H_0\) by \(e^{\theta x}\). Step 2 multiplies it by \(1+\theta x\). The discrepancy accumulates, enters the wave offsets, and can affect the refit. The final curve then uses the exact exponential. Its difference from the fitting recursion is deterministic even with noiseless counts. [Weekly input, p. 9](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Steps 2–4, pp. 12–13](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:388) · [Final inversion, p. 13](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:434)
- **Figure 1 fails a simple invariant as labeled.** For \(H,\theta\ge0\), the inversion always gives \(H_0\ge H\). At the right edge of Figure 1, the dose-2 curve is about 0.030 in the observed panel and 0.020 in the normalized panel. The reference curve also falls. A different origin, aggregation, or additional transformation could explain this, but the caption presents the panels as successive stages of the same inversion. This is a within-cohort discrepancy, independent of which cohort has the larger ratio. [Figure 1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/figures/fig_kcor_empirical_intuition.png) · [Caption](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:139) · [Figure metadata](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/figures/fig_kcor_empirical_intuition.json)

**Fix.** Use exact weekly increments throughout. One direct fit is to compare \(H_{0,\theta}(t+1)-H_{0,\theta}(t)\) with the integrated Gompertz increment in each quiet week, using uncertainty appropriate to the weekly counts. Under the core multiplicative model this uses the observed depletion history through the start of each week, including waves before the first quiet window. It also avoids freezing \(k\) at a seed fit that assumes a Gompertz history back to enrollment. Publish a small worked table with week, risk set, deaths, post-skip \(H_{\rm obs}\), fitted \(\theta\), reconstructed \(H_0\), and KCOR. Generate all three figure panels from that table. [Seed fit and fixed-\(k\) refit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371)

### 4. The optional wave correction does not follow from its hazard model

The wave model multiplies **instantaneous excess hazard** by \(F(t;\alpha)=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{alive at }t]\). The correction divides **accumulated, already inverted excess** by the factor at the current week. If \(F\) changes, dividing all accumulated excess by today’s \(F\) retrospectively changes the adjustment for earlier weeks. There is a decisive check at \(\alpha=1\). In that case an individual wave hazard proportional to \(Z\) already satisfies the core multiplicative model, so exact core inversion recovers the total baseline including the wave. Yet \(F(t;1)\) is the survivor mean frailty, generally below one after depletion. Dividing positive excess by it changes the answer again, despite the manuscript describing this case as inactive. For \(\alpha\ne1\), survival through a wave also reweights the frailty distribution by a factor involving \(Z^\alpha\), so the usual gamma survivor update generally ceases to hold. [Wave model, pp. 15–16](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Cumulative correction, pp. 16–17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module inactive for the Czech results, as the authors already conclude. Before using it elsewhere, derive both the survivor distribution and the weekly correction from one individual-level wave model. Require exact recovery of the core result at \(\alpha=1\), then test recovery of a known wave contrast when \(F\) varies over time. Fixing an external efficacy scale addresses one ambiguity in estimating \(\alpha\). It does not repair the cumulative correction. [Czech identification result, p. 33](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:904)

### 6. The intervals and diagnostic pass decisions are unvalidated in this edition

The bootstrap resamples blocks of weekly deaths and risk sets while retaining fixed calendar quiet windows. Moving a wave block into a quiet date changes the design. Carrying over its original risk sets can break the cohort accounting at the block boundary, while rebuilding the risk sets changes the denominators that generated the sampled deaths. The identity \(N_t=N_0-\sum_{s<t}d_s\) alone cannot establish the claimed covariance preservation. **Every empirical coverage cell in Table 12 is blank.** The manuscript appropriately calls the table a pilot, but elsewhere describes the intervals as calibrated. [Bootstrap procedure, p. 18](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:635) · [Coverage table, p. 55](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

For the stated fixed, uncensored, independent-person design, an aggregate bootstrap can resample the multinomial categories **week of death or survived to end** within each cohort and stratum. This preserves calendar dates and reconstructs valid risk sets. Refit the full procedure, including any data-estimated \(\alpha\), in each replicate. Validate against analytic simulation truth and report coverage, interval width, and diagnostic acceptance both overall and among accepted datasets. In the Czech application, every age band passes, while the dose-2 estimate for ages 60–69 is \(7.01\times10^{-18}\). A boundary estimate could be a precisely estimated zero or a poorly constrained variance. A one-sided profile and the resulting range of KCOR values would tell the reader which interpretation the data support. [Diagnostic gates, Table S6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:366) · [Frailty estimates, Table S7](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:398)

I would retain KCOR as a **model-based sensitivity analysis of cumulative mortality among survivors of the stabilization period**. Its most informative result would show how much a prespecified cohort contrast changes across defensible baseline and frailty assumptions, alongside the observed contrast. My first revision priority would be to reconcile Figure 1 and the exact weekly arithmetic. My second would be to demonstrate parameter recovery, false diagnostic acceptance, and interval coverage against known truth. Those changes would let readers judge how much the normalization adds. This review used the supplied combined PDF, manuscript sources, and figures read-only. The findings concern the estimator and evidence presented in this edition, without an independent rerun of the implementation.
