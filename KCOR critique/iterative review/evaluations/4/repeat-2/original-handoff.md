**I would request major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is sound under its assumptions, and this edition fixes several important problems in the previous review. The strongest remaining defect is in the fit that supplies parameters to the inversion. On the supplied Czech data, changing the wave exponent from \(1\) to \(1.000001\) changes one fitted frailty variance from **14.58 to 6.07**. Both fits report success. Four weeks switch from fitting the baseline to being treated as wave exposure. That change matters more than the sixth decimal place of the exponent.

### 1. The wave model is now coherent, but the fitted estimator jumps at its proportional limit

The revision correctly uses \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) to carry survivor selection through a wave and beyond its end. For fixed parameters, ordinary gamma inversion recovers \(B+W\) at \(\alpha=1\). This resolves the previous objection to dividing accumulated excess by the current week’s survivor mean. The problem is one step earlier. At \(\alpha=1\), the fitter includes the early anchor weeks in its baseline residuals. At a nearby nonlinear exponent, it excludes marked wave weeks even when they overlap those anchors. A proportional wave can still raise mortality during an anchor week. ### 3. A diagnostic pass still supplies too little information about frailty

The revised identification proposition is correct. Under an exact quiet-period Gompertz–gamma model with known \(\gamma\), \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) identifies \(\theta\) from a positive hazard trajectory. The revision also fixes the observed-hazard scaling identity and uses exact integrated weekly hazards. The remaining scientific limit is that, for the same observed cumulative hazard \(H\), every candidate \(\theta\) has a matching baseline \(H_{0,\theta}=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H)/\theta\), with limit \(H\) at zero. More people can tighten the fit under the chosen baseline. They cannot distinguish two mechanisms with identical survival. This matters especially at low mortality, where a slope error \(\delta\gamma\) changes locally implied frailty by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The Czech slope is 0.085/year. The supplement gives only “Fixed implementation value” where a reader would need the numerical sensitivity results supporting the cancellation claim.
