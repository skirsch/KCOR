**I would recommend major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is correct. The main problem is the interpretation of its output. The same aggregate mortality curves can arise from different combinations of baseline risk and frailty, with different answers to the question “what would the cumulative hazard be without depletion?” KCOR selects one answer through its baseline, quiet-window, and wave assumptions. It is defensible as a descriptive sensitivity analysis. The evidence in this edition does not establish it as a generally reliable correction for selection. I reviewed the 65-page combined manuscript and its sources in `documentation/preprint` read-only. The numerical study results below are manuscript-reported, except where I identify a calculation from its equations. [Combined manuscript](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.pdf)

### 1. The central limitation can be demonstrated exactly

Let \(H(t)\) be the observed cumulative hazard. For **every** \(\theta\geq0\), the baseline \(G_\theta(t)=(e^{\theta H(t)}-1)/\theta\), with \(G_0=H\), reproduces the same observed survival under gamma mixing. For a concrete example, give cohorts A and B the same baseline cumulative hazard of 0.20 at a chosen horizon and frailty variances 0 and 4. Their observed cumulative hazards are 0.20 and \(\log(1.8)/4\). The true baseline ratio in that construction is **1.00**. Now give both cohorts zero frailty and use their respective observed hazard curves as their individual baseline curves. Every weekly survival probability is identical to the first construction, yet the baseline ratio is **1.36**. No residual check, window perturbation, or larger registry can distinguish those explanations from the counts alone. [Gamma identity and inversion, §2.4.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297)

The paper’s narrower identification proposition is sound. In a genuinely quiet interval with a known Gompertz slope, \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). The choice of slope therefore does real inferential work. At a given hazard level and derivative, changing \(\gamma\) by \(\delta\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta/h\). Cohorts with different hazards can respond very differently to the same slope error. **Fix:** define KCOR up front as a ratio of cumulative hazards for reference frailty \(z=1\) within each cohort. State the numerical slope, its units, and the grounds for applying it to each cohort. Show the endpoint across plausible slopes and alternative baseline shapes. The supplement currently specifies only “Fixed implementation value.” [Identification proposition and cancellation claim, §§2.4.3–4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Supplementary defaults](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

### 2. Anchoring changes the scientific question and can reverse the answer

The anchored curve is \(K(t)/K(t_0)\). If one cohort has twice the reference-frailty hazard throughout follow-up, the unanchored ratio is 2 and the anchored curve is identically 1. Anchoring removes a persistent proportional difference regardless of its origin. Earlier deaths also remain in both cumulative hazards, so the anchored curve cannot be read as mortality accumulated only after \(t_0\). This matters in the paper’s own contamination experiment. With the **same fitted curves**, the largest dynamic disturbance gave mean anchored KCOR **0.782** when the reference included the first contaminated week and **2.116** when the reference ended one week earlier. [Definition, §2.1.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:163) · [Anchor sensitivity, §3.3.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:780)

**Fix:** report observed deaths and survival, unanchored KCOR, and anchored KCOR together. If the question concerns a later period, report the increments \(G_d(t)-G_d(t_0)\) for that period. Name the population at the time origin. After the stabilization skip, it consists of people who survived to that landmark, and deaths during the skip are outside the contrast. [Skip and rebasing, §2.7](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:428)

### 3. Passing the diagnostics does not establish adequate precision

In the Czech window scan, all **78 of 78** fits passed. For one dose-1 group, fitted frailty nevertheless ranged from **0 to 1.56** as the window moved. The windows overlapped and shared early fitting weeks. The paper’s own information formula helps explain the result. Using its weekly rate of 0.000456, a single 52-week window, and 50,000 people, I calculate an approximate model-conditional lower bound of **4.24** on the standard error of \(\theta\) under the stated low-mortality approximation. That is an illustration, not an estimate for the Czech multi-window fit. It shows why cohort size or a successful optimizer is an inadequate precision criterion. A poorly determined \(\theta\) can still yield a usable endpoint, so both need assessment. [Czech scan, SI §S5.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304) · [Information bound, §2.9](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:594) · [Weekly rate](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:780)

**Fix:** publish parameter profiles and the corresponding range of KCOR at each primary horizon, including the \(\theta=0\) boundary. State quantitative acceptance criteria. Show results with the four early anchors omitted and with a whole quiet window held out. The stated objective includes early fitting rows, while the identification section says only prespecified quiet weeks are used. Also reconcile the workflow figure, which holds \(k\) fixed, with §2.5, which jointly refits it. These choices affect what information supports the estimate. [Quiet-window rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:338) · [Fitting objective](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Workflow figure](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:604)

### 4. The validation evidence answers several different questions

| Evidence | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| Cox comparison | KCOR is supplied the **generating frailty variances**. This tests inversion with known inputs. The cohorts genuinely have different marginal hazards, so a nonunit Cox estimate under this design does not establish bias for every marginal survival estimand. The shared-frailty comparison curve is extrapolated from one result. [§2.11.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:643) |
| Positive controls | The fitted week-80 ratios, **0.834** and **1.162**, detect the direction of the injected shifts. The generator multiplies the hazard among survivors, and the quiet fit begins at week 81. Establishing magnitude recovery requires the population value of the precise cumulative estimand under that generator. [SI §S3.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:130) · [§3.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:754) · [Table 9](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1171) |
| Czech age-shift controls | Reassigning age groups to pseudo-doses removes a literal dose contrast. Age groups can still have different mortality trajectories. The authors correctly say the exact target is unknown. Six intervals containing one cannot establish equivalence or quantify bias. [§3.1.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:745) |
| Interval coverage | The studies giving **189/200** and **502/600** both predate the current weekly fitting equations. The newer nonlinear experiment gives **90/100** coverage, with Monte Carlo interval **82.4%–95.1%**. That result leaves substantial uncertainty about nominal 95% coverage. [§2.9.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:585) · [SI §S8, PDF p. 65](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457) |

**Fix:** freeze the final estimator and rerun the main validation grid with both \(k\) and \(\theta\) estimated from sampled counts. Give each method its own population target. Report endpoint bias, interval width, coverage, rejection rate, and coverage among accepted fits. Include smooth baseline misspecification that can pass the diagnostics. Retain the newer survivor-model results: the reported pre-entry correction moved the sampled mean from **1.204 to 0.998** in its specified design. That is direct evidence for a particular improvement. [SI §S8](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:459)

### 5. The optional wave correction has an unresolved attribution problem

The survival equation \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) is coherent. One weekly survival probability, however, supplies one equation for two exposure increments. The algorithm fixes the baseline increment in above-baseline weeks and sets the wave increment to zero in deficit weeks. Sampling variation alone can switch those branches. There is also limited independent evidence in agreement between the two proposed exponent diagnostics. For the same rows and equal weights, if \(u_i\) is a cohort’s adjusted log excess, then \(\sum_{i<j}(u_i-u_j)^2=n\sum_i(u_i-\bar u)^2\). The pairwise and variance objectives can therefore agree by construction. [Wave allocation, §2.7.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:515) · [Objective definitions, SI §S2.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:102)

**Fix:** retain the manuscript’s conclusion that the Czech data do not identify \(\alpha\). The updated fits reached the grid boundary, only **62 positive-excess cohort-week rows** survived filtering, and the historical excess was a difference from dose zero. Show the core result alongside sensitivity runs varying the exponent, external effect scale, and deficit-week rule. Use the weekly death counts directly in a joint survival model if estimating a shared wave trajectory. Report how much the endpoint changes across plausible allocations. [Czech replay and identification decision, §3.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:828)

The paper’s strongest publishable claim is narrower and testable: **given a specified baseline and survivor model, KCOR reconstructs a reference-frailty cumulative hazard, and the analyst can measure how sensitive the cohort ratio is to those choices.** I would retain the exact inversion and revised weekly survivor equations, make that sensitivity the primary empirical result, and remove claims that a flat or diagnostically accepted curve establishes successful removal of selection.
