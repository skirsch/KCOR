**I would still request major revision for m83, but several objections from the previous review are resolved.** The revised manuscript correctly derives identification under a *known* Gompertz slope, corrects the observed-hazard scaling law, uses exact weekly gamma increments in the core fit, and defines anchoring at a single reference week. Resampling people’s death weeks and survivor outcomes also fixes the earlier problem of stitching risk sets together across calendar weeks. The remaining question is whether the fitted variance and the resulting KCOR contrast are reliable in the applications shown. The edition’s own results give reasons to doubt that. ### 1. The first quiet window can give a perfect fit to the wrong baseline rate

This is a remaining defect in the four-step estimator, even with noiseless data. In §2.5, let \(q(s;k,\theta,a)\) denote the predicted weekly observed hazard and let \(a>0\) be cumulative excess from a wave **before** the first quiet window. Because the Gompertz cumulative hazard is linear in \(k\), the revised equation gives the exact identity
\[
q(s;k,\theta,a)
=
q\!\left(s;\frac{k}{1+\theta a},\theta,0\right).
\]
Thus a first-window fit that assumes zero prior excess can reproduce **every week exactly** while underestimating \(k\). For example, \(k=0.01,\theta=4,a=0.05\) gives a fitted scale of \(0.008333\), about 17% low, with identical predicted quiet-week deaths. Step 4 freezes this scale. The later offset calculation starts at re-entry to subsequent windows. Neither a small seed residual nor convergence resolves the error. The implementation also exposes two seed procedures: its function defaults to fixing the early rate, while the Table 8 replay explicitly selects the joint fit. [Seed and pooled fit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:380) · [Offset timing](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:409) · [Implementation default](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/changes.diff:711) · [Table 8 override](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/changes.diff:397)

**Fix.** For the proportional-frailty core, fit \(k\) and \(\theta\) jointly across quiet weeks using the **observed survival at each week’s opening**. Under the model, the opening denominator \(1+\theta B_{\rm start}\) equals \(\exp(\theta H_{\rm obs,start})\). This uses the recorded mortality before the first quiet week without assuming an uninterrupted Gompertz history. Fit the implied weekly death probabilities to deaths and risk sets, and propagate uncertainty in the full history. Make the manuscript estimator the ordinary implementation path. An acceptance test should put a known wave before the first quiet window and recover the original baseline rate.

### 2. The Czech scan demonstrates that a diagnostic pass can coexist with large parameter instability

All **78 of 78** window placements passed. I checked the saved window records: in *each* of the six birth-decade/dose groups, at least one accepted fit had \(\hat\theta=10^{-10}\). Among 1950s births, the accepted maxima were **18.25, 14.51, and 21.81** for doses 0, 1, and 2 respectively. The scan labels a fit as passing from optimizer success, an `ok` status, and a degeneracy guard. Those checks do not measure how much the answer changes when the window moves. The paper also says the final scan fit included four early post-skip anchor weeks in addition to the selected window. Those weeks need to appear in the formal definition of the fit set wherever they are used. **Fix.** Report numerical convergence, parameter precision, and stability of the **reported KCOR** separately. A wide range of \(\theta\) might have little practical effect on a particular contrast, so show that contrast across all 13 placements, alongside joint \(k,\theta\) profiles and predictions for withheld weeks. Give the numerical Gompertz slope, its units, and the results of varying it. The defaults table still says “Fixed implementation value.” More fundamentally, any observed cumulative hazard \(H\) can be reproduced with variance \(q>0\) and baseline \(\operatorname{expm1}(qH)/q\). The baseline restriction supplies the identifying information. Good fit alone cannot establish that depletion caused the observed curvature. ### 3. Table 8 does not establish that the revised method recovers an empirical null

The narrow intervals I cited in the previous review belong to **historical v4.3**. The current 2022_06 joint-fit intervals are much wider. For pseudo-dose 2 versus 0 the estimate is **1.0677 [0.335, 1.213]**. Including one in an interval that wide provides little evidence of accuracy near one. More importantly, four component fits had insufficient signal in the original 2022 analysis, and **999 of 999** bootstrap draws had at least one such flag. The corresponding count for 2021 was **307 of 999**. The replay counts the flags and computes percentiles across all draws. The published rule says that failure of a required diagnostic makes a contrast unreportable. Table 8 also fixes the Czech wave exponent at **1.18**, although §3.4 concludes that the Czech data did not identify it. The text discloses the fixed value, but this makes the table a sensitivity analysis of that choice. ### 5. The post-wave equation is sound conditional on its inputs. The in-wave calculation can give it the wrong inputs

The revision correctly makes \(\alpha=1\) an identity and derives post-wave survival from \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\). It also acknowledges the unresolved step: within the wave it divides **accumulated** excess by the current week’s mean-one gamma moment. The survivor distribution generally has a different mean and shape. The regression test takes the implementation’s own estimated exit exposure, then independently generates a subsequent survival increment from it. That tests the tail inverse; it cannot detect a wrong exit exposure. There is also a smaller, directly correctable inconsistency: the wave reference still adds \(ke^{\gamma s}\) per week, while the core uses the exact increment \(ke^{\gamma s}\operatorname{expm1}(\gamma)/\gamma\). With positive \(\gamma\), even noiseless no-wave data can consequently generate positive apparent excess. [In-wave approximation and reference](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:574) · [Conditional tail equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:598) · [Regression test](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/changes.diff:2123)

**Fix.** Use the same integrated Gompertz increments throughout. Given a prespecified baseline path and exponent, fit cumulative wave exposure to observed survival through the same \(L(B,W)\) equation during the wave, with nonnegative wave increments and a count likelihood for noisy weeks. Carry that survivor distribution into later quiet windows as well. A nonlinear wave changes the frailty distribution of survivors, so subsequent quiet weeks need not satisfy the original gamma fit with just an additive offset. Validate the entire path using independently specified baseline and wave exposures, including zero-wave and \(\alpha=1\) cases.

### 6. Several validation figures measure a different procedure or target

The Cox grid now correctly discloses that KCOR receives the **generating frailty variances**. It checks the inversion with known inputs. Under that simulation, the true marginal B-to-A hazard ratio is \(1/(1+\theta_B H_0(t))\), so a cohort-only Cox estimate away from one reflects a real marginal survival difference. The shared-frailty comparison curve is constructed by attenuating the Cox estimates from one reference row. Likewise, the multiwave recovery example fixes the generating baseline rate, the empirical workflow figure uses an older monthly method and selects for a favorable curvature change, and the positive control multiplies the **observed survivor hazard**. Each can illustrate a specific property. Together they still leave the accuracy of the current fitted pipeline unresolved. **My publication priority would be one end-to-end benchmark** using stochastic weekly deaths, hidden generating parameters, a wave before the first quiet window, and the ordinary released entry point. Publish the observed curves, generating cumulative target, fitted contrast, uncertainty, and diagnostic decision for every scenario. Include a second generating process with identical observed survival and a different baseline/frailty decomposition. That would establish both what KCOR can recover under its assumptions and what aggregate mortality alone leaves unresolved.
