The remaining issues concern what the adjusted quantity means, how the estimator handles sampling noise, and what the validation actually establishes.

### 1. The wave algorithm can create wave exposure from ordinary death-count noise

In a designated wave week, KCOR holds the planned baseline increment fixed when observed mortality is high and assigns the excess to wave exposure \(W\). When mortality is low, it holds \(W\) fixed and reduces baseline exposure \(B\). Thus upward and downward fluctuations in the same death count are assigned to different processes. For \(\alpha\ne1\), the two assignments have different effects on the reported quantity \(B+W\) and on the predicted composition of survivors. The paper correctly discloses this as an attribution convention. Its finite-sample consequence deserves a quantitative test. [Weekly rule, §2.7.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517) · [Implementation of the two branches](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/evaluated-to-current.diff:1607)

I checked a one-week example from the stated model. Start with 5,000 people, unit-exponential frailty (\(\theta=1\)), \(\alpha=2\), known baseline increment \(b=0.001\), and **zero true wave exposure**. The true death probability is \(b/(1+b)\). For each possible count from zero through 54, I solved the paper’s weekly equation and weighted the result by its binomial probability. These counts cover all but \(1.3\times10^{-37}\) of the probability. The resulting mean assigned \(W\) was **0.0000881** and mean reported \(B+W\) was **0.0009125**, about 8.8% below the true 0.001. Ordinary gamma inversion on the same counts averaged **0.0010002**. The direction and magnitude depend on the scenario. This example establishes that exact reconstruction of observed weekly survival can coexist with biased reconstruction of the target, even with known \(k,\theta,\alpha\). [Survival law and reported target](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:519)

**Fix.** Fit weekly deaths using their binomial likelihood and estimate a prespecified wave profile across weeks. If a common external wave intensity is assumed across cohorts, impose and test that restriction jointly. Report zero-wave simulations with a *marked* wave window, vary counts and bin width, and publish error in \(B\), \(W\), and their contrast separately. A small numerical residual in the survival equation checks the solver only.

### 3. A successful fit still gives too little evidence of identification

The revised Czech scan is more stable than its predecessor. All 78 placements pass, and the 1950s dose-1 variance now rounds to zero across all placements. A flat sequence at the lower bound, however, can represent either precise evidence for negligible heterogeneity or inadequate information to distinguish zero from a consequential value. The scan’s pass rule checks success, status, and a degeneracy flag. It does not measure that distinction. There is a concrete implementation issue as well. After removing marked wave weeks, if fewer than three usable fitting rows remain, the nonlinear fitter returns the preliminary proportional fit and its diagnostics. ### 5. The target and the published recipe need one consistent definition

Box 2 calls KCOR a “depletion-adjusted marginal estimand.” Under the individual-level model, the inverse cumulative hazard is the hazard at reference frailty \(Z=1\). Under the wave extension it is \(B+W\), again evaluated at \(Z=1\). Marginal mortality is obtained by averaging **survival** over people. Also, each cohort’s frailty is separately normalized to mean one, so a difference in mean baseline risk can remain in \(k\). The method can validly report a ratio on this reference-frailty scale. Interpreting it as a population mortality ratio, or as evidence that a particular amount of selection was removed, requires more. The Gompertz baseline restriction supplies the identifying information: for any observed \(H\) and candidate \(q>0\), baseline \(\operatorname{expm1}(qH)/q\) with gamma variance \(q\) reproduces the same observed survival. [Box 2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:85) · [Parametric identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Wave target](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:525)

There are also instructions a reproducing analyst cannot simultaneously follow. Section 2.5 jointly refits \(k,\theta\), while the workflow figure says to **hold \(k\) fixed**. Section 2.4.4 says identification uses only designated quiet bins, while §2.5 also includes early anchors. Section 2.6 describes nonlinear correction exclusively after ordinary gamma inversion, while §2.5 uses the nonlinear law during fitting. The defaults table still lists \(\gamma\) as “Fixed implementation value,” although the positive-control replay records **0.001634615 per week**. **Fix.** Publish one authoritative algorithm for each mode, with the exact fit mask, slope, wave dates, anchor, fallback status, and estimator version attached to each result. Retain historical figures with explicit labels. My publication judgment is that **the proportional core is now defensible as a conditional, descriptive sensitivity analysis**.
