**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound. The central problem is deciding which frailty variance to put into it. Aggregate mortality curves can fit the proposed model closely even when a different process generated the curvature. The manuscript acknowledges this ambiguity, but also claims that its diagnostics will expose it and that failures generally produce conservative results. Those stronger claims do not follow. I would retain KCOR as a **model-dependent descriptive comparison** and revise the estimator, diagnostics, and validation before interpreting a Czech cohort contrast as evidence that depletion has been removed. ### 1. The same mortality data admit different depletion explanations

Let \(H(t)\) be the observed cumulative hazard. For **every** candidate variance \(q>0\), define
\[
B_q(t)=\frac{\exp(qH(t))-1}{q}.
\]
A unit-mean gamma population with variance \(q\) and baseline cumulative hazard \(B_q\) reproduces exactly \(H\). A homogeneous population with baseline cumulative hazard \(B_0=H\) reproduces it too. This gives a direct test of the manuscript’s reasoning. Take a curve that fits Gompertz–gamma perfectly. Give every person in a second population the time-varying hazard observed in the first. The two populations then have the same distribution of aggregate death counts. Every quiet-window fit, residual plot, and window-perturbation check computed from those counts has the same distribution. Yet only the first population underwent frailty depletion. The Gompertz restriction supplies identifying information, and the diagnostics cannot independently verify that restriction against this alternative. [Inversion and baseline restriction, §§2.4.2–2.4.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Claim of transparent failure, SI §S2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:26)

**Fix.** Define the reported quantity as the cumulative-hazard ratio obtained *under the specified baseline, frailty law, and windows*. Show the observed survival curves and how far KCOR moves when plausible baseline slopes, shapes, and windows change. Restrict claims of depletion removal to settings with independent support for those choices. Also distinguish selection on **mean** risk from selection on **variance**. If one cohort’s underlying risks are uniformly twice the other’s, setting mean frailty to one within each cohort puts that factor into its baseline. Perfect variance recovery still leaves an unanchored KCOR of two under no intervention effect. Anchoring would erase that level difference and would also erase a genuine constant twofold baseline-hazard effect. [Static healthy-vaccinee effect, §1.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:45) · [Unit-mean convention, §2.4.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:275) · [Anchoring definition, §2.1.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164)

### 2. Two identification claims are algebraically wrong inside the working model

Write \(h\) for the observed hazard in a quiet interval. The paper’s own equations imply
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log h(t)=\gamma-\theta h(t).
\]
Thus with \(\gamma>0\), zero frailty can produce a curved cumulative hazard. Conversely, with \(\theta k=\gamma\) and a Gompertz baseline from the origin, \(h(t)=k\) exactly. The observed cumulative hazard is then a straight line despite **positive** frailty. The proposition’s equivalence between nonzero curvature and positive frailty is false. With a known slope, a positive hazard, and exact continuous observations, the equation above gives a valid parametric identification argument. Whether weekly deaths estimate its terms precisely is a separate question. The normalized quiet-window target is itself Gompertz, so its expected cumulative path is proportional to \(e^{\gamma t}-1\), up to a window offset. A straight-line diagnostic needs an approximation bound or a transformed time axis. [Baseline and proposition, §§2.4.3–2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323) · [Linearity diagnostic, §2.6.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:456)

The claim of invariance to scaling the **observed** hazard also has an exact counterexample. Scaling \(h\) by \(c\) maps the Gompertz–gamma parameters from \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\). Applying the same \(c\) to both cohorts can preserve their normalized ratio while changing both inferred variances. Multiplying the **individual baseline hazard before depletion** is a different operation and may leave the actual frailty distribution fixed. **Fix both claims** by deriving the parameter transformation explicitly, publishing the numerical \(\gamma\) and its units, and reporting joint parameter profiles and sensitivity of KCOR to \(\gamma\). The supplement currently gives its value as “Fixed implementation value.” [Scale invariance claim, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Operational defaults, SI §S4.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

### 3. The weekly fitting algorithm and the final inversion use different mathematics

The paper defines a weekly observation \(x=-\log(1-d/N)\), an **integrated** hazard. If \(B\) is the reconstructed baseline cumulative hazard at the start of that week, exact gamma inversion gives
\[
B_{\rm next}-B=(1+\theta B)\frac{\exp(\theta x)-1}{\theta}.
\]
Step 2 instead uses \((1+\theta B)x\). This is a first-order approximation that under-reconstructs every positive increment when \(\theta>0\). Step 3 uses that reconstruction to estimate wave offsets, while the final reported KCOR uses exact exponentiation. A second problem arises if a wave precedes the first quiet window. The seed fit assumes an uninterrupted Gompertz cumulative history up to that window, and the pooled refit then holds its estimated \(k\) fixed. Later offset correction cannot freely repair an erroneous seed scale. [Weekly observations, §2.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Seed, recursion, and pooled refit, §2.5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Final inversion, §2.6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:434)

**Fix.** Use the exact weekly update throughout and integrate the Gompertz hazard between the same bin edges. Fit \(k\) and \(\theta\) jointly across quiet windows using weekly deaths and risk sets. One direct check is to compare \(B_q(t+1)-B_q(t)\), computed from the observed cumulative history for each candidate \(q\), with the integrated Gompertz increment in each quiet week. This naturally carries forward observed pre-window mortality. Test the revised fit on noiseless weekly probabilities with a known wave **before** the first quiet window. It should recover the generating parameters and cumulative path before sampling noise is introduced.

### 5. The Czech diagnostic gate and uncertainty intervals need evidence

The supplement marks every age band as passing, yet reports dose-2 variance estimates of \(7.01\times10^{-18}\) at ages 60–69 and \(3.46\times10^{-17}\) at 70–79. A boundary estimate can be quite informative if the upper bound is tight. It can also come from an almost flat objective. The reported yes/no entries cannot distinguish these cases. Age composition is consequential here too. The observed dose-0/dose-2 cumulative-hazard ratio is **0.3159** in the all-ages population and **above one in every displayed age band**. An all-ages gamma fit is a separate population comparison. **Fix.** Give counts, profile bounds, exact windows, numeric pass thresholds, and held-out-window predictions by cohort. Show common-age-standardized survival alongside the pooled result. Specify whether the anchor is a single week or a window mean, since the Methods define both. Identify the post-skip population as survivors to that landmark and show early deaths separately. The bootstrap resamples contiguous blocks of death counts and risk sets. At a join, the original block’s starting risk set can differ from the number still alive in the resampled cohort. Recomputing the denominator restores the accounting identity, but the copied deaths arose under a different denominator and calendar history. This matters precisely because the method assigns different roles to particular calendar weeks. **Every empirical coverage cell in Table 12 is blank**, including the correctly specified gamma null. **Fix.** For fixed cohorts with complete follow-up and independent individuals within a stratum, resample the vector of death-week counts plus final survivors multinomially, reconstruct risk sets in calendar order, and refit the whole procedure. If inference also includes shared calendar shocks or clustering, model that dependence explicitly. Publish coverage, Monte Carlo error, and gate acceptance, including coverage among accepted fits. ### 6. The optional wave correction needs a separate derivation

The wave model relates an **instantaneous observed excess hazard** to \(F(t)=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{alive at }t]\). The algorithm divides an already-inverted **cumulative excess** by the current \(F(t)\). Even on a single scale, dividing an accumulated excess by its endpoint multiplier does not undo a time-varying multiplier on earlier increments. There is a decisive unit test at \(\alpha=1\). Under proportional frailty, ordinary gamma inversion already recovers the baseline-plus-wave cumulative hazard. The manuscript calls this case inactive, yet \(E[Z\mid\text{alive}]<1\) after depletion, so the stated division generally increases positive excess again. At \(\alpha\ne1\), a wave acting through \(Z^\alpha\) also changes which people survive, invalidating automatic propagation of the ordinary gamma survivor distribution. [Wave model, §2.7.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Cumulative operator, §2.7.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module inactive in the Czech analysis, as the authors already conclude. For future use, derive survival and wave increments from one individual-level hazard model, propagate the survivor distribution through the wave, and require exact recovery at \(\alpha=1\). Validate the *corrected cumulative path*, in addition to recovering a simulated exponent. [Czech identification result, §3.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:914)

My first publication requirement would be a worked example with weekly counts, exact parameter settings, known baseline paths, and a second generating process with the same observed mortality. It would show readers exactly what KCOR recovers when its assumptions hold and exactly what the data leave unresolved.
