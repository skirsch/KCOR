## Addendum to the saved review of m94

**My recommendation remains major revision, but several objections in the earlier review should be retired.** The revised edition corrects the curvature proposition, the hazard-rescaling algebra, the weekly gamma update, and the survivor law used for wave correction. The outstanding issues concern how the parameters are fitted, what the controls establish, and which uncertainties the reported intervals cover.

### 1. Identification and the quiet-period diagnostic

- **“Curved cumulative hazard implies positive frailty.” Resolved.** The revised proposition gives \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) under the fixed-slope Gompertz–gamma model. It explicitly gives both counterexamples from the earlier review. Gompertz growth alone can curve cumulative hazard, and positive frailty can coexist with a constant observed hazard. [Revised proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354)
- **“The diagnostic tests for a straight line in event time.” Resolved in the stated method.** The reference is now the fitted cumulative Gompertz curve with the offset carried into each quiet segment. Linearity is expected on the transformed time axis \((e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). The supplement describes approximate straightness in Czech event time as an observation. Its all-Yes diagnostic table still gives no numerical deviations or acceptance thresholds with which to check those decisions. [Diagnostic definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:411) · [Czech diagnostic table](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:362)
- **“Misspecification must reveal itself or make the answer conservative.” The categorical claim has been withdrawn.** The paper now says contamination can pass the checks and that the adjusted contrast can be attenuated or amplified. I would retire that wording objection. The identification limit still applies: for any candidate \(\theta\), the inverted baseline can reproduce the same observed survival. The remaining claim of “stronger validation” from an absence of failures should be restricted to the specific alternatives those checks can detect. ### 2. Hazard scaling and the fixed Gompertz slope

**The multiplicative-invariance objection is resolved.** Multiplying the observed cohort hazard by \(c\) sends \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\). Under that exact transformation, each reconstructed cumulative hazard scales by \(c\), so a common multiplier cancels in the between-cohort ratio. Multiplying the individual baseline hazard before depletion preserves the generating frailty variance. The manuscript now distinguishes the two operations, and the supplement identifies which one the positive control uses. [Scaling statement](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Positive-control injection](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:128)

**The separate objection about slope sensitivity still applies.** The paper says errors in \(\gamma\) tend to cancel to first order and refers to a confirming sensitivity analysis. The Czech configuration supplies the missing default, **0.085/year**, but the supplement’s defaults table still says “Fixed implementation value.” Its sensitivity section instructs the reader to perturb \(\gamma\). I found no corresponding manuscript table giving the tested slopes, fitted variances and resulting ratios. The scaling identity above does not establish cancellation for a wrong Gompertz slope. Publish those results for each reported contrast. ### 4. Optional wave correction

**The objection to dividing accumulated excess by the current survivor moment is resolved.** The revised method uses \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\), solves for each week’s exposure, and carries the selected survivor distribution beyond wave exit. At fixed parameters, \(\alpha=1\) reduces exactly to ordinary gamma inversion. There is also substantive new validation. A regression test independently integrates survival for \(\alpha=2\) and checks recovery of a known post-wave increment of 0.05. A noiseless unequal-rate, partially marked wave gives KCOR **1.05050** against truth **1.05381**. Recovery is demonstrable, and the remaining discrepancy illustrates the cost of incomplete wave labeling. [Survivor-law derivation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:515) · [Independent integration test](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py:39) · [Known-contrast result](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/analysis/survivor_validation/mixed-results.json:214)

**The Czech activation objection still applies to the executable configuration.** The paper reports \(\alpha\) as unidentified and labels 1.18 a sensitivity value. The configuration forces 1.18 and also stores it in `identified_alpha`. The saved review’s finding about the jump near 1 is a *different* defect: changing \(\alpha\) switches which weeks contribute fitting residuals. The fixed-parameter inversion identity survives that finding. The repairs are to make wave-off the empirical primary run, clear the identified value, and test the refitted estimator through 1 using a common fitting mask for a specified wave.
