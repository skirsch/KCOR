**I would recommend major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is correct under its stated model, and this edition fixes two substantive errors identified in the previous review. The fitter now uses survival accumulated before the first quiet window and jointly refits the baseline rate and frailty variance. For a specified nonlinear wave, it now uses the survivor distribution during the wave and carries that distribution into later quiet weeks. These changes matter numerically. In the reported proportional-entry simulation, the mean estimate moved from 1.204 to 0.998 for a target of 1. I would retain both changes. The remaining scientific question is whether the mortality counts distinguish depletion from other changes in underlying mortality well enough to support the reported contrast. A good fit alone cannot do that. The current edition contains enough evidence to show where the distinction matters.

### 1. The identifying assumption does more work than the diagnostics can verify

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any chosen \(\theta>0\), the baseline \(B_\theta(t)=[\exp\{\theta H(t)\}-1]/\theta\) reproduces exactly the observed survival under gamma frailty. A homogeneous cohort with baseline \(H(t)\) reproduces it too. The fixed Gompertz slope in quiet weeks is therefore a substantive restriction on the possible explanations. Within the proportional model, the paper correctly derives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). At the same observed hazard and derivative, a slope change of \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The manuscript claims that slope errors tend to cancel in KCOR, but the supplement lists “Fixed implementation value” and describes a sensitivity check without showing the claimed range of horizon-specific contrasts. The supplied Table 9 replay does give a numerical value, \(0.001634615\) per week. [Inversion and slope claims](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297>) · [Identification proposition](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354>) · [SI defaults](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169>) · [Replay configuration](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/2-evaluated-handoff-evaluated-to-current.diff:262>)

The updated Czech scan is more stable than the previous review reported. All 78 fits passed. Across the 13 overlapping windows, the dose-1 variance ranged from 0.00 to 1.56 in the 1940s cohort and rounded to 0.00 throughout in the 1950s cohort. The fits share four early anchor weeks as well as much of their quiet data. A repeated boundary estimate can be compatible with little depletion or with limited information. **Fix:** Show the joint uncertainty in \(k,\theta\), predictions in held-out quiet weeks, and the **unanchored and anchored endpoint at the same horizon** across slopes, window placements, and inclusion of the early anchors. The endpoint may be stable even when \(\theta\) is poorly resolved. That is worth measuring directly. ### 2. The new wave equation is sound, but fitting survival exactly leaves the wave attribution uncertain

One weekly death count supplies one survival transition. KCOR can solve it for wave exposure because it specifies the baseline increment. If deaths fall below that baseline, it sets the wave increment to zero and lowers the baseline instead. There is a simple consequence even when the assumed baseline is correct. At \(\alpha=0\), let \(q_B\) be the predicted baseline integrated hazard and \(\hat q\) the sampled hazard. The rule assigns wave exposure \((\hat q-q_B)_+\). In a marked period with **zero true wave exposure**, ordinary sampling variation on either side of \(q_B\) therefore produces positive expected assigned wave exposure. Reproducing the observed survival to numerical precision cannot detect this error in attribution. [Allocation rule, §2.7.3](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517>) · [Implementation in the supplied diff](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/2-evaluated-handoff-evaluated-to-current.diff:1605>)

There is also a scope limit to the identification proposition. After a nonlinear wave, the survivor law is tilted by \(e^{-ZB-Z^\alpha W}\). In a subsequent quiet period, differentiation gives \(h'=\gamma h-h^2\operatorname{CV}^2(Z\mid\text{alive})\). The surviving coefficient of variation squared can differ from the starting \(\theta\). For example, with starting mean-one exponential frailty, \(B=0,\alpha=2,W=0.5\), I obtain 0.722 for that coefficient, against a starting value of 1. The new full-survivor fitter handles this distinction. The paper should explicitly restrict the simple derivative argument to histories that preserve the gamma form. [Proposition](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354>) · [Full-survivor fitting objective](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:381>)

**Fix:** Test a prespecified but empty wave, misdated waves, and plausible baseline errors. Report recovery of \(B\), \(W\), and KCOR separately, with the frequency of deficit weeks. Compare the weekly allocation with a count likelihood that pools information across wave weeks. For the Czech analysis, put the current **module-off** estimate beside the \(\alpha=1.18\) sensitivity result in Table 8. The revised text correctly labels 1.18 as a sensitivity choice. Both updated exponent objectives reached the lower grid boundary, and the positive-excess filter retained only 62 cohort-weeks from eight cohorts. There is no empirical basis here for preferring 1.18. ### 3. The implementation can silently return a different fit from the one requested

The supplied nonlinear fitter first obtains a proportional gamma fit. It then excludes marked wave rows from the nonlinear fitting set. If fewer than three eligible observations remain, it returns the original gamma parameters **and their original diagnostics**. A successful initial fit can consequently appear as a success even though the requested nonlinear fit never ran. This is a reachable code path. I found no evidence that it affected the six published Table 8 points. **Fix:** Return an explicit insufficient-information status after applying the nonlinear mask, record which model actually produced each estimate, and test a wave that covers all but two fitting rows. Apply the same reporting rule in the main analysis and bootstrap. ### 5. Anchoring can change the scientific conclusion

KCOR compares cumulative hazards at reference frailty \(Z=1\), with frailty scaled to mean one **separately in each cohort**. A difference in cohort mean risk can survive intact as a difference in \(k\). Anchoring then divides out any persistent proportional difference, including a genuine effect. It can also show movement even when subsequent hazards are identical. For example, if normalized cumulative hazards at the anchor are \(2a\) and \(a\), then both cohorts accumulate another \(a\), anchored KCOR falls from 1 to 0.75. A claim about post-anchor mortality requires examining the post-anchor increments themselves. The stabilization skip also changes the population to survivors at the rebased origin. [Estimand and anchoring](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:149>) · [Rebased origin](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:428>)

The paper demonstrates the practical importance unusually clearly. On the **same fitted curves**, moving the anchor by one week changes the mean under the largest dynamic contamination from **0.782 to 2.116**, reversing the direction. In the larger population experiment, the smallest tested addition already shifts the mean. The earlier 66%-of-baseline comparison supplies no general contamination threshold. Separately, the raw Czech dose-0/dose-2 cumulative-hazard ratio is **0.316 pooled** and **1.28–2.13 in every displayed age band**. A pooled frailty fit does not standardize age composition. [Anchor and population-size experiments](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:780>) · [Czech raw hazards, SI Table S8](</C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:423>)

**Publication priority:** At one prespecified horizon, lead with observed mortality risk using common age weights, then show unanchored KCOR, anchored KCOR, and the post-anchor cumulative increments for the same population. Label the skip, reference week, fit windows, and wave setting. That would let a reader see how much of the result comes from observed mortality, how much comes from the frailty assumption, and how much comes from the reference week.
