### Objections that still apply, or need a narrower statement

**1. Quiet-window prespecification and diagnostic independence — still material.** The Czech configuration itself says that calendar 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm, immediately above the configured windows. That comment does not establish how every final boundary was chosen. It does mean the manuscript’s claim of outcome-independent prespecification needs a dated rationale. A scan of overlapping windows on the same outcomes cannot settle that question. Report how each window was selected, show the contrasts for all candidate windows, and distinguish exploratory selection from a window rule tested on independent data. The paper now correctly acknowledges that a treatment effect resembling depletion can pass the checks and that neither overlap nor crossover guarantees attenuation. **2. Frailty precision and Gompertz-slope sensitivity — partly addressed.** The old window-scan ranges of 7.70 and 14.51 should be retired. In the current 78-row file, all fits pass, yet **47 of 78 raw variance estimates are at or below \(10^{-8}\)**. All 13 dose-2 fits in each birth decade land there. This could be compatible with genuinely negligible frailty; the pass calculation checks fit status and a degeneracy guard, so it cannot distinguish a precise zero from a wide range of plausible variances. The new information calculations are a real addition. The Czech slope is also recoverable from configuration as **0.085/year**, or **0.085/52 per week**. What remains missing from the cited supplement is the numerical slope sweep supporting the paper’s claim that misspecification cancels in ratios. Show uncertainty in both \(\theta\) and endpoint KCOR across windows and slopes. **3. Age composition and the meaning of “pooled/ASMR” — still applies.** This was omitted from the open answer. The SI still reports a raw dose-0/dose-2 cumulative-hazard ratio of **0.3159** in the full population, while **every displayed age-specific ratio exceeds one**. The authors acknowledge the composition difference. Table 8’s replay also shows exactly what its pooled number means: it takes a weighted geometric mean of subgroup anchored ratios, with weights derived from early mortality and risk sets. That construction does not give the ratio of two absolute mortality outcomes standardized to a common age distribution. The repair is to name the pooled quantity explicitly and show common-weight, age-standardized absolute outcomes alongside it. **7. Positive controls — the magnitude objection should be narrowed.** The generator multiplies the treated cohort’s hazard *among survivors* during weeks 20–79. Thus 0.8 and 1.2 are not themselves week-80 KCOR targets. But there is a useful check I omitted. Applying the programmed frailty variances to the generator’s **unrounded expected hazards**, using the replay’s skip and anchor, gives anchored ratios of **0.8411 for benefit and 1.1624 for harm**. The fitted results are **0.8336 and 1.1617**. Both are close to that explicitly defined benchmark, especially harm. I would credit this as more than directional detection. Label the benchmark, retain the matched raw endpoints, and identify this as a retrospective exercise because the quiet window begins after the evaluated endpoint.
