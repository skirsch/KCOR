**I would request major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound, and this edition makes two substantive improvements. It jointly estimates the baseline rate and frailty variance across quiet windows, and it carries the changed survivor distribution forward after a specified nonlinear mortality wave. The central weakness is the interpretation of the result. KCOR can describe how a cohort contrast changes under a specified frailty model. The mortality counts alone cannot establish how much of the observed contrast selection actually caused or how much the adjustment removed. Several validation claims still exceed what the experiments establish. ### 1. The quiet-period baseline assumption determines what counts as depletion

There is a direct way to see the identification problem. Take any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\). For **every** candidate variance \(\theta\geq0\), the baseline
\[
B_\theta(t)=\frac{\exp\{\theta H(t)\}-1}{\theta},\qquad B_0(t)=H(t),
\]
reproduces exactly the same observed survival under the corresponding gamma model. The fixed Gompertz slope supplies the missing information. The paper’s own quiet-period identity gives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). Holding the observed hazard and its derivative fixed, changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta/h\). That matters especially at low mortality rates. A common slope error can therefore affect two cohorts differently. Agreement with the fitted curve cannot detect a different mechanism that generates the same curve. The manuscript recognizes this limitation in §2.6.2, yet calls normalization a “prerequisite” in §1.5. [Inversion and baseline assumptions](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Diagnostic limitation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:426) · [Prerequisite claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:83)

**Fix.** Define the primary output as a *model-implied cumulative hazard ratio at reference frailty \(Z=1\)*. Frailty has mean one **separately in each cohort**, so differences in mean underlying health can remain in the fitted baseline rates. Publish observed survival beside KCOR, give the numerical slope and its justification, and show how the endpoint moves across plausible slopes and quiet-window baseline shapes. The supplement currently lists the slope as “Fixed implementation value.” [Frailty definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:275) · [Operational defaults](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

### 2. Anchoring can erase a persistent difference or reverse the apparent direction

The anchored statistic is \(R(t)/R(t_0)\). If one cohort’s normalized cumulative hazard is twice the other’s throughout follow-up, the anchored curve is exactly one throughout. It also retains events before the anchor in both cumulative totals. The manuscript supplies a more consequential example. In its historical contamination experiment, the same fitted curves give mean anchored KCOR **0.782** when the reference includes the first contaminated week and **2.116** when it ends one week earlier. That reversal requires no change in the later mortality experience. The stabilization skip makes a separate choice by starting accumulation among people who survived the skipped weeks. [Anchor definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:153) · [Anchor reversal, §3.3.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:780) · [Stabilization rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:432)

**Fix.** Report the unanchored ratio, the anchor value and the anchored ratio together. If the question concerns mortality accumulated *after* \(t_0\), report the contrast of increments \(B_A(t)-B_A(t_0)\) and \(B_B(t)-B_B(t_0)\). Show deaths and survivors through the skip. These changes let the reader distinguish a level difference, a change in relative level and a post-reference mortality difference.

### 3. Passing diagnostics gives limited assurance about precision or interval coverage

In the Czech window scan, **78 of 78 fits passed**, although one dose-1 group’s fitted variance ranged from **0 to 1.56** and another stayed at zero across all windows. The windows overlap and share early fitting observations. The new simulations give genuine evidence of improvement. Correcting proportional exposure before quiet-window entry moved a sampled mean from **1.20386 to 0.99774**. In the main nonlinear-wave experiment the revised mean was **1.00093**, but the nominal 95% intervals covered in **90 of 100** datasets. In the stress experiment the mean was **1.04019** and coverage was **48 of 50**. Those results show why mean bias, individual-dataset coverage and successful fitting need separate reporting. The paper’s older 189/200 and 502/600 coverage studies both predate the current weekly fitting equations. [Window scan, SI §S5.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:302) · [Current simulation results, SI §S8](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457) · [Earlier coverage studies](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:587)

**Fix.** Publish numerical acceptance rules and uncertainty profiles for both \(\theta\) and the endpoint. A poorly resolved variance can coexist with a usable endpoint. Test the exact published fitter on independently sampled cohorts at registry-like event rates, including true zero variance, quiet-period contamination and misspecified baselines. Report the fraction accepted, bias, interval width and coverage among accepted analyses, with all attempted datasets accounted for. The paper’s model-conditional information calculation is a good starting point. [Information and precision, §2.9](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:592)

### 4. The controls answer narrower questions than the headline comparisons suggest

The main Cox grid gives KCOR the **generating frailty variance** in each cohort. The shared-frailty comparator curve is interpolated from one attenuation factor. This checks the inversion with known inputs. It cannot establish the performance advantage of estimating frailty from mortality counts. Moreover, cohorts with different frailty distributions can genuinely have different marginal survival and restricted mean survival time even with identical individual hazards at fixed frailty. Calling that difference “bias” evaluates those summaries against a target they do not estimate. [Cox experiment and supplied variances](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:627) · [Comparator construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:664) · [RMST comparison](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1189)

The age-shift control has **no known exact KCOR target**, as the authors acknowledge. All six updated intervals containing one cannot establish equivalence. The positive control is informative about direction, but its generator multiplies the weekly hazard *among survivors*. That differs from multiplying each individual’s hazard and allowing subsequent selection to evolve. Its week-80 estimate also uses a quiet window beginning in week 81. **Fix.** Retain these as an empirical stability check and a retrospective signal-detection example. For quantitative validation, generate individual event histories, derive each method’s target under that generator, estimate all unknown inputs, and report recovery of effect magnitude as well as direction. [Age-shift results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:745) · [Positive-control generator](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:128) · [Fit timing](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:754)

### 5. The nonlinear wave calculation is conditional on an allocation of deaths the registry cannot verify

During a wave, weekly survival supplies one equation for two exposure increments, proportional baseline \(b\) and wave exposure \(w\). KCOR obtains a solution by fixing \(b\) to its fitted Gompertz value. When mortality falls below that prediction, it sets \(w=0\) and reduces \(b\). This is a coherent attribution convention, and the paper now describes it explicitly. An exact numerical solution still inherits that convention. The Czech data provide little independent leverage on the exponent. The updated objective reaches the lower boundary even on the widened grid, and the positive-excess filter retains only **62 cohort-weeks across eight cohorts**. The authors’ conclusion that \(\alpha\) is unidentified is warranted. [Weekly survival equation and deficit rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517) · [Czech exponent results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:828)

**Fix.** Keep the proportional analysis primary in the Czech example. Show how each endpoint changes across assumed exponents, baseline paths and wave dates, with the number of deficit weeks for each cohort. Label the reported \(1.18\) analysis as sensitivity throughout. Where an external intervention-effect scale is used to estimate \(\alpha\), propagate uncertainty in that scale and in the fitted exponent. The stated bootstrap currently holds both fixed. [Czech sensitivity specification](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:747) · [Bootstrap specification](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:573)

My recommendation is to retain KCOR as a **descriptive sensitivity analysis** and narrow the claim of selection correction. Its strongest contribution is making assumptions about depletion explicit enough to calculate their consequences. Showing readers how far the answer moves when those assumptions change would make the paper substantially stronger. I reviewed the supplied combined PDF, manuscript sources and figures read-only. The numerical experiments above are reported results from that edition, without an independent code or registry-data rerun.
