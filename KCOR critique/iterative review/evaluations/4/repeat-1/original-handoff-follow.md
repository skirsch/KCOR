## Addendum to the open review — edition m94

The open review stands separately. On rechecking the earlier objections, I would **withdraw** the objections to the hazard-scaling proposition, the Euler update, the frozen baseline rate in the core fit, the calendar-week bootstrap, and the old cumulative wave-correction formula. I would also withdraw the claim that Figure 1’s arithmetic is unexplained. The objections about attribution, diagnostic reliability, and the interpretation of the controls still apply, with narrower wording in several cases.

### 1. Hazard scaling, curvature, and the Gompertz diagnostic

**The two mathematical objections are resolved.** The paper now states that multiplying the *observed* hazard by \(c\) changes \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\), while multiplying the *individual baseline* preserves the generating frailty variance. The revised proposition also gives \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) and explicitly covers both previous counterexamples. A common observed-hazard multiplier cancels in the ideal core ratio because the reconstructed cumulative hazards both scale by \(c\). [Scaling and proposition, §2.4.4](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345)

**The slope-sensitivity and straight-line diagnostic objections are only partly resolved.** Section 2.6.2 correctly asks for agreement with the fitted cumulative Gompertz curve. The production diagnostic still computes its maximum deviation from an ordinary straight line fitted against elapsed years. Also, §2.4.3 claims stability over a prespecified range of \(\gamma\), while the cited supplement tells the reader to perturb \(\gamma\) without giving the requested cohort-specific profiles. For a fixed observed \(h,h'\), changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the local implied \(\theta\) by \(\delta\gamma/h\). Cohorts with different hazard levels can therefore respond quite differently. I would publish the profiles and compute the diagnostic against the same curve used for fitting. ### 2. Attribution, conservativeness, and anchoring

**The attribution objection still applies. The objection to an explicit guarantee of conservative bias is largely resolved.** Sections 5.1–5.2 now say that bias can go in either direction and that a genuine effect resembling depletion can pass the diagnostics. That directly answers the earlier wording criticism. The scientific limitation remains: a homogeneous cohort given the fitted marginal hazard has exactly the same weekly death probabilities as the frailty model. The stronger statement in §4.1 that residual differences are unexplained by depletion still exceeds what those counts establish. Separately normalizing mean frailty to one leaves cohort-level vulnerability differences in \(k_d\). [Revised failure statement](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:955) · [Quiet-window treatment effects](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:973) · [Residual-contrast claim](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:872)

**The skip and anchor objections still apply.** The paper now explicitly acknowledges missing acute effects in skipped weeks. Its reported post-skip trajectory is conditional on survival to that landmark, and dividing by the four-week ratio removes a genuine constant ratio as well as a compositional one. This matters in the paper’s own experiment: moving the reference from immediately after to immediately before the first contaminated week changed mean KCOR from **0.782 to 2.116** on the same fitted curves. ### 5. Bootstrap and coverage

**The block-of-weeks objection is resolved, and “no demonstrated coverage” is obsolete.** Table 8 resamples disjoint person-outcome cells, restores overlapping memberships, reconstructs risk sets, and refits. The pooled draw allows cohort sizes to vary, as the revised text specifies. Current nonlinear validation gives **90/100** coverage in the main design and **48/50** in the stress design. The reported Monte Carlo intervals are wide enough that neither result alone settles nominal 95% calibration. [Resampling design](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:562) · [Count reconstruction](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:63) · [Current coverage experiments](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457)

**Coverage after diagnostic selection remains unresolved.** Table 8’s code records flags, can use a zero-variance fallback, and takes percentiles over the draws. The paper reports no flagged fits in that particular run, so this does not invalidate its reproduced quantiles. It does mean that the run cannot establish calibration in settings where the gate rejects analyses. The stress archive records **241 resamples with insufficient signal** despite zero flagged original fits. Report the probability of producing an interval and its coverage among accepted original datasets under the exact release rule. Person resampling also conditions on the observed calendar environment; inference over shared shocks would require a model for those shocks. ### 6. Optional wave correction

**The original mathematical objection is resolved.** The revised method uses \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\), carries past selection forward, and solves conditional survival week by week. This accounts for the altered survivor distribution after a nonlinear wave. At \(\alpha=1\), \(L\) depends only on \(B+W\), so ordinary gamma inversion recovers the total exactly. The independent continuity test checks a known post-wave increment, including a zero-death week. [Revised equations](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517) · [Numerical test](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py:32)

**The empirical attribution objection remains.** An observed weekly survival ratio gives one equation for baseline and wave exposure. The method supplies the missing restriction by prescribing the Gompertz baseline and changing the allocation in deficit weeks. The authors now identify that convention explicitly. The new tests establish recovery in specified synthetic settings and at selected endpoints; they cannot validate the actual week-by-week allocation in the registry. The Czech exponent objectives reach the search boundary, the manuscript reports \(\alpha\) as unidentified, and the shipped Czech configuration forces 1.18. The defensible interpretation of that run is sensitivity to a supplied exponent.
