### 1. Changing the wave exponent changes which observations fit the baseline

At \(\alpha=1\), the proportional fitter includes the early fitting weeks even when they fall inside a marked wave. Immediately above 1, the nonlinear fitter excludes those same weeks and uses them to reconstruct wave exposure. An exponent profile can therefore compare different fitting samples. The identity \(L(B,W)=L(B+W,0)\) at \(\alpha=1\) is correct. It does not justify changing which weeks are assumed to follow an undisturbed Gompertz baseline. ### 2. The proof identifies starting frailty only under a proportional exposure history

The revised identity \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) is correct in the proportional gamma model, including after proportional shocks. After a nonlinear wave, however, selection changes the surviving frailty distribution. In a subsequent quiet period, let \(m_t\) and \(v_t\) be the mean and variance of frailty among survivors. The paper’s own survivor law gives
\[
h'=\gamma h-\frac{v_t}{m_t^2}h^2.
\]
The derivative now identifies the **current survivors’ squared coefficient of variation**. For example, starting with mean-one gamma frailty of variance 2, with \(\alpha=2\) and cumulative exposures \(B=W=0.1\), I obtain \(v_t/m_t^2=1.586\). Recovering the original 2 requires information about the preceding wave and the assumed allocation of deaths to it. The new nonlinear fitter attempts that reconstruction, but the quiet-period proposition alone cannot establish it. [Identification proposition](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354) · [Nonlinear survival law and attribution convention](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517)

**Fix:** state the history assumption in the proposition and give separate identification conditions for the nonlinear model. Define the output plainly as the ratio of model-implied cumulative hazards at \(Z=1\), conditional on surviving the skip. Since each cohort sets its own mean frailty to one, a difference in *mean* baseline vulnerability can remain in \(k_d\). Stable fits cannot establish that a residual contrast measures intervention harm or benefit. The limitations section recognizes this ambiguity more accurately than the description of static healthy-vaccinee selection in the introduction. ### 3. The proportional gap calculation can accumulate exposure from noise

At quiet-window re-entry, the code calculates a raw cumulative offset, adds the positive part of its change, and saves the previous **raw** offset for the next comparison. Suppose successive raw offsets are \(0,-a,0\). The decline contributes zero, but the return to the original level adds \(a\). The fitted history now contains a persistent positive exposure despite a final raw discrepancy of zero. Repeated fluctuations can accumulate this way. The stress results make the issue worth testing: 47 of 50 nonlinear stress datasets had a clamped gap, and mean KCOR was 1.0402 for a truth of 1. Those figures do not isolate clamping as the cause. [Proportional offset calculation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/code/KCOR.py:1517) · [Stress results](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/analysis/survivor_validation/stress-results.json:34)

**Fix:** if additional exposures must be nonnegative and persistent, fit their cumulative totals jointly under that constraint. If below-baseline gaps are scientifically plausible, also evaluate signed deviations subject to nonnegative *total* weekly hazard. Show how the endpoint changes under the two assumptions. Include a zero-shock simulation with several noisy gaps, where any systematic accumulated excess can be measured directly.

### 4. A diagnostic “pass” gives much less assurance than the manuscript suggests

The quiet-window scan did produce 78 passes in 78 attempts. Its pass indicator checks optimizer success, status, and a parameter guard. It does not measure how precisely the data determine \(\theta\). Across those passing windows, the fitted variance for 1940s births at dose 1 ranged from effectively zero to **1.56**. The edition’s own information calculation makes the distinction quantitative: one design with **one million people** and true \(\theta=0.25\) has model-conditional standard error **1.13** for \(\theta\). A stable endpoint can coexist with a poorly determined frailty variance. There is also a concrete failure path. If fewer than three eligible fitting rows remain after excluding the wave, the nonlinear fitter can return its preliminary proportional fit and diagnostics unchanged. Separately, a fit that hits the upper variance bound can return an applied variance of zero. Those outcomes have different scientific meanings from a well-determined estimate near zero. [Too-few-rows fallback](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/code/kcor_survivor.py:247) · [Upper-bound fallback](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/code/KCOR.py:1629)

**Fix:** report three results separately for each cohort: numerical convergence, precision of \(\theta\), and sensitivity of the KCOR endpoint. Return an explicit “nonlinear fit unavailable” status when it cannot be performed. Show a profile over \((k,\theta)\) at several plausible Gompertz slopes, with boundary and clamping frequencies. Apply the same reporting rule in the bootstrap and state what happens to failed draws.
