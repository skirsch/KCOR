### 2. The ordinary workbook gives a materially different 95% interval from the manuscript

For the week-80 benefit control, the shipped workbook reports **0.8336 [0.8144, 0.8533]**. Table 9 reports the same point estimate with a refitted person-bootstrap interval of **[0.7741, 0.8971]**. The workbook calculates its point estimate as a ratio divided by an estimated anchor ratio. This affects what a reader gets through the ordinary software entry point. **Fix:** make the workbook’s primary interval propagate the full estimator, including the anchor, refitting, and pooling. ### 3. The baseline and quiet windows supply the identification

Given observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\), every candidate \(\theta>0\) yields a baseline \(B_\theta(t)=[e^{\theta H(t)}-1]/\theta\) that reproduces the same survival. The paper correctly shows what resolves this ambiguity *under its model*. With fixed Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the instantaneous quiet-period hazard implies \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). Holding the observed trajectory fixed, changing the slope by \(\Delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\Delta\gamma/h\). Cohorts with different mortality levels therefore have different sensitivity. The manuscript claims first-order cancellation in ratios and a prespecified slope sensitivity result. The cited supplement instructs the analyst to check the slope, and the Czech configuration says 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm. **Fix:** disclose the outcome-informed choice, give an independent basis for the slope and windows, and show endpoint estimates and held-out predictions across those choices. The positive control illustrates the stakes: its control generator uses a constant baseline and \(\theta=0.5\), while the Gompertz fit returns **1.393** with bootstrap interval **[1.065, 1.702]** despite detecting the injected effect in the expected direction. ### 4. Wave inversion has improved, but the exponent-recovery test uses a different survivor moment

The revised weekly inversion solves the stated survival equation for a supplied exponent. That addresses the previous review’s objection to the in-wave approximation. But the supplied exponent-estimation code uses a **mean-one gamma moment**, and its synthetic generator uses that same function to create the signal. Under the manuscript’s survival law, the required moment is \(E[Z^\alpha e^{-ZB-Z^\alpha W}]/L(B,W)\). A simple check exposes the difference. At wave entry with \(W=0\) and \(\alpha=1\), surviving mean frailty is \(1/(1+\theta B)\), while the exponent-estimation helper returns exactly one for every cohort. Synthetic recovery with that helper validates the calculation that generated the synthetic signal. **Fix:** derive the exponent objective from the same conditional survivor law used for inversion, generate weekly deaths from that law, withhold the generating exponent during fitting, and evaluate recovery with the external intervention-effect scale specified. Retain the Czech conclusion of non-identification. ### 5. The new validation supports conditional performance, with measurable limits

I recomputed coverage from the archived interval rows. In the main nonlinear-wave design, mean KCOR was **1.00093** for truth one, and the nominal 95% interval covered **90 of 100** datasets. Its Monte Carlo interval, **82.4%–95.1%**, leaves appreciable uncertainty about calibration. In the stress design, coverage was **48 of 50**, but mean KCOR was **1.04019**, 47 originals had a clamped gap, and 241 resamples had an insufficient-signal flag. One reason clamping matters is visible in the proportional fitter. It sums positive changes between successive *raw* offsets. Raw offsets of 0.02, 0.01, 0.02 consequently produce an applied final offset of 0.03, although the final reconstructed offset is 0.02. **Fix:** fit cumulative gap exposures under a coherent nondecreasing constraint, compare against the current rule under sampling noise, and report bias, width, coverage, and fallback frequency together. Distinguish coverage among accepted original datasets from the handling of failed resamples inside their intervals.
