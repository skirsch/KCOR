### Estimator and identification

1. **Frailty identification and cancellation of slope error — still applies.** Under the fixed-slope model, \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). At the same observed hazard and derivative, changing \(\gamma\) by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). Cohorts with different hazard levels can therefore respond differently to the same slope error. This alone does not determine the size of the change in their *ratio*. Section 2.4.3 still says a prespecified sensitivity analysis confirms cancellation. The supplement’s defaults table says only “Fixed implementation value,” although the Czech configuration gives **0.085 per year**. The new information calculation quantifies precision conditional on model inputs. I would retain the identification proposition and publish the actual slope grid, joint \(k,\theta\) uncertainty, and KCOR at each reporting horizon to support the cancellation claim. ### Interpretation of the reported mortality contrast

11. **Reference frailty, average health, anchoring and the stabilization skip — the distinctions still apply.** The model normalizes frailty to mean one **separately within each cohort**. Its inverted cumulative hazard is evaluated at that cohort’s \(z=1\). Equal relative frailty variance can coexist with a persistent baseline hazard ratio \(c\) between groups with different average health. Exact inversion retains \(c\); anchoring gives \(c/c=1\). The skip also sets early increments to zero while early deaths still remove people from later risk sets. The accumulated curve therefore starts among landmark survivors. M94 specifies these operations. The remaining fix is to label empirical curves accordingly and show observed deaths, risk sets and survival from enrollment alongside the post-skip unanchored and anchored estimates.
