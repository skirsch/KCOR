**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s core gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound. If individual hazard is \(Zb(t)\), frailty \(Z\) has mean one and gamma variance \(\theta\), and \(H(t)\) is the observed cohort cumulative hazard, then \(\{\exp(\theta H)-1\}/\theta\) recovers the baseline cumulative hazard for \(Z=1\). That is a legitimate model-based descriptive contrast. The central scientific problem is the claim that aggregate mortality and the proposed diagnostics can establish when the recovered curve represents depletion. There are mechanisms that pass the fit checks exactly and give the opposite substantive interpretation. ### 1. A genuine time-varying effect can look exactly like frailty depletion

Let \(b(t)=ke^{\gamma t}\) and \(B(t)=\int_0^t b(s)\,ds\). The paper’s gamma-frailty model gives observed hazard \(b(t)/(1+\theta B(t))\). Now consider a homogeneous cohort in which everyone has baseline hazard \(b(t)\) and experiences a real time-varying hazard multiplier \(r(t)=1/(1+\theta B(t))\). The two mechanisms give **identical survival curves, weekly death probabilities, and risk-set distributions**. Every quiet window can fit exactly. KCOR recovers \(B(t)\) in both cases, removing the real effect in the second. More observations, smaller residuals, and additional windows from the same trajectory cannot distinguish them. The paper acknowledges attribution ambiguity in §4.1, yet §5.2 says overlap will reveal itself through degraded fit or instability. The counterexample disproves that assurance. [Fitting equation, §2.5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Acknowledged ambiguity, §4.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:934) · [Conservativeness claim, §5.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1040)

**Fix.** Define the output as the contrast *under the specified depletion model*. Describe a diagnostic pass as compatibility with that model. Include the observationally identical example as a required stress test, and show how the scientific conclusion changes under each explanation. An interpretation that distinguishes the mechanisms needs independent information about baseline mortality, frailty, or exposure timing.

### 2. Two claims about curvature are mathematically incorrect

Under the exact Gompertz–gamma model, the observed instantaneous hazard \(h\) satisfies \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\). Thus, with known \(\gamma\) and an exactly observed positive hazard, \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). This gives a short identification proof *within* the restricted model and exposes two errors in the stated proposition. At \(\theta=0\), Gompertz aging alone gives a curved cumulative hazard. At \(\theta=\gamma/k>0\), depletion exactly offsets aging, \(h(t)=k\), and the cumulative hazard is linear. Nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature is neither necessary for model identification nor equivalent to positive frailty variance. After correct inversion, the expected quiet-window curve is \(B(t)\), which itself has Gompertz curvature. [Baseline and identification proposition, §2.4.3–4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:321) · [Linearity diagnostic, §2.6.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:454)

The claimed invariance to multiplying the **observed** hazard by \(c\) also fails. Within the paper’s own family, \(ch\) has parameters \(k'=ck\) and \(\theta'=\theta/c\). Even scaling both cohorts identically changes both fitted frailty variances. The normalized **ratio** can remain invariant because both reconstructed baselines scale by \(c\). An intervention that multiplies individual hazard *before* depletion is a different operation and can preserve \(\theta\). **Fix.** State these two transformations explicitly, replace the curvature proposition with the identity above, and assess departures from the fitted Gompertz curve. Use parameter profiles over plausible \(\gamma\) values to show how much information the finite weekly counts actually supply. [Effect-scale invariance claim, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345)

### 3. The weekly reconstruction and final inversion use different arithmetic

The manuscript defines a weekly increment \(u=-\log(1-d/N)\). For reconstructed baseline cumulative hazard \(B_j\), exact gamma inversion requires
\[
1+\theta B_{j+1}=(1+\theta B_j)e^{\theta u_j}.
\]
Step 2 instead multiplies by \(1+\theta u_j\). That is a first-order approximation. For positive increments and \(\theta>0\), it systematically reconstructs a smaller path than the final exponential inversion. The discrepancy feeds directly into the wave offsets and pooled refit. The seed fit also compares a weekly integrated hazard with a pointwise hazard. These differences may be small at low weekly risk, but their size is checkable. **Fix.** Use exact interval survival probabilities for fitting and the same exact inversion for reconstruction and reporting. Verify agreement across bin widths and report how the change affects the fitted offsets and KCOR. [Weekly hazard definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Reconstruction and offsets](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:388) · [Final inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:432)

### 4. The optional wave correction fails its own identity case

The manuscript says the module is effectively inactive at \(\alpha=1\). In that case a wave proportional to individual frailty already satisfies the core multiplicative model, so ordinary gamma inversion recovers the baseline-plus-wave cumulative hazard. Yet the optional formula divides positive excess **again** by \(F(t;1)=E[Z\mid\text{alive at }t]\). With positive frailty variance and prior depletion, this mean is below one, so the formula amplifies the recovered excess. More generally, dividing accumulated excess by the current \(F(t;\alpha)\) also reweights excess from earlier weeks. The manuscript motivates \(F\) using an *instantaneous* excess-hazard equation, which does not justify that cumulative operation. [Hazard model and inactive-case claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Cumulative correction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module inactive for the Czech analysis, as the authors conclude. Before using it elsewhere, derive the survivor distribution and interval probabilities under a joint individual hazard such as \(Zb(t)+Z^\alpha a(t)\). Require exact agreement with core inversion at \(\alpha=1\), then test recovery of the *corrected trajectory*, as distinct from recovery of \(\alpha\). [Czech non-identification result](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:902)

### 5. The empirical checks show more uncertainty than the conclusions convey

- **Anchoring can hide a persistent difference by construction.** If two recovered baseline curves differ by a constant factor of 1.3, unanchored KCOR is 1.3 and anchored KCOR is 1 throughout. A flat age-shift control therefore tests post-anchor drift only. Even on the reported control scale, two 2022 comparisons end at 1.0756 and 1.0911, with both reported intervals excluding one. The sensitivity heatmap also puts the 2021 dose 2 versus 0 contrast at about **1.27 with two baseline weeks and 1.11 with eight** at zero quiet-start offset. Report raw, unanchored, and anchored curves together, with an explicit tolerance for meaningful drift. Resolve whether the anchor is a single week or a reference-window mean. [Anchor definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [Window-mean alternative](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:619) · [Control results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1228) · [Sensitivity heatmap](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/figures/fig_sensitivity_overview.png)
- **The Czech diagnostic decisions need an audit trail.** Every age band passes, while dose-2 variance estimates include \(7.01\times10^{-18}\) and \(3.46\times10^{-17}\). A boundary estimate can be compatible with negligible depletion or with very weak information. A “Yes” entry cannot distinguish them. Composition also matters materially here. The raw dose 0 versus dose 2 hazard ratio is **0.3159 pooled**, while **every displayed age-specific ratio exceeds 1**. Publish parameter profiles, numerical pass criteria, window-specific decisions, and common-age-standardized contrasts. Define the order of fitting and standardization because the inversion is nonlinear. [Diagnostic gate](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:364) · [Fitted variances](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:398) · [Raw age-specific and pooled data](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:423)
- **The Cox comparison uses different truths.** In the paper’s simulation, cohort A has no frailty variation and cohort B has variance \(\theta\). Their individual hazards agree at equal frailty, but their *marginal* hazard ratio B/A is \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\). A cohort-only Cox fit can therefore fall below one in data generated exactly as intended. Scoring it against one conflates a genuine marginal survival difference with estimator error. Score each method against its own population target, and label the plotted shared-frailty curve as an illustration throughout or actually refit it across the grid. ### 6. The reported 95% intervals still need calibration

Moving calendar or event-time blocks of deaths and risk sets changes where aging, waves, and quiet windows fall. Retaining the sampled risk sets can break continuity between blocks. Rebuilding them restores the accounting identity, but does not establish the claimed sampling covariance. The coverage table contains **no empirical coverage values**, including for the correctly specified gamma null. **Fix.** For fixed cohorts with complete follow-up and independent people within strata, resample the counts across death-week categories plus the end-of-follow-up survivor category using a multinomial bootstrap. This preserves week labels and gives coherent risk sets. Refit the full pipeline, including anchoring and diagnostic decisions. Report coverage against analytic or sufficiently precise simulation truth, Monte Carlo uncertainty, pass rates, and coverage among passing analyses. Until then, the plotted intervals are exploratory. [Bootstrap algorithm](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:631) · [Coverage table, combined PDF p. 55](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

The strongest publishable version is a **sensitivity analysis** showing how much an observed cumulative contrast changes under explicit assumptions about baseline aging and frailty. I would retain the core inversion, correct the discrete estimator, and put observed absolute mortality alongside the transformed curves. The Czech data can then show readers how consequential those assumptions are. They cannot establish, from fit alone, which part of the observed difference was selection or an intervention effect.
