### 4. The post-wave equation is sound conditional on an exit exposure that the in-wave calculation has yet to recover

For individual cumulative hazard \(ZB+Z^\alpha W\), the revision correctly uses \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) to propagate survival after a wave. During the wave, however, it estimates \(W\) by dividing accumulated excess by the **current week’s** approximate frailty moment. The manuscript now acknowledges that approximation. The new test takes the algorithm’s own estimated exit exposure, generates subsequent survival conditional on that value, and recovers a known later baseline increase. That verifies the post-wave inverse. An incorrect exit exposure would pass the same test. [In-wave approximation and post-wave equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:576) · [Construction of the test](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/changes.diff:2123)

There is also a concrete residual arithmetic mismatch. The core fit uses the exact weekly Gompertz increment \(ke^{\gamma s}(e^\gamma-1)/\gamma\). The wave reference uses \(ke^{\gamma s}\). For \(\gamma>0\), the latter is smaller, so even a noise-free, wave-free Gompertz trajectory can acquire positive “wave excess” inside a designated window. **Fix.** Use the same exact weekly increment throughout. With a prespecified baseline path and exponent, estimate cumulative wave exposure from the observed survival using \(L(B_t,W_t)\) during the wave as well. Test the entire trajectory against independently generated \(B_t,W_t\), including zero-wave weeks, and measure error in the final cohort contrast. ### 5. The validation examples exercise different estimators and different targets

The Cox grid now discloses that KCOR receives the **generating frailty variances**. That is a valid check of inversion with known inputs. It provides no evidence about estimating frailty from registry counts. Further, in the stated simulation the marginal cohort hazard ratio is \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\), where \(B(t)\) is the common baseline cumulative hazard. A cohort-only Cox fit below one reflects a real difference between survivor populations. Calling its distance from one “estimator bias” scores it against the individual treatment-effect null. The positive control has a related issue. It multiplies the **observed survivor hazard** during an interval, so the multiplier alone does not specify the true cumulative KCOR at week 80. The empirical workflow illustration also uses an earlier monthly smoother and was selected partly for a final ratio near one. **Fix.** Choose one primary estimator and run it through its ordinary entry point with the generating parameters hidden. The supplied implementation defaults to the early fixed-rate fit, while the Table 8 replay explicitly substitutes the joint fit described in Methods. Label each existing result by fitter and version. For new controls, publish the generating cumulative target, then compare estimated KCOR with that target over time. Include a time-varying effect that mimics depletion exactly. For baseline \(b(t)\) with cumulative \(B(t)\), a homogeneous cohort with multiplier \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\) has the same observed mortality as the gamma-frailty cohort. Both can pass every trajectory-based check. Section 5.2 now acknowledges this limitation accurately, and it should govern the validation claims throughout. **Scientific conclusion.** KCOR can answer a useful question: how much would the cumulative cohort contrast change under a specified Gompertz–gamma depletion model? This edition improves the mathematics behind that calculation. Its own results also show why a passing fit, an interval containing one, or a successful known-parameter inversion cannot establish that selection caused the observed mortality pattern. The most consequential revision would be a **current, core-only, end-to-end validation** with prespecified reporting rules, followed by an empirical presentation of observed absolute mortality, unanchored KCOR, and anchored KCOR together.
