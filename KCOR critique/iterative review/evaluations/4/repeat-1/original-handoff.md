**I would recommend major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound, and this edition fixes several substantive problems in the previous review. ### 1. The data identify a parameter within the model, but cannot attribute the curvature

Let \(B(t)=\int_0^t k e^{\gamma s}\,ds\). A mean-one gamma-frailty population has observed hazard \(h(t)=B'(t)/(1+\theta B(t))\). A homogeneous population in which everyone experiences that same time-varying hazard has **exactly the same survival curve and weekly death probabilities**. Every quiet-window residual, stability check, and bootstrap based on those counts has the same distribution in the two populations. In the second population, inversion can remove a genuine time-varying exposure effect. The supplement recognizes this; the main text still says residual differences represent contrasts unexplained by depletion and calls the target “marginal.” Under the structural model, the reconstructed hazard is for reference frailty \(Z=1\). Each cohort’s separate mean-one convention can also put differences in average vulnerability into its fitted \(k_d\). [Estimand](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:89) · [Interpretation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:872) · [Supplement’s explicit qualification](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:26)

**Fix:** Define the target throughout as the ratio of post-skip, reference-frailty cumulative hazards under the stated model. Show observed survival, unanchored KCOR, and anchored KCOR together. The skip conditions on surviving to the landmark. Dividing by the four-week ratio removes any persistent multiplicative difference, including a real one. Anchor choice matters empirically here: in the paper’s own contamination experiment, moving the reference one week changed mean KCOR from **0.782 to 2.116** on the same fitted curves. ### 2. A successful fit can survive the loss of all identifying weeks

I exercised the production fitting entry point with 24 weekly bins, quiet weeks 0–11, a marked nonlinear wave covering those same weeks, four early anchors, and \(\alpha=1.18\). The nonlinear fitting mask correctly excluded all 12 weeks. The call nevertheless returned **`status="ok"`, `success=True`, `n_fit=12`, and \(\hat\theta=2.0\)**. With fewer than three eligible rows the nonlinear fitter returns the preliminary proportional fit **and its success diagnostics**. The 12 reported observations were precisely the weeks excluded from the requested fit. This demonstrates a software defect independently of any dispute about the Czech data. A separate issue concerns proportional gap offsets. The code accumulates positive changes from the previous **raw** offset. If successive raw offsets are \(0,-u,0\), with \(u>0\), the final applied offset is \(+u\), even though reconstructed exposure has returned to baseline. Noise followed by recovery can thus create a permanent positive offset. Fit nonnegative gap exposures jointly against survival at the boundaries, or explicitly compare the current rule against that constrained fit. In the nonlinear stress study, 47 of 50 original fits had a clamped gap, so the treatment of deficits deserves a reported sensitivity analysis. ### 4. The validation supports narrower claims than the paper makes

- **Interval coverage.** Person resampling fixes the previous review’s calendar-week and risk-set objection. There is now real coverage evidence. In the current nonlinear-wave study, mean KCOR was 1.00093 at truth 1, but nominal 95% intervals covered **90/100** datasets, with a Monte Carlo interval of 82.4–95.1%. The stress study covered **48/50** while its mean estimate was **1.04019** at truth 1. An earlier paired experiment also deserves reporting: on the same 200 datasets, joint fitting covered 194 times and fixed-rate fitting covered 160. Report estimator versions separately, then test the released production path across low information, boundary fits, misspecified waves, and the actual reporting gate. Give both the fraction reported and coverage among reported results. [Current validation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457) · [Paired experiment](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json:7)
- **Cox comparison.** With homogeneous A and gamma-frail B sharing baseline cumulative hazard \(B(t)\), the true marginal B/A hazard ratio is \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\). A cohort-only Cox fit can therefore give a value below one under zero individual treatment effect. KCOR also receives the generating frailty variances in this comparison, and the shared-frailty curve is extrapolated from one fitted comparison. Plot the marginal and reference-frailty truths, compare each estimator with its target, and include KCOR with estimated frailties. - **Positive controls.** The fitted week-80 endpoints of 0.834 and 1.162 detect the direction of the two injections. The matched anchored raw endpoints already do so at 0.822 and 1.113, with both raw intervals excluding one. The generator multiplies the weekly hazard **among survivors**, and the fitting window starts in week 81. This is a retrospective direction check. For quantitative validation, inject a known effect at the individual-hazard level, derive its anchored cumulative target, and compare raw and fitted bias against that target across independent datasets.
