**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s core gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound. The problem is the inference built on it. The ordinary software run, the estimator defined in the manuscript, and several validation experiments use different information to estimate frailty. In the Czech example, the analysis also reports normalized comparisons after its own diagnostics flag insufficient signal. I would retain KCOR as a model-dependent descriptive analysis and revise the evidence for when its normalization can be trusted. **3. The frailty interpretation depends on a baseline assumption that fit diagnostics cannot verify.** For an observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\), every candidate \(\theta>0\) gives a baseline \(B_\theta(t)=[\exp(\theta H(t))-1]/\theta\) that reproduces exactly the same observations. The paper correctly shows what supplies identification under its model. With a known Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the instantaneous hazard gives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). Thus a change in the assumed slope changes the locally implied variance by \(\Delta\gamma/h\), potentially substantially in a low-mortality cohort. The Czech scan illustrates the practical issue. **All 78 fits passed**, yet moving the quiet window took the dose-1 estimate from effectively zero to **7.70** in one birth decade and **14.51** in another. The Czech configuration also says 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm. That outcome-informed choice needs disclosure alongside the prespecification claim. **Fix:** justify the slope and window independently of the resulting contrast, show the range of *KCOR itself* over credible slopes and windows, and evaluate predictions in held-out windows. Report observed survival alongside the transformed contrast. Anchoring a ratio to one also removes any persistent proportional difference, so readers need the unanchored curve.
