### 1. Sampling noise can create wave exposure that later observations cannot undo

This is the most important remaining issue with the optional module. In a week above the fitted baseline, KCOR assigns the excess to wave exposure \(W\). In a week below baseline, it holds \(W\) fixed and reduces baseline exposure \(B\). Consequently, two weeks can finish with exactly the survival expected in the absence of a wave and still produce a different corrected endpoint. The manuscript acknowledges the attribution convention. Its statistical consequence deserves a direct test. [Weekly allocation rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:519) · [Implementation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/evaluated-to-current.diff:1607)

I checked a two-week example using the manuscript’s survival integral. Set \(\theta=1,\ \alpha=2\), with true baseline increments 0.0100 and 0.0101 and zero wave exposure. Suppose sampled survival is \(1/1.014\) after week one, then returns to the **exact no-wave expectation** \(1/1.0201\) after week two. The rule produces \(W=0.002036\) in week one. It ends week two at \(B=0.016124\), giving corrected cumulative hazard **0.018160**, about 9.7% below the true **0.020100**. Both weekly survival equations are satisfied. A small numerical residual therefore cannot validate the split. This example establishes a possible error; its average size and direction require simulation. [Definition of \(L(B,W)\) and corrected endpoint](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517)

**Fix.** Add a known-zero-wave experiment *inside a marked wave window*, with stochastic deaths, several cohort sizes and bin widths. Report inferred \(W\), endpoint bias, and deficit-week frequency. For inference, estimate wave intensity from a count likelihood over multiple weeks, using the common intensity across cohorts when that assumption is invoked. Let the sampling model account for ordinary weekly fluctuations.

### 2. The quiet-window identification proof has a narrower domain than the full estimator

The identity \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) is correct under proportional gamma frailty. After a nonlinear wave, surviving individuals have density proportional to \(f_\Gamma(z)\exp(-zB-z^\alpha W)\). During a subsequent quiet period the corresponding identity is
\[
h'=\gamma h-h^2\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid\mathrm{alive})}
                              {E[Z\mid\mathrm{alive}]^2}.
\]
That coefficient can change with time and differ from enrollment \(\theta\). For example, with enrollment \(\theta=1,\alpha=2,W=0.1\), I obtain coefficients **0.865** at \(B=0.2\) and **0.911** at \(B=0.7\). The revised nonlinear fitter correctly carries survivor history forward. The simple curvature proposition cannot establish identification for that fit. Also, even in the proportional case, a homogeneous cohort with a time-varying hazard multiplier \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\) produces exactly the same mortality trajectory as gamma depletion. No diagnostic calculated from that trajectory can distinguish those explanations. [Proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354) · [Nonlinear fit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:383) · [Diagnostic limitations](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:422)

**Fix.** Limit the proof explicitly to proportional exposure history. For the nonlinear model, show which combinations of \(k,\theta\), prior exposure, and wave placement the observed counts support, and propagate that range to KCOR. Also correct a concrete implementation issue: with fewer than three eligible non-wave fit rows, the nonlinear fitter returns the preliminary proportional fit and its existing diagnostic status. That path can carry a successful status even though the nonlinear fit never took place. Return an explicit insufficient-information status and identify any fallback in the output.
