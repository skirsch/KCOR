### 1. An arbitrarily small change in the wave exponent changes which data fit the model

I replayed the **2022_06 pseudo-dose 2 versus 0** comparison at the Table 8 reporting date, keeping the people, counts, horizon and pooling weights fixed. The first two rows show what anchoring alone does to the observed cumulative-hazard ratio.

| Calculation | Pooled ratio |
|---|---:|
| Observed, unanchored | 0.13583 |
| Observed, anchored | 1.02873 |
| Core KCOR, anchored, \(\alpha=1\) | 1.01662 |
| Anchored KCOR, \(\alpha=0.999999\) | 1.02888 |
| Anchored KCOR, \(\alpha=1.000001\) | 1.02888 |
| Published sensitivity, \(\alpha=1.18\) | 1.05566 |

At exactly one, the proportional fitter includes early anchor weeks. On either side of one, the nonlinear fitter excludes marked wave weeks from its fitting residuals and applies a different gap reconstruction. For this enrollment the wave overlaps the anchors. The resulting jump of about **0.0123** occurs even though the underlying survival law varies continuously with the exponent. It also matters that the ordinary Czech configuration forces 1.18, while the empirical exponent search ended at the lower boundary on both the original and expanded grids. **Fix:** use one fitting mask and one rule for allocating gap exposure across a sensitivity curve that includes \(\alpha=1\). Make the wave-off analysis the Czech default and publish the observed, core and forced-exponent results together. ### 2. The fitter can report success with zero eligible fitting weeks

Section 2.5 says marked wave weeks reconstruct exposure and are excluded from the quiet-week objective. If fewer than three eligible rows remain, the nonlinear implementation returns the preliminary proportional fit **with its existing diagnostics**. I tested this using 12 weekly observations beginning in `2021-41` from the Table 8 workbook. I deliberately marked all 12 as both quiet and wave to exercise the documented precedence rule. All 12 should therefore have been excluded from fitting. The function returned `success=True`, `status="ok"`, and `n_fit=12`. This is a reporting defect independent of whether it affected the six published points. **Fix:** compute eligibility and event counts after wave exclusion. If support is insufficient, return that status and the actual eligible count. Carry it through pooling and interval reporting. ### 3. The proportional gap calculation can create excess exposure when an earlier deficit disappears

At each quiet-window re-entry the code calculates a raw cumulative offset. It adds the positive part of the difference from the **previous raw offset** to the **previous applied offset**. Once a negative increment has been clamped, those two histories differ. In the 2022_06 core replay, one subgroup had successive raw offsets of **−0.00072, −0.00285 and +0.02534**. The applied offsets were **0, 0 and +0.02819**. The last fitted offset overshoots the exposure reconstructed from observed survival by 0.00285. I found this overshoot in six of the nine subgroup fits for that enrollment. **Fix:** compare each raw cumulative offset with the exposure already applied, or estimate nonnegative gap increments jointly. Report the remaining survival discrepancy at each re-entry. A regression test should include a deficit followed by recovery. ### 4. The new validation supports recovery under specified conditions, with limited evidence for interval calibration

The main nonlinear simulation recovered a mean ratio of **1.00093** at truth one. Its nominal 95% intervals covered truth in **90 of 100** datasets, with a coverage interval of **82.4%–95.1%**. That result leaves material undercoverage plausible. The stress study covered **48 of 50**, but its mean estimate was **1.04019** at truth one, 47 of 50 original datasets had a clamped gap, and 241 bootstrap draws had an insufficient-signal flag. In another sampled design with all pulses inside the marked window, the revised method improved absolute error in **39 of 200** datasets and worsened it in **161**, despite moving the mean toward truth. These results distinguish recovery of the average from accuracy for an individual cohort comparison. **Fix:** put bias, absolute error, coverage, width, boundary frequency and fallback frequency together in the main results. Report coverage for all datasets and separately for accepted datasets under an explicit acceptance rule. Supply runnable generators and seeds for these studies alongside the archived outputs. ### 5. “Depletion-neutralized” remains a conditional interpretation

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any proposed positive \(\theta\), the baseline \(B_\theta(t)=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H(t))/\theta\) reproduces the observed survival. The paper correctly shows where its identifying information comes from: fixing the Gompertz slope and restricting the baseline within selected windows. An excellent fit cannot establish that frailty caused the curvature. The Czech configuration also says 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm, which qualifies the claim of prespecification. And the revised contamination experiment shows why the anchor belongs in the scientific interpretation: moving it by one week changed mean KCOR at the largest dynamic perturbation from **0.782 to 2.116** on the same fitted curves. **Fix:** describe the endpoint as a *model-implied cumulative hazard at reference frailty*, show observed survival and unanchored results alongside it, and evaluate window choices on held-out periods selected independently of the contrast. The known-frailty Cox experiment can remain as a check of the inversion.
