## Addendum to the saved open review of `m94`

This is a separate follow-up. The saved open answer and repository files remain unchanged.

**I would retain the major-revision recommendation, but withdraw several earlier objections.** The primary fitter now jointly estimates baseline level and frailty, the nonlinear wave correction carries survivor selection through wave exit, and the revised control tables distinguish historical results from current estimates. The most consequential remaining problem is that KCOR can report an adjusted contrast when the data used to estimate the adjustment are inadequate. The following are my rulings on the individual objections.

### Identification and implementation

1. **Did the paper infer frailty from curvature alone or claim the fitted variance was invariant to hazard scaling? Resolved in the identification argument.** The revision gives both counterexamples. Gompertz growth alone can curve cumulative hazard at zero frailty, and positive frailty can produce a constant observed hazard when \(k\theta=\gamma\). It also correctly states that multiplying the *observed cohort hazard* by \(c\) maps the fitted parameters to \(ck,\theta/c\). The residual objection concerns the claim that baseline-slope error tends to cancel in KCOR ratios. The identification argument does not establish that cancellation for two differently depleted cohorts. I would show the endpoint and fitted variance for each cohort across a stated range of slopes and alternative baseline shapes. [Identification argument](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Cancellation claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:334)

2. **Does the primary fit still hold \(k\) fixed or use the old weekly Euler reconstruction? No. Does it use only quiet weeks? Not always.** The revised code uses the week-boundary exponential increment, recomputes offsets inside the objective, and jointly optimizes \(k\) and \(\theta\). The schematic still says \(k\) is held fixed. Early anchor weeks can also enter the objective outside a quiet window. The detailed methods disclose this, while the identification section says only quiet-window bins are used. I would correct the schematic and report the actual fitting weeks for each cohort, including early anchors. 3. **Can a failed identification step silently become a usable adjustment? Yes, although the old objection about the first nonpositive offset is resolved.** A negative offset is now clamped and recorded while fitting continues. The sparse-death branch, however, returns \(\theta=0\) with `success=True` and status `insufficient_deaths`. More seriously, the nonlinear fitter returns the preliminary gamma fit and its diagnostics when fewer than three eligible fitting weeks remain. Those diagnostics describe the preliminary fit and can indicate success even when *zero* weeks qualify for the requested nonlinear fit. I would propagate the requested fit’s eligibility and failure status to the endpoint, withhold that adjusted endpoint, and display the observed contrast. A genuinely fitted boundary estimate of zero should have its own status. 4. **Does the wave correction still use the wrong survivor moment or divide past excess by the current week’s factor? Resolved for the primary correction.** The revision solves the weekly survival equation using the joint accumulated proportional and wave exposures. After wave exit it holds accumulated wave exposure fixed and solves for subsequent proportional exposure under the selected survivor distribution. The older mean-one moment calculation remains in an exponent-analysis script, so its historical output should retain that provenance. A separate objection **still applies** to the Czech exponent. The revised empirical objective reaches the lower grid boundary, and the paper explicitly calls \(\alpha\) unidentified. The Czech configuration nevertheless forces 1.18, while the operational summary says an unidentified exponent is inactive. I would make the exponent-off result primary and label every forced-exponent output consistently. ### What the validation exercises establish

5. **Was the opening Czech illustration selected for the desired appearance? Yes, now explicitly disclosed.** The selection restricted final KCOR to 0.85–1.15 and favored examples where normalization reduced the curvature difference. The paper also identifies the earlier monthly normalization used for the figure. That makes it an intelligible illustration. It cannot establish how often the current weekly estimator removes drift or preserves a real effect. For that claim I would show all eligible examples, or apply a selection rule independent of the resulting KCOR. 6. **Does the Cox comparison give KCOR the generating frailty variances? Yes, now disclosed even for its intervals.** It tests inversion with known nuisance parameters. It cannot validate estimating those parameters from the same mortality histories. The interpretation of Cox and restricted mean survival time also remains too strong. Cohorts with different survivor distributions can genuinely differ in marginal hazards and mean survival time under a null individual intervention effect. Calling those differences “spurious” or “depletion bias” requires specifying which target each statistic was supposed to recover. I would state each target and add a KCOR arm that estimates frailty afresh in every simulated dataset. ### Contamination and interpretation

11. **Does the paper still support a universal contamination threshold near 66% of baseline, or treat the fraction above one as a false-positive rate? No.** The revised comparison uses the zero-addition arm. At an added weekly hazard of 0.0003, the mean shift is **+0.010 [−0.012, 0.032]**. At five million people per cohort, a smaller addition of 0.0001 produces a detectable mean shift of **+0.01969 [0.01698, 0.02240]**. The intervals concern simulation means, and the paper now reports the fraction of endpoints above one as a descriptive fraction. The anchoring objection is stronger than before: at the largest dynamic contamination, changing the reference on the same fitted curves moves mean KCOR from **0.782 to 2.116**. Any interpretation of direction needs the reference week and the unanchored contrast beside it. [Zero-addition comparisons and anchor reversal](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:784) · [Population-size experiment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:808)

12. **Can anchoring erase persistent differences, can the skip omit acute effects, and must crossover bias the estimate toward one? The first two objections remain properties of the estimand. The categorical crossover claim has been corrected.** Dividing by the reference ratio makes any constant ratio equal to one after anchoring. An effect confined to skipped weeks is outside the reported follow-up. The revision explicitly acknowledges both the missing acute signal and the possibility that a quiet-window fit absorbs a genuine effect while diagnostics remain stable. It also correctly makes crossover depend on who crosses and when. I would present observed absolute risks from enrollment, the unanchored contrast, and the anchored post-skip contrast together. Each answers a different scientific question. [Anchor definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [Crossover, absorption, and skip](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:968)

The first fix I would make is the eligibility check in item 3. A reader can interpret a model-dependent estimate if the assumptions, fitting weeks, and uncertainty are visible. When no qualifying weeks support the requested fit, the scientifically meaningful result is that the adjustment could not be estimated.
