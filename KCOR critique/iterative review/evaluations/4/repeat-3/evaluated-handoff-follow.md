### 1. Can a quiet gap erase genuine frailty? The archived failure is fixed.

I reran the archived example with exact weekly hazards, \(k=0.001\), \(\gamma=0.085/52\), and true \(\theta=4\). Weeks 0–19 alone gave **4.00000000000003**. Adding quiet weeks 40–59 gave **4.00000000000141**. The second fit recorded a raw offset of \(-2.67\times10^{-11}\), clamped it, returned `status="ok"`, and retained the frailty estimate. In a separate check, adding proportional exposure of 0.04 in week 8 before a quiet window beginning in week 20 also recovered \(k=0.001,\theta=4\). These results resolve both the no-excess-gap example and the earlier claim that the first quiet window permanently fixes the wrong baseline rate. ### 4. Does good fit establish depletion or window stability? The objection remains, with revised numbers.

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H\), every candidate \(\theta>0\) has a baseline \(B_\theta=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) that reproduces \(H\). Fixing a Gompertz slope restricts those explanations. At fixed observed \(h,h'\), a slope change \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The paper acknowledges that an effect inside a quiet window can be absorbed even with stable fits, yet retains the general slope-cancellation and “prerequisite” claims. ### 6. Do the validation and contamination objections still apply? The answer differs by experiment.

- **Comparator and recovery claims remain too broad.** The headline Cox comparison supplies KCOR with the generating variances. The shared-frailty curve is interpolated, the multiwave recovery illustration uses expected deaths and a fixed rate, and Table 11 uses an earlier single-window estimator. A real difference in marginal survival can coexist with identical individual hazards at fixed frailty, so a nonzero RMST difference alone establishes no error in RMST. The positive controls likewise address different targets. Table 9 multiplies the observed hazard among survivors, while SI §S4.6 multiplies individual hazards outside the quiet windows. Neither design tests how much of a genuine effect the released fitter absorbs **inside** a fitting window.
