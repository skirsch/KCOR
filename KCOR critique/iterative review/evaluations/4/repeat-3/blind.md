**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically correct. The main problem is what the manuscript infers from a successful fit. Weekly deaths can be consistent with the proposed depletion mechanism even when a different mechanism produces exactly the same observations. The paper acknowledges this in its limitations, but still describes normalization as a prerequisite for cohort comparison and presents several checks as validation of the revised estimator. The evidence supports a narrower, useful claim: KCOR shows how a cumulative-hazard comparison changes under a specified model of frailty. [Combined manuscript, pp. 3–4, 9–12 and 31](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.pdf)

### 1. The counts cannot tell the analyst how much curvature to remove

There is an exact counterexample. Give cohorts A and B the same individual baseline cumulative hazard \(G(t)\), with gamma-frailty variances 0 and 4 respectively. At a horizon where \(G=0.1\), the model gives observed cumulative hazards of \(0.1\) and \(\log(1.4)/4=0.08412\). In this model, inversion correctly recovers a B/A baseline ratio of **1**. Now give both cohorts zero frailty and give B the time-varying baseline \(\log(1+4G(t))/4\). The two explanations produce identical survival curves, hence identical distributions of weekly counts at every sample size. In the second explanation, the true baseline ratio is **0.841**. Every diagnostic computed from those counts has the same distribution in both cases. The fixed-Gompertz proposition remains valid; the imposed baseline restriction selects the explanation. [Gamma identity and inversion, §2.4.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Identification proposition, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354)

**Fix.** Put an example like this beside the estimand. Report the observed contrast, the fixed-Gompertz result, and the range obtained under plausible changes in baseline slope or drift. The paper claims slope errors tend to cancel in ratios, but its own relation \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) shows that changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta/h\). Cohorts with different mortality levels can therefore respond differently. The supplement gives the default slope as “Fixed implementation value” and instructs readers to check sensitivity. Publish the numerical slope, its time units, the tested range, and the resulting endpoint ratios. [Slope-cancellation claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Supplementary defaults](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:174) · [Sensitivity specification](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:294)

### 2. The estimand needs a more precise name

Under the written model, inversion returns the cumulative hazard of a hypothetical person with \(Z=1\). Frailty is scaled to mean one **separately in each cohort**. If one cohort enters twice as frail on average on a common biological scale, that factor can be absorbed entirely into its fitted baseline \(k_d\). Correct inversion can then leave a twofold contrast despite identical treatment effects. Calling KCOR a “depletion-adjusted marginal estimand” and saying it “equates cohorts based on how they die” obscures which differences survive the adjustment. The stabilization skip also makes the reference population the people alive at the first accumulating week. [Box 1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:53) · [Box 2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:89) · [Frailty definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:275) · [Time origin](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:445)

**Fix.** Call the primary quantity a *ratio of model-implied cumulative hazards at cohort-relative mean frailty*, state the survivor landmark, and show observed absolute mortality alongside it. For a population mortality comparison, specify common age weights and report standardized risks. The Czech table illustrates why this matters: the raw dose-0/dose-2 cumulative-hazard ratio is **0.316 overall** and **above 1 in every displayed age band**. Those bands cover ages 40+ and the overall row includes all ages, so the target population matters even before any frailty adjustment. [Czech raw hazards and population definitions](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:410)

### 3. The anchor can reverse the scientific reading

The clearest empirical warning comes from the paper itself. In the historical contamination experiment, moving the anchor by **one week**, across the first week of added deaths, changes mean anchored KCOR from **2.116 to 0.782 using the same fitted curves**. Dividing by the early cumulative ratio makes that ratio part of every later result. An anchored value below one can therefore coexist with added mortality in the numerator cohort. The age-shift controls also use anchoring and have no known exact target. Their six updated intervals include one, but that does not establish accurate recovery or equivalence. [Anchor definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [One-week reversal](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:780) · [Age-shift results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:745)

**Fix.** Show unanchored KCOR, the numerical anchor ratio, and the anchored curve together. If the question concerns mortality accumulated *after* a reference date, report an explicitly defined post-landmark contrast using increments \(H_d(t)-H_d(t_0)\). Give the raw-count comparison the same anchor so readers can see how much the frailty fit itself contributes.

### 4. Several headline comparisons test a different estimator or a different target

The Cox comparison supplies KCOR with the **generating frailty variances**, including in its bootstrap. The shared-frailty Cox curve is scaled from one result. The multiwave recovery example uses expected deaths and fixes the baseline at its generating value. These are checks of algebra and implementation. They do not measure how well the proposed procedure learns both parameters from noisy counts. Moreover, the marginal hazards genuinely differ in the Cox experiment. A non-unit marginal comparison under a null effect at fixed frailty does not establish estimation bias for the marginal target. The positive control has a related limitation: it multiplies the weekly hazard **among survivors** by \(r\), and the reported week-80 endpoint uses a fit window beginning in week 81. It demonstrates retrospective detection of direction. [Cox comparison and supplied parameters](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:635) · [Comparator construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:664) · [Recovery example](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:723) · [Positive-control generator](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:130) · [Fit timing](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:754)

**Fix.** Freeze one production specification and rerun the central comparisons on independently sampled cohorts, estimating every unknown each method needs. Score each method against its own known population target. For KCOR, report quantitative endpoint bias and interval coverage under equal and unequal mean frailty, sustained and transient effects, and effects overlapping the fit window. Retain the known-parameter experiment as an explicit benchmark.

### 5. Diagnostic acceptance and interval calibration are separate questions

The updated results deserve prominence. With a labeled proportional pre-entry shock, the revised fit moves mean KCOR from **1.204 to 0.998**. In the main nonlinear experiment the consistent fit gives **1.00093**, but nominal 95% intervals cover in **90/100** datasets. Its reported coverage uncertainty extends to 95.1%, so this run alone cannot settle calibration. In the stress design the mean is **1.04019** despite coverage in **48/50**. An earlier fitter covered in only **502/600** datasets although every original fit passed diagnostics. In the Czech window scan all **78/78** fits passed, while one group’s fitted variance ranged from **0 to 1.56** across overlapping placements. These results distinguish successful optimization, parameter precision, endpoint bias, and coverage. [Current validation, SI §S8](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457) · [Earlier coverage studies](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:585) · [Czech window scan](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304)

**Fix.** Publish numerical diagnostic thresholds, parameter profiles, endpoint sensitivity, and failed-fit counts. For the final fitter, report the probability of issuing a result and coverage among issued results, with Monte Carlo uncertainty for both. Include low-event cohorts and the \(\theta=0\) boundary. A precise estimate near zero and a broad range of compatible variances call for different interpretations.

### 6. The nonlinear wave model solves an allocation problem only after assumptions supply the missing information

The revised survivor law is a substantive improvement. During a marked week, however, one observed survival decrement supplies one equation for two exposure increments, baseline \(b\) and wave \(w\). KCOR fixes \(b\) from the Gompertz fit and solves for \(w\). In a below-baseline week it sets \(w=0\) and lowers \(b\). Thus positive and negative fluctuations are allocated differently by construction. The Czech exponent is also unresolved: only **62 positive-excess cohort-weeks across eight cohorts** enter the replay, and the exact-survivor optimum follows the lower search boundary from **1.00 to 0.70** when the grid is widened. The manuscript is right to report \(\alpha\) as unidentified. [Weekly allocation rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:517) · [Czech exponent replay](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:828)

**Fix.** Put the current core fit beside the \(\alpha=1.18\) sensitivity fit in the empirical results table. Report deficit-week frequencies, vary wave dates and baseline allocations, and show the resulting endpoint range. If the external effect scale is uncertain, propagate that uncertainty. Turning the module off imposes proportional frailty loading for the core inversion; it does not resolve whether an actual wave had that loading.
