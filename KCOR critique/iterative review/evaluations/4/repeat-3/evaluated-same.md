### 2. KCOR identifies a model-dependent reference hazard. Mortality counts alone cannot establish that the removed curvature was depletion.

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any candidate \(\theta>0\), choosing \(H_0(t)=(e^{\theta H(t)}-1)/\theta\) reproduces exactly the same observed survival. A homogeneous cohort with cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) reproduces it too. The fixed Gompertz trend is what lets KCOR distinguish those explanations *within its model*. A perfect quiet-window fit cannot distinguish them in the population. This matters for the claim that normalization is a prerequisite and for calling the result a marginal estimand. The inverted quantity is the fitted cumulative hazard at \(Z=1\), where each cohort defines its own frailty scale to have mean one. [Inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354) · [Claims about the estimand](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:83)

There is a precise qualification to add for m94. In a quiet week **after a nonlinear wave**, the survivor law gives
\[
h'=\gamma h-\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Z\mid\text{survival})}{E(Z\mid\text{survival})^2}h^2.
\]
The coefficient generally differs from enrollment \(\theta\), because nonlinear wave selection changes the shape of the survivor distribution. The revised fitter accounts for this. The local identification proposition should explicitly restrict its use to proportional selection histories. **Fix the interpretation** by reporting observed survival and absolute mortality alongside KCOR, and showing endpoint sensitivity to baseline shape and \(\gamma\). ### 3. An exact survival fit leaves the allocation of deaths to a wave uncertain

Each wave week supplies one mortality observation for two possible increments, baseline and wave exposure. KCOR resolves this by assigning excess above the planned baseline to the wave and assigning a shortfall to a lower baseline. Even in a marked period with **zero actual wave exposure**, upward count fluctuations can accumulate positive fitted wave exposure, while downward fluctuations cannot undo it. A tiny survival-equation residual establishes numerical agreement with observed deaths. It says little about how accurately the method divided those deaths between the two components. The proportional gap rule has a related constraint on negative departures. [Gap rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Weekly allocation rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:519)

**Fix.** Add a stochastic control with a marked but absent wave. Report recovered wave exposure, deficit weeks, post-wave hazard, and KCOR across event-count levels. Compare the weekly allocation with a count model that estimates a shared wave pattern across several weeks. For proportional gaps, test allowing signed departures from the Gompertz baseline while keeping total hazard nonnegative. The supplied stress experiment recorded a clamped gap in **47 of 50** original nonlinear fits, so the behavior of this constraint matters in a design already used by the paper. [Stress results, diff lines 1297–1318](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/3-evaluated-same-evaluated-to-current.diff:1297)

### 4. The new simulations demonstrate improved point estimation in one design. Interval calibration remains open.

The main nonlinear simulation had target 1 and mean KCOR **1.00093**, an improvement over **0.98982** with the gamma working fit. Both procedures covered the truth in **90 of 100** datasets. The reported uncertainty on that coverage rate spans **82.4%–95.1%**. In the stress design, the new procedure covered in **48 of 50**, but its mean was **1.04019** with a Monte Carlo interval of **1.01789–1.06249**, and its mean interval width was **0.327**. Coverage and accuracy tell different stories here. The local information bound in §2.9 is useful for planning, but it cannot calibrate percentile intervals at a parameter boundary.
