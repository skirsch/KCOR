### 1. The mortality data identify frailty only after the analyst specifies the baseline shape

Let \(G\) be a cumulative Gompertz hazard. Gamma frailty acting on individual baseline \(G'\) produces the observed hazard \(h=G'/(1+\theta G)\). A homogeneous cohort in which everyone actually has hazard \(h\) produces exactly the same survival and weekly death probabilities. Compare either cohort with a homogeneous cohort whose hazard is \(G'\). KCOR can fit both histories perfectly and return 1. In the homogeneous explanation, individuals in the two cohorts genuinely have different hazards. More observations or cleaner residuals cannot distinguish the explanations. Calling normalization a “prerequisite” or its output a “marginal estimand” gives the transformation more scientific authority than the data support. [Gamma identity](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Estimand and prerequisite claims](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:83) · [Manuscript’s attribution limitation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:882)

**Fix.** Define the primary output as the ratio of fitted cumulative hazards at each cohort’s own reference frailty \(Z=1\), conditional on the baseline shape, frailty law, and analysis windows. Report observed deaths, survival, and absolute risk alongside it. The paper’s own identity \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) shows that a slope change \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The claimed first-order cancellation in cohort ratios needs evidence. Publish the cohort fits and endpoints across stated slopes and alternative baseline shapes. The supplement currently instructs readers to check slope sensitivity without giving the claimed range and results there. ### 2. A successful fit can contain no eligible quiet weeks

I checked a concrete case in memory using the current fitter. I supplied 20 weeks, marked all 20 as overlapping the nonlinear wave, and supplied enough deaths to clear the count threshold. The fitter returned **`success=True`, `status="ok"`, `n_fit=20`**. Under §2.5, zero weeks were eligible to estimate quiet mortality. The code first fits a proportional model, then excludes wave weeks. If fewer than three eligible rows remain, it returns the earlier fit *with its original diagnostics*. This directly contradicts the stated rule that marked wave rows contribute exposure reconstruction only. [Eligibility rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/documentation/preprint/paper.md:381) · [Wave exclusion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/code/kcor_survivor.py:189) · [Fallback that returns the earlier diagnostics](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m94/code/kcor_survivor.py:247)

There is a separate problem in the proportional fit. It accumulates positive differences between successive **raw** cumulative offsets. If the raw offsets at three re-entries are \(a,0,a\), with \(a>0\), the applied offsets are \(a,a,2a\). A noisy dip and recovery has been counted as a second persistent exposure. **Fix both paths.** Compute event counts, precision checks, and fit status on the final eligible mask. Return an explicit insufficient-data result when that mask cannot support a fit. For gaps, fit nonnegative increments jointly to the observed survival transitions, or compare each reconstructed total with the previously *applied* total and report the remaining discrepancy. Test overlapping waves, deficit-and-recovery sequences, and sparse anchors.
