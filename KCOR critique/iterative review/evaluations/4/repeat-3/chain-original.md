**I would recommend major revision.** KCOR has a sound mathematical core. If individual hazards share a baseline multiplied by mean-one gamma frailty, and the frailty variance \(\theta\) is known, then \(T_\theta(H)=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) correctly recovers the baseline cumulative hazard from the observed cohort cumulative hazard. The scientific problem is deciding **which part of the observed trajectory warrants that inversion**. The manuscript acknowledges that its contrast is descriptive, but makes stronger claims that its diagnostics detect wrong decompositions, that failures are conservative, and that the empirical checks support those claims. Several are contradicted by the paper’s own equations or results. I reviewed the 78-page combined manuscript, its Markdown sources, and the cited local figures read-only. ### 1. A perfect quiet-window fit can coexist with a spurious contrast

Here is a counterexample using the paper’s model. Give two cohorts the same individual baseline hazard, with cumulative hazard \(G\), and different frailty variances. Let every quiet window follow the assumed model exactly. After the last fitting window, give everyone the same additional hazard, independent of frailty, with cumulative hazard \(W\). There is no intervention effect or difference in exposure to the shock. The observed cumulative hazard in each cohort is then
\[
H_d=\frac{\log(1+\theta_d G)}{\theta_d}+W.
\]
Even with the **true** variances, KCOR’s full-trajectory inversion gives
\[
T_{\theta_d}(H_d)=e^{\theta_d W}G+\frac{e^{\theta_d W}-1}{\theta_d}.
\]
At \(G=0.05\), \(W=0.01\), and variances 4 and 0.5, the ratio is **1.033**. Before the shock it was exactly 1. Both cohorts have the same hazard at unit frailty throughout. Every quiet-window check can pass. Exchanging the cohort labels reverses the direction. This is a failure of the full-trajectory interpretation even with infinite data and perfect parameter recovery. The paper recognizes additive shocks as a limitation, yet later says violations lead to conservative behavior. [Full-trajectory inversion, §2.6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:432) · [Conservativeness claim, §5.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1023) · [Acknowledged wave limitation, §5.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1072)

**Fix.** State explicitly that the full-trajectory interpretation requires the *same individual frailty multiplier* to govern hazards outside the fitting windows as well. Show the observed contrast alongside KCOR, and report how KCOR changes when some share of a common shock acts independently of frailty. Describe diagnostic failure as evidence against a fit. A diagnostic pass cannot rule out this counterexample.

### 2. The curvature and scale claims need algebraic correction

Under the stated Gompertz–gamma model, the quiet-period marginal hazard \(h\) satisfies
\[
h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2.
\]
This gives a precise identification result in the ideal continuous-time model. With fixed, correct \(\gamma\) and positive \(h\), the level and slope determine \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). The manuscript’s claimed equivalence between nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature and positive frailty variance is false. With \(\theta=0\), a growing Gompertz hazard already produces curvature. With \(\theta=\gamma/k>0\), the marginal hazard is constant and the cumulative hazard is exactly linear. A related diagnostic tests for linearity *after* inversion, although the model predicts \(k(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). [Baseline specification, §2.4.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:321) · [Identification proposition, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Linearity diagnostic, §2.6.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:454)

The same equation resolves an important ambiguity. Multiplying the **observed marginal hazard** by \(c\), as §2.4.4 explicitly does, changes the fitted pair from \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\). A common \(c\) can cancel in the final ratio even though each fitted variance changes. Multiplying the **individual baseline hazard** by \(c\) preserves the individual frailty variance and changes subsequent depletion. These are distinct experiments. **Fix:** derive the proposition for the specified scale, distinguish parameter identification from finite-sample precision, and assess residuals against the integrated Gompertz curve. Publish the numerical \(\gamma\), its time units, and the claimed sensitivity results. The defaults table gives only “Fixed implementation value.” [Scaling claim, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:346) · [Supplement, Table S5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

### 3. The weekly algorithm and its illustrative figure need reconciliation

Step 2 updates \(1+\theta G\) by multiplying by \(1+\theta h_t\), where \(h_t\) is the observed weekly integrated hazard. The final inversion instead multiplies that quantity by \(e^{\theta h_t}\). The two procedures reconstruct different histories, and Step 3 uses the first history to calculate wave offsets. Also, the seed fit assumes the unperturbed Gompertz history from rebased time zero and then fixes \(k\). If a wave occurs before the nearest quiet window, its cumulative offset can already be absorbed into that seed scale. Matching the reconstructed and reference paths at a wave endpoint is built into the offset formula, so agreement there supplies no independent validation. [Weekly hazard definition, §2.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Seed, recursion, offsets, and refit, §2.5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371)

There is a visible check in Figure 1. At the final time, the dose-2 curve is about **0.030** in the raw panel and **0.020** in the normalized panel. For \(\theta\ge0\), \(T_\theta(H)\ge H\) on the same input and time origin. A different origin or additional transformation could explain the plot, but the caption does not identify one that reconciles those numbers. **Fix:** use exact interval increments \(T_\theta(H_t)-T_\theta(H_{t-1})\) throughout, fit the quiet-window increments with the prior history accounted for, and validate on a held-out window. Regenerate Figure 1 with the actual post-skip input, fitted parameters, and final values for both panels. ### 4. The proposed wave correction fails its own neutral case

The optional module defines \(F_d(t;\alpha)=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{survival to }t]\) and says \(\alpha=1\) is effectively inactive. After depletion, however, \(F_d(t;1)=E[Z\mid\text{survival to }t]<1\) for positive frailty variance and accumulated hazard. Dividing positive, already-inverted wave excess by this factor therefore **increases** it. A wave proportional to \(Z\) is precisely the case the core inversion already handles. For time-varying \(F\), dividing the entire cumulative excess at week \(t\) by that week’s \(F(t)\) also reweights excess accrued in earlier weeks. A cumulative maximum can enforce monotonicity without resolving either problem. [Wave model, §2.7.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Correction formula, §2.7.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module inactive for the Czech analysis, as the authors concluded. Before using it elsewhere, derive the survivor distribution and weekly excess increments from a joint individual-level baseline-and-wave model. A wave depending on \(Z^\alpha\) generally changes the survivor distribution away from the gamma form when \(\alpha\ne1\). Verify exact identity at \(\alpha=1\) and recovery of known cumulative excess through and after a wave. Supplying an external intervention-effect scale helps constrain the cross-cohort comparison, but does not establish the correction formula. [External scale, §2.7.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:532) · [Czech non-identification result, §3.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:914)
