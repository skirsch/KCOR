**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically correct. If individual hazards are \(Z h_0(t)\), frailty is mean-one gamma, and its variance \(\theta\) is known, the observed cumulative hazard \(H\) gives \(H_0=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\). The manuscript’s week-boundary recursion also implements that identity correctly. The consequential questions are whether the data identify \(\theta\), what comparison survives the normalization, and whether the reported experiments validate the procedure readers are asked to use. I reviewed the combined PDF and its sources in `documentation/preprint` read-only. [Core identity, PDF pp. 9–10](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Weekly recursion, p. 12](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:396)

### 1. A perfect fit can remove a real mortality difference

The paper correctly proves that, **given the true quiet-period Gompertz slope** \(\gamma\), the instantaneous hazard identifies \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) under its model. But the same mortality records admit a different explanation. For example, let \(G\) be a cumulative Gompertz baseline. An observed hazard \(G'/(1+\theta G)\) can arise either from gamma frailty acting on \(G'\), or from a homogeneous cohort in which everyone’s hazard actually follows \(G'/(1+\theta G)\). Compare either population with a homogeneous cohort whose hazard is \(G'\). The two explanations give identical weekly deaths in expectation and identical KCOR fits. Under the second explanation, KCOR maps a genuine difference in individual hazards to a ratio of exactly one. More people, cleaner residuals, and additional quiet windows cannot distinguish these explanations. The manuscript acknowledges the ambiguity, but also calls normalization a “prerequisite” and describes residual differences as unexplained by depletion. Those stronger claims exceed what the data establish. [Identification proposition, p. 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Prerequisite claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:83) · [Discussion of attribution, pp. 33–34](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:943)

**Fix.** Define the output as a descriptive ratio of fitted cumulative hazards at each cohort’s own reference frailty \(Z=1\), conditional on the specified baseline and frailty law. Report observed survival and absolute mortality beside it. Show how the ratio changes under plausible baseline slopes and alternative baseline shapes. The proposition itself gives the reason for doing this by cohort: at a fixed observed \(h,h'\), a slope error \(\delta\gamma\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). A common slope error therefore gives no general guarantee of cancellation in the ratio. [Gompertz specification and cancellation claim, pp. 10–11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323)

### 2. The first quiet window can lock in the wrong baseline rate

This is a specific defect even **within** the paper’s additive-offset model. Step 1 fits the first quiet window with cumulative offset zero. Step 4 holds that fitted \(k\) fixed. Suppose a wave between the rebased origin and the first quiet week has already added cumulative baseline hazard \(a>0\). Because \(G(s;k)\) is linear in \(k\), the manuscript’s exact weekly prediction satisfies
\[
q(s;k,\theta,a)=q\!\left(s;\frac{k}{1+\theta a},\theta,0\right).
\]
The first window can therefore fit *perfectly* while estimating \(k/(1+\theta a)\) as the original baseline rate. A residual check cannot detect the error, and the pooled refit cannot update \(k\). This matters especially when an analysis deliberately waits for a wave to end before choosing its first quiet window. [Seed equation and zero-offset objective, p. 12](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Offset reconstruction and fixed-rate refit, p. 13](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:409)

**Fix.** Require and document an undisturbed interval from the rebased origin through the seed window, or change the fit. One direct alternative uses the observed survival history at each quiet week’s start. Under the core model its population equation is
\[
e^{\theta H_{\rm obs}(s)}
  \frac{e^{\theta h_{\rm week}(s)}-1}{\theta}
   =G(s+1;k)-G(s;k).
\]
This equation allows \(k\) and \(\theta\) to be estimated jointly across quiet weeks while accounting for depletion before each week. Fit with uncertainty appropriate to the weekly death counts, and test the procedure on stochastic cohorts with a wave *before* the first quiet window. [Derivation from the manuscript’s weekly inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:399)

### 3. The Czech diagnostics and controls support a narrower conclusion

All **78 of 78** quiet-window fits passed the pipeline. Yet shifting the window moved the fitted dose-1 variance from effectively zero to **7.70** in one birth decade and **14.51** in another. That is direct evidence that passing an individual fit does not establish stability across placements. It also leaves an important question open: how much did the *reported KCOR* move across those same fits? Separately, the age-shift control uses an anchored ratio. If the unanchored ratio is any constant \(c\), dividing by its week-4 value produces one everywhere. This control can detect subsequent drift, but it cannot demonstrate recovery of the level. One revised 2022 control estimate is **1.0462 with interval 0.322–1.268**. Inclusion of one at that precision gives little evidence of close agreement. [Window scan, pp. 65–66](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:302) · [Anchor definition, p. 7](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [Table 8, p. 50](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1237)

**Fix.** Publish the unanchored and anchored estimates, event counts, and interval widths for every window at common horizons. Set a material tolerance for drift or error before running the control. Label the post-skip analysis as conditional on surviving to the landmark, and report deaths during the skip separately. For the all-ages comparison, show a common age-standardized result alongside the pooled result. The supplement already illustrates why: the raw dose-0/dose-2 ratio is above one in every listed age band from 40 upward, whereas the full-population ratio is **0.3159**. [Stabilization rule, pp. 14–15](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:487) · [Raw age-specific and full-population results, p. 74](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:423)

### 4. The validation combines materially different estimators and targets

The headline Cox comparison supplies KCOR with the **generating frailty variances**. It checks the inversion with oracle inputs. The shared-frailty comparator curve is interpolated from one null comparison. The multiwave recovery example uses **expected weekly deaths and fixes the baseline rate**. The broader comparison grid uses an **earlier single-window fitter**. And the two person-bootstrap studies achieved **189/200 (94.5%)** and **502/600 (83.7%)** coverage under different designs. All 600 original fits in the latter passed diagnostics. Both studies predate the current week-boundary equations. The manuscript discloses these facts, but taken together they leave the current estimator’s coverage unresolved. A non-unit Cox summary or a real RMST difference between differently composed cohorts also does not demonstrate bias for those methods’ own targets. [Oracle comparison, pp. 21–24](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:718) · [Comparator construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:737) · [Recovery design, p. 26](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:798) · [Coverage, p. 19](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:669) · [Earlier fitter, Table 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

**Fix.** Freeze one specification and run it end to end on independently sampled cohorts, estimating all fitted inputs in every dataset and bootstrap draw. Report error against the known cumulative target, interval coverage, rejection rate, and errors among accepted fits. Include effects inside the quiet window and before the anchor. Distinguish the positive control that multiplies the **observed hazard among survivors** from the separate experiment that multiplies **individual hazards**. A temporary multiplier of 1.2 does not itself imply a cumulative ratio of 1.2 at week 80. Likewise, the quiet-window contamination experiment adds actual mortality to one cohort. Its reported 66% threshold describes the tested grid and anchor, not a bound on Czech confounding. [Positive-control construction, pp. 57–58](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:128) · [Individual-hazard experiment, p. 64](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:244) · [Contamination design and interpretation, pp. 28–30](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:849)

### 5. The optional nonlinear wave correction uses incompatible approximations

For a wave hazard proportional to \(Z^\alpha\), the paper gives the appropriate survival model \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\). For \(\alpha\ne1\), survivors generally cease to follow a gamma distribution. The in-wave procedure nevertheless applies ordinary gamma inversion, divides **accumulated** excess by the **current week’s** approximate moment, and repairs decreases with a cumulative maximum. The paper acknowledges the approximation. Its post-wave equation is exact *conditional on the exit exposures*, so an error in the in-wave exposure carries forward. There is also a concrete weekly inconsistency: the no-wave reference adds \(k e^{\gamma s}\), while the core fitter uses the exact Gompertz increment \(k e^{\gamma s}(e^\gamma-1)/\gamma\). Finally, the Czech analysis declares \(\alpha\) unidentified, yet the revised negative-control table fixes it at **1.18**, near the rejected optimum. [Wave equations, pp. 16–17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:574) · [Czech identification result, pp. 32–33](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:913) · [Table 8 configuration, p. 27](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:820)

**Fix.** Make the core-only Czech analysis primary and show fixed-\(\alpha\) results as sensitivity analyses. For the extension, use the same \(L(B,W)\) during and after the wave, with exact weekly baseline increments. Given a specified baseline path and exponent, estimate nonnegative wave increments from weekly survival under that model and carry the resulting survivor distribution forward. Validate recovery of wave exposure and subsequent mortality as well as recovery of \(\alpha\).

The paper has a publishable mathematical core as a **model-dependent descriptive comparison**. The two changes that would most affect my assessment are correcting the first-window baseline fit and demonstrating calibrated end-to-end performance for that exact estimator. Until then, the Czech curves show how mortality contrasts change under the chosen normalization. They leave open how much of that change represents actual depletion of susceptible people.
