### 2. The published fit, the default fit, and the diagnostic pass are different things

Section 2.5 estimates \(k\) and \(\theta\) jointly in the first quiet window. The supplied implementation defaults to `joint_seed=False`, estimating \(k\) from early observed hazards. The Table 8 replay explicitly overrides the default. This choice matters even with noiseless data. With positive frailty, an observed weekly hazard increment is smaller than its individual-baseline counterpart. Dividing early observed increments by unit Gompertz growth therefore need not recover the generating \(k\). Conversely, the joint seed fixes the prior wave offset at zero. If a shock precedes the first quiet window, the seed can absorb earlier depletion into \(k\), which the pooled refit then holds fixed. [Seed and pooled objectives, §2.5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:380) · [Implementation default and early-rate calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/3-chain-evaluated-changes.diff:719) · [Table 8 override](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/3-chain-evaluated-changes.diff:397)

For dose 1 among people born in the 1950s, moving the window start by four weeks changed \(\hat\theta\) from **9.750 to 0.00793**. Both fits passed. The scan also includes four early post-skip weeks in the final refit, although the main objective sums over the specified quiet windows. These results establish that the pipeline returned estimates across window placements. They leave open whether the variance, or the KCOR ratio derived from it, is stable enough to interpret. ### 4. The post-wave equation is sound conditional on its inputs. The in-wave calculation does not establish those inputs

The new post-wave survival equation correctly accounts for selection of survivors **given** the baseline exposure \(B_e\) and wave exposure \(W_e\) at exit. The regression test checks that conditional statement: it takes the algorithm’s estimated \(W_e\), uses it to generate subsequent survival independently, and recovers a known later baseline increment. The test never asks whether the algorithm recovered a known *wave exposure*. During the wave, m83 still divides accumulated excess by the current week’s approximate gamma moment. The paper now acknowledges that approximation. There is also a discrete-time inconsistency: the wave reference adds \(k e^{\gamma s}\) each week, while the revised core uses the exact increment \(k e^{\gamma s}(e^\gamma-1)/\gamma\). Even a no-wave Gompertz trajectory can therefore acquire a small artificial excess when \(\gamma>0\). [In-wave reference and exit equation, §2.7.3](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:576) · [Core weekly equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:372) · [Post-wave test construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/turns/3-chain-evaluated-changes.diff:2123)

**Fix.** Use the exact baseline increments throughout. For a prespecified baseline path and exponent, use the paper’s own survival model \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) during the wave as well. Check whether a nonnegative wave-exposure path can reproduce the observed survival, and report incompatibility when it cannot. Test the entire calculation on independently generated baseline and wave paths, including zero wave, \(\alpha=1\), and \(\alpha\ne1\). The baseline/wave split remains assumption-dependent even if that numerical inversion is exact.

### 5. Rebuild the validation around the estimator readers will actually use

Several earlier discrepancies now have clear explanations. Figure 1 uses an **older monthly normalization** and selected examples with final KCOR between 0.85 and 1.15. The Cox-grid figure gives KCOR the **generating frailty variances**. The multiwave recovery example fixes the baseline rate using expected deaths. Table 11 uses an earlier single-window fitter. The positive control multiplies the **hazard among survivors** and restores its unperturbed trajectory outside the effect window, which differs from generating an individual-level effect and allowing subsequent survivor selection to evolve. These exercises test individual pieces. Together they still leave the current estimator’s end-to-end performance unresolved. [Figure 1 selection and caption](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:137) · [Known-variance Cox comparison](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:718) · [Recovery example](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:798) · [Table 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266) · [Positive-control construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:209)

I would retain those exercises with their narrower interpretations and add one decisive benchmark: generate individual outcomes from known baseline, frailty, and exposure histories, reduce them to the weekly counts available to the analyst, and run the primary pipeline without supplying any generating parameters. Include a common additive shock after the fitting windows. If cumulative baseline is \(G\) and the common frailty-independent shock is \(W\), ordinary gamma inversion returns \(e^{\theta W}G+(e^{\theta W}-1)/\theta\). Two cohorts with identical \(G\) and \(W\) can therefore acquire different adjusted hazards even with perfectly known \(\theta\). M83 now acknowledges that errors can go in either direction. Showing the size of this distortion alongside the observed, unanchored, and anchored contrasts would turn that acknowledgment into a usable sensitivity analysis. The 20-replicate quiet-window experiment does not bound this post-window mechanism. [Full-trajectory inversion, §2.6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:440) · [Contamination design and interpretation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:849) · [Revised limitation, §5.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s4-approved/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1032)

The publishable contribution is a **descriptive sensitivity analysis under an explicit frailty model**. My first revision would be a wave-off Table 8 with comparison-specific diagnostic outcomes, followed by a paired coverage study of that exact estimator. Those two results would tell readers how often KCOR yields a reportable answer and how much confidence that answer deserves.
