# Independent scientific review of candidate m161

**Question.** Please examine KCOR closely. What is wrong, if anything, and how would you fix it?

The proportional gamma inversion and the identification formula under a known Gompertz slope are algebraically consistent. The paper also recognizes that an anchored trajectory can be flat for proportional reference hazards and that its registry illustrations do not identify treatment effects. I found three reproducible errors in the implementation or its scientific reporting. The first affects where a model violation is diagnosed, the second changes the population entering an ordinary endpoint, and the third concerns wave attribution diagnostics.

## 1. A protective first gap can generate a false deficit in a later, ordinary gap

**Severity: major for the per-gap assumption diagnostic.** Section 2.5 gives the signed entry-rate identity and says a gap with an upper percentile limit below zero is flagged individually. In `code/KCOR.py` around lines 1435–1442, the estimator computes the log multiplier in the identity but replaces it with its positive part before constructing the baseline scale used by the diagnostic. The signed first-pass offsets at lines 1537–1540 inherit this clamp. After a protective first gap, the effective seed rate exceeds the true rate. Subtracting its increasingly overestimated Gompertz cumulative baseline biases later signed changes downward, even when those gaps follow the baseline exactly.

I checked a 65-week mean-one gamma cohort with true weekly baseline rate 0.006, annual Gompertz slope 0.085, frailty variance 8, four initial fitting anchors and quiet windows 15–34 and 45–64 (zero-based). I multiplied baseline increments by 0.3 only in weeks 4–14. Weeks 35–44 were ordinary baseline weeks. Given exact model hazards, the seed recovered frailty 8 and had status `ok`. At re-entry weeks 15 and 45, the true incremental offsets were −0.046924 and 0. The production fitting function reported −0.101682 and −0.113628. With a sample of five million people and 50 matched multinomial person-bootstrap draws (NumPy default RNG seed 921), the percentile range for the untouched second gap was [−0.12384, −0.10697]. The first gap genuinely violates the proportional excess assumption. The second flag is a localization error even though the overall cohort warrants a flag.

**Minimal fix.** Use the signed entry-rate identity for the diagnostic baseline. Keep the existing nonnegative incremental constraint in the fitting objective. If a signed diagnostic seed is outside the numerical rate bounds, mark the affected diagnostic unassessed. Recheck the gap-specific counts for the reported proportional applications and add an exact two-gap regression with a protective first gap and zero extra exposure in the second.

## 2. The same valid cohort yields different all-ages KCOR depending on whether a redundant total is provided

**Severity: major for general workbook inputs containing post-2020 births.** The ordinary fit unconditionally removes individual birth years after 2020 (`code/KCOR.py:6304`). It then either trusts a supplied all-ages row or sums the remaining individual rows to construct one (around lines 6379–6390). The premature filter removes real children alive at enrollment from a reconstructed total but retains them in the supplied total. The separate display-age filter already occurs after all-ages construction.

I used two complete, death-only birth-year strata (1950 and 2021), two dose groups and a 2023-01 enrollment. Both strata had 20,000 people per dose at enrollment. I used 20 weeks, prespecified fitting weeks 2–13, and doubled deaths in weeks 16–19 in just the 2021 birth stratum of dose 1. I ran the ordinary workbook point-estimation path twice with identical disjoint counts, varying only whether their exact sum was also supplied as the all-ages rows. The final anchored dose-1/dose-0 all-ages result was 1.000000 without supplied totals and 1.116576 with them. Both runs reported OK for the numerator and denominator fits. No bootstrap is needed to expose this discrepancy. The stated S6 Czech extract restricts known birth years to 1920–2005, so this example does not imply that its published table changes.

**Minimal fix.** Remove the hard-coded 2020 upper limit before ordinary source validation and aggregate reconstruction. Apply an expressly defined population-eligibility rule consistently to supplied and reconstructed totals. Any calendar plausibility check should use the enrollment year and identify incompatible source counts. Extend the aggregate-encoding parity check to an eligible post-2020 birth year.

## 3. The wave report loses or misclassifies deficit weeks for supported identity cases

**Severity: moderate for the optional module's diagnostic output.** Section 2.7.3 specifies a week-by-week deficit count when observed mortality is below that week's planned conditional baseline. `code/kcor_survivor.py` around lines 147–153 diverts near-zero frailty and alpha equal to one to the older correction function. At alpha equal to one it returns before counting any marked weeks (`code/KCOR.py:2621`). For zero frailty it compares cumulative observed exposure with cumulative planned baseline (around lines 2674–2702). A previous excess can therefore mask a later weekly deficit. The workbook exports the resulting fields in its wave report (around lines 7826–7831).

For three marked weeks with observed integrated hazards 0.002, 0.030 and 0.002, planned baseline rate 0.010 and annual slope 0.085, weeks one and three are deficits. At zero frailty and alpha 1.18, the correction reported three wave points, one skipped nonpositive excess, no deficit count and final wave exposure 0.003926. The documented weekly attribution gives two deficit weeks and wave exposure about 0.01998. At frailty 0.5 and alpha 1, the function reported zero wave weeks despite all three dates being marked. Both shortcuts give the correct *total* reference-frailty cumulative hazard for these cases. Their partition and diagnostic report are wrong or unavailable.

**Minimal fix.** Keep the exact identities for the cumulative endpoint and compute the weekly baseline/wave split and deficit counts in the identity branches. At zero frailty split each integrated hazard at the planned weekly baseline. At alpha one use the exact proportional exposure increment implied by conditional survival. Return the same wave-report fields as on the nonlinear path.

I used the candidate manuscript, supplement, source and small controlled checks. I did not refit the published 999-draw analyses. The three examples establish the defects and their scope without establishing any unmeasured change to published estimates.
