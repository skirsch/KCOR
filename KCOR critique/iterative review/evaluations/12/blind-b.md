# Independent review of the KCOR candidate

I found a misattributed validation result, an all-ages population error in the ordinary workbook, and an unsupported premise in the interpretation of the empirical control. The proportional gamma inversion and the exact-model identification argument in §2.4 are internally consistent given the fixed Gompertz slope. The latter is an identification statement within a model. The Czech counts alone cannot establish that frailty produced the observed curvature.

## 1. A displayed synthetic validation uses a different estimator

**Major for the validation claim.** Main §3.1.1 groups the synthetic negative-control figure with evidence for the revised working-model alignment. SI S4.2.1 calls the setting a gamma-frailty null, and the S4 settings table gives the estimator as fixed-slope Gompertz. The figure actually uses a five-class discrete mixture and separate four-parameter relaxation fits to each cohort’s log hazard across the entire displayed follow-up. The script divides out those fitted shapes and anchors the ratio of the resulting cumulative hazards. It never estimates a gamma frailty variance or fits the stated quiet-window objective. When each relaxation curve fits its cohort closely, this procedure leaves two nearly constant hazards, so anchoring yields a curve close to one.

The source is `code/generate_pathological_neg_control_figs.py`, especially lines 55, 230, 261, 359–360 and 436–451. The displayed figure has the same panel titles, legends, mixture hazards, reference week and terminal week as this generator. I replayed its functions with `example/Frail_cohort_mix.xlsx`, defaults and weeks 0–250. Initial hazards were 0.053401 and 0.062940. The plotted statistic ranged from 0.996591 to 1.000335 and ended at 0.997533, matching the figure. The current-fitter simulation in SI S9 is separate and remains relevant.

**Minimal fix.** Identify the figure, its discrete mixture and its full-follow-up relaxation fit as historical in the heading, caption, settings table and main-text cross-reference. Remove the attribution to the revised estimator. If the figure is to validate the current procedure, supply a prespecified fitting window and regenerate it through the current fitter and inversion.

## 2. Adding a redundant aggregate changes the ordinary all-ages estimate

**Major for affected workbook inputs.** Before constructing the all-ages series, the ordinary workbook unconditionally filters birth years to 2020 or earlier (`code/KCOR.py:6304`). A later enrollment can legitimately include people born after 2020. When the input includes a correct all-ages row, the program retains its counts. Without that row, the program reconstructs the total from the filtered component rows (`code/KCOR.py:6382`). These representations of the same cohort produce different estimates.

I ran `process_workbook` twice on the same 75 weekly death-only histories beginning 2022-02-07, with birth years 1940 and 2021, doses 0 and 2, and 10,000 people per cell at enrollment. Both 1940 cohorts had 12 deaths each week. The 2021 cohorts had 1 weekly death at dose 0 and 2 plus the integer part of week/12 at dose 2. I used the Czech fitter with NPH off, skip 2 and reference week 5. Without a supplied total, the terminal unanchored and anchored dose-2/dose-0 ratios were both 1. With the exact rowwise population totals supplied, the ratios were 1.249732 and 1.162427. Birth year 2021 disappeared from the individual-stratum output in both runs. The Czech S6 product-restricted input already excludes known birth years after 2005, so this example does not change its reported estimates.

**Minimal fix.** Apply explicit population eligibility consistently to all-ages inputs. Remove the calendar-year constant from the ordinary path. If an input birth-year plausibility screen is desired, validate against the actual enrollment date and the input’s birth-year precision. Test that supplying the exact aggregate and reconstructing it from components give identical results, including for a valid post-2020 birth.

## 3. Census enrollment does not imply little depletion or poor frailty resolution

**Moderate interpretive error.** SI S4.2.2, line 199, reasons that because the age-shift cohorts contain complete registry strata, selection-induced depletion is reduced and fitted variances should be small or weakly identified. Including every eligible person removes an additional sampling step. It places no upper bound on heterogeneity among people alive at enrollment or on subsequent selective mortality. For example, a complete stratum with mean-one gamma variance 4 and proportional cumulative baseline exposure 0.25 has mean survivor frailty 1/(1+4×0.25) = 0.5. The depletion occurs in the complete population. With sufficiently many deaths under the specified Gompertz model, that variance can also be estimated precisely. In the paper’s own age-shift figure, the three raw fitted variances were 0.0160, 0.0858 and 1.9552 (`analysis/age_shift/diagnostics.json`).

**Minimal fix.** Say that the construction introduces no extra selective subsampling. Remove the implied prediction about depletion or parameter precision from completeness. Keep the explicit proportionality condition for a flat anchored comparison and report the observed subgroup fits and diagnostics.

The review used the candidate manuscript and supplement, figure generator, ordinary method code and archived numerical evidence. The two targeted workbook runs and the historical figure replay wrote no results into the candidate. I did not rerun the large bootstrap studies.
