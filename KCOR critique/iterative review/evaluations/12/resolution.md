# Round 12 independent resolution review of m156 against m161

**Closure of all 18 round 11 objections is not supported.** Fifteen corrections meet their original requested scope. Three need qualification. The demonstrated unknown-birth-year case in 11.1 is fixed, but a legitimate known birth year still changes the all-ages population when the same total is supplied explicitly. These findings concern cohort membership, reporting and implementation consistency. The targeted checks found no new defect in the proportional gamma inversion under its stated model. The Czech primary Table 8 arrays support the published numerical summaries. They do not establish that gamma frailty identifies a treatment effect in observational cohorts.

## Remaining scope and concrete corrections

1. **High for the affected all-ages point.** The ordinary entry point drops all birth years above 2020 before validating or rebuilding the aggregate (`code/KCOR.py:6304`). For enrollment 2023-19 I used a complete 20-week death-only history containing people born in 1950 and 2021. In the 2021 dose-1 group, the first ten weeks have 20 deaths per week and the next ten have 90. Every subsequent opening risk equals the preceding opening risk minus deaths. Without supplied totals, all-ages KCOR was 1.000593. Adding the exact sums as year −2 gave 1.722622. Both rows had OK normalization. The cutoff predates m157. Apply the same enrollment eligibility rule to both input representations and aggregate all eligible components before filtering displayed ages. Include post-2020 births in the representation test.
2. **Minor for the new wave diagnostic.** On a 2021-24 workbook with an active exponent of one and three observed marked weeks, 2021-29 through 2021-31, the record exported identity mode, zero wave points, missing strict deficits and zero zero-excess weeks. The identity shortcut returns before measuring the mask. Count eligible marked dates before branching and leave unevaluated diagnostic counts blank. Preserve the unchanged identity inversion.
3. **Minor for the published supplement.** I read the y-axis in the m161 image embedded by SI §S4.2.1 and matched it to `test/sim_grid/code/plot_cox_bias_sim.py:109`. Change that axis to “Observed week-52 KCOR minus one” and rebuild the figure from the archived endpoints. The legacy CSV field can retain its name.

## Related discrepancies and their origin

1. **Dedicated replay and ordinary workbook disagree on an undefined stratum reference.** Starting from the published 2021-24 person cells, I moved the first six weeks' 1940 dose-2 deaths to terminal survivors in that same subgroup, preserving enrollment counts and complete weekly histories. The ordinary workbook's available-strata rule returned 2/0 = 1.209732 and 2/1 = 1.219862 using 1950 and 1960, retained weight 0.704731. The dedicated replay returned unavailable for both because it requires every positive-weight stratum to have a finite ratio. Both gave 1/0 = 1.023409. This pre-existing divergence differs from exhaustion and from the early-rate weighting fallback. Use the ordinary contributor and failure rules, retain the contributor set for each draw and apply the existing rule for changes from the point population. The earlier [composition audit](../../../objections/9.8/README.md) found positive anchor deaths in every published history.
2. **The revised positivity check introduced a direct/pooled disagreement for very small reference hazards.** A single 1950 stratum with two cohorts of 10¹³ people, four accumulating anchor weeks with one or two deaths per week, and positive subsequent deaths gives a pooled point of 0.509146. Its only direct contributor and the direct all-ages row are both unavailable for an undefined reference. The pooled path accepts finite positive hazards while `_set_kcor_reference` still requires values above 10⁻¹². This is a numerical stress case. Share the same reference and finite-range rules and test both ends of the representable range. The 11.3 large-ratio example itself passes.
3. **Pooled fit-status labels still select one age.** The pooled numeric frailty fields are now blank, but `theta0_status_num` and `theta0_status_den` still come from the first contributors with finite fitted values (`code/KCOR.py:4851–4891`). In the checked Czech row all three matched, so those displayed labels happened to agree. Leave pooled parameter-status cells blank or label an explicit summary of all contributing statuses. Keep the existing separate fit-flag counts. This selection predates m157.
4. **The Cox figure overstates the cause of the individual estimates.** The caption attributes departures from one solely to differential depletion and nonproportional hazards. At zero variance in both cohorts, the archived estimate is 0.988080 with nominal limits [0.968753, 1.007793]. The equal-variance zero cell has no differential depletion. Describe the systematic trend separately from sampling variation in individual points. This wording predates the new nominal-inference labels.
5. **Historical S7 has a first-week risk-accounting discrepancy.** The quantile-bin masses sum to 0.998. The generator allocates that fraction of the nominal two million to its bins but sets opening risk to the full two million. In a two-week check at theta 0.3, opening risk was 2,000,000, first-week expected deaths were 3,973.8828 and the next opening risk was 1,992,026.1172, leaving 4,000 unexplained people. The theta-0.8 case had the same discrepancy. The two skipped weeks keep that first denominator out of the archived fitted hazard. Document the discrepancy in the historical record and use consistent mass and opening risk for any future rerun. This concerns the historical generator newly cited by S4.6.
