## Two additional defects

**A supplied total in one Monte Carlo replicate can remove another replicate's direct all-ages result.** With aggregate reporting enabled, the first replicate from the 16.2 fixture returned 20 direct all-ages rows (`YearOfBirth=-2`) and a final KCOR of 1.571914777. It retained all 20 when batched with the same second age-stratified replicate. I then added only the exact weekly all-ages totals for both doses of replicate 2, leaving every original row in place. Both m177 and m180 returned zero direct all-ages rows for replicate 1. I verified the disappearance in the written m180 worksheet. Its age-standardized series still had all 20 rows and endpoint 1.428233994. The MC summary takes its input from the resulting rows, so this layout can exclude a valid realization from its all-ages summary.

The source-selection helper checks for a supplied total across the whole frame. The all-ages fit and hazard construction likewise decide at sheet level whether reconstruction is needed (`code/KCOR.py` lines 4301–4306, 7010–7012 and 7555–7561 in m180). Apply that decision separately inside each replicate and use the resulting populations consistently for fitting and reporting. The demonstrated impact is confined to batched simulations that mix supplied and reconstructible totals.

**An exhaustive total can fail against its fractional component sum by one floating-point unit.** In the same 20-week nested fixture, I set the integer opening risks of the 1940 and 1960 groups to 20,001 and 10,003 in each dose, retaining their weekly death counts and valid death-only histories. I constructed the inclusive totals in integers, then divided all risks and deaths by ten. At opening, the component values are 2,000.1 and 1,000.3 and the supplied full total is 3,000.4. Their floating-point sum differs from that total by 4.55 × 10⁻¹³ person. All individual histories pass. In both candidates the ordinary writer returned all-ages KCOR 1.003222773 when reconstructing from the two components. Supplying their correct inclusive total yielded `invalid_aggregate_counts` and a missing all-ages point. The 1960+ point in the supplied-total workbook remained 1.003941436.

The separate exhaustive comparison uses exact array equality before reaching the corrected fractional complement logic (`code/fixed_cohort_history.py` lines 84–96 in m180). A scale-aware floating-point equality check for fractional component sums would restore the point while preserving exact integer reconciliation. This affects point availability in fractional expected-count inputs. The integer Czech study and person bootstrap use a different input regime.
