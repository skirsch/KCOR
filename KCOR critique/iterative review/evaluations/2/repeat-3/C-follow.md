# KCOR follow-up on the baseline objections

The three requested checks were addressed in the open review. Their status in the supplied revision is as follows.

- **Last gap bin — still omitted.** The exact weekly increment replaces Euler reconstruction in both code paths. But `code/KCOR.py` sets `gidx = next_start - 1` (lines 1385–1390), saves the cumulative state before adding that bin’s hazard (1453–1458), reads the saved state at `gidx` (1464–1465), and applies the resulting offset starting at `gidx + 1` (1535–1538). The repeated calculation at 1617–1623 does the same. Holding the iterate fixed, a shock confined to the last gap week changes the state at the start of the next quiet week but never enters the offset applied there. Read both observed and reference states at that next boundary.
- **Crude versus age-specific ratio — still present.** SI §S6, lines 427–433, gives six raw Dose 0/Dose 2 cumulative-hazard ratios for ages 40–99 of 1.2776, 1.5622, 1.9655, 2.1343, 1.8355 and 1.5008. Its full-population row gives 0.023160/0.073323 = 0.3159. The note recognizes aggregation. The full-population row also has broader age support, so the table alone cannot establish how much of the change comes from mixing those six bands. Report the crude and common-weight standardized comparisons on identical age support. A cumulative-hazard ratio \(H_A/H_B\) also differs from the mortality-risk ratio \((1-e^{-H_A})/(1-e^{-H_B})\).
- **Shared-frailty Cox curve — the plotted comparator was never fitted across the grid.** In `test/sim_grid/code/plot_estimator_bias_vs_theta.py`, lines 20–24 and 51–56, the displayed HR is $1+[(0.94-1)/(0.87-1)](HR_{Cox}-1)$. The constants come from one comparison-table row. The paper now explicitly describes the approximation in §2.11.1 and the figure caption (lines 729 and 737). This resolves any ambiguity about what the authors disclose. The curve supplies no empirical comparison of separately fitted shared-frailty models at the plotted variance levels. This finding concerns that figure and does not establish how the source table’s single calibration value was obtained.

**Other details whose status could be unclear**

- **Sparse fits still return zero with success.** The insufficient-deaths path at `code/KCOR.py` lines 1253–1277 explicitly returns `(k_fixed, 0.0)`, `success=True`, and `identifiability_flag=False`. The separate first-pass insufficient-signal branch does likewise at lines 1486–1513. A zero used in inversion passes through the observed cumulative hazard. The status fields distinguish the causes internally. Published estimates still need to identify substitutions and apply their declared reporting rule.
- **The original 2022 control intervals still exist as historical results.** Table 8 explicitly labels the v4.3 analytic estimates of 1.0756 [1.055, 1.097] and 1.0911 [1.070, 1.112]. Its corresponding revised joint-fit person-bootstrap estimates are 1.0575 [0.238, 1.234] and 1.0068 [0.806, 1.209]. The historical intervals exclude unity. The revised ones include it but are too wide to establish close equivalence. The open review addressed the anchoring and disparate-age control targets separately.
- **The original coverage defects persist in the historical pilot only.** I recounted `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage_replicates.csv`. The null, harm, benefit and sparse scenarios each have one distinct outer point estimate across 500 rows. Their reported coverage is 100%. The non-gamma scenario has 500 distinct estimates and 25.4% coverage against a single sampled reference. The pilot uses expected fractional deaths in its gamma generator and independent, survivor-capped Poisson week counts in its resampler. Its table in the paper still leaves coverage pending. The statement that §2.9 specifies contiguous blocks **no longer applies**. It specifies resampling complete people, and the newer coverage archive has stochastic outer draws. The ordinary workbook path still produces analytic log intervals and clips the upper bound at ten times the point estimate (`code/KCOR.py`, lines 4445–4475). These three interval procedures have different calibration evidence.
- **Scaling is partially corrected.** Section 2.4.4 now gives the correct effect of rescaling an observed hazard. Scaling an individual Gompertz baseline by $c$ instead changes the observed rate from $kG'/(1+\theta kG)$ to $ckG'/(1+\theta ckG)$, generally a time-dependent relative change. A constant individual effect remains confounded with baseline scale. The abstract and §1.5 retain the broader invariance claim. The open review documented the uncorrected curvature proposition, slope sensitivity and limits of same-count diagnostics.
- **The remaining validation and attribution claims were answered in the open review.** The Cox generator receives true frailty, the noiseless multiwave example fixes $k=h(0)$, and the old single-window grid calls `fit_k_theta_cumhaz` (`generate_cox_bias_sim.py`, lines 209–235, 279–285; `theta0_nwave.py`, line 183; `generate_sim_grid.py`, lines 857–867). The contamination study still tests positive additive excess and one dynamic shape. It does not establish a Czech sign-reversal magnitude or a false-positive rate. The revised post-wave conditional inversion addresses propagation **given correct exit exposures**. The in-wave survivor moment and division of cumulative excess by a current factor remain unsupported. The open review also covered the primary/sensitivity configuration, window choice, result-selected illustration, landmark exclusions and uncertainty in the external effect scale.
