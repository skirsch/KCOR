# Review of KCOR

**Major revision.** KCOR’s core inversion is correct under its stated proportional gamma-frailty model. The revised weekly increment is also correct, and the new post-wave calculation solves a well-defined conditional survival problem. The evidence still falls short of establishing that KCOR can distinguish depletion from changes in individual risk in registry data. Two empirical results change my assessment of its reliability. One nominal 95% interval covered in only 502 of 600 simulated datasets despite every original fit passing the diagnostics. In the revised 2022 Czech negative control, four of nine original stratum fits had insufficient signal and 998 of 999 bootstrap draws contained such a fit. I would publish the core as a descriptive, model-dependent sensitivity analysis. Claims of validated depletion removal or calibrated uncertainty require further work.

## What the revised evidence shows

1. **The new person-bootstrap study is informative, and its most precise setting exposes undercoverage.** Resampling a person’s complete history, equivalently sampling joint cells of membership and death week or final survival for these fixed cohorts, preserves risk sets. The revised implementation also separates Monte Carlo clocks and fit diagnostics by replicate. In the independent outer replicates archived in `validation/person_bootstrap/replicates.csv`, I count 502/600 covered in the million-person unequal-frailty scenario, or 83.7% (Wilson interval 80.5%–86.4%). All 600 passed. The corresponding 100,000-person scenario covered in 96.2%. In the sparse scenario the unconditional fraction was 97.3% but zero of 600 passed, so pass-conditional coverage is undefined. Report these outcomes next to the diagnostic rule and mean error. A bootstrap can propagate sampling variation around a persistently biased estimator while producing increasingly narrow intervals.

   The archive contains a **paired comparison** in addition to the 600-dataset study. Counting the 200 pairs in `validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json` gives 160/200 covered with fixed initial scale and 194/200 with joint initialization on the same datasets, using 199 bootstrap draws each. There are 38 joint-only and four fixed-only coverages. The mean interval width changes from 0.0478 to 0.1488 and the point-estimate RMSE from 0.0136 to 0.0375. The joint method has better coverage here and materially poorer precision. Section 2.9 instead cites 189/200 for a joint study and contrasts it with 502/600 from another design. The 189 is not the tally in the supplied paired archive. Identify that study and its run separately if it exists, and report the actual matched comparison. The paired archive names a source hash different from the distributed `code/KCOR.py`. Ship a runnable generator, exact source versions, independent truths, and design manifests for both studies. Validate the complete empirical estimator, including the wave module, gaps, anchor, pooling and fit-failure policy. Neither of these no-wave comparisons establishes coverage for Table 8. Retire the deterministic expected-count/weekly-Poisson pilot from the evidence for calibration and replace the pending coverage table with the completed results.

2. **Table 8 is evidence of instability in a real control.** The table now distinguishes historical analytic intervals from the joint-fit person bootstrap. It preserves the overlap of the vaccinated, unvaccinated and all-person pseudo-strata within an enrollment when resampling. The published 2022 ratios of 1.0503, 1.0575 and 1.0068 have percentile intervals [0.233, 1.299], [0.238, 1.234] and [0.806, 1.209]. The fixed-scale version on the same revised settings gives 1.0065, 1.0115 and 1.0049. Inclusion of one across a range spanning 0.233 to 1.299 gives little information about equivalence. The odds of these controls detecting substantial drift depend on their precision.

   The first-pass insufficient-signal branch returns numerical zero for frailty and optimizer success. The Table 8 script includes these estimates. Its summary reports four insufficient-signal fits in the original 2022 enrollment, at least one in 998/999 draws, and zero unsuccessful stratum fits. The 2021 counts are zero in the original and 512/999 draws. Thus the table describes the sampling distribution of an algorithm that sometimes substitutes zero. Section 2.9’s diagnostic-conditioning language and Box 2’s withholding rule require explicit reconciliation. Only one 2022 draw was free of that flag across all nine fits. Publish pair-specific contributions, fitted parameters, boundary/fallback frequencies, a prespecified pooling rule, and the fraction for which the target can be reported. Distinguish a true zero, a constrained estimate and an unidentified fit. If a mandatory component fails, mark that comparison unidentified under the declared rule. Label a fallback-inclusive analysis explicitly. Cite `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json` for the revised results. State that its outcome-weighted geometric mean uses overlapping pseudo-strata and that the three pairwise contrasts are related. Section 2.9 says frailty is never fitted separately by stratum, whereas this script fits all nine stratum-by-pseudo-dose cells.

## Identification, estimation and interpretation

3. **State exactly what information identifies frailty.** Write $G(t)=(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma$. The quiet-period model gives $H(t)=\log(1+\theta kG(t))/\theta$ and $h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2$. For positive $h$ and an independently known slope $\gamma$, an exact trajectory yields $\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2$. At $\theta=0$ a positive Gompertz slope alone produces cumulative curvature. At $\theta k=\gamma>0$ the cumulative hazard is straight despite positive frailty. The proposition in §2.4.4 equating nonzero curvature with positive frailty fails both checks. An error $\delta\gamma$ changes the local implied variance by $\delta\gamma/h$, especially consequential for low-risk cohorts. Use the actual grouped probabilities, report joint profiles for scale and variance, and vary the assumed slope using independent constraints. Assess linearity of the fitted quiet baseline against $G(t)$ or inspect interval count residuals. It is generally curved in elapsed calendar time.

   Section 2.4.4 now correctly distinguishes multiplying the **observed** hazard by $c$ (giving $k'=ck,\ \theta'=\theta/c$) from multiplying the individual baseline by $c$ (preserving the underlying frailty variance). The abstract and §1.5 retain the old invariance and unsupported “second-order” language. A common factor can cancel from a ratio of the two rescaled reconstructed baselines even as both fitted variances change. Propagate the correction throughout.

4. **Passing diagnostics cannot identify the mechanism.** For any observed cumulative hazard $H$ and any $q>0$, the baseline $B_q=\operatorname{expm1}(qH)/q$ with gamma variance $q$ generates exactly the observed survival. A homogeneous population with baseline $H$ does too. Fixing a quiet-period baseline family restricts that equivalence by assumption. Checking residuals against the same counts cannot determine whether the restriction describes biology. Sections 2.6.2 and 5.1–5.2 now explicitly acknowledge undetectable contamination and errors of either sign. Other claims that failures explicitly signal unreliability, residual deviations reflect real risk, or crossover necessarily biases toward unity still go beyond the evidence. Report alternative baseline/frailty specifications and resulting contrasts, including a no-frailty baseline-drift model. Use independent measurements of prior health, calendar risk and uptake to constrain interpretation. Define the skipped period as a survivor landmark, identify its risk set and report early event risks. Its unit-frailty baseline is standardized to those survivors if the mean-one convention is reset there.

5. **The manuscript, production code and Table 8 still fit different estimators.** Section 2.5 specifies a joint first-quiet-window fit. Production fixes $k$ from early detrended rates and estimates only $\theta$ in that window. The Table 8 adapter modifies the production function’s source at runtime to insert a joint fit. Both then include anchor-only bins in the final objective, despite the paper’s quiet-only specification. The joint initializer uses a continuous Gompertz integral, the subsequent fitter sums left-endpoint rates, and both fit weekly integrated observations using instantaneous-rate predictions. Use one documented, versioned callable in the workbook, validation and replay. For example, given the modeled marginal cumulative hazard $H_m$, fit interval deaths conditional on their actual starting risk sets with $p_i=1-\exp\{-[H_m(t_{i+1})-H_m(t_i)]\}$. This uses the same time convention as the risk calculation and gives high- and low-information weeks appropriate weights.

   The update $(1+\theta B_i)\operatorname{expm1}(\theta u_i)/\theta$ for observed integrated hazard $u_i$ fixes the previous Euler approximation. One boundary error survives it. Both offset calculations record $B_i$ **before** consuming bin $i$, choose `gidx = next_start - 1`, and then read the start state at `gidx`. The final gap week’s excess is omitted from the offset applied to the next quiet week. A shock confined to that bin produces no change to this offset on a fixed-parameter reconstruction. Difference observed and reference states at the start of the next quiet interval and test this case. See `code/KCOR.py` lines 1227, 1377, 1385–1392, 1453–1466 and 1617–1624, and `analysis/table8/compare.py`.

6. **Specify the clinical and statistical targets before comparing curves.** The unit-frailty baseline ratio, its ratio relative to a reference week, the observed risk ratio and the age-standardized risk difference answer different questions. A constant true level difference is identically erased by anchoring. Section 2.1.1 uses a single reference time, §2.8 a window mean, and production uses one indexed ratio with a fallback to one. Declare one anchor, its origin after the skip, and an undefined result when necessary. Display the raw and anchored ratios alongside observed risks by age and standardized to stated weights. The SI reports a pooled crude hazard ratio of 0.3159 while all six displayed age-specific ratios exceed one. Composition and age support matter before any frailty transformation. A pseudo-dose label within vaccination status has no intervention meaning, but ages separated by one or two decades can have different underlying hazards and trends. Prespecify a plausible relative-drift target and an equivalence margin. Use a simultaneous band if a claim concerns the entire curve. Distinguish historical figure bands, ordinary-workbook analytic intervals and revised pointwise person-bootstrap intervals. If combining repeated enrollments for inference, resample people jointly across their memberships.

## Epidemic-wave adjustment and external validation

7. **The post-wave equation is sound conditional on its inputs. The in-wave calculation does not supply those inputs under the stated model.** Let $L(B,W)=E[\exp(-BZ-WZ^\alpha)]$ for starting frailty $Z$. Given correct cumulative baseline and wave exposures $(B_e,W_e)$ at exit, surviving a further baseline exposure $u$ has probability $L(B_e+u,W_e)/L(B_e,W_e)$. The ratio decreases in $u$, so the revised inversion is principled. Ordinary inversion of the *combined* baseline and wave is exact at $\alpha=1$, consistent with the revised identity shortcut.

   Under baseline-only selection from a mean-one gamma, however, a survivor still has shape $1/\theta$ and its scale is $\theta/(1+\theta B)$. Its moment is $\Gamma(1/\theta+\alpha)[\theta/(1+\theta B)]^\alpha/\Gamma(1/\theta)$. At $\alpha=1$ the moment is $1/(1+\theta B)$. The code computes a fresh mean-one gamma moment using a declining parameter. A factor defined *relative* to proportional amplification could equal one at $\alpha=1$, but that is a different definition from the absolute survivor moment stated in §2.7.1 and SI §S2.1. During a nonlinear wave, the survivor distribution is tilted by $\exp(-BZ-WZ^\alpha)$, generally outside the gamma family. Even with the correct instantaneous moment $F(t)$, dividing a cumulative excess by its terminal value $F(t)$ does not undo $\int A(s)F(s)\,ds$. Positive-only scaling, zero excess by construction on the first wave bin and a cumulative maximum can alter the inferred exit exposures. After a monotonicity repair the displayed exit total can even differ from $B_e+W_e$. Exact conditional propagation carries the error in those starting values forward.

   Prespecify or independently estimate the reference baseline. For a fixed $\alpha$, propagate the tilted survivor law through each bin and solve or fit the survival ratio for its wave increment. An all-cause count gives only one survival probability per bin, so estimating two unrestricted baseline and wave increments requires additional information. Test known simulated exposures, wave-onset and exit bins, multiwave histories and uncertainty in the external intervention scale. Present $\alpha=1.18$ separately as sensitivity-only. The Czech YAML sets both the forced and identified fields to 1.18 while §3.4 reports non-identification. Table 8 discloses the override, but Box 2 says the module is inactive when identification fails. Release the primary control with the optional correction off and provide the forced counterpart. Its YAML comment gives “least vaccine harm” as a reason for choosing 2023. Document a dated, independent window-selection rule and disclose outcome-informed choices.

8. **Other validation studies continue to support narrower claims.** The Cox comparison passes the generating variance into KCOR (`test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py`, lines 209–235 and 279–285). The multiwave recovery example uses noiseless hazards and the true initial rate, and the simulation grid uses a different fitter. Keep these as algebraic or historical benchmarks. Fit the actual estimator blind to truth on stochastic cohorts and compare each method against its own population target. Real marginal differences in hazards or restricted mean survival time can arise when frailty mixtures differ even when the individual intervention effect is identically zero. A calibrated proxy for shared-frailty Cox does not constitute a fitted competitor. The illustrative-figure selector filters final KCOR to [0.85, 1.15] and favors improved curvature. Disclose the selection in the caption or use a prespecified example.

   The contamination experiment does not establish a sign-reversal threshold for Czech mortality. In the archived comorbidity sweep, the **zero** contamination runs average 1.034, with 75% above one. At the claimed 66%-of-baseline threshold the mean is 1.062, with 65% above one. Crossing a null of one has no original effect sign to reverse. The contaminating mortality is in observed deaths while the simulated frailty depletion path accumulates only baseline exposure. Define the target, compare paired contaminated and uncontaminated runs, vary timing, persistence, sign and cohort, and report bias and rejection rates against known truth. An empirical bound additionally needs an independent estimate of plausible contamination.

The most useful revision would report, for each empirical contrast, observed age-standardized mortality, the unanchored and anchored core estimates, and their sensitivity to the quiet-period slope, initial fit and optional wave correction. Put identification status and the matching interval procedure in the same row. This would let a reader see exactly which conclusions follow from observed counts and which depend on the assumptions. I reviewed the supplied repository, manuscript/PDF, cumulative diff, prior objections and archived numerical outputs. I checked coverage tallies from the saved replicates and did not independently rerun the Czech registry or the full simulation studies.
