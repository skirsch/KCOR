# Review of revised KCOR

**Major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is valid under its model, and the revised exact weekly reconstruction fixes a real error. I reproduced all six revised Table 8 point estimates from the supplied input workbook. The important remaining issues are what the fitted curvature identifies, which estimator the paper validates, and whether estimates with failed identification diagnostics can be reported as normalized contrasts. The new bootstrap and post-wave calculations improve the analysis but also make those issues directly testable.

## 1. The revised empirical control fails its own reporting rule

I checked the workbook’s recorded input hash, both enrollment risk-set histories and the joint-fit calculation. The bootstrap reconstructs disjoint cells for death week or survival, samples people from these cells, and then restores overlapping pseudo-strata. That is an appropriate person-level resample for this fixed-cohort representation. The unique enrollment populations were 2,511,927 and 2,450,449.

| Enrollment | Joint fit, 1 vs 0 | Joint fit, 2 vs 0 | Joint fit, 2 vs 1 | Original component fits flagged | Bootstrap draws with an insufficient-signal fit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021_24 | 1.0091 | 1.0317 | 1.0225 | 0 of 9 | 512 of 999 |
| 2022_06 | 1.0503 | 1.0575 | 1.0068 | 4 of 9 | 998 of 999 |

The four 2022 fits were pseudo-year/dose cells (1940,1), (1940,2), (1950,0) and (1960,1). Two hit the upper frailty bound, and two had first-pass nonpositive offsets. All four received \(\theta=0\) and numerical success. Every reported 2022 pair includes an affected cell. The intervals include the fallback draws. They describe an algorithm that sometimes substitutes unadjusted hazards, whereas §2.9 says inference is conditional on diagnostic acceptance and SI §S2 says failed analyses are withheld. An interval such as [0.233, 1.299] containing one establishes little about proximity to one. These age-shift comparisons have different actual baseline risks, so even perfect reproduction supplies no known unanchored null.

**Fix.** Define whether the target is a hybrid algorithm with a declared fallback or a model-based contrast requiring all contributing fits to pass. Publish pass status, raw fit, applied value and contribution weight with each estimate. Apply the rule to originals and define the bootstrap target explicitly. Simply deleting 998 draws would leave inadequate information. Report the 2022 exercise as an identification failure under the stated gate. Use an equivalence margin for claims of near-null behavior. See **analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py**, functions **read_population** and **estimate**, **table8_bootstrap.json**, paper §3.1.2 and Table 8.

## 2. The confidence intervals have a documented calibration failure

The new person-bootstrap evidence is more informative than the older grid. In the million-person unequal-frailty, no-wave scenario, nominal 95% production-fitter intervals covered 502/600 times, or **83.7%** (Monte Carlo Wilson interval 80.5%–86.4%). All 600 originals passed. Smaller unequal-frailty and equal-frailty scenarios covered 96.2% and 97.7%, respectively. The problem is scenario-specific and can occur with ample data under the working model. Increasing the number of bootstrap draws does not remove estimator bias.

The PDF and §2.9.1 quote 189/200 for the joint fit. Counting the supplied **validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json** records gives **194/200 joint and 160/200 fixed** on the same 200 datasets. Mean widths were 0.149 and 0.048, respectively, and joint-fit point RMSE was 0.0375 versus 0.0136 for fixed fitting. If 189/200 describes another experiment, identify and supply its generating code and results. The matched evidence already available allows a direct comparison. It shows a coverage–precision tradeoff, not general superiority of either fit. The supplied 600-dataset design uses one quiet window and no nonproportional wave, so it does not calibrate Table 8.

**Fix.** Publish one reproducible simulation entry point per reported study, with its revision, generating truths, estimator, window configuration and interval rule. Report conditional and unconditional coverage, failures, widths, bias and Monte Carlo intervals by scenario. Test the actual multi-window, anchored, pooled analysis with wave adjustment on and off. Retire the old calibration table as validation evidence. Its four deterministic gamma scenarios repeat a single outer point estimate 500 times and designate the fitted reference draw as truth. Its Poisson resampling is distinct from the corrected person bootstrap. See §2.9.1, **validation/person_bootstrap/{design.json,summary.json,paired.json}**, and **test/sim_grid/code/{generate_sim_grid.py,compute_bootstrap_coverage.py}**.

## 3. There are still multiple estimators behind one name

Section 2.5 seeds \(k,\theta\) jointly in the first quiet window. The ordinary production fitter fixes \(k\) to mean detrended mortality in the early post-skip bins. The Table 8 adapter implements joint fitting by replacing an exact line of the production function’s source at runtime. Both variants refit over quiet **plus anchor** bins, while the published objective uses quiet bins only. For the same 2022 data and forced exponent, the fixed-\(k\) points are 1.0065, 1.0115 and 1.0049, all closer to one than the joint-fit points. Closeness to one cannot decide which fit estimates the scientific target.

The corrected \(\operatorname{expm1}\) increment in Step 2 is exact for integrated weekly hazards. The gap alignment still reads the cumulative hazard at the **start** of the last gap bin, then uses that offset in the next quiet interval. A shock wholly in the last gap bin is omitted from its offset. Also, the fitting objective compares weekly integrated hazards to pointwise Gompertz–gamma rates. Exact reconstruction downstream does not make that grouped fitting equation exact.

**Fix.** Expose the seed strategy in a single versioned production function. Specify the exact bins used at every step. Fit integrated interval predictions, preferably using binomial death counts and start-of-bin survivors. Compute offsets at the boundary after the last gap bin. Add a known-answer test placing the entire shock in that bin and a test in which an anchor lies outside every quiet window. See **code/KCOR.py** lines 1225–1243, 1336–1395, 1450–1477 and 1534–1549, and **analysis/table8/compare.py**.

## 4. Identification is conditional on the baseline restriction

Section 2.4.4 now correctly distinguishes multiplication of the observed hazard by \(c\), which transforms \((k,\theta)\) into \((ck,\theta/c)\), from multiplication of the individual baseline, which leaves true frailty variance unchanged. The abstract, §4.1 and SI assumption A3 still assert invariance to common multiplicative scaling. A common scale can cancel from a cohort ratio even while changing both variance estimates. The targets must be distinguished throughout.

The proposition also equates nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature with \(\theta>0\). In a quiet Gompertz–gamma interval, \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\). Thus \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) under the exact model with fixed known \(\gamma\). At \(\theta=0\) the cumulative hazard is curved for \(\gamma>0\). At \(\theta k=\gamma\) it is exactly linear despite positive frailty. Sensitivity to the assumed slope is particularly important in low-rate strata. Plot profile uncertainty in \(\theta\), give the promised numerical slope sensitivity, and check residuals against the fitted Gompertz cumulative baseline or its time transform.

Even a perfect fit cannot identify the biological source of the shape. For any observed cumulative hazard \(H\), every \(q>0\) and individual baseline \(B_q=\operatorname{expm1}(qH)/q\) produce the same survival under gamma frailty of variance \(q\). So does a homogeneous cohort with baseline \(H\). The revised §§2.6.2 and 5.2 correctly acknowledge undetectable departures. Remove the remaining claims of guaranteed visible failure in SI §S2, and claims that treatment-induced frailty or crossover necessarily attenuate the contrast in §§5–5.2. Report a range under plausible alternative baseline trajectories and make independent evidence for quiet windows part of the interpretation. The Czech YAML comment cites the observed harm estimate as a reason to choose 2023. It cannot document an outcome-blind choice.

## 5. Post-wave survival is corrected conditional on an unvalidated wave attribution

The new post-wave equation is valid given known exit exposures. Under individual hazard \(b(t)Z+a(t)Z^\alpha\), define \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-BZ-WZ^\alpha)]\). For fixed \(B_e,W_e\), solving \(L(B_e+u,W_e)/L(B_e,W_e)\) against observed relative survival correctly propagates selection after wave exit. The explicit identity branch at \(\alpha=1\) is also correct.

The in-wave calculation that supplies \(B_e,W_e\) still lacks that derivation. Before an external wave, the survivor gamma law has shape \(1/\theta\), scale \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\), and mean \(1/(1+\theta B)\). The implementation instead feeds a decreasing parameter into a new *mean-one* gamma law. At \(\theta=1,B=1,\alpha=2\), the true survivor moment \(E[Z^2\mid T>t]\) is 0.5. The implemented factor is 1.5. Even the factor relative to proportional depletion, \(E[Z^2\mid T>t]/E[Z\mid T>t]\), is 1 in this example. Once the wave acts, a \(Z^\alpha\) hazard generally tilts survivors out of the gamma family. Dividing all accumulated excess by the current factor also fails to account for its earlier values. Anchoring the no-wave path to the **end** of the first wave bin assigns that bin’s entire excess to baseline. The new post-wave solver can accurately continue from the wrong exit state.

**Fix.** With an independently justified baseline and fixed \(\alpha\), use the same joint survival function to infer interval wave increments while the wave is active. Test recovery of baseline, wave exposure, exit survival and the retained all-cause contrast against independently generated individual event histories. Where attribution or the external scale is weak, report core KCOR and label forced-exponent runs as sensitivity analyses. The Czech configuration forces 1.18 although §3.4 reports non-identification. In my identity-module replay the joint-fit core points were 1.0123, 1.0428, 1.0301 for 2021 and 1.0517, 1.0589, 1.0069 for 2022. This shows the effect of that switch on these controls; it does not validate the module. See §§2.7.1–2.7.3, **code/KCOR.py** lines 1028–1048, 2486–2536 and 2650–2720, and **data/Czech/Czech.yaml** lines 14–32.

## 6. Match the remaining evidence to the claim it tests

- **Cox comparison.** The revision correctly defines the null as a treatment HR of one at fixed frailty. Section 2.11.1 still says KCOR estimated both variances without simulator knowledge. **test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py** lines 209–235 passes their true values directly to the inversion. Label this an oracle check and provide a repeated fitted-variance comparison. Different marginal HRs or RMST values can be correct summaries of different survival curves. The shared-frailty reference curve is an acknowledged proxy.
- **Contamination.** Section 3.3.2 studies one cohort, specified positive perturbations and 20 repeats per cell. Even zero contamination in the comorbidity branch gives mean 1.034 and 75% of estimates above one. At the claimed threshold it gives 1.062 and 65%. A null has no sign to reverse. Extra deaths from a cohort-specific hazard change the all-cause descriptive target even if the treatment effect remains null. Use paired uncontaminated runs, distinguish the targets, report uncertainty and examine both signs, different timings and differential trends. This grid cannot bound Czech confounding. See **test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv**.
- **Anchoring and presentation.** Section 2.1.1 uses one anchor ratio, §2.8 describes a mean, and **code/KCOR.py** lines 4421–4430 uses a single index with fallback to 1. Anchoring cancels any constant level difference, including a genuine one. Display the unanchored model ratio, anchored ratio, observed absolute risks and age-standardized results. State the post-skip survivor population. In the empirical illustration the script screens for near-null final KCOR and favors reduced curvature. Disclose that selection when calling the illustration representative. See **code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py** lines 101–145.

I would publish KCOR as a descriptive sensitivity analysis conditional on explicit frailty and baseline assumptions. The revisions support the exact gamma inversion, the revised person-resampling construction, the reproducibility of the six control points, and the post-wave conditional survival identity. They do not yet support general depletion attribution, reliable diagnostic screening, or nominal intervals for the complete estimator. I checked the combined PDF, source text, supplied diff, implementation, workbook point estimates and archived replicate results. I did not rerun the full bootstrap ensembles or the national registry analysis.
