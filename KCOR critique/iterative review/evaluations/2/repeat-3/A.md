# KCOR methods review

**Major revision.** The gamma-frailty identity and its cumulative inverse in §2.4.2 are correct for a specified frailty variance and proportional individual hazards. The weekly recursion in §2.5 Step 2 is the exact difference of that inverse. These are defensible foundations for a descriptive statistic. Three matters change what a reader can conclude from this edition: the wave correction uses the wrong survivor distribution, the advertised fitter differs from the ordinary production fitter, and several central validation results were produced by other methods.

## 1. Correct the in-wave calculation

Under the paper’s individual wave model, accumulated baseline exposure $B$ and wave exposure $W$ give survivor weights proportional to $\exp(-zB-z^\alpha W)$. Even **before** a wave, survivors have gamma shape $1/\theta$ and scale $\theta/(1+\theta B)$. With $\theta=1$, $B=1$ and $\alpha=2$, their second moment is $2/2^2=0.5$. At these inputs, `propagate_theta` returns 0.5 and `gamma_moment_alpha` treats that number as the variance of a new **mean-one** gamma law, which returns 1.5. The distinction affects the localized exponent as well as the correction. Once $W>0$ and $\alpha\ne1$, the survivor distribution generally ceases to be gamma. See §2.7.1–3, SI §S2.1 and `code/KCOR.py` lines 1028–1048 and 2653–2655.

Further, §2.7.3 divides a **cumulative** excess after ordinary gamma inversion by the current **instantaneous** moment. At wave entry the derivative of that inverse with respect to a small $W$ is $(1+\theta B)E[Z^\alpha\mid B]$. In the example it equals 1, so division by the implemented 1.5 recovers only two-thirds of the true small wave exposure. Anchoring at the end of the first in-wave bin also forces its excess to zero. A cumulative maximum can enforce ordering without recovering the missed exposure.

The post-wave conditional-survival equation in §2.7.3 is sound **given the exit exposures**. Use the same joint survival function $L(B,W)$ through the wave. With the no-wave baseline path and exponent fixed externally or adequately constrained, solve $L(B(t),W(t))=S_{\rm obs}(t)$ on week boundaries, starting before wave entry. Derive the moments from that tilt, propagate the resulting exit state and model infeasible or negative excess explicitly. Where baseline and wave exposures cannot be separated, report that identification limit. Test simulated trajectories with known exposures, substantial depletion, a one-bin wave, $\alpha=1$ and $\alpha\ne1$. The existing `test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py` asserts equality to the old uncorrected increments after exit, which is generally incompatible with the new conditional equation.

## 2. Make the advertised estimator reproducible and consistent in weekly time

Section 2.5 jointly estimates $k,\theta$ in the first quiet window. The usual `code/KCOR.py` entry point estimates $k$ from the mean of early scaled hazards, then holds it fixed for the initial and pooled fits. The pooled mask includes early anchor weeks outside the quiet windows when those differ. Table 8 obtains its joint fit by substituting code into the production function in `analysis/table8/compare.py`. These choices change estimates and uncertainty. Expose the chosen primary fit as a single supported option used by the workbook, bootstrap and simulation entry points. Give the fixed-anchor alternative its own name and results.

Both seed and pooled objectives compare **weekly integrated observations** to **instantaneous model hazards**. Fit the exact weekly model increment, $\{\log[1+\theta B(t+1)]-\log[1+\theta B(t)]\}/\theta$, with its zero-variance limit and the specified offset included at the proper boundary. A binomial count likelihood can also use the implied weekly event probability directly. There is an additional indexing error: the last gap bin has index $g$, but the code uses the cumulative exposure at the **start** of $g$ to calculate the offset applied at $g+1$. A wave confined to the final gap bin is consequently omitted from that offset. Compute both exposures at the first subsequent quiet boundary. Test that case against exact survival. See `code/KCOR.py` lines 1226–1243, 1321–1356, 1377–1392, 1449–1473 and 1534–1549.

## 3. Enforce the stated reporting rule and explain the intervals

Section 3.4.4 concludes that the Czech exponent is unidentified. Section 2.7 says the module is then inactive. Table 8 nevertheless uses $\alpha=1.18$ and presents the resulting intervals under negative-control evidence. The archive records **four insufficient-signal fits in the original 2022 enrollment**, and at least one such fit in **998 of 999 draws**. The 2021 count is 512 of 999 draws. `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` pools these draws, including the zero-frailty fallback, then takes their percentiles. This is a distribution for an algorithm with fallbacks. Calling it conditional on passing the same identifiability gate in §2.9 is misleading. All six intervals containing one is particularly weak evidence here: one interval spans 0.233 to 1.299. The pseudo-dose age contrasts can also have genuine differences in age-specific time trends despite the absence of an intervention contrast.

Run the primary control with NPH off, report original and replicate diagnostic outcomes, and apply the predeclared gate to the original contrast. Show the fixed-exponent and fallback analyses separately as sensitivity results. Specify the target of an interval when a replicate fails and evaluate that complete rule in simulations. Report how many samples produce a usable estimate as well as their coverage. Specify the pooling formula and the actual unit of fitting: §2.9.1 says parameters are never fitted by stratum, whereas the Table 8 script fits each stratum and pseudo-dose before combining anchored ratios. The script’s use of disjoint person categories to reconstruct overlapping groups within each draw is worth preserving. Sources include `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json`, `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` lines 29–106 and 157–173, and SI §S6.1.2.

## 4. Repair the validation comparisons

- **Cox comparison.** Section 2.11.1 explicitly says the investigators estimated frailty from the simulated data under identical information constraints. `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py` lines 207–235 and 278–285 pass the generating $\theta$ directly to the inverse. The KCOR plotting function in `plot_cox_bias_sim.py` plots points and a line, although the caption claims bootstrap bands. Retain an explicitly labeled known-parameter benchmark and rerun the stated fitted procedure with interval estimates. Different frailty distributions produce genuinely different marginal survival and RMST even at conditional treatment HR 1. Compare each estimator with its own population target before labeling deviations bias.
- **Empirical illustrations.** The §2.1 Czech workflow figure reads monthly slope-adjusted data. `code/kcor_mortality.py` lines 355–375 multiply hazards by $\exp(-\text{slope}\,t)$. `code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py` selects final ratios between 0.85 and 1.15 and preferentially selects reduced curvature. SI §S5.4’s revised-estimator window scan actually fits the constant-baseline curve $\log(1+\theta kt)/\theta$. Its script filters to passing records *before* computing the pass rate. For 1940s pseudo/dose group 1 the underlying scan has 3 passes in 13 windows, while the summary gives 1.0. Regenerate both figures from the primary weekly fitter, disclose the illustration selection, and calculate pass rates with all attempted windows in the denominator. See `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py` lines 77–114 and 243–275 and its two output CSVs.
- **Coverage.** The paired experiment already archived in `validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json` gives 160/200 covered with the fixed fit and 194/200 with the joint fit on the same datasets, with mean interval widths 0.048 and 0.149. All 200 originals were accepted for both. This is a more informative comparison than the two unpaired runs reported in §2.9.1, although it tests one design. Publish its design, generating script and precision as well as the accuracy–width tradeoff. In the legacy pilot, `test/sim_grid/code/generate_sim_grid.py` constructs deterministic fractional deaths for the gamma scenarios and `compute_bootstrap_coverage.py` defines truth by applying the estimator to a reference draw. Changing the outer seed does not produce independent deaths in those scenarios. Four archived rows report 100% coverage. Increasing bootstrap draws cannot fix that experiment. Generate independent person histories or equivalent multinomial death-time counts per outer dataset and calculate truth from the population survival model.

## 5. Narrow the identification and interpretation claims

With known Gompertz slope $\gamma$, the exact continuous marginal hazard obeys $h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2$. This provides a short proof and a useful counterexample to §2.4.4’s asserted equivalence between nonzero curvature and positive variance. At $\theta=0$ Gompertz growth itself curves the cumulative hazard. At $\theta=\gamma/k>0$ the marginal hazard is constant $k$ and the cumulative hazard is straight. Replace the curvature criterion with the model identity, then quantify sampling information and sensitivity to the imposed slope. Under the fitted baseline, check linearity against Gompertz cumulative time $(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma$ or check model residuals. Straightness in ordinary time is a different hypothesis.

For any observed $H$ and candidate variance $q\ge0$, the positive baseline cumulative hazard $(e^{qH}-1)/q$ reproduces the same observed survival, with the continuous limit $H$ at zero. Thus an alternative time-varying baseline can give perfect diagnostics and a different frailty-adjusted contrast. Also, scaling **observed** hazards by $c$ changes fitted variance to $\theta/c$ under the model, as §2.4.4 correctly states. A common scaling can cancel in a ratio while changing both variance estimates. Revise the abstract and SI claims of invariant identification and transparent detection of assumption failure accordingly. Give observed absolute risks and the unanchored contrast alongside any anchored curve: a constant baseline ratio $r$ yields anchored KCOR exactly 1 for every $r$, including one arising from a persistent intervention effect. The skip makes the calculation conditional on reaching the first accumulating week.

I would retain the core inversion and the genuinely conditional post-wave result. The evidence for a usable empirical method should come from one documented fitter, known population targets, and reporting rules that operate the same way in the analysis and in its validation. This review checks the supplied equations, source code and archived outputs. I did not independently regenerate the full registry or bootstrap runs.
