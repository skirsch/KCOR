# Targeted follow-up on the revised edition

**The illustration-selection and contamination-threshold objections both still apply.** The open review addresses both in its final section. The revised paper corrected other claims about quiet-window contamination, but those edits do not resolve the empirical threshold claim.

- **Illustration selection.** Paper line 139 calls the cohort “representative” and “deterministically selected.” The script requires final KCOR between 0.85 and 1.15 and maximum KCOR at most 1.5. When any eligible cohort shows improved curvature, it selects among those with positive improvement. Deterministic selection can still depend on the outcome shown. Describe these filters in the caption, select using prespecified cohort characteristics, or show the distribution for all candidates. Separately, the Czech configuration still comments that 2023 showed the least vaccine harm. See `code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py` lines 101–142 and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml` lines 22–32.
- **Contamination threshold.** Paper lines 851–857 still describe 0.0003/week (66% of reference mortality) as a sign-reversal threshold and an empirical bound relevant to Czech cohorts. The comorbidity sweep gives mean KCOR **1.034, 0.989, 1.062** at amplitudes **0, 0.0001, 0.0003** respectively. At zero amplitude, 15/20 replicates already exceed one; in the independent dynamic-control arm 14/20 do. The “first positive amplitude” comparison omits the zero-amplitude result. The simulated hazard addition also creates actual deaths and begins at the anchor. The revised caveats at paper lines 473 and 1042 correctly acknowledge undetectable contamination, but do not convert this grid into a bound. Report paired changes from uncontaminated data and a prespecified material deviation with Monte Carlo uncertainty. See `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv` rows 2 and 7–9 and `run_contamination_test.py` lines 163–175, 210–215 and 257–279.

**Specific details abbreviated in the open review**

| Baseline concern | Does it still apply? |
|---|---|
| Sensitivity to the fixed Gompertz slope | **Yes.** At fixed observed hazard and slope, the local implied change in θ is δγ/h. The same γ error need not cancel between cohorts with different h. Paper line 334 still asserts first-order cancellation. The correction to observed-hazard rescaling at line 346 addresses a different issue. |
| Precise anchor and age standardization | **Yes.** Paper lines 164–169 use a point anchor; line 624 specifies a window mean. `code/KCOR.py` lines 4423–4425 use one indexed ratio and substitute 1 if invalid. Table 8 does specify data-dependent weights and pools log ratios (`analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` lines 74–97). Its target should be distinguished from ordinary direct age standardization. Supplement §S6.1.2 still shows the all-ages raw ratio 0.316 versus 1.28–2.13 within age groups. |
| Positive-control plotting fallback | **Yes.** On any exception while reading the processed results, the plotting script can form the ratio directly from raw cumulative hazards. A figure produced that way does not test frailty fitting. The existence of this fallback does not establish which branch produced the published figure. See `test/positive_control/code/plot_positive_control.py` lines 81, 130–137 and 269–271. |
| Workbook intervals and old pilot numbers | **Yes for those outputs; the new Table 8 uses a different procedure.** Ordinary workbook bands use raw event-count variance and clip the upper endpoint at ten times KCOR (`code/KCOR.py` lines 4452–4474). The Poisson pilot's archived 500 × 200 run still reports 100% KCOR coverage in four scenarios, 25.4% in one and gamma-null θ coverage of 35.4% and 18.8%. The final pilot table remains blank. Those results cannot calibrate the person bootstrap. See `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage{,_theta}.csv` and paper lines 1267–1275. |
| Dependence across overlapping enrollments | **Conditional.** The new Table 8 multinomial construction correctly preserves overlap within each enrollment. Its independently seeded draws for each enrollment support separate marginal intervals. A joint cross-enrollment contrast would still require linked resampling of shared individuals (`analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` lines 27–40 and 65–71). |
| Negative-control provenance | **Partly resolved.** Table 8 separates historical v4.3 values from reproducible joint-fit values. Its source citation still points to `test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log`, whose header says v6.0. The sibling `KCOR_summary_neg_control.log` identifies v4.3. Cite the appropriate historical artifact and the new Table 8 JSON separately. |
| Uncertainty in external efficacy and wave exponent | **Yes for inference beyond the fixed sensitivity scenario.** Section 2.9.1, line 651, explicitly holds the external scale and α fixed. Its interval omits their uncertainty. Fixed-input variability is appropriate when labeled conditional. Report a sensitivity range or propagate external uncertainty for broader claims. |

The remaining baseline objections have explicit dispositions in the open review. Exact weekly reconstruction is repaired. The post-wave survival equation is repaired conditional on exit exposures, while in-wave attribution remains inconsistent. Model nonuniqueness, ordinary versus joint fitting, fallback gates, Cox's use of generating theta, the quiet-window scan denominator and the interpretation of crossover remain as documented there. The baseline objection that the manuscript describes a time-block bootstrap is superseded by revised §2.9.1 and the person-level Table 8 resampling.
