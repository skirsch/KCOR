# Targeted follow-up on the baseline objections

1. **Identification and quiet-window treatment overlap — partly resolved.** The specific objection to the earlier wording in §§2.6.2 and 5.2 has been answered locally. Paper lines 473 and 1042 explicitly allow undetected contamination and say the diagnostics cannot establish the direction of bias when a treatment effect resembles depletion. Line 346 correctly distinguishes observed-hazard scaling from individual-baseline scaling. The broader objections still apply. The abstract retains the invariance claim, line 355 equates nonzero curvature with positive frailty, and lines 1007, 1037 and 1048 retain claims of conservativeness or failure signaling. Health-dependent later uptake supplies no general attenuation guarantee. Carry the revised qualifications through those passages.

2. **Fit specification, reporting gates and linearity — partly resolved.** Both weekly reconstruction loops now use the exact increment. The distinct objection to using post-inversion *linearity* as a diagnostic still applies. Paper line 458 and algorithm-table line 1212 still expect linearity in ordinary time, although the fitted Gompertz cumulative baseline is curved for a nonzero slope. Assess residuals against the fitted Gompertz cumulative path. The joint-seed Table 8 replay does not change the ordinary fixed-seed entry point, and its percentiles include flagged fits. The point-anchor versus window-mean mismatch, interpretation of the post-skip population, and pooling-order concerns remain as detailed in the open review. In particular, correcting the recursion does not remove early mortality or shock contamination from the early-bin estimate of the baseline scale.

3. **Cox and positive controls — still apply.** Some comparator language now distinguishes the conditional treatment target, but §2.11.1 line 710 still says KCOR estimated frailty without simulator-only knowledge. The generator supplies both true variances and the KCOR figure still claims an uncomputed band. The shared-frailty line remains a rescaled reference. The positive-control simulator still multiplies a marginal hazard and uses fractional expected deaths. Removing the uncertainty claim from the supplement's positive-control caption addressed that caption alone. The raw-ratio plotting fallback remains.

4. **Uncertainty — partly resolved.** §2.9.1 and the revised Table 8 analysis now use a person bootstrap. The original contiguous-block description is superseded. Ordinary workbook intervals still use event-count log-Wald bounds and cap the upper endpoint at ten times KCOR (`code/KCOR.py` lines 4440–4476). The historical single-window cumulative fitter, Poisson resampling and reference-draw target still underlie the blank pilot table. Its existing 500 × 200 CSV reports 100% in four scenarios and 25.4% under non-gamma frailty. Its gamma-null target and median both equal 1.00944, while the companion variance-coverage CSV reports 35.4% and 18.8%. These are separate from the new person-bootstrap studies, whose production-fitter large-cohort coverage was 502/600 with every original accepted.

5. **Empirical diagnostics — still apply, with new Table 8 evidence.**
   - **Composition and gates.** The table now separates the historical rows, including two intervals excluding one, from six joint-fit estimates with resampled intervals, all including one. The latter do not demonstrate equivalence or a known age-shift null. The cited v6 log uses one quiet window. The separate constant-baseline window scanner still filters out failed windows before computing pass rates. Supplement §S6.1.2 still prints an all-“Yes” gate table. The scanner's denominators and the age-specific numerical metrics supporting that separate table both need reporting.
   - **Outcome-conditioned illustration.** The open review answered this objection and it still applies. Paper lines 137–139 label the figure illustrative and representative. The selection rule conditions on final KCOR in [0.85, 1.15], peak KCOR ≤1.5, and preferentially retains comparisons whose curvature gap fell. Within that set it ranks by sample size, curvature improvement and closeness to one (`code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py` lines 101–142). The figure's JSON records a selected endpoint of 0.895. Disclose the filters in the caption and avoid claiming representativeness from this selection. Separately, `data/Czech/Czech.yaml` records that 2023 was selected because it showed the least apparent vaccine harm.
   - **Contamination threshold.** The open review answered this objection and it still applies. Paper lines 845–857 retain the claimed Czech bound and approximately 66% sign-reversal threshold. At zero injection, 15/20 comorbidity and 14/20 dynamic runs already ended above one, with means 1.034 and 1.063. The generator changes cohort A's death probability starting at rebased week 4, the very week used to anchor the ratio, and seeds depend on the amplitude. The outcome mixes actual changes in mortality, a noisy anchor, and fitted-frailty error (`test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py` lines 136–172, 210–215 and 350–355). Use paired uncontaminated controls, known-parameter comparisons, an externally meaningful effect size, and varied onset and anchoring. These simulations cannot bound contamination in the registry.

6. **Optional wave adjustment — partly resolved.** The post-exit conditional-survival equation and proportional identity are correct conditional on the supplied exit exposures. The in-wave survivor-moment and cumulative-rescaling objections still apply and undermine those exposures. Section 2.9.1 line 651 explicitly holds external efficacy and the exponent fixed in each bootstrap draw, so those intervals omit uncertainty in these inputs. Disclose that conditioning and propagate external uncertainty when making a broader inference. The Czech log confirms an active forced exponent of 1.18, and the Table 8 replay requires it. The paper judges the exponent unidentified. A core-only primary result with that forced setting reported separately remains warranted.
