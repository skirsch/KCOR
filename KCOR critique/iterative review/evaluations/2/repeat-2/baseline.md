# KCOR methods review

**Major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically correct for a known frailty distribution and variance. Fixed enrollment cohorts and transparent weekly risk sets are also worth retaining. The central problem is the jump from “this assumed model can reproduce these mortality curves” to “the data tell us how much depletion to remove.” Several of the reported validations bypass the fitting step, use an older estimator, or use the estimator itself to define truth. I would retain KCOR as a model-dependent descriptive sensitivity analysis. The evidence supplied does not establish the reliability of the proposed adjustment or its confidence intervals for registry applications.

## 1. State what identifies frailty, and what the data can test

Let `H` be any observed cumulative hazard. For **every** `θ ≥ 0`, the baseline `B_θ=(exp(θ H)-1)/θ`, with `B_0=H`, produces exactly that same `H` under gamma frailty. In particular, a cohort with no frailty and a changing baseline can give exactly the same data as one with a Gompertz baseline and positive frailty. Every diagnostic based solely on those data will give the same result in the two worlds. This is an objection to the claimed ability to detect or exclude wrong mechanisms, even for a descriptive method. The manuscript recognizes the ambiguity in §4.1, yet promises failure signaling or conservativeness under contamination and treatment overlap in §§2.6.2 and 5.2.

There **is** an identification result under the exact continuous-time quiet-period model. For marginal hazard `h`, `h'=γh-θ h^2`. With known `γ` and exact positive `h`, this identifies `θ=(γh-h')/h^2`. Nonzero curvature of `H` is neither necessary nor equivalent to positive frailty. With `θ=0`, a growing Gompertz baseline already produces curvature. With `k>0, θ=γ/k`, the marginal hazard is constant and `H` is linear despite positive frailty. Multiplying the **observed** hazard by `c` changes the implied variance to `θ/c`, including when both cohorts receive the same multiplier. Scaling the **individual baseline** leaves the generating frailty distribution intact and generally does not multiply the marginal hazard by `c`. A ratio can cancel a common scale while its underlying estimates of `θ` change. These distinctions invalidate the equivalence and invariance claims in the abstract and §2.4.4 (paper lines 332–359). An error `δγ` changes the local implied variance by `δγ/h`, a particular concern for low-event groups.

Give the conditional result with its assumptions. For weekly data, fit interval death probabilities with a binomial likelihood using exact differences of cumulative hazards. Profile the plausible Gompertz slopes, frailty parameters and wave offsets. Plot the resulting range of KCOR alongside observed survival, absolute mortality and the unadjusted ratio. Test predictive fit on reserved windows. Passing such tests supports compatibility with the model. It cannot establish that depletion generated the curvature.

## 2. Define and implement one estimator

The abstract and §2.5, lines 371–385, specify a **joint** seed fit of `k,θ` in the nearest quiet window. The main function fixes `k` from the first few post-skip hazards divided by the Gompertz growth factor, estimates only `θ` in the first quiet window, and includes anchor bins in the pooled fit even when they are outside the quiet windows. Those early hazards are themselves affected by selection and any early shocks. A nonpositive first-pass gap offset, insufficient events, hitting the upper bound, or a poor fit can return an applied `θ=0`, including with `success=True`. A failure in one cohort can therefore give an apparently computable ratio with only the other cohort corrected. Inspect `code/KCOR.py` lines 1226–1257, 1375–1396, 1464–1502 and 1652–1712. This differs materially from the promise to withhold unidentified contrasts in Box 2.

The reconstruction in §2.5 uses an Euler increment `h(1+θ B)`. For a weekly log-survival increment `q`, the exact baseline increment is `(1+θ B)expm1(θ q)/θ`. Applying the exact expression consistently makes the offset reconstruction agree with the final inversion. Assess quiet-window fit against the predicted **Gompertz** curve, which is curved in ordinary time even after successful inversion.

Choose the implemented estimator explicitly, propagate failure flags to plots and exports, and distinguish a well-supported small variance from an uninformative or failed fit. Retain an unadjusted fallback only under its own label. Specify the post-skip survivor population, the reporting horizon and anchor rule. Equation 2.1.1 uses a point anchor, §2.8 describes a window mean, and `code/KCOR.py` lines 4346–4362 use an indexed ratio with fallback to 1. Anchoring removes *all* constant ratios, including a genuine sustained exposure-associated difference. It consequently changes the scientific question. Report both ratios, deaths during the skip, and absolute risks. Define the age-standardization population and the order of aggregation and inversion. The pooled raw ratio 0.316 reverses the age-specific ratios 1.28–2.13 in Supplement §S6.1.2, illustrating why this matters. Later uptake also need not attenuate the result toward one when uptake is related to evolving health (§5.2).

## 3. Rerun the controls without access to simulation truth

The strongest direct discrepancy is the Cox experiment. Section 2.11.1, line 709, explicitly says KCOR estimated frailty from the same information as Cox. The generator calls `compute_kcor_from_data(df, theta_B=theta, ...)` and inverts using the **true generating variance**, while assigning the other cohort its known zero. It produces one point per grid value. The plotting function draws a line without KCOR interval data, although the caption describes 95% bootstrap bands. See `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py` lines 207–235 and 278–295 and `plot_cox_bias_sim.py::plot_kcor_vs_theta`. This tests the inversion algebra. It supplies no evidence about estimating `θ` from mortality.

With shared individual baseline `B`, the actual marginal hazard ratio for the heterogeneous cohort versus the homogeneous one is `1/(1+θ B(t))`, not one. Cox summarizes genuinely different survivor hazards, and RMST measures genuinely different observed survival. Comparing both to the unit individual-level effect does not measure bias for their own estimands. The “shared-frailty” grid curve is a rescaling of the standard Cox points, as the caption acknowledges. Fit each comparator to a prespecified target or omit that curve. Run repeated, independently sampled cohorts through the same production fit with truth masked. Compare fitted-parameter KCOR with the known-parameter benchmark and score each method against its own population target.

The positive-control generator multiplies an already-marginal mixed hazard during the effect window and uses expected fractional deaths (`test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py::simulate_cohort_with_injection`). This differs from multiplying the individual baseline as specified in Supplement §§S4.3–S4.6, because an individual-level intervention changes subsequent depletion. Generate survival from the stated individual-level model, derive its horizon-specific cumulative target, and disallow the plotting fallback that labels a raw cumulative-hazard ratio KCOR.

## 4. Rebuild the uncertainty study

Section 2.9 describes a contiguous-block bootstrap. The coverage program instead samples independent weekly Poisson counts around observed counts and reconstructs the risk sets. The ordinary workbook path constructs log-Wald bands from event counts without refitting frailty or the anchor, and clips the upper interval. The coverage study runs the older cumulative-hazard fitter on one window. It uses a KCOR estimate from one reference simulation as “DGP truth,” and four scenarios generate identical expected-count datasets across outer replicates. See `test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py` lines 76–125 and 201–225, `generate_sim_grid.py` lines 372–392 and 850–875, and `code/KCOR.py` lines 4377–4408.

The archived 500 × 200 run already exists despite the blank manuscript table. Its `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage.csv` reports 100% in four scenarios and 25.4% in the non-gamma scenario. Its gamma-null “truth” is 1.00944, exactly the median estimate. Neither result measures nominal coverage of the advertised estimator. The companion theta summary reports 35.4% and 18.8% for the two gamma-null variances. These outputs diagnose the study design and cannot simply be copied into the paper.

Generate new stochastic cohorts for every outer replicate, define truth analytically or by independent population integration, and run the release estimator. For fixed cohorts with complete follow-up, a multinomial draw of event-week counts plus the terminal survivor category resamples the observed individuals' discrete survival histories. A correctly specified sequential binomial alternative retains the fixed calendar and reconstructs the risk set. Resample by person if combining overlapping enrollment cohorts. Refit every estimated component in each draw. Report coverage, interval width, failures, and the fraction accepted, both overall and among prespecified diagnostic passes.

## 5. Correct the empirical diagnostic claims

- The Czech age-shift experiment is a useful composition check. Giving different birth cohorts artificial dose labels does not establish a true normalized mortality ratio of one. An anchored curve is exactly flat for proportional trajectories even if the raw risk levels differ substantially. The cited log is explicitly v6 with a single quiet window (`test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log`, lines 2, 21–22, 45–55). Paper lines 1232–1237 include endpoints 1.0756 and 1.0911 with intervals excluding one. Show raw and adjusted drift with uncertainty, declare an equivalence margin, and rerun the current implementation.
- The quiet-window scan called the “same revised estimator” in Supplement §S5.4 fits `log(1+θ kt)/θ`, a constant-baseline model. Its summary computes the pass rate **after filtering to passing windows**, guaranteeing 100%. For the 1940s birth decade the underlying CSV gives 8/13, 3/13 and 8/13 passes for doses 0, 1 and 2. Inspect `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py` lines 77–113 and 244–272 and its two output CSVs. Restore all denominators and publish the underlying metrics supporting the all-“Yes” gate in §S6.1.2.
- The quiet-window contamination “threshold” is not an empirical bound on Czech confounding. With **zero** contamination the comorbidity arm already has mean anchored KCOR 1.034 and 75% of estimates above one. The dynamic arm at zero has mean 1.063 and 70% above one (`test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv`, rows 2 and 7). The injection adds actual deaths to one cohort and starts at the anchor week. Comparing an estimate to one conflates that direct change, the random anchor and error in frailty estimation. Compare paired draws against zero-contamination controls and known-parameter analyses, use a meaningful deviation threshold and uncertainty, and vary onset, direction, cohorts, and anchor. Separate a null causal effect from a true difference in observed or model-scale outcomes.
- The Czech configuration comments that 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm (`data/Czech/Czech.yaml`, lines 22–32). The empirical intuition figure also screens on final KCOR and curvature improvement (`code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py`, lines 101–142). Disclose outcome-informed choices, use external quiet-window criteria on an untouched cohort, and label the selected figure as illustrative.

## 6. Keep the optional wave correction separate until it has a coherent survivor model

Even before a nonlinear wave, starting with `Z ∼ Gamma(a,a)`, `a=1/θ`, proportional survival through baseline cumulative hazard `B` gives `Z | T ≥ t ∼ Gamma(a,a+B)`, using shape and rate. Its mean is `a/(a+B)`, its variance `a/(a+B)^2`, and its `α` moment is `Γ(a+α)/[Γ(a)(a+B)^α]`. The code instead passes `θ/(1+θ B)` as the variance of a new unit-mean gamma and returns exactly one at `α=1` (`code/KCOR.py`, lines 1028–1047 and 2591–2594). This is not the stated survivor moment. If the intended quantity is normalized by the survivor mean or by an already-inverted proportional component, derive that quantity and name it.

During a wave with an individual hazard component proportional to `Z^α`, the survivor density gains a factor `exp(-A_cum Z^α)`, so the ordinary gamma update generally fails. Also, dividing accumulated excess at time `t` by today's `F(t)` is different from summing contemporaneous corrected increments; a changing `F` rescales earlier events retrospectively. Clipping negative excess and applying a cumulative maximum do not derive a correction from the model (§2.7.3). Derive and simulate the joint survival model, including external efficacy uncertainty. Until then, keep this branch as a labeled sensitivity analysis. The paper correctly reports Czech `α` as unidentified, but `data/Czech/Czech.yaml` lines 14–20 forces 1.18 and `data/Czech/KCOR_summary.log` lines 24 and 91–98 confirms an active correction. Regenerate a core-only primary output under the stated gates and identify which specification produced every table and plot.

The most informative next deliverable would be a single reproducible run that takes fresh cohort counts, estimates all parameters without access to truth, returns either a contrast or a failed gate, and recreates the headline tables with prespecified targets. That would establish what this version can recover, how often it declines to report, and how accurately it quantifies uncertainty. This review checked the supplied manuscript, supplement, source code and archived outputs. It did not regenerate the registry workbooks.
