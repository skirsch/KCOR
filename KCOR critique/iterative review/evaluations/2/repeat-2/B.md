# KCOR review of the revised edition

**Major revision.** The weekly gamma inversion is now correct, the six revised Table 8 point estimates reproduce from the supplied workbook, and the new person-bootstrap results are informative. The principal conclusion I would support is narrower than the paper's. KCOR describes how a cohort contrast changes under an assumed depletion model. The evidence does not yet establish that the amount of depletion can be recovered reliably from these registry curves, or that a nominal 95% interval for the reported fit has 95% coverage.

### 1. The revised empirical result reveals a reporting-gate failure

I checked the input workbook's blob identifier, its two enrollment sheets and its nested population counts. Using the supplied joint-fit code, I obtained all six Table 8 point estimates. For 2022 week 06 the three were 1.05034, 1.05750 and 1.00682. With the ordinary fixed-baseline-rate fitter and otherwise identical inputs, they were 1.00654, 1.01152 and 1.00495. The paper now describes this change accurately. The bootstrap reconstruction of disjoint death-week and survivor cells, followed by reconstruction of their overlapping summaries, is a sound way to resample whole individuals for each enrollment under the stated design. See §3.1.2, Table 8 and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py::read_population`.

The problem is **which fitted estimates enter those numbers**. In the 2022 joint fit, four of nine stratum fits returned insufficient signal. In the 1940 pseudo-stratum these were doses 1 and 2, in 1950 dose 0, and in 1960 dose 1. Two reached the upper bound near 100 and were assigned zero. Two had a nonpositive first-pass offset and were also assigned zero. All three pooled comparisons use a flagged component. The archive reports at least one signal flag in **998/999** 2022 draws and **512/999** 2021 draws. These are flags in otherwise numerically successful fits. Calling the intervals conditional on diagnostic acceptance is incompatible with retaining those draws. The 2022 interval 0.233–1.299 shows how little the data constrain even the direction. Containing one is weak evidence for a negative control when the interval is that wide. See `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json`, `table8_bootstrap.py::estimate`, and `code/KCOR.py::fit_theta0_gompertz`.

Specify the pooled contrast's acceptance rule before inspection, publish component flags and weights, and report the probability of producing an accepted estimate. If reporting the fallback-inclusive calculation, label it as such and interpret its interval as variability of that algorithm. Under the manuscript's stated rule to withhold unidentified normalized contrasts, the three 2022 estimates belong in a diagnostic table. For a null-performance claim, define a substantively meaningful equivalence range and show the improvement over the raw anchored trajectories. Mapping ages to artificial doses preserves a null intervention contrast but does not guarantee equal age-specific mortality trajectories.

### 2. The paper, production fitter and coverage evidence concern different procedures

The abstract and §2.5 specify a joint fit of baseline rate and variance in the first quiet window. The ordinary entry point still fixes the rate from early post-skip observations, and pools early anchor bins even when outside the specified windows. The Table 8 adapter uses source-text substitution at runtime to implement the joint fit. The standalone bootstrap program also selects an archived mode by default that its initializer rejects unless called through the replay workflow. See `code/KCOR.py` lines 1226–1242 and 1375–1396, `analysis/table8/compare.py` lines 21–37 and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` lines 129–136. The correct exact weekly inversion is used in reconstruction, but the seed and refit objectives still compare integrated weekly hazards to instantaneous hazard predictions. Use exact interval probabilities in the fit as well.

The revised person-bootstrap evidence is stronger than the old Poisson pilot. I recounted the archived replicate rows: the large unequal-frailty null achieved **502/600 (83.7%)** percentile coverage, even though all original fits passed. The quiet-contamination scenario also accepted all 600 originals. In the separate paired experiment, the archived records give **160/200** covered with the fixed rate and **194/200** with a joint fit on the same datasets; 38 were covered only by the joint procedure and four only by the fixed one. Average width rose from 0.0478 to 0.1488 and RMSE from 0.0136 to 0.0375. This is evidence that fitter choice matters and that wider intervals buy coverage in that scenario. It does not establish calibration for the Czech multiwindow procedure with an active wave correction. Section 2.9.1 also cites a joint result of **189/200**; I could verify 194/200 for the archived paired run, and could not trace 189/200 to a separately documented run in the supplied edition. See `validation/person_bootstrap/{design.json,replicates.csv,summary.json,paired.json}` and paper line 663.

Expose the two fitters as explicit options on one ordinary entry point. Supply the generators and commands for the person-bootstrap coverage runs and identify which code and settings generated each claim. Report the paired evidence, the failed large-cohort calibration, and uncertainty in coverage. Validate the chosen estimator on new stochastic replicates spanning the actual windows, bounds, gates, waves and pooling. Preserve the improved per-replicate clock and sample-aware diagnostics. Distinguish the per-enrollment record bootstrap from ordinary workbook log-Wald bands and the older Poisson pilot. That pilot still fits a different model, uses deterministic expected deaths in several scenarios, and computes “truth” by fitting one reference draw. Increasing its replication counts cannot fix that design. See §5, the final coverage table, `test/sim_grid/code/generate_sim_grid.py` lines 372–392 and 850–875, and `compute_bootstrap_coverage.py` lines 201–225.

### 3. The new post-wave equation is correct conditional on the exit exposures. The in-wave calculation can supply the wrong exposures even with exact data

Given baseline and wave exposures B and W at exit, the proposed conditional survival ratio using L(B,W) = E[exp(−ZB−Z^α W)] follows from the joint model. The ordinary inversion is also the appropriate identity case for α = 1. Those are genuine corrections to the old constant-offset continuation (§2.7.3).

I checked the remaining attribution step against an exactly specified example. Take unit-mean exponential frailty (θ = 1), α = 2, and the configured Gompertz slope 0.085/52. Start at zero, give the first in-wave increment baseline exposure 0.1 and wave exposure 0.2, and give the next, post-wave week only its Gompertz baseline increment. From exact mixture survival, the wave-exit survival is 0.73733. Given those exact observations and the correct baseline, the production function reports exit wave exposure **0.14749**. Its inferred exposures imply exit survival **0.76856**. The resulting corrected cumulative hazard is **0.24749 instead of 0.30000** at exit and **0.34166 instead of 0.40016** a week later. This deliberately visible example demonstrates a model inconsistency, not an estimate of its magnitude in Czech data. It is reproducible with `code/KCOR.py::apply_nph_correction_post_inversion`, weekly reference scale k = 0.1 exp(−0.085/52), and L(B,W) = ∫ exp[−(1+B)z−Wz²] dz over nonnegative z.

Dividing the entire already-inverted cumulative excess by the current power moment does not solve the model's survival equation. In addition, ordinary proportional selection from a starting Gamma(a,a), a = 1/θ, gives survivors Gamma(a,a+B) in shape-rate notation. The code instead substitutes θ/(1+θB) as the variance of a new mean-one gamma. Nonlinear wave selection generally takes survivors outside the gamma family entirely. Monotonicity repair cannot correct the exit exposure. See `code/KCOR.py` lines 1028–1047 and the in-wave calculation preceding `post_wave_baseline_increases`'s use.

Use a prespecified baseline during the wave and fit nonnegative cumulative wave exposures against the **same** Laplace survival used after exit, with an explicit observation model for noise. If baseline and wave exposure are both free, specify independent information or present a sensitivity range. Test recovery against exact joint-model data through entry, wave and exit. Running the existing `test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py` fails at line 44 because it asserts the old post-wave increment rule. Retain continuity as a test and replace the obsolete expected increments with independently calculated conditional-survival targets.

The Czech result for α is appropriately described as unidentified. Table 8 discloses the forced α = 1.18, but it is presented as a core validation and its helper requires that override. In an identity-wave sensitivity run I obtained 2021 joint-fit points 1.0123, 1.0428 and 1.0301, and 2022 points 1.0517, 1.0589 and 1.0069. This particular switch changes the points modestly; the 2022 signal flags persist. Report core-only primary estimates and put the forced correction alongside them. See `data/Czech/Czech.yaml` lines 14–20 and §3.4.

### 4. Correct the remaining identification and interpretation claims

The new scale statement in §2.4.4 is right: multiplying an observed hazard by c maps (k,θ) to (ck,θ/c), while multiplying the individual baseline is a different operation. Update the incompatible invariance language in the abstract and §4.1. The revised quiet-window-overlap paragraph also correctly acknowledges that contamination may escape diagnostics. The conclusion and §5 still imply much stronger failure detection.

For the exact continuous-time Gompertz–gamma model with known growth γ, the observed hazard satisfies h′ = γh − θh². This supplies a short proof of model-conditional identification from a positive hazard and its slope. The proposition's equivalence between nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature and positive frailty is false: a frailty-free Gompertz baseline curves; with positive θ = γ/k the marginal hazard is constant. Outside the parametric restriction, every θ ≥ 0 gives exactly the same observed cumulative hazard H with a different baseline B = expm1(θH)/θ. A time-varying individual baseline can perfectly mimic depletion. Good fit to the observed trajectory cannot distinguish those explanations. Use profile or sensitivity intervals across baseline growth rates and offsets, prediction in held-out periods, and diagnostics against the fitted Gompertz trajectory instead of straight-line fit in ordinary time. See §§2.4.3–2.6.2.

The skip changes the target population to survivors of the early weeks. Early anchoring removes any constant ratio, including a real sustained exposure-associated difference. Fixed enrollment avoids exposure-driven reclassification, but health-related subsequent uptake need not push the contrast toward one. State the landmark and anchor precisely, show deaths in the skipped interval, and report observed survival, absolute mortality and unanchored contrasts with a common standardization population. Different quantities are called the primary contrast in §2.1.1, §2.8 and the workbook. Section 2.9.1 says frailty is never fitted by stratum while Table 8 fits nine separate age-label/dose combinations.

### 5. Rebuild the other headline controls with the release estimator

- **Cox comparison.** Section 2.11.1 still explicitly says theta is estimated from the same information as Cox. The simulator passes the generating theta straight to KCOR and assigns the other group's known zero. It produces one KCOR point per theta and the plot draws no KCOR uncertainty bands despite its caption. With common individual baseline B, the heterogeneous versus homogeneous marginal hazard ratio is actually 1/(1+θB). A nonunit Cox summary or differing RMST can accurately describe different observed survival distributions under a null individual-level exposure effect. Label the current curve an oracle check, estimate all parameters blind to truth in repeated data, and compare each estimator with its own target. The shared-frailty curve is explicitly interpolated from a single comparison. See `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py` lines 207–235 and 278–295, and `plot_cox_bias_sim.py::plot_kcor_vs_theta`.
- **Positive and contamination controls.** The positive-control generator multiplies an already mixed marginal hazard and uses expected deaths. This changes different quantities from the individual-level intervention described in the supplement. Inject at the individual baseline, simulate stochastic histories, and score the known cumulative target. The contamination experiment's zero-injection rows already have mean anchored ratios 1.063 and 1.034, with 70% and 75% above one. The injection also changes actual mortality in one group starting at the anchor week. The first tested positive dose crossing one cannot bound Czech confounding. Compare paired uncontaminated and contaminated data, evaluate meaningful effect sizes, and distinguish direct additions of deaths from error estimating theta. See §3.3.2, `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv`, and `test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py`.
- **Window diagnostics.** The Czech robustness scan still fits a constant-baseline model. Its summary filters to passing rows before computing its pass rate. For the 1940s decade, actual counts are 8/13, 3/13 and 8/13 for doses 0, 1 and 2 rather than the summary's three 100% values. The Czech YAML describes choosing 2023 based on observed vaccine harm, and the intuition-figure code restricts eligibility by final KCOR and curvature improvement. Disclose those selections, rebuild the diagnostics with the released estimator, and validate the stated rules in independently selected calendar periods. See Supplement §§S5.4 and S6.1.2, `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py` lines 77–113 and 244–272, `data/Czech/Czech.yaml` lines 22–32 and `code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py` lines 101–142.
