# KCOR methods review

**Major revision.** KCOR can describe how two fixed cohorts' mortality trajectories compare after a specified gamma-frailty transformation. The exact weekly inversion, the person bootstrap, the paired fitter comparison, and the conditional post-wave survival equation are substantive improvements. The evidence still falls short of showing that the fitted transformation recovers depletion in registry data or that the reported intervals are reliable when the diagnostics pass. The strongest evidence for that last concern now comes from the authors' own within-model experiment. Of 600 large-cohort datasets that passed the study diagnostics, the nominal 95% percentile interval covered the known target in 502 (83.7%).

## 1. State exactly what the model identifies

For any observed cumulative hazard $H$ and any $\theta\ge0$, the baseline $B_\theta=(\exp(\theta H)-1)/\theta$, with $B_0=H$, gives the same marginal survival under the gamma model. A homogeneous cohort with a changing baseline can therefore produce exactly the data attributed to frailty depletion with a Gompertz baseline. No diagnostic on those same observations can distinguish these two explanations. This matters even for a descriptive paper when it claims that violations reveal themselves through poor fit, instability or post-normalization drift (§§2.4.1, 5.1–5.2).

There is a simple positive result. Under the exact quiet-period model with known Gompertz slope $\gamma$, the marginal hazard obeys $h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2$. Thus $\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2$ wherever $h>0$. Nonzero curvature of $H$ is not equivalent to positive frailty. When $\theta=0$, Gompertz growth itself curves $H$. When $k>0$ and $\theta=\gamma/k>0$, $h=k$ is constant and $H$ is linear. The new sentence at paper line 346 correctly distinguishes multiplying the observed hazard by $c$, which gives $\theta/c$, from scaling the individual baseline, which preserves the frailty variance. The abstract still asserts invariance, and the proposition at line 355 retains the false equivalence. A common multiplier can cancel in a contrast while changing both fitted variances.

Replace the proposition and abstract claim with the conditional result. Show sensitivity to the assumed slope: a change $\delta\gamma$ changes the locally implied variance by $\delta\gamma/h$. For weekly deaths $D_j$ among $Y_j$ survivors, fit interval risks $1-\exp[-(H_{j+1}-H_j)]$ with a binomial likelihood using the same Gompertz integrals in fitting and prediction. Profile the slope, frailty and gap offsets. Compare predicted and observed mortality in held-out windows. This tests predictive compatibility under the restrictions actually used.

## 2. Make the fitted algorithm and its reporting rule agree

The exact weekly update $(1+\theta B)\operatorname{expm1}(\theta q)/\theta$, for observed log-survival increment $q$, correctly replaces Euler accumulation in both reconstruction loops. A separate discrepancy survives. The ordinary fitter fixes $k$ from early post-skip bins and fits only $\theta$ in the first quiet window. Its pooled objective includes anchor bins outside the quiet windows when these differ. The abstract, §2.5 and the algorithm table specify a joint first-window fit and a quiet-only pooled objective. The Table 8 replay obtains a joint seed by rewriting the production function's source at runtime. It otherwise inherits that function's masks, fallbacks and pooled refit. One result cannot establish operating characteristics for all three specifications. See `code/KCOR.py` lines 1226–1238 and 1375–1396 and `analysis/table8/compare.py`.

Use an explicit, versioned fitter shared by ordinary workbooks, bootstrap runs, controls and plots. Expose the two seed choices as named specifications if both are of interest. Match the integrated weekly observation to its integrated prediction throughout. A failed optimizer, inadequate event count, a nonpositive first gap or a bound hit needs a distinct recorded outcome. Present a zero-frailty estimate supported by the data separately from a zero substituted after a gate fails. Propagate both cohorts' statuses through each ratio, bootstrap replicate and figure. Numerical convergence alone does not establish either cohort's identifiability.

Specify the reporting population, time origin, anchor and pooling estimand. Equation 2.1.1 uses a point anchor, §2.8 describes a window mean, and the ordinary workbook uses a row-indexed point with fallback to one for an unusable denominator. Anchoring removes any constant normalized ratio, including a genuine persistent difference in levels. Plot absolute survival and mortality, deaths during the skip, the unanchored ratio and the anchored change. §2.9.1 says that frailty is not estimated by stratum, yet the Table 8 code fits nine subgroup-by-pseudo-dose series and takes a mortality-weighted geometric mean of anchored ratios. One subgroup contains the other two. This calculation has a different interpretation from an age-standardized population risk. Name the subgroups and weights and display the component estimates. In the separate registry illustration, the pooled raw ratio of 0.316 reverses the reported age-specific ratios of 1.28–2.13 (Supplement §S6.1.2).

## 3. Use the new bootstrap evidence, including the failures

For a fixed cohort followed completely for death, a multinomial draw over mutually exclusive subgroup, death-week and terminal-survivor cells is a valid individual bootstrap. The Table 8 code explicitly removes overlap before sampling and reconstructs the overlapping subgroups afterward. It refits the cohorts and recomputes the pooling weights. The revised Monte Carlo workbook handling also keeps the clock and fit diagnostics specific to each replicate. These changes address real problems in the earlier procedure. Joint comparisons across overlapping enrollment dates would additionally require resampling the same person's enrollment histories together.

I recounted the saved coverage indicators against the saved targets and endpoints. In `validation/person_bootstrap/replicates.csv`, percentile coverage was 577/600 with unequal frailty at the smaller enrollment size and 502/600 with unequal frailty at the larger one. All original fits passed in both scenarios. For the larger scenario the Wilson interval is 80.5%–86.4%. All 600 quiet-contamination datasets also passed, whereas zero of 600 sparse datasets passed, although the latter still had computable intervals in the archive. A pass can coexist with material undercoverage. The manuscript should retire its suggestion that undercoverage is expected to coincide with a diagnostic flag.

The saved matched comparison is more informative about the seed choice. On the same 200 datasets, `validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json` gives 160/200 covered for fixed $k$ and 194/200 for the joint seed, with all originals accepted. Thirty-eight datasets were covered only by the joint fit and four only by the fixed fit. Mean interval width increased from 0.0478 to 0.1488, and point-estimate RMSE increased from 0.0136 to 0.0375, even as average bias declined. The gain in coverage came with a substantial precision cost. Section 2.9.1 instead cites 189/200 for a joint fit and compares it to the separate 502/600 study. If 189/200 is a different run, identify it and provide its inputs and outputs. Report the matched evidence as such. The supplied coverage directory contains designs and results but no script that recreates those person-bootstrap experiments from their seeds. Its paired output names a different source-file hash from the supplied `code/KCOR.py`. Bundle the driver, exact fitter and configuration for each run. Test independent datasets with a range of slopes, event rates, multiple windows, and estimated offsets. Distinguish single-horizon coverage from any claim about an entire curve.

Table 8's 2022 joint-fit original has four insufficient-signal fits. At least one was flagged in 998/999 draws for that enrollment and 512/999 draws for 2021. The sidecar reports zero optimizer failures; its percentile endpoints include the flagged draws. These are **unconditional percentiles of the implemented fallback procedure** over the resampled records, conditional on the chosen analysis specification. They are not intervals restricted to accepted fits as §2.9.1 implies. The 2022 1-vs-0 interval, for example, is [0.233, 1.299]. Including one here says little about equivalence. Give per-contrast acceptance and flag reasons, distinguish the full fallback distribution from any accepted-only results, and evaluate each reporting rule's coverage and reporting frequency. Separate ordinary workbook event-count Wald bands from person-bootstrap intervals at every point of use. The old Poisson run with a reference-draw target and four identical-data scenarios is unsuitable as a calibration table, regardless of the number of repetitions.

## 4. Correct the validation experiments and their captions

- **Cox comparison.** §2.11.1 says both methods had only registry-like information. The generator passes the true $\theta_B$ directly into inversion and gives arm A its known zero (`test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py` lines 207–235 and 278–285). This is a known-parameter benchmark. The plotted grid has one run per variance and its KCOR plotting routine supplies no 95% bootstrap band, despite the caption. With common individual baseline $B$, the true marginal survivor hazard ratio B versus A equals $1/(1+\theta_B B(t))$. It genuinely differs from one. Cox and RMST describe marginal outcomes with different targets from the conditional treatment multiplier. Give each method its population target, run repeated samples, show fitted and oracle KCOR separately, and replace the rescaled shared-frailty reference with actual fits if making a performance claim.
- **Positive controls.** `test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py` multiplies an already mixed marginal hazard and generates expected fractional deaths. An intervention on the individual baseline changes subsequent survivor composition. Simulate the stated individual model and define the horizon-specific cumulative target before fitting. Remove the plotting fallback that can label a raw cumulative-hazard ratio KCOR.
- **Registry composition control.** The revised six Table 8 estimates and their bootstrap intervals provide a genuine new calculation. A different age distribution with a synthetic dose label has no known normalized null. The three revised 2022 fixed-seed ratios (1.0065, 1.0115, 1.0049) happen to be closer to one than the joint-seed ratios (1.0503, 1.0575, 1.0068). Closeness cannot select the correct fitter. Report raw drift, anchored drift and a prespecified equivalence margin. Supply the historical reproduction artifact explicitly: the cited summary log identifies itself as v6, while the caption attributes reproduction to v4.3. Distinguish the historical figures and their actual intervals from the revised reporting-date table.
- **Quiet windows and contamination.** The scanner in `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py` fits a constant baseline and computes pass rates after filtering to passing windows. Its 100% rates conceal actual passes of 8/13, 3/13 and 8/13 for doses 0, 1 and 2 in the 1940s stratum. Refit the current procedure and expose all denominators. With zero added contamination the archived dynamic arm already averages 1.063, with 70% above one, and the comorbidity arm averages 1.034, with 75% above one. These data do not establish the purported threshold for Czech confounding. Compare paired contaminated and uncontaminated draws against their respective known targets, vary the anchor and contaminated cohort, and specify a material change in advance. The Czech YAML records that 2023 was chosen because it showed the least apparent vaccine harm, and the empirical intuition figure screens on endpoint and improvement. Disclose these choices and evaluate holdout windows chosen independently of the contrast. Later uptake selected on evolving health can bias in either direction.

## 5. Finish the survivor model inside the wave

The new post-wave formula is correct **conditional on the exit exposures**. With $L(B,W)=E[\exp(-BZ-WZ^\alpha)]$, the ratio $L(B_e+u,W_e)/L(B_e,W_e)$ is the conditional survival for a subsequent baseline increase $u$. The implementation uses that ratio and observed post-exit survival. The proportional case $\alpha=1$ also correctly reduces to ordinary gamma inversion.

The unresolved problem is how the algorithm obtains $W_e$. Even before a wave, $Z\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(a,a)$, $a=1/\theta$, implies that survivors have shape $a$ and rate $a+B$. Their $\alpha$-moment is $\Gamma(a+\alpha)/[\Gamma(a)(a+B)^\alpha]$. The code instead evaluates a *unit-mean* gamma moment with parameter $\theta/(1+\theta B)$. This matters even if one seeks an amplification factor on the already inverted scale. For $\theta=1, B=1, \alpha=2$, the true survivor second moment is $1/2$. The derivative of the ordinary gamma-inverted cumulative hazard with respect to cumulative wave exposure at $W=0$ is then $(1+\theta B)/2=1$, while the implemented factor is $1.5$. Dividing the inverted excess by that factor already misattributes a small wave at first order. Once wave deaths occur, survival further tilts the distribution by $\exp(-W Z^\alpha)$. Dividing total accumulated excess by today's factor, clipping negative excess and taking a cumulative maximum do not solve the in-wave survival equation. A correct post-wave calculation propagates whatever exit distribution these choices supplied. See `code/KCOR.py::gamma_moment_alpha`, `propagate_theta`, `apply_nph_correction_post_inversion` and `post_wave_baseline_increases`.

Use the same joint survival law during and after the wave. Given a prespecified baseline and external scale, estimate nonnegative wave increments using sequential survival probabilities from (L), then carry the resulting survivor law through exit. Check whether the observed data actually admit that decomposition, and test recovery under misspecified baselines as well as known ones. For the Czech illustration, the exponent fails the authors' own localization criterion. The configuration and Table 8 still use forced $\alpha=1.18$. Make the core-only result primary and display this forced value alongside alternative external inputs as a sensitivity analysis.

**Publication decision.** Retain the exact inversion and the fixed-cohort descriptive comparison. Make one estimator reproducible from its normal entry point, align its gates with the quantities actually reported, and show its bias, width, coverage and refusal rate against independent population targets. Until then, the Czech results support a comparison of model specifications. They do not establish how much observed mortality difference was caused by depletion or how accurately it was removed.

I checked the supplied manuscript, supplement, source changes and archived outputs, and recomputed the cited coverage counts and paired mean widths from the saved per-dataset results. I did not rerun the registry workbooks or regenerate the simulation cohorts.
