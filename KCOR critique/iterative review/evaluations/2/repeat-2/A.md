# Independent review of KCOR

**Major revision.** The core transformation is mathematically sound for a specified mean-one gamma frailty distribution and hazards proportional to individual frailty. The methods paper could make a contribution by showing how sensitive a descriptive cumulative-hazard comparison is to that model. The current evidence does not establish that the pipeline reliably recovers frailty from registry mortality curves, that its diagnostics detect important failures, or that its reported intervals have their nominal coverage.

## What the curve can identify

1. **Correct the identification proposition.** In a quiet period with Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the marginal hazard \(h\) satisfies \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\). Thus, with known \(\gamma\), \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) in the ideal continuous model. A perfectly constant marginal hazard \(h=k\) identifies \(\theta=\gamma/k>0\), even though the observed cumulative hazard is exactly linear. Conversely \(\theta=0,\gamma>0\) produces a curved cumulative hazard. The equivalence asserted in §2.4.4 fails in both directions. A common slope error \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally inferred variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\), which can differ greatly across cohorts. Demonstrate cancellation in the ratio for the designs where it occurs. Report joint parameter profiles and a slope-sensitivity grid for each cohort and contrast. Assess the inverted curve against the fitted Gompertz cumulative hazard, which is itself curved in calendar time. [Paper §§2.1.2, 2.4.3–4, 2.6.2; supplement §§S2, S5.]

2. **Distinguish a fitted decomposition from observed depletion.** For every \(\theta\ge0\), \(B_\theta(t)=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H_{\rm obs}(t))/\theta\), interpreted by continuity at zero, reproduces exactly the same observed survival under its corresponding gamma model. The Gompertz restriction and assumptions about departures during waves select among these explanations. A time-varying baseline can therefore reproduce every residual and stability check calculated from the same curve. The paper acknowledges this in §§4.1 and 5.1, but the supplement promises transparent failure. Report sensitivity over plausible baseline shapes, identify which restrictions have evidence independent of the fitted outcomes, and show a deliberately observationally equivalent counterexample. The Czech configuration even says 2023 was chosen because it “showed the least vaccine harm.” Document when and how each window was chosen, label outcome-informed analyses exploratory, and test a locked specification in a held-out period. [Paper §§2.1.3, 2.4.4, 3.3.2; supplement §S2; data/Czech/Czech.yaml, comments preceding theta_estimation_windows.]

## What was actually estimated

3. **Choose one production estimator.** Section 2.5 jointly estimates \(k,\theta\) in the first quiet window and then refits \(\theta\) using quiet weeks. The ordinary fitter fixes \(k\) from the first post-skip weeks, estimates only \(\theta\) in the seed window, and includes anchor weeks in the final objective even when they fall outside quiet windows. Table 8 uses runtime source replacement to introduce a joint seed. The difference matters. For 2022_06 the three joint-fit point estimates are 1.0503, 1.0575 and 1.0068, versus 1.0065, 1.0115 and 1.0049 with the early fixed rate. Implement named modes in one function, make the manuscript specification an ordinary entry point, and record the exact mode on every result. For late first quiet windows, explicitly account for prior wave exposure before fixing the seed scale. [Paper §§2.5, 3.1.2; code/KCOR.py, fit_theta0_gompertz; analysis/table8/compare.py and table8_bootstrap.json.]

4. **Finish the weekly-time correction.** The revised \(\operatorname{expm1}\) inversion in §2.5 Step 2 correctly inverts an integrated weekly marginal hazard. The fitted prediction still uses the instantaneous expression \(k e^{\gamma t}/(1+\theta B_t)\). If \(b_t\) is the baseline increment over a week starting at \(B_t\), the corresponding marginal increment is \(\log[1+\theta b_t/(1+\theta B_t)]/\theta\). Fit this increment or the equivalent binomial death probability with the observed start-of-week risk set. There is also a boundary error. The code saves cumulative values **before** each week's increment, reads the offset at the last gap-week index, and applies it starting next week. An excess confined to the last gap week is omitted from the offset. In the NPH module the first in-wave cumulative value is already end-of-week and is assigned entirely to the baseline. Use explicit start/end arrays and test one-week waves and pulses on both boundaries. [Paper §§2.3, 2.5, 2.7.3; code/KCOR.py, _build_H_gom_discrete, _fit_theta, gap_end_idx, H0_eff and H_ref.]

5. **Enforce the diagnostic reporting rule.** The code can return success with \(\theta=0\) for insufficient deaths or a nonpositive first-pass offset. A true positive frailty variance can coexist with zero wave excess. Substituting zero variance in that circumstance changes a scientific assumption. In Table 8, four original 2022 stratum fits flagged insufficient signal. At least one fit flagged in 998/999 draws in 2022 and 512/999 in 2021. Those draws enter the percentiles. The 2022 interval [0.233, 1.299] for pseudo-dose 1 versus 0 illustrates how little the result establishes about agreement with unity. Separate optimizer completion, information about frailty, and fallback output. Trace failed components to each comparison, label any fallback-based result, report failure frequencies and calibrate the complete reporting rule. Dropping failed draws alone does not establish conditional coverage. [Paper Box 2, §§2.9.1, 3.1.2; supplement §S2 I7; code/KCOR.py, fit_theta0_gompertz; analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py and table8_bootstrap.json.]

6. **Define the reported contrast and its population.** The primary equation gives an unanchored cumulative-hazard ratio. Table 8 uses a weighted geometric mean of stratum-specific ratios of growth since an anchor, with weights calculated from early mortality and risk sets. Section 2.9.1 says frailty parameters are never fitted by stratum, whereas the table runner fits each subgroup-by-pseudo-dose cell. An anchored ratio is exactly one for any constant proportional difference in the two inverted curves, including a lasting twofold difference. Give the precise formula, weights, target population, landmark and horizon alongside each result. Show observed survival or risks, unanchored KCOR and anchored change separately. Describe the skipped weeks as conditioning on survival to a landmark. Frozen enrollment groups can receive later doses, and the direction of the resulting difference need not be toward unity. [Paper §§2.1.1, 2.2, 2.7–2.9, 5.2; supplement §§S5.6, S6; analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py, estimate.]

## The optional wave model

7. **Use one survival law during and after the wave.** With starting frailty \(Z\sim\mathrm{Gamma}(1/\theta,\mathrm{scale}=\theta)\) and baseline exposure \(B\), the survivor shape stays \(1/\theta\) and its scale is \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\). For \(\theta=B=1\), the survivor second moment is \(1/2\). The code passes the propagated value \(1/2\) to a new *mean-one* gamma and obtains \(3/2\). During a wave the survivor density is proportional to \(f(z)\exp[-zB-z^\alpha W]\), generally outside the gamma family. Dividing positive cumulative excess after ordinary gamma inversion by a single current moment does not invert this law. The post-wave conditional-survival equation is valid given correct exit exposures, but inherits their in-wave errors. Use the same Laplace integral \(L(B,W)\) during the wave, specify or independently estimate baseline increments, and solve for wave exposure through time. Test against simulated individual death times with known \(B,W,\theta,\alpha\), including identity and zero-wave cases. The paper calls the Czech exponent unidentified, while Czech defaults and Table 8 force 1.18. Show the module-off analysis as primary and label the forced exponent a sensitivity analysis. [Paper §§2.7.1–4, 3.1.2, 3.4.4; code/KCOR.py, propagate_theta, gamma_moment_alpha, apply_nph_correction_post_inversion and post_wave_baseline_increases; data/Czech/Czech.yaml.]

## What the evidence supports

8. **Rerun the validation with the estimator that readers will use.** Section 2.11.1 explicitly says the Cox comparison withheld simulator knowledge. Its generator passes the true frailty variance straight into KCOR inversion. The quiet-window scan presented as the revised estimator in supplement §S5.4 fits a constant-baseline cumulative model \(\log(1+\theta kt)/\theta\). The positive-control generator multiplies an already marginalized hazard, although §S4.3 describes multiplying the individual's baseline hazard. These are distinct experiments. Retain the known-variance result as an oracle benchmark, fit KCOR from counts on the same replicates, generate effects before marginalization and compare to the exact horizon-specific truth. A non-null marginal hazard contrast or RMST difference with unequal frailty distributions can be a correct description of population survival. Specify each comparator's target before reporting bias. Age-shifted groups have genuinely different possible mortality trajectories, so flatness after anchoring is an empirical check with an assumed reference, not a known-null outcome. [Paper §§2.11.1, 3.1–3.3 and comparator tables; test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py, lines 209–235 and 278–285; test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py, lines 77–106; test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py, lines 115–126.]

9. **Reconcile the uncertainty evidence and report its trade-off.** The person-level resampling scheme is sensible for independent fixed-cohort records, and the Table 8 runner preserves overlapping subgroup membership. In the one-million-person unequal-frailty scenario, the production percentile interval covered 502/600 times (83.7%) with all original fits accepted. The manuscript reports 189/200 for a joint-fit study. Recounting the archived paired file gives **194/200 joint versus 160/200 fixed on the same datasets**, with mean widths 0.149 versus 0.048 and RMSE 0.0375 versus 0.0136. Its recorded code hash differs from this edition's code. Supply the artifact for 189/200 if it is a separate run, label each historical version, and regenerate a paired table for the chosen release with Monte Carlo uncertainty, coverage, widths, bias, RMSE, failure rates and acceptance. Use analytic population truth or a verified numerical equivalent. The separate Poisson pilot sets its target from a fitted finite reference draw. Include actual Table 8 settings, late-window and weak-signal scenarios, the anchoring operation and the prespecified wave policy in the calibration. [Paper §2.9.1 and bootstrap coverage table; validation/person_bootstrap/{design,summary,paired}.json; test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py, lines 205–234.]

I would retain the gamma inversion and the fixed-cohort descriptive question. The first publishable comparison should use one versioned, wave-off estimator with an explicit target, a verified weekly likelihood or increment model, and reproducible evidence for its precision and reporting limits. The review used the supplied manuscript, supplement, source and archived outputs. I independently recounted the paired interval outcomes and checked the model identities. I did not rerun the registry or bootstrap simulations.
