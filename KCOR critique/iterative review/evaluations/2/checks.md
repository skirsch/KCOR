# Checks on the review findings

Paths below refer to the frozen inputs listed in [the manifest](inputs.json). The findings concern the particular code and analyses inspected. A confirmed discrepancy in a validation script does not establish the bias of KCOR on a different dataset.

| Finding | Check and scope |
|---|---|
| Simulator supplies frailty in the Cox comparison | In both editions, `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:234–235,278–284` inverts the two arms with zero and the value passed to the simulation generator. The revised paper correctly defines the null conditional on frailty, but §2.11.1 still says the parameters were estimated without privileged information. The numerical grid checks inversion given known frailty. |
| Weekly reconstruction | The baseline's two weekly reconstruction loops use the first-order increment. The revision uses the exact increment in both places, including its zero-frailty limit. See [the four-case replay](../../../1.6/verification.md). The separate weekly fitting objective still predicts a point hazard, and the gap offset is read from a start-of-week array indexed by the last gap week. A shock confined to that last bin cannot enter the value sampled there. |
| Identification and curve shape | Under fixed Gompertz slope and proportional gamma frailty, differentiating the marginal hazard gives $h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2$. With $k\theta=\gamma>0$, marginal hazard is constant despite positive frailty. With $\theta=0,\gamma>0$, the cumulative hazard is curved. The proposition in §2.4.4 still equates nonzero curvature with positive frailty. The exact de-frailtied cumulative baseline is linear in transformed Gompertz time, generally curved in calendar time. The revised constant-scaling sentence correctly distinguishes marginal from individual hazard scaling. |
| Initial fitter | `code/KCOR.py` fixes the initial rate from early post-skip weeks. §2.5 specifies a joint first-window fit. The Table 8 replay selects the joint fitter explicitly. Project records already identify adoption as the intended default pending lead approval. |
| Interval coverage | In the selected unequal-frailty scenario, fixed-rate production covered 502/600. In the archived paired comparison, fixed and joint fitting covered 160/200 and 194/200 with mean interval widths about 0.048 and 0.149. The manuscript's 189/200 belongs to a distinct person-versus-block study. The three figures answer different questions. The 189/200 study was available to the coordinator in the original evidence archive, but its underlying file was omitted from the blinded export. A reviewer's failure to find it there is an input limitation. [189/200 study](<../../../1.3/objection3_paper_method/paired_resampling/ASSESSMENT.md>) · [194/200 study](../../../1.3/paired-seed-fit/assessment.md) |
| Table 8 | I ran the supplied revised workbook replay with two bootstrap draws as an entry-point check. All six original point estimates matched the workbook to the built-in tolerance. The preserved 999-draw output records four flagged fits among the nine contributing 2022 fits, with at least one flag in 998/999 draws for 2022 and 512/999 for 2021. Those counters describe draws with any flag. They do not assign every flag to every dose comparison. An interval containing one does not demonstrate equivalence. Interpretation and any change to the reporting rule remain for the authors. |
| Wave correction | At the last wave boundary, the revised equation inverts conditional survival using a supplied split of baseline and wave exposures. Its mathematical validity is conditional on that split. During a nonlinear wave, survivors have density proportional to $f(z)\exp(-zB-z^\alpha W)$. Even before the wave the survivor gamma has shape $1/\theta$ and rate $1/\theta+B$, so its mean generally differs from one. Feeding its updated scale into a freshly mean-one gamma moment in the in-wave module requires separate justification. The Czech configuration forces 1.18 and Table 8 discloses that setting. The data's weak localization does not by itself invalidate the method. |
| A legacy regression changed status | I ran `py -3.11 -B test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py` against both frozen editions. It passed for the baseline and failed at line 44 for the revision. The assertion requires the post-wave increment to equal the uncorrected increment, which was a property of the old constant tail shift. The failure establishes a stale expectation and does not demonstrate a discontinuity in the corrected model. |
