# Review of the revised KCOR manuscript and implementation

**Major revision.** KCOR can describe how a specified gamma-frailty transformation changes a retrospective mortality comparison. The exact weekly inversion, the person bootstrap for closed cohorts, and the conditional-survival equation after wave exit improve the method. The principal scientific limitations remain. Cohort death counts alone cannot determine how much curvature came from depletion. The software, validation experiments and registry example still use different estimators. Most immediately, the 2022 Table 8 results include fits that failed the paper’s own information check. I would retain the descriptive estimand and change the evidence and interpretation before presenting these results as validated.

## 1. What the data identify

Write H for the observed cumulative hazard. For every θ ≥ 0, the baseline $B_θ=\operatorname{expm1}(θH)/θ$, with $B_0=H$, reproduces the same survival when mixed with unit-mean gamma frailty of variance θ. A population in which everyone follows the marginal hazard $H′$ and a heterogeneous population with baseline $B_θ′$ consequently give the same law for the observed counts. Even an independent sample from that same mortality trajectory cannot distinguish those explanations. The revised §2.6.2 and §5.2 recognize this. SI S2 still promises observable failure when an assumption is violated. Section 5 also asserts conservatism for endogenous frailty and crossover without specifying assumptions that bound the direction. Replace these assurances with the conditional interpretation throughout.

There is a precise positive statement under a fixed Gompertz slope γ. The marginal hazard satisfies $h′=γh-θh²$, so $θ=(γh-h′)/h²$ wherever $h>0$ in an ideal quiet interval. Nonzero curvature of cumulative hazard is neither equivalent to nor necessary for positive θ, contrary to §2.4.4. With θ = 0 and γ > 0, cumulative hazard is curved. With θk = γ > 0, it is linear. At low mortality even a small error in γ can cause a large error in θ. Replace the curvature proposition with this result, assess residuals against the expected Gompertz path, and show the range of θ and of the final ratio compatible with sampling error, credible slopes and smooth cohort-specific baseline deviations. The revised scaling formula $(k,θ)\mapsto(ck,θ/c)$ for scaling the *observed* hazard by c is correct. The abstract, §4.1 and SI S2 still describe invariance too broadly. A common c can cancel in the normalized ratio while both fitted variances change.

The primary ratio $B_A/B_B$ includes real differences in baseline scale between cohorts, including age effects. Dividing by its value at an anchor measures change in the ratio since the anchor. A persistent proportional difference vanishes in that presentation. Name which contrast each result estimates, specify whether the reference is one week or a mean (§2.1.1 versus §2.8), and put observed survival, counts and absolute risk differences alongside the normalized curves.

## 2. One estimator must connect specification, figures and intervals

The manuscript fits k and θ jointly in the first quiet window. The ordinary production path still fixes k from early post-skip weeks, fits θ alone in the first quiet window and uses quiet **or anchor** weeks in the pooled fit. Table 8 uses a third execution path. Its adapter replaces source text inside the production function to inject a joint seed fit, then uses the production iteration. The seed compares an instantaneous hazard with observed weekly integrated hazards, using a continuous Gompertz integral, while the pooled step uses a left-endpoint discrete cumulative baseline. A comparison with a workbook using the same injected function verifies agreement of point calculations, but it cannot validate the estimator equations or bootstrap calibration. Select a public, versioned fitter, make the alternative an explicit sensitivity setting, and call it from the main analysis and simulations. State whether fits are by age and dose. Section 2.9.1 says age strata are used only for aggregation. Table 8 loops over age and dose, and SI S6 reports distinct age-specific parameters. See `code/KCOR.py:1226–1238,1317–1394`, `analysis/table8/compare.py` and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:73–99`.

The new `expm1` update correctly differences the gamma inversion over a weekly bin. A separate offset error survives. Both iterations save cumulative hazards at the **start** of the last gap bin, measure the gap excess there and apply it to following weeks. A shock confined to that final bin is absent from its inferred offset. Keep start and end arrays and transfer excess through the end of the gap. Under a Gompertz baseline, use the integrated weekly baseline increment and predict the interval event probability from the survival ratio at the two boundaries. Fit binomial counts from start-of-week risk sets. Test a last-gap-bin shock, θ = 0, and invariance under subdivision of an exactly generated interval. See `code/KCOR.py:1321–1365,1450–1470,1613–1629`.

## 3. The Table 8 inferential claim conflicts with its diagnostic record

The archived person bootstrap removes overlap between the source groups before multinomial sampling, reconstructs it afterward, preserves event weeks and recalculates the weights. This is a coherent way to resample whole empirical histories for a closed cohort. The revised points and percentiles agree with the archived JSON. The 2022 1-versus-0 result is 1.0503 with interval [0.233, 1.299]. Four of the nine original stratum-by-dose fits had insufficient signal. At least one fit was flagged in **998 of 999** draws. For 2021 the original had no such flags, but 512 of 999 draws had at least one. Numerical “success” can include setting θ to zero after the scientific fit fails. The code reports quantiles over all draws. These are quantiles of a procedure that can fall back to identity. In particular, the 2022 intervals cannot establish that the normalization worked simply because they contain 1. See `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json`, the quantile and flag logic in its sibling Python file, and `code/KCOR.py:1650–1721`.

Prespecify whether the estimand is reported on a failed original fit, which constituent fits each pooled comparison requires, and how an identity fallback changes its interpretation. Report flags by arm and pair, the fraction of intervals the procedure can provide, and the sampling behavior of the entire rule. Dropping failed bootstrap draws after observing them could itself distort inference. Make the status visible in Table 8. Distinguish the historical table’s v4.3 reproduction claim from its cited log, whose header says v6.0. The 2022 fixed-anchor estimates in the revised configuration are all closer to one than the joint-fit estimates. That comparison is informative about dependence on the fitting procedure, without making proximity to one a selection criterion.

Relabeling age bands within a common vaccination category removes a vaccination contrast but does not establish a unit baseline-hazard ratio or constant subsequent drift across ages. Run a random-label negative control within common age/calendar strata, show both levels and anchored drift for the age-shift exercise, and prespecify an acceptable drift margin. Give the pooling formula in the table. The reported weighted geometric mean of stratum-specific anchored ratios need not equal a ratio of directly age-standardized population hazards.

## 4. The resampling studies now reveal where inference fails

I recounted the percentile intervals in `validation/person_bootstrap/replicates.csv`. With unequal frailties at the smaller size, 577/600 included the analytic target. In the million-person unequal-frailty setting, only 502/600 did (83.7%), although all 600 original analyses passed. The two post-quiet effect settings covered 568/600 and 567/600. All 600 contaminated fits passed; no sparse fit passed, so conditional coverage in the sparse scenario is undefined. These results distinguish a useful variance calculation from a calibrated 95% confidence interval. They also demonstrate that checking the fit cannot certify an uncontaminated window.

Reconcile the sources before publication. Section 2.9.1 reports 189/200 for a joint fit. The supplied **paired** archive independently counts 194/200 for joint and 160/200 for fixed k on the same datasets with 199 bootstrap draws each, with average widths 0.149 and 0.048, respectively. There are 38 joint-only and four fixed-only covered datasets. The unpaired 189/200 could describe a different study. Its provenance is missing here. The paired results actually permit a fitter comparison in their tested setting and show the corresponding loss of precision. The archive identifies a production-source checksum different from the reviewed source. Supply a runnable driver, exact code and configurations for each study, and identify which results apply to the final estimator. The 600-dataset design used 999 draws. Do not combine those denominators or treat the wave-free studies as calibration of the forced-wave Table 8 analysis.

Replace the blank pilot coverage table with the appropriate completed experiment, clearly labeled. The older weekly-Poisson program compares with an estimate from a reference draw; several scenario generators reuse deterministic expected deaths. Its committed 500 × 200 file gives 100% coverage in four settings and 25.4% in another. These are poor benchmarks for sampling coverage of the person bootstrap. For the selected final fitter, use independent integer-count histories, analytically computed truth, and report bias, RMSE, width, gate pass rate, failures and coverage with Monte Carlo uncertainty. Include weak signals, misspecified slopes, differential trends and the exact weighting, anchoring and wave settings used in published applications. The new per-replicate clock, diagnostic keys and exclusion of the original point from percentile draws are sensible implementation repairs.

## 5. The wave tail is improved, while its starting state remains unsupported

The post-wave equation using $L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^αW)]$ correctly inverts conditional survival after the wave, given correct baseline and wave exposures at exit, a fixed α and the assumed model. The α = 1 identity is also correct. The in-wave calculation supplies neither exposure by solving this survival model. Before any wave, survivors from a starting mean-one gamma have shape $1/θ$ and rate $1/θ+B$. Their α moment is $\Gamma(1/θ+α)/[\Gamma(1/θ)(1/θ+B)^α]$. The implementation takes $θ/(1+θB)$ and uses it as the variance of a **new mean-one gamma**, which changes the shape. At α = 1 that moment routine returns one, while the survivor mean is $1/(1+θB)$. A unit correction at α = 1 after ordinary inversion is warranted, but substituting one for an observed-risk-set moment in the identification equations is a different operation. During a wave, the additional tilt by $\exp(-WZ^α)$ affects the moments further. See §§2.7.1–3 and `code/KCOR.py:1028–1047,2649–2717`.

The in-wave method also divides cumulative excess by its *endpoint* factor, clips negative excess and takes a running maximum. The exit state is defined before that repair, so its two exposures need not even sum to the displayed exit level. An exact tail conditional on these inputs inherits their error. Fit a specified baseline and wave path with the same survival law throughout, using interval survival probabilities or increment-by-increment inversion for the wave exposure when the baseline is externally fixed. Weakly identified inputs warrant sensitivity ranges. Validate against an independent individual-level generator through entry, peak, exit and recovery. Update the stale continuity test: it requires every post-wave increment to equal the uncorrected increment, a property the new survivor model deliberately changes. Evaluating its six-week fixture against the revised numerical functions gives a first post-exit difference of about 3.87 × 10⁻⁸ against a tolerance of 10⁻⁹. Test continuity at exit and reproduction of subsequent conditional survival instead.

The Czech configuration still forces α = 1.18, and the Table 8 replay requires it. The paper also says a weakly identified module is inactive and reports weak localization in the Czech data. Produce the core empirical results with the module off, and label the forced-α curve and intervals separately as conditional sensitivity results. An external intervention-effect scale can reduce amplitude confounding. The data must independently support the in-wave survivor equation.

## 6. The other controls still need the production estimator and their own targets

- **Cox comparison.** The paper now correctly distinguishes a null treatment hazard ratio at fixed frailty. However, §2.11.1 still says KCOR learns θ from the same data. Its grid explicitly hands the true θ to the inversion function. With θA = 0 and θB > 0 the marginal B-to-A hazard ratio is $1/(1+θ_B B(t))$, below one for positive cumulative baseline hazard. Marginal survival and RMST can genuinely differ. Present the current result as an oracle check, estimate θ from observations over many datasets, compare each method with its own target, and either fit or remove the interpolated shared-frailty comparator. Its plotting code does not draw the advertised KCOR bootstrap bands. See `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:207–235,278–297` and `plot_cox_bias_sim.py:67–82`.
- **Recovery and injected effects.** The multiwave recovery script fixes k at the exact initial hazard on a noiseless trajectory. The standalone positive control multiplies a marginal hazard and then restores its original schedule, even though a true individual baseline pulse changes subsequent survivor composition. Its plotting script can fall back to the raw cumulative ratio. Keep these as limited checks. Require an end-to-end fitted result for published figures and include persistent effects, pulse overlap with fitting windows, and non-gamma individual heterogeneity. See `test/theta0/theta0_nwave.py` and `test/positive_control/code/`.
- **Window choice.** SI S5.4 describes a scan using the revised estimator. The scanner uses a single constant-baseline window and calculates its summary pass rate only among rows that passed. The Czech YAML says 2023 was selected because it showed the least vaccine harm. Establish the window-selection history, label outcome-informed choices as exploratory, show all windows in the denominator, and evaluate a frozen specification in independent cohorts or periods. See `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py:77–113,242–271` and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:21–39`.
- **Contamination threshold.** At zero contamination the two sweeps already have mean anchored ratios of 1.063 and 1.034, with 70% and 75% of the 20 draws above one. At the claimed 66%-of-baseline reversal threshold the mean is 1.062 and 65% are above one. Counting above one cannot establish false-harm frequency, and a positive decaying pulse after the anchor differs from enrollment-time suppression. Compare paired perturbations with their corresponding null, allow either sign and relevant timing in both arms, report meaningful effect margins and sampling uncertainty, and withdraw the general claim about adequacy for Czech cohorts. See §3.3.2 and `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv`.

**Publication criterion.** A reader should be able to start from the archived counts, run one versioned estimator, recover the points and their validity statuses, and reproduce a calibration table for that same estimator at the reported horizon. The scientific claim supported now is how a chosen depletion model changes the observed comparison under named assumptions. The data do not establish that the change measures the actual amount of depletion or the effect of vaccination.

I reviewed the supplied PDF and manuscript sources, the prior objections, the cumulative diff, the cited code and the archived numerical results. I independently recounted the two coverage archives and checked the numerical tail-test fixture. I did not rerun the national registry analysis or the full bootstrap experiments.
