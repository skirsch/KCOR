# Review of the revised KCOR paper

**Major revision.** KCOR can describe how a specified gamma-frailty model changes a fixed-cohort cumulative-hazard contrast. The exact weekly inversion now works, and I reproduced all six revised Table 8 point estimates from the supplied workbook. The strongest remaining problems are that the paper and the production software fit different estimators, the claimed diagnostic gate permits visibly unidentified results to be published, and the optional wave correction fails an exact, noiseless test under its own individual-level model.

## 1. What the Czech example actually shows

The Table 8 workbook's Git blob hash matches the archived identifier. I reconstructed the pooled enrollment populations of 2,511,927 and 2,450,449 and obtained the following point estimates on December 26, 2022. Comparisons are pseudo-dose 1/0, 2/0 and 2/1 in that order.

| Enrollment | Joint seed, α forced to 1.18 | Production fixed seed, same wave setting | Joint seed, wave module off |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021_24 | 1.0091, 1.0317, 1.0225 | 1.0114, 1.0281, 1.0165 | 1.0123, 1.0428, 1.0301 |
| 2022_06 | 1.0503, 1.0575, 1.0068 | 1.0065, 1.0115, 1.0049 | 1.0517, 1.0589, 1.0069 |

The overlap-aware bootstrap is a real improvement. The workbook includes two subgroups and their encompassing total. Subtracting the subgroups from the total before multinomial sampling of death-week/survivor categories, then reconstructing the overlap, preserves their statistical dependence. The six revised joint-fit intervals in the PDF agree with the archived quantiles. The historical source citation still points to a log headed v6.0 although the text names v4.3. Cite the actual artifact for each column. See paper §3.1.2 and Table 8, `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:24–98`, `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json`, and the two `test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary*.log` files.

**The 2022 intervals fail the stated interpretation rule.** Four of nine contributing original joint fits flagged insufficient signal. Two hit the upper θ bound near 100 and were replaced by zero. Two returned zero on the first negative-offset check. Every pair draws on a flagged stratum. Across bootstrap draws, at least one fit flagged in 998/999 draws for 2022 and 512/999 for 2021. The program keeps these draws and reports their quantiles. Numerical success and scientific identification have different meanings here. Including one in an interval as broad as [0.233, 1.299] offers little evidence of normalization performance. Report original and bootstrap gate outcomes by stratum and pair, and display fallback results as sensitivity outputs with their own estimand. Apply the scientific gate before presenting a pooled contrast as interpretable. Simply discarding the failing draws would also change the procedure and require new coverage evaluation. See `code/KCOR.py:1485–1510,1650–1676`, `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:78–101,153–163`, paper lines 175–179 and supplement lines 58–70.

All these contrasts are anchored twice in the sense that they compare each cohort's cumulative growth relative to its initial reference value. The unanchored model-scale ratio is the paper's primary estimand. A time-constant level ratio becomes one on the anchored scale whatever its magnitude. Relabeling cohorts 10–20 years apart supplies no known unit target for the unanchored contrast and no guarantee of equal growth during an epidemic. Moreover, 26 of the 30 quiet weeks used for the 2022 point estimate occur **after** its reporting date (26 of 41 for 2021). That is a permissible retrospective smoother, but the date indexes the outcome horizon, not the information available for the fit. State the dates, overlapping subgroup weights and target explicitly. Show raw risks, unanchored and anchored estimates side by side, add a genuine random-label null within common strata, and report an equivalence margin if the intended claim concerns negligible drift.

## 2. Choose one production estimator

The main method jointly estimates k and θ in the first quiet window. The production fitter fixes k to the average growth-adjusted marginal hazard in the first post-skip weeks and fits θ alone. That average can already reflect depletion or early suppression, independently of whether the anchor lies inside a quiet window. The Table 8 trial changes the seed by editing the source of the function in memory. It retains the production final mask of **quiet weeks plus anchor weeks**, whereas the printed final objective uses quiet weeks alone. A generated workbook matching a replay of the same estimator demonstrates reproducibility of its output, not agreement with the paper's statistical objective. Select and version the exact estimator, use one callable implementation, and expose the alternative as a named sensitivity analysis. Refit simulations and figures through the same entry point. See paper lines 371–386, 414–430, `code/KCOR.py:1226–1238,1375–1394` and `analysis/table8/compare.py:9–39`.

The corrected weekly inverse uses ΔB = (1+θB) expm1(θu)/θ for observed weekly log-survival increment u, with the correct θ = 0 limit. That addresses my earlier objection to the Euler reconstruction. Two adjacent operations still need revision. First, the algorithm stores **start-of-bin** B at each index, reads the index for the last gap week, then applies the resulting offset in the subsequent week. A shock confined to that last gap week is omitted from the offset. Second, both the seed and final residual compare weekly integrated observed hazards with instantaneous hazard evaluated at a bin boundary. For a true individual Gompertz baseline and a quiet bin [a,b], ΔB = k(exp(γb)−exp(γa))/γ. The exact marginal increment is log1p(θΔB/(1+θB(a)))/θ. Use boundary-indexed arrays and integrated-bin probabilities consistently in the fit, reconstruction and offset. Test a shock confined to the final gap week and stability under changes of bin width. See `code/KCOR.py:1321–1355,1384–1390,1450–1466,1534–1543,1613–1629`.

## 3. The identification proposition still needs a proof and corrected conditions

With fixed known Gompertz slope γ, marginal hazard h under this model obeys h′ = γh − θh² inside a quiet interval with proportional individual frailty. Thus θ = (γh − h′)/h² wherever h > 0 in the ideal continuous model. Non-zero curvature of observed cumulative hazard is **not equivalent** to θ > 0. At θ = 0 a positive Gompertz slope gives curvature. At kθ = γ a positive frailty variance gives a constant marginal hazard and linear cumulative hazard. A well-estimated zero and a practically unidentifiable variance also have different meanings. Use the differential identity to state a correct model-conditional result, fit it through interval counts rather than numerical differentiation, and report profile intervals and sensitivity to γ. At low hazard, even a small baseline-slope error can have a large effect on θ.

The revised §2.4.4 correctly says scaling observed hazards by c changes (k,θ) to (ck,θ/c). Scaling each individual's baseline instead preserves θ. The abstract, introductory claim, §4.1 and SI assumption A3 still describe θ as invariant under common observed-hazard scaling. The common c can cancel in the reconstructed **ratio** while changing both fitted variances. Fix the remaining claims. See paper lines 15, 81, 323–357, 945–950 and supplement line 36.

There is an exact limit on the evidence available from aggregate deaths. For every increasing H and every θ ≥ 0, Bθ = expm1(θH)/θ produces exactly H under gamma mixing, using B0 = H. A homogeneous cohort with common hazard H′ gives the same observed counts as the heterogeneous cohort. Windows, residuals, sample size and computational precision cannot discriminate observationally identical curves. The improved §2.6 and §5.2 acknowledge this. Carry that qualification into SI S2, §3.3 and the conclusion, where failure is still presented as reliably signaled. Neither treatment-dependent cohort crossover nor endogenous frailty generally guarantees attenuation. Use external baseline information, sensitivity ranges for cohort-specific secular trends, and prespecified failure criteria. In the Czech YAML, a comment says CY 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm. Establish prospective provenance or describe that choice as exploratory. See paper lines 472–473, 838–855, 1007, 1037–1042 and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:21–32`.

## 4. The wave-tail fix is correct conditional on quantities that the in-wave correction misestimates

For starting mean-one gamma Z and individual cumulative hazard ZB + Z^αW, write L(B,W) = E[exp(−ZB−Z^αW)]. If B and W are known at wave exit and W then stays fixed, solving L(B+u,W)/L(B,W) = exp(−ΔHobs) correctly gives the subsequent baseline increase. The α = 1 identity also follows because B+W then shares one proportional frailty multiplier. These are substantive corrections.

The **in-wave** formula still divides the accumulated post-inversion excess by a moment evaluated at its endpoint. Even before a wave, survivors under proportional hazard have Z distributed as Gamma(shape 1/θ, rate 1/θ+B). The survivor moment is Γ(1/θ+α)/Γ(1/θ) × (1/θ+B)^(−α). The code inserts θ/(1+θB) into a new unit-mean gamma distribution, altering the shape as well as the scale. With θ = 1, B = 1, α = 2, the true survivor moment is 0.5 and the implemented moment is 1.5. During a nonlinear wave there is an additional exp(−Z^αW) tilt. The moment then depends on the wave exposure history. A hazard-level moment and a factor for a cumulative excess on the already inverted scale also require separate derivations.

I checked this against independent numerical integration. Take starting Z exponential with mean one, α = 2, B at wave entry 0.2, subsequent weekly increments 0.01 exp(0.085 t/52) and cumulative W = (0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.10). Feed exact observed H = −log L(B,W) and the true θ and baseline settings into the production correction. At wave exit the true reference-frailty cumulative hazard is **0.32005**, the returned value **0.29817**, and assigned W is **0.07812** instead of 0.10. At the next week the true total is 0.33010 and the returned value 0.30790. Supplied directly with the true exit exposures, the new post-wave solver recovered the exact increase of 0.01004916. The error therefore arises before the new tail solver and survives it.

Given an independently credible B path and α, solve L(B(t),W(t)) = Sobs(t) at week boundaries, with coherent monotone W, and derive an explicit noisy-data likelihood. If both paths are unknown, specify what independently identifies them or report sensitivity bounds. Include wave-entry deaths at the correct boundary, validate the entire fit on independently generated person-level outcomes, and account for changed survivor composition in post-wave quiet-window fits. The exponent was not identified in the Czech application, yet the default configuration forces 1.18 and Table 8 expressly requires it. Use the off results as the primary analysis and the forced results as a labeled sensitivity analysis. See paper lines 508–558, 568–608, 892–913, `code/KCOR.py:1028–1047,2486–2535,2653–2717` and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:47–54`.

## 5. Report the new coverage evidence by fitter, including its cost

The production person bootstrap with unequal frailty and one million subjects covered in **502/600 (83.7%)**, even though all 600 original fits passed. The empirical gate did not detect this undercoverage. The archived paired study is especially informative. I independently recounted its 200 rows: **160/200 fixed-seed** and **194/200 joint-seed** intervals covered. Joint alone covered 38 cases, fixed alone four. Mean interval width rose from 0.0478 to 0.1488, and point-estimate RMSE rose from 0.0136 to 0.0375. Better coverage in that comparison came with substantially wider intervals and less precise point estimates. No single design establishes general superiority of either estimator.

The PDF cites **189/200** for a joint-fit study and compares it with the separate 600-dataset design. The supplied paired file instead records **194/200**, carries a different code hash from this edition, and contains an actual within-dataset contrast absent from the manuscript. If 189/200 is a distinct study, supply its design, generator, code revision and replicates. Publish the paired result and interval widths separately, including the Monte Carlo uncertainty. There are design and output files for the person studies in the edition, but no corresponding complete study generator in that directory. Pin one executable production revision. Re-evaluate both fitters on identical stochastic samples and analytic truths. Include gate passage, interval coverage overall and conditional on passage, bias, width and RMSE, with boundary, sparse, wave and misspecification settings. See paper p. 19, lines 630–663, and `validation/person_bootstrap/{design.json,summary.json,paired.json,replicates.csv}`.

The earlier Poisson pilot remains a separate, invalid calibration benchmark for several scenarios. The “truth” is an estimate from one reference draw and most gamma branches generate deterministic fractional deaths, so changing the outer seed need not change the curve. That explains several 100% coverage entries. The paper's blank pilot table still asks for a full run although the archive contains 500 × 200 results. Running the same design longer cannot fix the target or event generation. Also identify which real-data figures use a person bootstrap and which workbook intervals are analytic. See `test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py:76–125,201–232`, `generate_sim_grid.py:372–392,865–870`, `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage.csv`, and paper Table 12 (label `tbl:bootstrap_coverage`).

## 6. Other evidence still attributed to the wrong procedure

- **Cox comparison.** The paper now correctly identifies the treatment HR at fixed frailty as one. The marginal B-to-A hazard ratio in its design is 1/(1+θB(t)), and changes genuinely over time. The comparison code still passes known θ directly from the simulator into KCOR while §2.11.1 says θ was estimated under identical information constraints. One run per θ with oracle inversion cannot estimate practical bias. Label the oracle result, fit both methods using only the available observations over repeated samples, benchmark Cox/RMST against their own targets, and either fit or remove the interpolated shared-frailty curve. The KCOR figure caption still advertises bootstrap bands absent from its plotting script. See paper lines 690–737 and `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:209–235,278–297`.
- **Synthetic recovery and positive controls.** The recovery study fixes k to the noiseless initial hazard in a recurrence it later inverts. The standalone positive-control generator multiplies the already-depleted marginal hazard during the pulse. An individual baseline intervention changes later survivor composition too. Generate deaths from specified individual hazards or their exact marginal survival, use independent stochastic draws, include effects overlapping the fit and anchor, and compare the whole cumulative trajectory against independently derived truth. See `test/theta0/theta0_nwave.py:92–105,179–194`, `test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py:111–129` and SI S3–S4.
- **Quiet-window evidence.** The empirical scan fits log(1+θkt)/θ, the constant-baseline model, and computes its summary pass rate after filtering to passing rows. Every reported pass rate must then be one. Re-run the scan with the specified Gompertz fitter, use all windows for the pass-rate denominator, and show profiles and failures by arm. See SI S5.4 and `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py:77–113,242–271`.
- **Contamination threshold.** At zero contamination, the two relevant simulations already yield mean anchored KCOR 1.063 and 1.034, with 70% and 75% of draws above one. At the advertised “reversal threshold,” the mean is 1.062 and 65% are above one. A positive decaying additive hazard first applied after the anchor also differs from the motivating early deferral/suppression mechanism. Use paired draws, realistic time profiles in either arm, an explicit material-effect margin and an uncertainty-based decision rule. Restrict claims to the tested scenarios. See paper §3.3.2, `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv:2–10` and `run_contamination_test.py:259–278` in that test directory.

**Publication priority.** Make the scientific target, the executable fitter and its gate agree. Then rebuild the evidence for the core method, report the observed risks alongside the model-dependent contrast, and evaluate the optional wave method separately. The corrected weekly inverse and conditional post-wave equation should be retained.

I reran the Table 8 original-sample calculations with both fitters, checked a wave-off sensitivity, and tested the wave equation against independent integration. I did not regenerate all 999 empirical bootstrap draws or the national registry pipeline. All review calculations used the revised edition read-only.
