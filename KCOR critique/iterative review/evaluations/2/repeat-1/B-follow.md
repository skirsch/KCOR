# Follow-up on baseline objections

**Both requested wording objections still apply.** Locations below refer to the revised edition.

1. **One anchor week versus a window mean — yes.** The equation in `paper.md:164–169` divides by KCOR at a single time. Line 624 instead says it divides by the mean over a short window. Production takes one cumulative-ratio value at index `skip + normalization_weeks − 1` (default raw index 5). Table 8 uses that same single index. The contamination test uses `skip + 4` (default index 6). These give three distinct prescriptions. My earlier phrase “anchored twice” could obscure the calculation: Table 8 forms one ratio of two cohort-specific growth factors, algebraically equivalent to dividing the raw ratio by its value at one anchor. Specify the anchor and indexing explicitly and reuse one function. See `code/KCOR.py:815,4421–4429`, `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:95–97` and `test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py:210–216`.

2. **Whether frailty is fitted separately by stratum — yes.** Paper line 636 explicitly includes age bands among strata used solely for aggregation and says frailty is never estimated separately by stratum. Production groups by year of birth and dose within enrollment, and by resample ID when applicable. Table 8 calls the fitter for each age-label × dose cell. SI lines 398–413 describe age-band-by-dose fits and an independent all-ages fit. The Methods should name the fitting units, distinguish any pooling of sex or other variables within those units, and explain how fitted age-specific contrasts are combined. See `code/KCOR.py:6954–6957` and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:81–88`.

3. **Linearity after inversion and cancellation of baseline-slope error — yes.** The specified baseline cumulative hazard is proportional to exp(γt)−1. The paper still evaluates linearity against t and treats remaining curvature as evidence of contamination. A short window may make the approximation reasonable even for a poorly identified fit. Use integrated Gompertz time or direct interval-probability residuals and show the range of γ over which the *ratio* stays stable. A common γ error need not cancel between cohorts with different hazards. See `paper.md:326–334,458` and `supplement.md:299,362`.

4. **Mean frailty versus frailty variance — yes.** Paper line 47 describes a cohort being healthier on average as a difference in θ. With mean-one frailty specified separately in each cohort (lines 275–282), a multiplicative difference in average baseline risk can reside entirely in k with identical θ. Inversion preserves that level difference; anchoring can remove it regardless of cause. The revision fixes the observed-versus-individual scaling formula at line 346 but leaves this separate interpretation problem.

5. **Positive-control figure provenance and interval captions — partly.** The SI has removed the unsupported bootstrap-band claims from the positive and synthetic negative control captions (lines 143 and 195). The positive-control plotting code still catches any processed-data error and can graph the raw cumulative-hazard ratio as KCOR. That establishes a possible silent substitution; the branch taken for a particular archived PNG needs a build record. Require the fitted output for publication. The separate Cox-grid KCOR caption still promises bands (`paper.md:735`). See `test/positive_control/code/plot_positive_control.py:83–137,262–277`.

6. **Whether the revised bootstrap validates uncertainty in θ itself — still open.** The new person-bootstrap studies supply coverage evidence for the week-52 **KCOR ratio**. The previously cited 35.4% and 18.8% θ coverage figures still occur in the older gamma-null Poisson pilot (`test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage_theta.csv:2`). They belong to that different experiment. The new outputs do not establish θ-interval calibration under the person bootstrap. Report parameter coverage separately if claiming it. Likewise, ordinary workbook per-age intervals still use analytic post-anchor increments (`code/KCOR.py:4445–4472`), whereas the new Table 8 joint-fit intervals use person resampling. Identify the provenance of each remaining figure band.

7. **The old moving-block and Euler objections — resolved for the newly specified operations.** Revised Methods lines 638–657 describe drawing whole people. The Table 8 resampler reconstructs overlapping groups from disjoint event-history cells. Revised Step 2 and production lines 1453–1457 now use the exact weekly cumulative inverse. Neither old objection should be charged against these new operations. The mismatch of weekly predictions and gap boundary indexing, the legacy Poisson pilot, and the distinction between a raw marginal RMST target and a frailty-neutral target remain as detailed in the revised review.
