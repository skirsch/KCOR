# Review of KCOR

**Major revision.** The gamma-frailty inversion is correct for a unit-mean gamma frailty multiplying a common within-cohort baseline hazard. A ratio of the resulting cohort-specific baseline cumulative hazards is a legitimate descriptive quantity under that model. The evidence in this edition does not establish that the fitted transformation separates depletion from other sources of curvature in the Czech data. Several central validation results were produced using different estimators, including one supplied with the true frailty variance. I would retain the descriptive construction and rebuild the validation before interpreting a normalized registry contrast.

## 1. What identifies the frailty parameter

For any increasing observed cumulative hazard H and **any** θ ≥ 0, define Bθ = expm1(θH)/θ, taking B0 = H. Gamma mixing Bθ gives exactly the original H. A cohort of identical individuals whose common hazard is H′ and a heterogeneous gamma cohort with baseline Bθ′ can therefore produce identical deaths and risk sets in every week. All diagnostics computed from those counts, including held-out weeks of an identical trajectory, agree. For a concrete example, generate data under the manuscript's Gompertz–gamma model, then make the marginal hazard from that model the time-varying hazard of every member of a homogeneous cohort. KCOR will assign the same positive variance to both worlds. This directly refutes the promises that overlapping effects necessarily produce diagnostic failure or conservative attenuation. The manuscript recognizes attribution ambiguity in §4.1, then gives stronger assurances in §5.1–5.2 and SI S2. Remove the assurances. A failed check can reject an assumption. A passed check can quantify consistency with it. Report sensitivity to quiet-period baseline shape and to cohort-specific smooth deviations, using independent baseline information where available. See paper lines 297–319, 345–361, 940–950, 1023–1045, and supplement lines 24–70.

There is a positive parametric result worth stating precisely. With fixed known Gompertz slope γ and no unresolved wave inside the interval, the marginal hazard satisfies h′ = γh − θh². Thus θ = (γh − h′)/h² wherever h > 0 in the ideal continuous model. The paper's equivalence between non-zero cumulative-hazard curvature and positive frailty is false. θ = 0 with γ > 0 yields a curved cumulative hazard. Conversely, kθ = γ gives a constant marginal hazard and a linear cumulative hazard despite θ > 0. The same equation shows why a small error in γ matters at low h, and why estimating h′ from sparse weekly deaths is difficult. Report profile intervals, including one-sided limits near zero, and a joint sensitivity surface for γ and θ. Assess the ratio directly across that surface. Approximate linearity of the normalized cumulative hazard should be assessed against the fitted *Gompertz* trajectory on its correct time scale. See paper lines 175–179, 323–357.

The claimed invariance to multiplying **observed** hazards by a constant c also has an exact answer. The marginal curve for (k, θ), multiplied by c, is the curve for (ck, θ/c). A common c can cancel in the ratio of reconstructed baselines while still changing each estimated variance. Multiplying the **individual baseline** by c instead leaves the structural frailty variance unchanged and changes marginal depletion through the denominator. Distinguish those operations throughout §2.4.4 and the abstract. Allowing each cohort its own unit-mean frailty and k also leaves true between-cohort differences in mean baseline risk inside k. An unanchored ratio can depart from one under a treatment null, and anchoring can erase a genuine sustained proportional difference.

## 2. The estimator described and the estimator run are different

The paper jointly fits k and θ in the nearest quiet window, fixes k, and updates θ using the union of quiet windows. The production code sets k to the mean of hazard divided by the Gompertz growth factor in the first post-skip anchor weeks, fits only θ in the first quiet window, and includes **quiet or anchor** weeks in the pooled fit. The Czech configuration uses four anchor weeks. This matters when early mortality is transiently suppressed or those weeks lie outside the first quiet window. Some failure paths return θ = 0 with a successful numerical status and a finite uncorrected trajectory. Choose one estimator, state its units and masks explicitly, and have every figure, simulation and export call it. Propagate a failed scientific gate separately from numerical success, including to the ratio when either arm fails. See paper lines 371–386 and 414–430, `code/KCOR.py:1226–1238,1253–1275,1375–1394,1650–1676`, and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:34–39`.

There is also a discretization inconsistency. The weekly observed quantity is a log-survival increment u. Given the previous baseline cumulative hazard B, its exact inversion gives ΔB = (1 + θB) expm1(θu)/θ. The delta iteration uses (1 + θB)u, and the final reported trajectory uses the exact cumulative exponential. They diverge for positive θu. The gap-offset calculation reads the **start** of the last gap bin and applies the result after that bin, potentially missing its shock. Compute start/end cumulative arrays from the same exact inversion, integrate the Gompertz baseline over each bin, and fit interval probabilities using risk sets. A single large last-gap shock and refined time bins make simple regression checks. See paper lines 246–254, 388–409, 432–439 and `code/KCOR.py:1321–1336,1384–1390,1449–1467,1613–1629`.

## 3. The strongest validation examples do not test the claimed algorithm

- **Cox comparison.** The paper says information is identical and θ is estimated. The grid passes the simulator's true θ directly into KCOR, including θ = 0 for the control. This validates oracle inversion. It does not validate estimation. Moreover, with θA = 0 and θB > 0, the true marginal B-to-A hazard ratio is 1/(1 + θB B(t)), already below one for positive follow-up. The conditional no-treatment effect is compatible with a real marginal survival difference. Standard Cox and RMST estimates must be judged against their own targets. Refit the production estimator on the same observed counts over many new draws, retain an explicitly labeled oracle benchmark, and report bias against analytic target-specific truths. Remove the interpolated shared-frailty comparison or fit it. The KCOR grid is single-run per θ, and its plotting script lacks the bootstrap bands advertised in the caption. See paper lines 687–736, `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:209–235,278–297` and `plot_cox_bias_sim.py:67–82` in the same directory.
- **Recovery and positive controls.** The multiwave recovery program generates a noiseless recurrence and fixes k exactly at h(0). This tests an algebraic round trip. The standalone positive-control generator multiplies the already depleted marginal hazard and returns to the original hazard schedule after the pulse. An individual baseline effect instead changes subsequent survivor composition. Its plotting code also supports a raw cumulative-hazard-ratio fallback. Simulate independent people or binomial counts from the exact marginal survival under an individual-level effect, derive the cumulative target at each horizon, and require the production fit and interval path for published figures. Include persistent effects, overlap with the anchor and quiet windows, non-gamma mixtures, differential secular trends and truly absent frailty. See SI S3–S4, `test/theta0/theta0_nwave.py:92–105,179–194` and `test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py:111–129`, `plot_positive_control.py:83–137`.
- **Empirical negative control.** Relabeling age groups holds the exposure category fixed while deliberately changing baseline mortality. The normalized baseline ratio has no known unit target solely because the labels were reassigned. Anchoring a constant ratio forces it to one algebraically. Even the anchored results include 1.0756 [1.055, 1.097] and 1.0911 [1.070, 1.112], which deserve explanation. The table's cited source log identifies the fit as v6.0. Report unanchored and anchored curves alongside raw age-specific risks, define an acceptable drift margin in advance and show an actual random-label control within common strata. See paper lines 793–809, 1228–1237 and `test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log:2`.
- **Quiet-window evidence.** The SI says its Czech window scan used the revised estimator. The scan actually fits log(1 + θkt)/θ, a constant-baseline, single-window cumulative model. Its summary computes the pass rate after selecting only passing rows, so every reported rate is one. The Czech configuration also states that 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm. Resolve the provenance of this selection before calling the windows prespecified. Rebuild the scan with the published function, display all windows and counts, and reserve genuinely independent time periods or cohorts for validation. See supplement lines 292–310, `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py:77–113,242–271`, and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:21–32`.

## 4. The uncertainty and contamination claims need new experiments

The manuscript describes a moving-block bootstrap. The coverage script draws independent Poisson counts around each observed weekly death count and reconstructs survivors. The standard per-age export computes analytic Nelson–Aalen-style intervals from post-anchor increments. These quantify different things. Resampling calendar blocks is also questionable when age, waves and quiet masks have deterministic time positions. More fundamentally, the coverage benchmark is the *estimator applied to one reference draw*. Several scenario generators return fractional expected deaths deterministically. Running 500 seeds on the same expected curve cannot measure repeated-sample coverage. The committed 500 × 200 output reports identical median point estimate and reference “truth” and 100% KCOR coverage in four scenarios, 25.4% in the non-gamma case, and only 35.4%/18.8% coverage for the two θ values in the gamma-null case. These are evidence about the existing experiment, not calibration of the proposed procedure. See paper lines 627–662, 1266–1275, `test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py:76–125,201–232`, `generate_sim_grid.py:372–392,865–870`, `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage*.csv`, and `code/KCOR.py:4377–4404`.

Generate independent integer-count datasets from specified event-time laws; compute truth from the analytic baseline path independently of the estimator. For closed cohorts, a multinomial draw over event weeks plus end survivors resamples empirical individual event histories in aggregate, preserving calendar order. A sequential binomial bootstrap conditional on fitted interval probabilities is an alternative for a specified model. Repeat all fitting and anchoring. Report the gate pass rate, bias, RMSE, interval width, coverage both overall and conditional on passage, and Monte Carlo errors, including boundary cases. Label uncertainty conditional on fixed external inputs separately.

The contamination study does not establish a threshold for apparent harm. In its own zero-contamination cells, mean anchored KCOR is already 1.063 and 1.034, and 70% and 75% of estimates exceed one. The selected comorbidity cell at 66% of baseline gives 1.062 and 65%. Counting estimates above a null value is not a calibrated false-harm rate. The simulated “dynamic HVE” component adds a *positive* decaying hazard beginning after the anchor, whereas the motivating mechanism is early hazard suppression around enrollment. These are different perturbations. Use paired null simulations, realistic signs, timing and persistence in both arms, clinically meaningful margins and interval decisions. Show Monte Carlo uncertainty and give the range supported by the tested settings. Remove the extrapolation to adequacy for Czech cohorts. See paper lines 842–870, `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv:2–10` and `test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py:149–175,259–278`.

## 5. The optional wave correction needs a coherent survivor model

Under the proportional gamma model before a wave, initial Z with shape 1/θ and rate 1/θ has survivor distribution Gamma(1/θ, 1/θ + B). Hence E[Z^α | alive] = Γ(1/θ + α)/Γ(1/θ) × (1/θ + B)^(−α). At α = 1 the survivor mean is 1/(1 + θB). The implementation propagates θ/(1 + θB) into a new **unit-mean** gamma moment and returns one at α = 1. This changes the shape parameter and computes a different moment. During a wave with individual hazard Zb(t) + A(t)Z^α the survivor density has a further tilt by exp(−Z^α ∫A), which generally breaks gamma conjugacy. A cumulative excess divided by the factor at its endpoint also differs from the integral of excess increments divided by their contemporaneous factors. Clipping negative excess and taking a cumulative maximum alters the target again. A prespecified external effect scale addresses an amplitude ambiguity but cannot repair these identities. See paper lines 508–555, 562–598, supplement S2.1 and `code/KCOR.py:1028–1047,2558–2562,2591–2632`.

Retain the paper's empirical conclusion that α was weakly localized. The supplied Czech configuration actually sets `mode: forced_alpha` and both α fields to 1.18. Set the published primary configuration to off and verify the reproduced primary outputs. If a mechanistic wave correction is retained, derive it from the full survivor distribution, integrate weekly increments, and validate on individual-level data generated independently of the fitted moment function. Until then the forced-α run can only be an explicitly labeled sensitivity exercise. See paper lines 477, 892–912, 1072–1076 and `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:13–19`.

**Practical publication criterion.** A clean reproduction should build every main table and figure using one versioned specification from counts to output, label identity fallbacks, distinguish the primary ratio from change since anchor, and show which checks passed with quantitative thresholds. Specify whether the anchor is one week or a mean over weeks (paper lines 164–169 versus 621) and whether frailty is fit per age stratum (paper line 633 versus supplement lines 400–417). Report observed deaths, survival or risk differences alongside the constructed contrast. The defensible scientific claim is how much a chosen depletion model changes a particular cohort comparison, with the identifying assumptions and sampling uncertainty visible to the reader.

I reviewed the combined PDF, its Markdown sources, the cited code paths and committed outputs. I did not rerun the national registry pipeline or change the supplied edition.
