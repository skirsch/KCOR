# KCOR methodological review

**Major revision.** The core gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound when the starting frailty law and its variance are known, the individual hazard is proportional to frailty, and the cumulative hazard has the correct time origin. The exact weekly reconstruction in §2.5 and the post-wave conditional survival equation in §2.7.3 are useful. The evidence in this edition does not establish that the published pipeline reliably estimates the required parameters, detects its own failures, or improves inference about a treatment effect. Several results described as validation of the fitted procedure were generated by different procedures.

1. **The principal Cox comparison gives KCOR information unavailable to Cox.** The manuscript explicitly says that KCOR estimated frailty from the same data using §2.5 and had no simulator-only information (paper.md:690, 710). The script passes the generating variance directly to inversion for one arm and fixes it to the true zero for the other (test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:234–235, 278–284). Its CSV reproduces the numbers in the paper. The KCOR plotting function draws points and a line, although the caption promises 95% bootstrap bands (paper.md:735, test/sim_grid/code/plot_cox_bias_sim.py, plot_kcor_vs_theta). The marginal populations really do have different survival distributions in this experiment. A non-unit Cox summary or difference in RMST therefore does not establish bias for their respective population targets. Keep the known-variance calculation as an oracle benchmark, repeat with the production fitter blinded to truth, report Monte Carlo error and rejection rates, and compare estimates against the matched target for each method.

2. **There are materially different estimators behind the same name.** Paper §2.5 fits baseline rate and variance jointly in the nearest quiet window. The default code fixes the rate at the mean of the first few post-skip weekly rates and only then fits variance (code/KCOR.py:1226–1236, 1375–1382). It includes those early weeks in the final fit even if outside the quiet windows (1227, 1541). The Table 8 replay substitutes a joint fit by editing the function source at runtime (analysis/table8/compare.py:22–37, replay.py:16–23). This affects inference. In validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json, using the same 200 datasets, coverage was 160/200 for the fixed-rate fit and 194/200 for the joint fit. All original fits passed. The joint intervals were about three times as wide and point-estimate RMSE was larger in that design. Paper §2.9.1 already describes separate studies with 502/600 and 189/200 coverage, but the paired study isolates a comparison those studies cannot. Reconcile versions, implement named fit modes in the ordinary entry point, specify one primary method, and regenerate each result with auditable configuration and source revision. Match the weekly fitting objective to the weekly integrated hazard as well: the exact prediction is the difference of marginal cumulative hazards at consecutive boundaries. The objectives at paper.md:371–383, 414–425 currently use point hazards.

3. **The example used to validate the diagnostic rules would be withheld under the stated rules.** Czech wave-exponent localization fails its prespecified criteria (paper.md:895–919), yet Table 8 uses 1.18, and data/Czech/Czech.yaml:14–20 forces it and also populates the identified-value field. The table's own output records four insufficient-signal fits in the original 2022 enrollment, a flag in 998/999 draws for that enrollment and in 512/999 draws for 2021 (analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json). Such fits can produce a numerical answer by falling back to zero frailty. All six intervals containing one is weak evidence, especially an interval of 0.233–1.299. Different ages can have different calendar-time mortality trends even under identical vaccination state, and anchoring forces agreement at the anchor. This construction supplies no known unity target at subsequent horizons without an additional shape assumption. Report the primary Czech analysis with the wave correction inactive, show forced values only as sensitivities, and report each fallback, its pooling weight and its effect on the ratio. Distinguish an estimable zero from an uninformative fit. State in advance when the output is withheld and when a fallback becomes part of the estimator. Use an independent null construction or simulation with a known target and prespecified equivalence margins to assess calibration.

4. **The in-wave operator does not follow from the proposed hazard model.** With baseline exposure B and wave exposure W the model implies survival L(B,W) = E[exp(−ZB−Z^α W)]. Even before a wave, exponential tilting of the starting gamma preserves shape 1/θ and changes scale to θ/(1+θB). Code instead uses that new scale as the *variance of a new mean-one gamma* (code/KCOR.py:1028–1047, 2653–2655). At θ=1, B=1 and α=2 the true survivor second moment is 0.5, while the code returns 1.5. During a nonproportional wave the survivor law generally ceases to be gamma. Moreover, an instantaneous observed-hazard multiplier cannot simply divide an already inverted cumulative excess (paper.md:542–555, 568–599). For a small wave starting at B the derivative of the inverted cumulative hazard with respect to W is E[Z^α | alive]/E[Z | alive]. The code uses neither that derivative nor the finite-exposure analogue. Anchoring to the end of the first wave week also assigns that week's excess to baseline. The post-wave equation correctly conditions on the *supplied* exit exposures, so it carries errors in those exposures forward. With the quiet-window baseline and external scale specified, estimate nonnegative wave exposure increments against the full survival equation, from the boundary before the first wave bin, and propagate the tilted survivor distribution throughout. Validate the entire calculation against forward-generated individual hazards at high depletion, including α=1, one-week waves, strong waves, and the post-wave tail. Leave the module inactive where evidence cannot identify its inputs.

5. **A good fit cannot establish a biological depletion mechanism.** For any observed increasing cumulative hazard H and any proposed θ, the baseline [exp(θH)−1]/θ reproduces exactly the same survival. With known Gompertz slope γ, the restriction does supply parametric identification: writing h for the marginal instantaneous hazard gives h′/h = γ−θh, hence θ = (γ−h′/h)/h. This also exposes the error in the proposition's equivalence between nonzero curvature and θ>0 (paper.md:345–359). At θ=0 a growing Gompertz baseline has curvature; at θk=γ, h is constant and θ can be positive. Multiplying the *observed* hazard by c changes θ to θ/c, as §2.4.4 itself states. Multiplying the individual baseline has a different meaning. Common misspecification of γ need not cancel between arms whose hazards differ. Fix the proposition, distinguish identification under a chosen baseline from precision and causal attribution, and publish sensitivity to plausible age- and calendar-time trends. Diagnose residuals relative to the fitted Gompertz cumulative curve, which need not be linear in time. Independent baseline information, held-out calendar periods and controls also help distinguish plausible mechanisms. No diagnostic based only on identical observed trajectories can resolve exact observational equivalence.

6. **Several other validation artifacts do not implement the described experiment.** The robustness scan advertised as a check of the revised estimator fits log(1+θkt)/θ, a constant-baseline model, to cumulative observations (supplement.md:302–310, test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py:77–106, 222). The positive-control generator multiplies the *already mixed marginal hazard* only inside the effect window, then resumes the original marginal hazard, bypassing differential survivor selection (supplement.md:132–141, 209–213, test/positive_control/code/generate_positive_control.py:114–123). Compare against the analytic cumulative target 1+(r−1) times the fraction of cumulative baseline exposure inside the effect window, for a correctly simulated individual-level intervention with equal baselines. The contamination grid cannot bound Czech confounding. Even at zero added contamination, its two control batches give mean anchored ratios of 1.063 and 1.034, with 70% and 75% of 20 replicates exceeding one (test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv). Thus “first” crossing one at 66% of baseline is not a detection threshold, and crossing the true null is not sign reversal. Use paired uncontaminated replicates, prespecify a material deviation, report simulation uncertainty and avoid extending two chosen disturbance shapes into a bound on real-world bias.

7. **Separate conditional inference, target definition and validation of the reporting rule.** The pilot coverage script computes its “truth” by running the estimator on one random reference dataset (test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py:201–229). Even with infinitely many outer simulations that is calibration against a random reference estimate. For each data-generating process compute the target from its population hazards or independent numerical integration. Use person resampling or its exact multinomial equivalent on disjoint death-week/survivor cells, as Table 8 does; run the entire fitting and gating procedure on every draw. Report probability of producing an estimate, unconditional and accepted-case coverage, interval width, and behavior near parameter boundaries. Specify whether uncertainty conditions on fixed external scales and wave parameters or includes their estimation. Report absolute observed survival alongside KCOR, specify the landmark survivor population after the skip, and distinguish the raw ratio, the single-week anchored ratio and the reference-window-mean version (paper.md:164–169, 475–498, 624). A ratio of transformed cumulative hazards is a contrast on the cohort-specific mean-one frailty scale. It is not an observed mortality risk ratio, and anchoring can remove a sustained intervention effect as readily as a pre-existing level difference. Frozen enrollment measures baseline group membership; later crossover can bias the ratio in either direction when subsequent uptake depends on prognosis (paper.md:1037).

The most informative revision would demonstrate the same fitted estimator, including its abstention rule, on simulations with prespecified population targets, then publish the Czech results and their sensitivities from that exact entry point. The core transform can already be presented as a conditional descriptive analysis. Claims about reliable failure detection, comparative superiority or vaccination-attributable harm are not established by this edition.
