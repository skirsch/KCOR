# Targeted follow-up on the baseline objections

1. **One-week anchor versus a window mean — still applies.** The paper defines the anchored ratio as KCOR(t)/KCOR(t₀) at lines 164–169. Line 624 instead prescribes division by a mean over a subsequent reference window. The ordinary per-age path divides by one indexed value, `K_raw.iloc[t0_idx]`, substituting 1 if that value is unusable (`code/KCOR.py:4416–4430`). Table 8 also uses exactly one index per stratum, dividing the target cumulative hazard by the anchor cumulative hazard before pooling (`analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:95–97`). Document the single-week calculation, its index relative to the skip, and its fallback. Identify any figures that actually use a reference-window mean.

2. **Stratum-fit wording — still applies.** Paper line 636 says explicitly that frailty parameters are never fitted separately by stratum. SI lines 397–413 give separate estimates for each age-by-dose combination and describe a separate all-ages fit. The Table 8 replay allocates a 3 × 3 array and calls the fitter inside its age-by-dose loop (`analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:76–86`). Explain which dimensions define a fitted cohort for each analysis, and which results pool its estimates.

3. **Calendar-block resampling — resolved for the revised procedure. Ordinary workbook interval labels — still an issue.** Paper §2.9.1 now resamples whole enrollment records, and Table 8 resamples the corresponding disjoint person/event-week cells. The former concern about shuffling quiet periods and waves does not apply to that design. Ordinary per-age workbook `CI_lower` and `CI_upper` still use post-anchor Nelson–Aalen increments and analytic standard errors (`code/KCOR.py:4445–4476`), with analogous pooled calculations at lines 4775–4828. Identify the interval method beside each export and table. Section 2.9.1 step 4 does now say α, the external intervention-effect scale and the wave window are held fixed in the bootstrap. The resulting intervals are conditional on those choices.

4. **Boundary uncertainty for θ and Gompertz slope sensitivity — still applies.** SI S4.1 lists a fixed slope, and S6 lists near-zero estimates without one-sided limits or a joint sensitivity surface for the estimated ratio. The archived legacy gamma-null coverage for θ is 35.4% and 18.8% in the two arms (`test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage_theta.csv`, first data row). The new study’s published summary and replicate interval endpoints concern KCOR. They do not establish coverage for θ. Report boundary-aware uncertainty for θ, sensitivity of KCOR to the assumed slope, and calibration of each interval against the corresponding known target.

5. **Historical negative controls and raw risks — partly addressed.** Table 8 retains both old 2022 estimates with intervals above one, identifies them as historical and gives new estimates and intervals. The historical log/version discrepancy and revised diagnostic failures remain as described in the open review. SI S6 already gives raw cumulative hazards by age for the real 2021_24 dose-0 versus dose-2 comparison through week 2024-16 (lines 423–437). These are useful context. The age-remapped Table 8 comparison has different groups and a December 2022 reporting horizon. Give its own event counts and observed risks next to the anchored and unanchored results.

6. **Numerical recovery versus sampling validation — partly addressed.** The new person-bootstrap design explicitly includes absent frailty, post-quiet individual-effect scenarios, a sparse setting and quiet-window contamination. Those are additions to the evidence. The older Cox grid still simulates one dataset for each frailty value (`generate_cox_bias_sim.py:278–297`), its KCOR curve uses supplied θ, and the shared-frailty curve is calculated as 1 + κ(Cox HR − 1) (`plot_estimator_bias_vs_theta.py:46–54`). The new person-bootstrap experiment does not change what these separate figures demonstrate.

7. **Uniform decision thresholds and fail status — partly addressed.** The new simulation design specifies an acceptable coverage range of 0.92–0.98 and a minimum of 300 accepted repetitions. The baseline objection about operational checks for the empirical estimate remains. SI S2 uses qualitative terms such as “plausible” and “stable,” and SI S6 calls major sensitivity or sign reversals concerning. Table 8 reports ratios for an enrollment with four original insufficient-signal fits. State each analysis-level acceptance rule and its outcome for each published contrast.
