# Independent review of KCOR

The gamma inversion is correct under the specified mean-one gamma frailty model. The fixed-slope Gompertz argument identifies a model parameter under proportional survivor history. Mortality counts by themselves cannot establish that the fitted curvature came from frailty. I would retain KCOR as a descriptive reference-frailty cumulative-hazard contrast. I found the following issues in this edition.

1. **Ordinary workbook analyses can use the wrong enrollment and reference weeks.** The date parser uses the first Monday in January as ISO week 1. Ordinary workbook filtering repeats the calculation, then numbers each group’s observed rows consecutively. The reference selector trusts those row numbers. In a 20-week test of `process_workbook`, a complete `2025_24` sheet lost the June 9 enrollment week and anchored on July 21 instead of July 14. In `2021_24`, deleting the July 19 reference row from both dose groups produced a July 26 anchor labeled `ok`. The complete 2021 control anchored correctly. The first bug affects ordinary inputs even with complete histories. The second lets a missing observation shift the skip, fitted event times and reported reference. Use `date.fromisocalendar(year, week, 1)` consistently, derive elapsed weeks from dates, and carry missing cumulative history as unavailable unless the counts reconstruct it. Add these two workbook regressions. The complete Czech 2021–22 sheets use years for which the first-Monday calculation agrees with ISO. See `code/KCOR.py:4234–4244, 6628–6636, 6727–6730, 4246–4263` and `documentation/preprint/paper.md:554`.

2. **The Czech application claims a diagnostic pass that the evidence does not establish.** The combined PDF and supplement say all prespecified criteria were satisfied, then state that window-omission stability was unevaluated. The criteria explicitly require assessing stability. I used the primary module-off fitter on the supplied 2021-24 workbook, reproducing all seven endpoint estimates, then omitted each of the four configured windows in turn, retaining the four early anchor rows as production does. At ages 40–49 the dose-2 variance ranged from 24.89 to 83.70 versus 71.83 jointly. Anchored KCOR ranged from 1.336 to 1.555 versus 1.507 jointly. The all-ages endpoint was steadier, 1.115–1.134 versus 1.136. A separate, more severe reduction to the *first window alone* moves the ages 80–89 contrast from 1.204 to 0.099 in the archived results. This last comparison does not establish failure of a single-window omission criterion. The youngest dose-2 group also reached the upper bound in 287 of 999 archived draws. These draws applied zero frailty. The published interval therefore includes sampling variability of that fallback. Replace the blanket pass with a group-by-group status, report window sensitivity and the rule for material change, and reserve substantive interpretation for groups whose criteria are established. Keep numerical convergence, residual screening and parameter resolution separate. See `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:49, 62–70, 341, 366–380, 400–423`, `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:29–33`, `analysis/s6/all-age-precision.py:20–39`, `analysis/s6/all-age-precision.json:891–902`, and `code/KCOR.py:1665–1685`.

3. **Identification also assumes quiet early fitting anchors.** The manuscript says that only designated calendar quiet weeks identify frailty. The actual objective includes the first four post-skip weeks even when they lie outside all designated windows. This is consequential for an enrollment mechanism with early selection. I generated 100 noiseless weeks with weekly Gompertz rate 0.002, slope 0.085/52, theta 4 and designated quiet weeks 20–79. The fitter recovered 4. Multiplying the individual baseline by 1.25 in weeks 0–3 alone, propagating survival exactly thereafter, produced 5.503 with numerical status `ok`. The 60 designated quiet weeks still follow the original baseline, and the extra pre-entry exposure is proportional. The check isolates what happens when **only the additional early fitting assumption** fails. State that assumption wherever the fitting sample is defined, report the anchor dates and their overlap with quiet windows, and show a sensitivity using early rows for initialization only in applications where their stability is doubtful. See `documentation/preprint/paper.md:340–354, 373–385` and `code/KCOR.py:1245–1249, 1540–1555`.

4. **The empirical eligibility rule limits the population comparison.** For 2021-24 the eligibility audit records 363,033 otherwise eligible people excluded solely because of a disqualifying product recorded during the enrollment week or later. Of them, 354,604 had no earlier recorded dose. Future product receipt requires survival to receipt. It can therefore change the makeup of the enrollment groups before the hazards are ever fitted. The manuscript discloses this and correctly says the sign and size of its effect are unknown. If the Czech example is to support conclusions about cohorts defined at enrollment, compare the published result with one using only product history available at enrollment. Otherwise retain the narrower retrospective population description. See `analysis/eligibility/counts.json:12–17`, `documentation/preprint/paper.md:745–749` and `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:349`.

**Uncertainty and verification.** The two module-off coverage studies in the text predate the current fitting equations. The reported S8 nonlinear experiment covered 90 of 100 independently sampled datasets. Neither result establishes 95% calibration of the current ordinary module-off workbook in sparse or boundary-heavy groups. Keep the percentile bands labeled as working-model variance propagation until coverage of that configuration is measured. I checked the combined PDF, source, frozen summary/draw files and targeted point fits. I did not rerun full bootstraps. See `documentation/preprint/paper.md:592–595`, `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457–459` and `analysis/survivor_validation/main-full-results.json:34–40`.
