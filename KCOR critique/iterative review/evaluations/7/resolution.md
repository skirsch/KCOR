# Round 7 targeted resolution review of m128

**Original question.** Please examine KCOR closely. What is wrong, if anything, and how would you fix it?

The proportional gamma inversion and the fixed-slope identification result survive this review within the stated model. The main remaining correction concerns which Czech population the paper describes. The age-shift figures and Table 8 use a September 2025 extract that predates the full-history product restriction. The current Czech application in SI S6 uses the restricted extract. Both sets of published numbers can be reproduced on their respective inputs. Applying the restriction's description to both changes the meaning of the age-shift example.

Eleven round-6 findings have their specific correction supported in m128. For 6.3, the numerical tables and scan are corrected, but the discussion still claims Czech diagnostic validation beyond the checks shown. For 6.13, the revised instantaneous identity and weekly qualification are sound, but the conclusion and one caption still give historical alpha results the appearance of current results. Weekly alpha localization remains deferred. For 6.14, the intended restricted population and exclusion counts are supported for the current application, but the description also reaches the older control. The effect of the product rule alone remains unknown.

I used the frozen m128 manuscript, supplement, code and records, the round-6 evaluations of m117, and the evidence for 6.1–6.14. I tested the production estimator and replayed selected calculations with temporary outputs, inspected earlier Git objects for provenance, and recomputed percentiles from saved draws. I did not inspect the round-7 blind reports, run a new 999-draw experiment or change either candidate.

## Czech populations and the age-shift control

Section 3.1.2 introduces the retrospective Pfizer/Moderna restriction within the description of the age-shift control. The figure captions identify the data as Czech registry controls. The restriction is accurate for the current converter input used in S6.1.2. It is historically inapplicable to the archived input used by both age-shift figures and Table 8.

* The frozen `analysis/table8/input.xlsx` hashes to Git blob `f2dbb3d7a39d47a06821048de042285df46785ba`, present in commit `bb2d6824` on 10 September 2025. Its contemporary source is `data/Czech/KCOR_CMR.xlsx`, blob `38e9b13ef413bed60356fe5c286575c6be03478f`. I applied `test/negative_control/code/generate_negative_control.py::build_negative_control_sheet` to that source for the dose-2, unvaccinated and all-status groups. Grouping by pseudo birth year, pseudo dose and ISO week reproduced **all 2,169 weekly rows in each of the two archived sheets**. The maximum discrepancies in opening counts and deaths were both zero. The converter introduced the full-history product filter in `f58d8a8f` on 23 November 2025.
* On the common 2021-24 opening date, the all-status pseudo-cohorts have the following numbers. They use pairs of adjacent five-year birth bands. For example, the 1930–39 group combines the 1930 and 1935 source labels.

| Birth years | Archived input for figures and Table 8 | Current CMR with S6 eligibility |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1930–39 | 310,466 | 258,041 |
| 1940–49 | 935,607 | 733,339 |
| 1950–59 | 1,265,854 | 1,088,104 |

I also applied the published age remapping to the current CMR, restricted follow-up to the same 14 June 2021 through 7 October 2024 dates, reconstructed the disjoint histories through `bootstrap_reporting.histories(..., "table8")`, and ran `analysis/age_shift/replay.py::estimate(0)` with the module off and the same anchor. The 10- and 20-year points were **1.008972 and 1.038658**, compared with **1.003561 and 1.027059** on the archived input. All three fits succeeded in each comparison. The older group flagged the exploratory residual screen on each input. This calculation compares two entire versions of the extract. It does not isolate the effect of changing one product-eligibility rule, and I did not compute replacement intervals.

**Fix.** Identify the September control extract and its population in §3.1.2, both figure captions and Table 8. Associate the November product restriction and its audit with the applications that actually use it. Keeping the historical control is a defensible reproducibility choice. Replacing it with a current-population analysis would be a separate scientific decision. The older converter history also contains later risk-set revisions. The point comparison provides no basis for attributing the entire difference to manufacturer eligibility.

## What the Czech diagnostic checks establish

Section 5.2 says the absence of instability, dose reversals, age incoherence or failure to converge in the Czech 2021-24 comparison provides “stronger validation than goodness-of-fit alone”. SI S6.1.2 reports actual numerical statuses and residual flags and explicitly says that window-omission stability was not evaluated there. The count flags alone establish neither rejection nor validity.

I performed that bounded point-only check for the same seven S6 groups, dose pair, dates and reference. I used `analysis/s6/all-age-precision.py::estimate`, held its fitting anchor fixed, and in turn omitted each configured interval returned by `get_theta_estimation_windows()`. All seven original point estimates matched the archived all-window values, and every omitted-window fit returned `ok`. For ages 40–49 the full-fit dose-2 variance was 71.83; dropping individual windows gave **24.89–83.70**. The anchored KCOR ranged from **1.3362 to 1.5551** versus 1.5075 with all four windows. The aggregate was considerably steadier, with **1.1153–1.1341** versus 1.1356. The omissions cover 2023-19–40, 2022-22–25, 2021-26–36 and 2024-11–14. No universal instability threshold was specified. These data support reporting sensitivity by group and retaining the existing poor-resolution qualification. They do not support a blanket pass or establish failure of the entire application.

The same distinction matters for §4 (around manuscript line 894) and SI S2 criterion I7, which state that results are not reported when required diagnostics fail. The ordinary workbook intentionally exports flagged fits and labels affected intervals. S6 even publishes the poorly resolved youngest cohort's descriptive KCOR. Specify which diagnostics actually suppress substantive interpretation, which flag an output retained for audit or illustration, and which checks were evaluated in each reported application. This is a continuation of the 5.11/6.3 reporting issue. A new automatic rejection rule is unnecessary to correct the wording.

## Older unresolved issues

* **Baseline and frailty assumptions (1.1).** For each admissible theta, the same observed cumulative trajectory H can be represented by baseline expm1(theta H)/theta, with continuous limit H at theta zero. Fixing a Gompertz slope and a restricted baseline family makes the model-based identification proposition meaningful. Observed hazards alone cannot certify that family or rule out contamination that mimics it. The paper discloses this. The Czech diagnostic-validation wording identified above is stronger than the evidence.
* **Information and fallback (2.7, 4.9, 5.11).** The S6 youngest dose-2 raw interval spans essentially the entire implemented [0,100] range. A boundary-piled percentile interval in another age group is not calibrated evidence of absence of frailty. The policy of retaining a theta-zero fallback in designated fits persists, as do secondary analytical intervals. I reran the earlier numerical-prefix regression: the hazard for fraction 0.9999 was 9.21034037, theta 90 gave the same early inverse 0.0162178123 when follow-up was extended, exponent 711 remained representable and the later unrepresentable value was unavailable. This supports the numerical fixes, while leaving the fallback policy's operating characteristics open.
* **Coverage and control target (1.3, 1.4, 4.6).** Recomputing 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles from draws 1–999 of each archived Table 8 array reproduced both JSON files exactly. All twelve intervals include 1 and stored flags sum to zero. Selected replays only check parts of the new numerical path. The age-shift target requires proportional normalized trajectories, with no known exact empirical null. SI S8 gives 90/100 coverage in one sampled nonlinear design and 48/50 with mean 1.04019 at truth 1 in its stress design. Those are conditions of those studies. They do not establish general coverage of the current workbook procedure or equivalence in the control.
* **Wave history and attribution (3.2, 4.3).** The supplied-alpha integral now evaluates the intended law with greatly improved numerical fidelity. Unmarked earlier nonlinear exposure can still change the entering survivor distribution. The deficit-week rule still holds the wave exposure fixed and assigns the increment to baseline when the planned baseline would exceed observed loss. A shared accurate integral cannot identify the split from a single observed weekly survival ratio. The paper discloses the assumption and the possibility of finite-sample attribution error. I did not repeat the earlier sampling-bias experiments.
* **Alpha localization (4.7, 5.10).** Current data and objectives continue to yield grid-boundary optima. External scale, a compatible measure of the instantaneous component or an explicit integrated-bin model, and validation under the corresponding law remain scientific requirements for a stronger claim. The Czech primary analysis keeps the nonlinear module inactive. Reporting 1.18 as an illustrative supplied value is supported.

The immediate fixes are to label the two Czech inputs where results are shown, narrow the claim about diagnostic validation, and distinguish the historical alpha diagnostics from the current results in the summary and caption. The estimator's mathematical assumptions, the optional wave allocation, and the intentional choice of a retrospective study population should stay explicit. The remaining scientific questions about coverage, weekly alpha identification and alternative eligibility require different evidence.
