# Fresh independent review — m184

## Overall assessment

The core estimator is mathematically coherent for its stated, restrictive working model. Gamma frailty inversion is the exact Laplace-transform identity for mean-one gamma frailty and multiplicative individual hazards; the fixed-slope Gompertz model supplies the structure needed to identify frailty variance from hazard shape. The method does not recover an equal-baseline counterfactual when the mean hazards of enrollment cohorts differ, and anchoring changes the estimand. Those are explicitly disclosed design choices, not defects to be fixed. A constrained nonnegative excess-exposure gap model, a fixed Gompertz slope, retrospective quiet windows, and the optional externally calibrated NPH extension are also explicit assumptions. I would not redesign these on the basis of their limitations alone.

## Substantive implementation/reporting defect — MC summary conceals failed identification

`code/KCOR.py:9883–10010`, especially the finite-result branch and interval output at approximately 9970–10010, prints any finite KCOR as `ok`, counts it as a valid bootstrap draw, and publishes percentile/basic 95% intervals without a fit-warning label. A cohort can have finite fallback-normalized output and `theta0_status_num/den=NOT_IDENTIFIED` while `normalization_status=ok`. This is intentional for audit in the main estimator, but not evidence of identification. Ordinary bootstrap reporting separately checks the fit statuses (`code/bootstrap_reporting.py:181–198`), counts flagged draws, and labels intervals involving flagged fits. Paper §§2.1.2, 2.5 and 2.9.1 explicitly restrict these numbers to descriptive audit.

**Verified actual written-workbook path**, no bootstrap run: using the candidate's `test/test_round19_mc_reader.py` 20-week `case(i,20)` inputs with 3 ages and two doses, concatenate iterations 0–4, assign `MCPlannedDraws=4`, and call its `run(f, write=True,low=1940,high=1960)`. At the May 15, 2023 reporting endpoint, all five All Ages rows had `theta0_status_num=NOT_IDENTIFIED`, `theta0_status_den=NOT_IDENTIFIED`, `normalization_status=ok`, and KCOR 1.456776. The MC summary printed all five statuses `ok`, `Bootstrap draws: 4 valid / 4 required`, and both `Percentile 95% CI: [1.4568, 1.4568]` and `Basic bootstrap 95% CI: [1.4568, 1.4568]` with no flagged-fit qualification. The identical draws are deliberate for this regression probe, not a claimed calibrated sampling experiment. The summary logic, not the identical values, is wrong. There was no small-draw disclaimer because its current threshold is three.

**Minimal fix:** retain numeric fallback draws without conditioning the bootstrap on successful fits, but apply the same fit-status predicate as ordinary `bootstrap_reporting.flagged`. Print separate numeric-completion and fit-flag counts, explicitly identify flagged original/draws, and label intervals involving any such fits as audit-only / not evidence of identification, while preserving interval arithmetic and the missing-draw rules. Include the actual five-iteration regression and a well-identified counterpart. If the MC path also exposes residual or gap-screen results, report their flags and unavailable status separately as ordinary reporting does. No change to the estimator or recalculation of existing empirical tables is necessary unless a table used this misleading MC summary.

## Minor hardening / remaining limits

- MC per-age fitting parses the supplied `ISOweekDied` string independently of validated `DateDied` (`KCOR.py:6743–6781`), while ordinary fitting derives the ISO date from the calendar. For valid converter inputs the fields agree. External MC input should derive the quiet mask from the validated calendar (or explicitly verify equality); contradictory calendar labels can otherwise change which hazards are fit. This is input-validation hardening, not an identified error in the paper's input data.
- The registry negative controls' widespread residual flags, outcome-informed historical quiet-window selection and uncertain chronological provenance, and the prospective-vs-retrospective distinction mean they cannot substantiate a generally valid empirical normalization. The manuscript explicitly acknowledges these, retains them for audit, and does not claim equivalence from intervals crossing one. This is a limitation of the available evidence, not an undisclosed estimator error. The historical model-conditional coverage is also openly limited and should not be taken to guarantee nominal coverage for current fitter results.
- The present paper is descriptive/model-conditional. Do not infer causal effects or general robustness from good fits; a non-gamma selection process and a time-varying baseline can share the same observed curve. The manuscript expressly states this and provides stress tests and diagnostic restrictions.

**Conclusion:** fix the MC reporting discrepancy; otherwise I did not establish a defect requiring a substantive revision to the core method. The review was restricted to the frozen candidate, and did not rerun a full person bootstrap.
