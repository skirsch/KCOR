**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s core gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound. Given a cohort’s frailty variance and the assumed multiplicative model, it recovers the cumulative hazard at reference frailty \(Z=1\). The main scientific difficulty is estimating that variance from the same mortality curve and then deciding how much of the curve to attribute to selection. The manuscript recognizes this limitation in places, but its diagnostic and validation claims go further than its evidence. There is also a specific mathematical error in the optional epidemic-wave correction. I reviewed the combined PDF, manuscript sources, and figures in `documentation/preprint` read-only. The numerical results below are those reported in this edition, rather than independent registry reruns. ### 1. The identification proposition gives the wrong criterion

Let \(h=H'_{\rm obs}\). Under the paper’s quiet-window Gompertz–gamma model, direct differentiation gives
\[
h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2,\qquad
\theta=\frac{\gamma h-h'}{h^2}.
\]
With known \(\gamma\) and an exact positive hazard trajectory, this explains precisely what identifies \(\theta\). It also gives two counterexamples to the paper’s claim that nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature is equivalent to positive frailty. If \(\theta=0\) and \(\gamma>0\), the Gompertz cumulative hazard is curved. If \(\theta=\gamma/k>0\), the observed hazard is exactly \(k\) and the observed cumulative hazard is perfectly straight. A straight line can therefore be evidence for *positive* frailty under the stipulated model. The corresponding diagnostic should compare the inverted curve with the fitted **Gompertz cumulative hazard**. Straightness in ordinary time is only a short-window approximation. [Model and proposition, PDF pp. 10–12](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323) · [Linearity diagnostic](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:458)

The distinction matters even with unlimited data. For *any* observed cumulative hazard \(H\) and any proposed \(\theta>0\), the baseline \((e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) reproduces exactly the same survival curve. At \(\theta=0\), the baseline \(H\) does too. The fixed baseline shape supplies the identifying information. Residuals and nearby quiet windows cannot distinguish two mechanisms that generate the same curve. The manuscript actually states this limitation well in §5.2. I would use that wording throughout, replace the proposition with the derivation above, and report how the **final contrast** changes across plausible baseline slopes and shapes. I would also correct the abstract’s scale-invariance claim. As §2.4.4 correctly observes, multiplying the *observed* hazard by \(c\) changes the fitted variance to \(\theta/c\). [Inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:304) · [Scaling identity](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Quiet-window limitation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1041)

### 2. The in-wave correction fails even under its own model

The manuscript defines \(F=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{alive}]\) as a multiplier for **observed instantaneous excess hazard**. It then divides **cumulative excess after nonlinear gamma inversion** by the current \(F\). Those operations have different scales. An immediate check is the limit \(\alpha\to1\). At \(\alpha=1\), ordinary inversion already recovers baseline plus wave exposure exactly, as this edition correctly states. But the adjacent formula divides positive excess by \(E[Z\mid\text{alive}]\), which is below one after depletion. Thus an arbitrarily small change in the exponent can produce a finite jump in the corrected answer. The cumulative-maximum repair enforces monotonicity but cannot recover the missing identity. [Definition of \(F\), PDF p. 16](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:536) · [Two correction branches, pp. 16–17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**The paper already contains the basis for a fix.** Its post-wave conditional-survival equation is correct given the exit exposures \(B_e,W_e\). Use the same survival law *during* the wave:
\[
S(t)=E\!\left[\exp\{-ZB(t)-Z^\alpha W(t)\}\right].
\]
Given a justified baseline path \(B(t)\) and specified \(\alpha\), estimate nonnegative weekly wave increments from the weekly deaths and risk sets, carry that survivor distribution forward, and report \(B(t)+W(t)\) for \(Z=1\). This preserves continuity at \(\alpha=1\) and uses the same model on both sides of wave exit. One all-cause survival curve cannot determine two unrestricted exposure paths, so the baseline restriction and its sensitivity must be explicit. For the Czech example, the paper declares \(\alpha\) unidentified yet fixes it at 1.18 for the revised negative-control table. Report the module-off result as primary and label 1.18 as a fixed-input sensitivity analysis. All 600 original fits in the second study passed the diagnostics. Conditioning on a diagnostic pass therefore offers no protection against the undercoverage observed there. The first result is encouraging for its study design. The two designs differed, so their difference cannot establish that changing the fitter caused the improvement. Both omitted wave adjustment. The blank weekly-Poisson pilot table is a third exercise. The person bootstrap described in this edition does preserve people’s subgroup and death-week histories and rebuild risk sets. ### 4. Anchoring, skipping and age pooling change the scientific question

If the two reference-frailty cumulative hazards differ by a constant factor \(r\), primary KCOR equals \(r\) throughout follow-up and anchored KCOR equals **one throughout**, for *any* \(r\). A flat anchored age-shift control therefore tests for drift. It cannot validate recovery of the primary contrast’s level or preservation of a sustained effect. The paper also gives two anchor definitions, division by the value at \(t_0\) and division by a short-window mean. Skipping early weeks further makes the comparison conditional on surviving to the new origin and omits early deaths from the target. [Point anchor, PDF p. 7](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [Window anchor, p. 17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:624) · [Skip rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:479) · [Age-shift control design](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:197)

Age pooling has a visible consequence. The raw dose-0/dose-2 cumulative-hazard ratio is **above one in every displayed age band** and **0.3159 in the full population**. The authors flag that the populations differ. This makes the choice of target population consequential. Methods say frailty is never fitted separately by stratum, while the supplement reports a fit for each age band and an independent all-ages fit. Inversion is nonlinear, so pooling first and inverting first can give different answers. I would specify the fitting unit, age weights, order of operations, anchor and time origin for each result. Show observed mortality from enrollment, unanchored KCOR, and anchored drift side by side. ### 5. The fit and validation should be scored against what each quantity estimates

- **Fit weekly deaths on their actual scale.** Step 2’s weekly inversion is exact. The seed and pooled objectives, however, compare an integrated weekly hazard with an instantaneous model hazard evaluated at one time point. Use the interval probability \(1-S(t+1)/S(t)\) in a grouped binomial likelihood. This also gives weeks with different risk-set sizes their appropriate precision. Assess parameter uncertainty with profiles, especially at the zero boundary. The Czech gate labels every age band a pass while several dose-2 estimates are around \(10^{-17}\). A point estimate at the boundary alone cannot distinguish a tight upper bound from a nearly uninformative fit. [Seed fit and exact weekly update](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Pooled fit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:414) · [Czech gate and estimates](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:366)
- **Give each comparator its own truth.** The Cox simulation makes the hazard ratio at *fixed frailty* one and deliberately generates different *marginal* hazards. A nonunit marginal comparison is expected. RMST can likewise correctly measure a real survival difference in these cohorts. Figure 5’s shared-frailty curve is an extrapolation from one attenuation factor. Fit the comparator at each grid point or remove that curve. For the positive controls, publish the true cumulative KCOR at week 80 from the generating model. A hazard multiplier active only in weeks 20–80 is insufficient as the numerical target for a ratio accumulating earlier weeks too. [Simulation design](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:692) · [Figure 5 construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:729) · [Positive-control Table 9](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1240)
- **Narrow the contamination conclusion.** The reported 66%-of-baseline value is the first *tested* constant increment whose **mean anchored ratio** exceeded one in one design, with 20 replicates per cell. It is neither a general reversal threshold nor an empirical bound on Czech confounding. Show the uncontaminated distribution, bias relative to it, and uncertainty across a wider set of baseline trends and treatment timings. A particularly informative test would deliberately generate a time-varying effect with the same observed curve as depletion and report how often the diagnostics accept it. [Design and interpretation, PDF pp. 28–30](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:843)

I would retain the core estimator as a **model-based sensitivity analysis for fixed cohorts**. Its strongest defensible output is how much a prespecified frailty model changes a cohort comparison, alongside the observed mortality and the range obtained under other plausible assumptions. The priorities before relying on the reported intervals or wave-adjusted results are to correct the identification criterion, use one coherent survival model through the wave, and calibrate the exact fitter and pooling rule used for each published result.
