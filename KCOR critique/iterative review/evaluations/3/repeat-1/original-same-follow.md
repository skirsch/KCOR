# Follow-up on the original objections

1. **Curvature and multiplicative scaling — corrected.** The earlier proposition about nonzero curvature and the claim that multiplying observed hazard leaves fitted variance invariant have both been replaced. The revised differential identity, including both counterexamples, is correct for a known positive Gompertz slope. The revised diagnostic reference is also correct: the quiet-period cumulative baseline is linear in transformed Gompertz time, and it can curve in ordinary time. The distinct question of baseline sensitivity remains. The paper claims empirical cancellation under slope changes, while the supplement tells readers to check that sensitivity without giving the slope grid and corresponding KCOR values in that section. Publish those numbers. See paper lines 332–357 and 462–481, supplement lines 292–310.

2. **Joint seed, exact increments and signed offsets — mixed.** I withdraw the Euler-recursion criticism for the core estimator. Its weekly hazard prediction integrates the model over the bin, its reconstruction uses the exact exponential increment, and the offset now includes the entire preceding gap. A functioning joint-seed option also exists. The default workbook still takes its rate from early weeks, and even the joint option includes early anchor weeks in the final fit. The signed-offset objection remains: Step 3 defines signed differences, while a nonpositive first-pass offset returns an applied variance of zero and later negative offsets are clamped. A gap with no excess can trigger that rule even when the cohort has positive frailty. Specify the offset constraint, its justification, and the fallback estimand. See paper lines 371–436, code/KCOR.py lines 1133–1158, 1236–1253, 1373–1404, 1462–1516, 1542–1556 and 7006–7015.

3. **Cox comparison — disclosure corrected, interpretation still overstated.** The new text says KCOR uses the generating variances, and the new figure bars resample people while holding those values fixed. I withdraw the allegation that this experiment is still presented as estimating variance from the same inputs as Cox. The ordinary Cox result can reflect an actual difference in marginal survival, and the figure’s seven intervals all containing 1 does not test coverage across repeated cohorts or recovery of frailty. The shared-frailty “bias” curve is explicitly an approximation from one shrinkage factor. It should be labeled as a schematic or replaced with fits. The same estimand distinction applies to RMST. See paper lines 708–745 and 1257–1272, analysis/cox_intervals/figure_intervals.py lines 19–52 and test/sim_grid/code/plot_estimator_bias_vs_theta.py lines 20–25.

4. **Block bootstrap, circular truth and interval provenance — corrected for some outputs.** The current person-bootstrap procedure no longer resamples calendar blocks. Table 8 samples disjoint death-week and survivor cells, rebuilds overlapping pseudo-cohorts and refits the comparison, which implements complete-history resampling for that dataset. The old Poisson experiment still defines truth by running the estimator on one dataset, and its four deterministic scenarios still cannot measure repeated-sample coverage. The paper now acknowledges that and no longer presents that exercise as current calibration. The issue requiring work is coverage of the exact released estimator and reporting policy. Historical analytic intervals and new person-bootstrap intervals are now distinguished in Table 8. Ordinary workbook paths still calculate Wald intervals, and the empirical age-control figure calls its bands bootstrap intervals. I did not find a corresponding per-week replay establishing the provenance of those particular bands. The demonstrated Table 8 bootstrap is for a reporting date. See paper lines 648–671, 818–820 and 1237–1246, analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py lines 24–40, 63–98 and 161–169, code/KCOR.py lines 4473–4478, and test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py lines 201–225.

5. **Czech provenance and diagnostic rejection — still applicable, with a specific improvement.** Table 8 now distinguishes the reproduced historical v4.3 results from the joint fit, and the new scan counts all 78 attempted placements. Those are improvements over my original assessment. They do not resolve the 1950/dose-0 supplementary example, the unsupported all-age-band pass assertions, the outcome-related rationale for the 2023 window, or fallback fits entering pooled estimates. The current scan passes every placement while reporting a dose-1 variance range from effectively zero to 14.51 in one decade. In the 2022_06 Table 8 run four original subgroup fits and every bootstrap draw had insufficient-signal flags. These are different analyses, so the 78 passes cannot establish that Table 8 passed. See supplement lines 272–277, 302–310 and 366–421, test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log line 48, data/Czech/KCOR_summary.log lines 214–245, data/Czech/Czech.yaml lines 22–32 and analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json.

6. **The wave model — post-exit objection resolved conditionally, in-wave objection remains.** The revised tail solver uses the correct conditional survival ratio given its boundary exposures. The old rule of adding a constant offset throughout the tail is no longer the general method. The experiment with an independently integrated tail verifies this part. The in-wave survivor moment still resets the gamma mean to 1, and a time-varying endpoint factor still divides accumulated excess. At baseline exposure 1 with exponential frailty and exponent 2, the true survivor second moment is 0.5 while the implemented moment is 1.5. The revised text now calls this an approximation. The test feeds back the algorithm’s estimated wave exposure, so it cannot establish recovery of the generating exposure. My objection about uncertainty in an externally supplied effect scale applies when inference is intended to include that source of uncertainty. Conditional intervals with externally fixed inputs are legitimate if labeled. The inconsistency between “inactive when unidentified” and forcing 1.18 in a reported control remains. See paper lines 94, 550–555, 576–607, 659, 820 and 923–929, code/KCOR.py lines 1028–1047 and 2658–2722, and test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py lines 33–55.

7. **Controls, anchoring and conservativeness — distinguish the historical and revised claims.** Two *historical* 2022 pseudo-dose intervals exclude 1. All six new joint-fit intervals include it, so my earlier observation about two non-null intervals cannot be transferred to the revised estimates. The age groups can still have real differences in mortality, and an anchored ratio exactly removes a persistent proportional difference on the reconstructed scale. The falsification result therefore concerns post-anchor drift, with limited precision in 2022. The old blanket claims that crossover, endogenous frailty or treatment overlap necessarily attenuate a contrast have been corrected. The revised paper explicitly allows amplification and undetected overlap. I withdraw that wording objection. The separate claim that one contamination grid bounds plausible confounding for Czech cohorts remains too strong: the simulation adds real deaths to one arm, uses 20 replicates per cell, and tests particular perturbation shapes. Distinguish the uncontaminated latent target from the all-cause process actually generated and restrict the conclusion to the tested designs. See paper lines 164–169, 818–820, 849–863, 1015, 1032, 1045–1050 and 1237–1246, and test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py lines 151–175.
