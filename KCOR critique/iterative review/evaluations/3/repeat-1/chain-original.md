**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically correct. The main problem is the inference the manuscript draws from a successful fit. Aggregate mortality can follow the fitted curve exactly even when the curvature comes from a changing baseline hazard or a real time-varying exposure effect. In those cases KCOR can pass its diagnostics and remove the very difference the investigator wants to understand. That problem affects the descriptive claim that a contrast has been “depletion-neutralized,” even after accepting the paper’s explicit disclaimer about causality. [Main text §§2.4.1–2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:273), [§5.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1038)

### 1. Two different mechanisms can give KCOR exactly the same data

Let \(b(t)\) be the assumed Gompertz hazard and \(B(t)\) its cumulative hazard. Consider a reference cohort with hazard \(b(t)\). The comparison cohort could arise in either of two ways:

| Mechanism in the comparison cohort | Observed cohort hazard |
|---|---|
| Individuals share baseline \(b(t)\), have mean-one gamma frailty of variance \(\theta\), and experience no exposure effect | \(b(t)/(1+\theta B(t))\) |
| Individuals have no frailty heterogeneity and experience a time-varying hazard multiplier \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\) | \(b(t)/(1+\theta B(t))\) |

The two mechanisms give the same survival probabilities in **every week**, including every prespecified quiet window. KCOR can fit \(\theta\), invert the comparison curve back to \(B(t)\), and return a ratio of one in both cases. Under the second mechanism it has erased a genuine hazard difference. More people, more windows, excellent residuals and a stable estimate cannot distinguish these cases. This directly contradicts the assertion that overlap with an effect will reveal itself through poor fit or instability. **Fix:** define the output as a *Gompertz–gamma model-implied cumulative hazard ratio*. Keep the diagnostics as opportunities to reject a fit. Stronger attribution to depletion requires independent information that constrains the baseline or the timing of effects. [Gamma identity and hazard relation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297), [claimed behavior under overlap](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1040)

### 2. Two identification claims are algebraically wrong

For fixed Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the paper’s marginal hazard \(h\) satisfies
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log h(t)=\gamma-\theta h(t).
\]
This gives a legitimate **within-model** identification argument with a known slope and an exactly observed hazard. It also exposes two errors.

- **Scaling the observed hazard changes fitted frailty.** If \(h\) is replaced by \(ch\), the same model fits with \(k'=ck\) and \(\theta'=\theta/c\). This holds even when both cohorts receive the same multiplier. Common scaling can cancel in the final ratio because both reconstructed cumulative hazards scale by \(c\). The fitted frailty itself changes. Multiplying an *individual baseline* before depletion is a different operation.
- **Nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature is not equivalent to positive frailty.** At \(\theta=0\), a growing Gompertz baseline already gives curved cumulative hazard. Conversely, at \(\theta k=\gamma>0\), marginal hazard is constant and cumulative hazard is exactly linear despite positive frailty. The paper also checks whether the inverted cumulative hazard is linear in time, although its own model predicts \(k(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\).

**Fix:** replace the proposition with the model identity above. Evaluate residuals against integrated Gompertz time, publish the numerical \(\gamma\) and its units, and show how the fitted parameters **and the final contrasts** change across plausible slopes. The supplement currently specifies the slope only as “Fixed implementation value.” [Proposition and scaling claim, PDF p. 11](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323), [linearity diagnostic](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:454), [supplement defaults](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

### 3. The fitting algorithm and final estimator use different discretizations

The input for week \(t\) is an **integrated** hazard \(x_t=-\log(1-d_t/N_t)\). Write \(U_t\) for reconstructed baseline cumulative hazard at the start of the week. The paper’s final inversion implies the exact update
\[
U_{t+1}-U_t=(1+\theta U_t)\frac{\operatorname{expm1}(\theta x_t)}{\theta}.
\]
Step 2 instead uses \((1+\theta U_t)x_t\). That is a first-order approximation which understates each positive increment when \(\theta>0\). The difference feeds into wave offsets and the refitted \(\theta\) before the final exact inversion. There is a second vulnerability when a wave precedes the first quiet window. The seed fit assumes the unperturbed Gompertz trajectory up to that window, then holds its fitted \(k\) fixed during offset estimation. A biased seed scale can therefore persist in the final fit. [Weekly hazard definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241), [seed, recursion and pooled refit, PDF pp. 12–13](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371)

**Fix:** use exact interval survival probabilities throughout. A direct implementation can fit weekly deaths with a binomial likelihood and model-implied probabilities \(1-\exp(-\Delta H_{\rm obs})\). Estimate or profile \(k,\theta\) and any necessary offsets together. Test recovery with a wave *before* the seed window, and verify that changing the bin width preserves the answer on noiseless model-generated data. The size of the current discrepancy in the Czech estimates needs a rerun.

### 4. The empirical controls and diagnostic gates warrant a different reading

- **Anchoring makes a constant ratio equal one.** If the two reconstructed cumulative hazards are proportional, anchoring returns one throughout, regardless of the original level difference. The age-shift control therefore tests post-anchor drift. It cannot establish recovery of the unanchored target or removal of baseline health selection. Even on the reported control summaries, the 2022_06 comparisons are **1.0756 [1.055, 1.097]** and **1.0911 [1.070, 1.112]**. Both reported intervals exclude one. Show raw, inverted and anchored results together, define a practically acceptable null range in advance, and reconcile the single-week anchor in §2.1.1 with the reference-window mean in §2.8. [Anchor definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164), [alternative definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:619), [Table 8, PDF p. 51](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1228)
- **The Czech diagnostic gate needs numerical evidence.** Table S6 marks every age group as passing. Table S7 gives dose-2 frailty estimates as small as \(7.01\times10^{-18}\), while the text treats near-zero estimates as possible weak identification. A boundary estimate could be tightly constrained near zero or compatible with a wide range of consequential corrections. The point estimate alone cannot tell which. Publish parameter profiles and the range of KCOR values they imply, alongside numerical gate thresholds and all failed window placements. Age composition also matters materially here. The raw dose-0/dose-2 ratio is **0.3159** in the full population and **1.2776–2.1343** in each displayed age band. Report a common age-standardized comparison and specify whether fitting precedes or follows aggregation. [Tables S6–S8, PDF pp. 75–77](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:366), [window scan, PDF p. 69](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:302)
- **The contamination experiment does not bound the real-world bias.** The paper defines dynamic healthy-vaccinee selection as immediate hazard *suppression*. Its Type 1 experiment **adds a positive decaying hazard** to cohort A. That tests one perturbation with a specified sign. The reported 66% threshold for a different perturbation is the first tested grid point where the *mean* crossed one, based on 20 replicates per cell. It is neither a lower bound on confounding nor evidence that Czech cohort differences are too small to matter. Simulate deferral at enrollment, suppression and recovery, contamination in either cohort, and effects overlapping the fit window. For each, report how often a materially wrong contrast **passes** the diagnostics. [Definition of dynamic selection](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:45), [experiment and numerical results, PDF pp. 28–30](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:840)

### 5. Interval coverage and comparator claims remain unvalidated

Resampling contiguous blocks of weekly death counts can move a wave into a quiet period or transplant counts to a different risk set. Recomputing \(N_t\) enforces an accounting identity. It does not establish the claimed covariance or preserve the calendar-time process used for fitting. **Every empirical coverage cell in Table 12 is blank**, including the correctly specified gamma null. For independent individuals in a fixed cohort with complete ascertainment, a direct aggregate bootstrap is available: resample the multinomial counts of death in each original week plus survival to the horizon, within each stratum, then reconstruct risk sets and refit. If inference should include variation in shared calendar shocks, specify a model or replication unit for those shocks. Report coverage, interval width, diagnostic acceptance and coverage among accepted runs. [Bootstrap procedure, PDF p. 18](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:635), [Table 12, PDF p. 55](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

The Cox comparison also uses the wrong benchmark for a claim of estimator bias. In the paper’s null simulation, cohort A has no frailty and cohort B has variance \(\theta\). Their **true marginal hazard ratio** is \(1/(1+\theta B(t))\), even though their individual hazards at equal frailty are identical. A non-unit Cox summary is therefore expected. Likewise, RMST can correctly capture a real difference in marginal survival. Compare each estimator with its own data-generating target. For KCOR’s positive control, that target is the ratio of **integrated** baseline hazards through the evaluation week, including the weeks before injection. Also remove or actually fit the shared-frailty comparison curve, which the caption says was constructed by scaling the standard Cox results. [Cox simulation and comparison curve](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:691), [positive-control construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:128)

### 6. The optional wave correction needs its own derivation

The proposed hazard model scales instantaneous excess by \(F(t;\alpha)=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{alive at }t]\). The operational rule instead divides **accumulated, already gamma-inverted excess** by the value of \(F\) at the current week. An excess accumulated over many weeks generally cannot be corrected using its final week’s multiplier. There is also a simple internal check. At \(\alpha=1\), a wave proportional to individual frailty is already handled by ordinary gamma inversion. Yet \(F(t;1)\) is the mean frailty among survivors, generally below one, so the written correction changes a positive excess even in the case described as inactive. For \(\alpha\ne1\), survival through a wave proportional to \(Z^\alpha\) also changes the survivor frailty distribution beyond ordinary gamma depletion. **Fix:** derive weekly survival and survivor moments from one individual-level wave model, verify exact recovery at \(\alpha=1\), and validate the corrected trajectory separately. The manuscript’s decision to leave this module inactive for the Czech example is justified. [Wave model](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508), [correction rule, PDF pp. 16–17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568), [Czech identification result](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:914)

**What I would retain:** the fixed-cohort mortality curves, the exact gamma inversion, and KCOR as a sensitivity analysis showing how a specified frailty model changes a descriptive comparison. The highest-priority revision is to put the observationally equivalent example in §2, then validate the exact weekly estimator against known targets and publish how often misleading results pass the proposed gates. Those changes would give readers a defensible interpretation of both a near-one KCOR and a persistent departure from one.
