**I would request major revision.** KCOR has a sound mathematical core. If individual hazards share a baseline multiplied by mean-one gamma frailty, and the frailty variance \(\theta\) is known, the manuscript’s inversion recovers the baseline cumulative hazard. Its revised week-boundary update is also correct. The scientific difficulty is deciding how much of an observed mortality curve to attribute to frailty. That decision drives the adjustment, and several of the paper’s strongest demonstrations either supply the answer to the estimator or use a different estimator from the one under review. I would retain KCOR as a **model-dependent sensitivity analysis**. The evidence in this edition does not establish that it reliably removes selection-induced mortality differences in registry data. ### 1. The mortality curve cannot establish how much depletion occurred

Take any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\). A population with no frailty and baseline cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) produces that curve. So does a population with gamma frailty of variance \(\theta>0\) and baseline cumulative hazard \((e^{\theta H(t)}-1)/\theta\). The two populations have identical observed survival and different depletion-adjusted hazards. The paper correctly states what breaks this ambiguity *inside its model*. With a known Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the instantaneous hazard satisfies \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). But that also shows the sensitivity directly. At the same observed level and derivative, changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). A shared slope error can therefore affect two cohorts differently. The assertion that such errors tend to cancel in KCOR needs a quantitative demonstration. The supplement gives the slope only as “Fixed implementation value.” [Identification proposition and cancellation claim, §§2.4.3–2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Operational defaults, Table S5](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169)

The paper’s own window scan shows why this matters. **All 78 fits passed**, yet moving the window took dose-1 frailty from effectively zero to 7.70 in one birth decade and to 14.51 in another. That does not establish that the final ratio moved substantially. It does establish that a passing fit is insufficient evidence of stable frailty recovery. **Fix:** publish the numerical slope and its units, the joint uncertainty in \(k,\theta\), and the resulting KCOR at prespecified horizons for every window and slope examined. Show whether the scientific conclusion survives even when the fitted frailty does not. Assess prediction in quiet weeks held out from both fitting and offset alignment. [Window design and results, §S5.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:302)

### 2. The first quiet window can give the algorithm the wrong baseline rate

There is a concrete problem even under the paper’s **own additive-offset model**. Suppose a wave before the first quiet window has added a persistent cumulative baseline offset \(a>0\). Step 1 fits that window with \(a=0\). Because the Gompertz cumulative hazard \(G(s;k)\) is linear in \(k\), the manuscript’s weekly prediction satisfies exactly
\[
q(s;k,\theta,a)=q\!\left(s;\frac{k}{1+\theta a},\theta,0\right).
\]
Thus even noiseless observations in that window can give a perfect fit at the wrong enrollment-scale \(k\). Step 4 then holds that \(k\) fixed. This matters for the no-wave reference used later. It also explains why a small seed residual cannot validate the seed. The empirical window scan includes windows beginning in 2022 or 2023 for people enrolled in June 2021. [Seed equation and zero-offset fit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Fixed-\(k\) refit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:422) · [Empirical window dates](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304)

**Fix:** use the observed survival accumulated up to the first quiet boundary to constrain its depletion state, or fit baseline rate, frailty and prior exposure jointly with an explicit identifying constraint. If the data support only the effective rate in that window, report that rate. Test the revised fit on a known shock *before* the first quiet window, and check recovery of both \(k\) and \(\theta\). The current multiwave recovery example fixes the baseline rate at its generating value. [Recovery example, §3.1.1](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:794)

### 3. The Czech wave correction fails its own decision rule

Box 2 says that when the wave exponent \(\alpha\) is unidentified, the module is inactive. The Czech analysis explicitly declares it unidentified after shallow objectives, boundary-seeking bootstrap estimates and unstable leave-one-cohort-out results. Yet the revised empirical negative control in Table 8 fixes the Czech exponent at **1.18**, in the same region as the failed pooled estimate. Its bootstrap treats wave inputs as fixed. Readers therefore lack a primary empirical control for the procedure the manuscript says they should use in this circumstance. **Fix:** report the core, wave-module-off Table 8 as primary. Show 1.18 as a labeled sensitivity analysis, identify the source of any externally fixed effect scale, and show how the estimates change over its plausible range. [Box 2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:94) · [Czech identification result](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:903) · [Table 8 settings](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:820) · [Bootstrap treatment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:659)

There is also a mathematical repair available. Under the proposed nonlinear wave model, survivor frailty is weighted by \(\exp(-zB-z^\alpha W)\). For \(\alpha\ne1\) it generally ceases to be gamma. The in-wave algorithm nevertheless divides **all accumulated excess** by the **current week’s** proxy moment, clips negative excess and repairs decreases with a cumulative maximum. The authors acknowledge the approximation. Their post-wave survival equation is exact conditional on the exit exposures, so its accuracy inherits the in-wave error. **Fix:** use the manuscript’s own \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) during the wave too. Given a specified baseline path, fit wave increments to observed weekly survival and report lack of fit when the proposed exposures cannot explain the counts. Use the same integrated Gompertz increment \(G(s+1)-G(s)\) in both modules. The wave reference currently uses the left-endpoint rate \(k e^{\gamma s}\). [In-wave approximation and reference path](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:574) · [Exit exposures and conditional survival equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:596)

### 5. Give the reported number a precise interpretation

Under the core model, inversion yields cumulative hazard **at frailty \(z=1\)**, with frailty normalized to mean one separately in each cohort. Two groups can differ in average underlying health, have the same relative frailty variance, and retain a constant baseline hazard ratio after exact inversion. Anchoring then turns that ratio into one for the entire follow-up. The stabilization skip also changes who contributes. Deaths before the landmark leave the risk set and contribute nothing to the accumulated hazard. These distinctions matter whenever an anchored curve is described as cumulative mortality since enrollment or as evidence that a healthy-vaccinee difference has been removed. [Individual model and frailty normalization](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:273) · [Stabilization and rebasing](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:487) · [Primary and anchored estimands](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:149)

I would lead the empirical results with observed deaths, risk sets and survival from enrollment, then show the post-skip unanchored KCOR and the anchored change as separate quantities. For the Czech illustration, age standardization deserves the same prominence: the supplement’s raw dose-0/dose-2 hazard ratio exceeds one in **every listed age band**, while the all-ages ratio is **0.3159**. The populations covered by those rows differ, which is precisely why the standard population and pooling weights need to be explicit. [Raw Czech hazards, Table S8](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:423)

The most informative next result would be one end-to-end table for the exact revised estimator, with unknown frailty, independently sampled deaths, prespecified windows, known cumulative targets and every failed fit counted. That would tell readers when estimating frailty improves the comparison, how often the diagnostics miss a consequential error, and how much confidence to place in the final ratio.
