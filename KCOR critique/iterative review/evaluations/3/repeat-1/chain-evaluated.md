### 2. The manuscript, production program, and coverage studies need one estimator specification.

Section 2.5 says to estimate \(k\) and \(\theta\) jointly in the first quiet window. The ordinary program fixes \(k\) from early weeks and fits only \(\theta\) there. The Table 8 driver obtains the joint version by replacing a line of production source at runtime. Even that version inherits a final fit mask that includes early anchor bins outside the specified quiet windows. This matters scientifically. Under the paper’s own model, \(h_{\rm obs}(t)e^{-\gamma t}=k/(1+\theta B(t))\), where \(B\) is cumulative baseline hazard. Averaging this quantity over early weeks can systematically underestimate \(k\) when depletion is present, even with arbitrarily many people. ### 3. The post-wave equation is sound given the exit state. The in-wave algorithm can supply the wrong exit state.

The revision correctly defines \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) and uses a conditional survival ratio after wave exit. Inside the wave, however, the program still divides accumulated gamma-inverted excess by a moment evaluated at the current week. Even *before* a wave, baseline selection tilts a mean-one gamma distribution to shape \(1/\theta\) and scale \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\). The code instead passes \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\) to a function that constructs a **new mean-one gamma** with that variance. These give different moments. During a nonlinear wave, the additional tilt \(\exp(-WZ^\alpha)\) also matters. [Moment implementation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1028), [in-wave calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2653), [exit-state definition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:582)

A noiseless numerical check isolates the consequence. Take \(\theta=1,\alpha=2\), give the algorithm the correct exit baseline \(B=0.20\), and generate survival with true wave exposure \(W=0.15\). Direct integration gives exit survival **0.717855**. The implemented in-wave formula returns \(W=0.112372\), whose implied survival is **0.739812**. After a true additional baseline exposure of 0.10, the conditional solver consequently recovers **0.095396**. This is an illustrative model calculation, not an estimate of the size of the error in Czech data. [In-wave division and handoff to the solver](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2686)

**Fix.** Use the same survival model on both sides of wave exit. Given a specified baseline path and exponent, estimate the wave increments from weekly survival under \(L(B,W)\), then propagate the fitted survivor distribution forward. Test recovery on noiseless trajectories with \(\alpha=1\), nonlinear waves, and zero wave exposure before interpreting an adjusted empirical contrast.

### 4. The descriptive interpretation is defensible. Attribution to depletion still needs independent evidence.

Section 5.2 now states the central limitation accurately. A cohort with gamma frailty and baseline \(b(t)\) has marginal hazard \(b(t)/(1+\theta B(t))\). A homogeneous cohort experiencing that same time-varying hazard has exactly the same weekly survival. Every fit, window check, and bootstrap based on those counts sees the same data. Calling the contrast a **Gompertz–gamma model-implied cumulative hazard ratio** makes clear what the data and assumptions deliver. The archived quiet-contamination stress test illustrates the limitation directly: all 600 original datasets passed the study diagnostics despite the designed quiet-window violation. [Revised limitation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1041), [stress-test design](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/validation/person_bootstrap/design.json:76), [stress-test results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/validation/person_bootstrap/summary.json:451)

Two manuscript corrections follow. Section 2.4.4 now correctly says that scaling the **observed** hazard by \(c\) changes the fitted variance to \(\theta/c\), but the abstract and discussion still claim invariance. And the identification proposition still equates positive frailty with nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature. Under the stated model, \(d\log h/dt=\gamma-\theta h\). At \(\theta=0\), Gompertz growth alone produces curvature. At \(\theta k=\gamma>0\), marginal hazard is constant despite positive frailty. Replace the equivalence with that identity and check fitted cumulative hazards against integrated Gompertz time. Also disclose how the quiet windows were chosen. The Czech configuration says 2023 was used because it showed the least vaccine harm, which needs reconciliation with the manuscript’s claim of prespecification.
