# Independent review of the current KCOR edition

**KCOR has a defensible narrow use as a sensitivity analysis for a specified frailty and baseline model. The Czech results do not establish that the method reliably separates selection from outcome differences.** The revision corrects several substantive issues. The fixed-Gompertz identification formula distinguishes curvature from frailty, the weekly inversion now exactly inverts an integrated hazard, and reconstruction at quiet-window re-entry includes the final gap week. The Cox experiment discloses its use of known variances. Resampling complete individual event histories is also a sounder basis for the stated sampling experiment. I would keep those changes. See paper §§2.4.4, 2.5, 2.9.1 and 2.11.1, and code/KCOR.py lines 1241–1245, 1373–1403 and 1462–1484.

**1. Specify and ship one primary estimator.** The abstract and §2.5 fit baseline rate and variance jointly in the nearest quiet window. The ordinary workbook uses the default `joint_seed=False`, which fixes the rate from early weeks. Table 8 gets the joint fit by replacing the workbook’s fitter, and the quiet-window check does likewise. Furthermore, even with joint fitting, the pooled refit includes early anchor weeks outside the quiet windows, while the published objective sums only over quiet weeks. The joint path still requires a positive early-anchor rate before attempting its quiet-window fit. These choices affect estimates. For the 2022_06 pseudo-dose 1/0 comparison, changing only the fitter moves the estimate from 1.0073 to 1.0462. Give the primary configuration an ordinary supported entry point, record it in every output, specify exactly which bins contribute, and test the published estimates through that entry point. Fit exact weekly binomial probabilities if cohorts with very different event counts are to inform the fit with appropriate precision. See paper lines 371–395, 420–435 and 820, code/KCOR.py lines 1133–1158, 1233–1253, 1373–1404, 1544–1556 and 7006–7015, and analysis/table8/replay.py lines 15–24.

**2. The reported diagnostic rule is not the rule used to make Table 8.** For 2022_06, four original subgroup fits had insufficient signal. Every one of 999 person-bootstrap draws had at least one insufficient-signal subgroup. For 2021_24 the corresponding count was 307 of 999 draws. The routine proceeds with the applied variances and pools the results. The 2022_06 joint interval for 2/0 is 0.335–1.213. Including 1 in an interval this wide provides little evidence of practical equivalence. The code can return success with zero applied variance after insufficient deaths or a nonpositive gap offset. Those are scientific dispositions distinct from successfully estimating a small variance. Choose between an estimand explicitly defined to use zero as a fallback and a reporting rule requiring identification. Show the fallback frequencies, affected weights and sensitivity of pooled estimates. Report such rows as descriptive fallback results or withhold them according to the chosen rule. Predefine an equivalence margin if the control is supposed to establish near-null behavior. See paper lines 94–98, 352, 663–671 and 1237–1246, code/KCOR.py lines 1268–1289, 1474–1516 and 8106–8124, and analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py lines 73–98, 161–169 with the accompanying JSON.

**3. Parameter identification holds inside a restrictive model. The Czech stability evidence is weaker than the pass rate suggests.** For a given positive observed cumulative hazard path H, any candidate variance a produces a baseline path expm1(aH)/a that generates the same observations. For a known Gompertz slope, the revision correctly recovers variance from the level and derivative of the quiet-period hazard. Changing the assumed slope by a small amount at a fixed observed hazard h changes the locally implied variance by that amount divided by h. This is consequential in low-mortality groups. In the new scan all 78 fits pass, yet the dose-1 variance goes from effectively zero to 7.70 or 14.51 depending on birth decade and window. These are overlapping windows from one dataset. The Czech configuration also comments that 2023 showed the least vaccine harm. Give the independently dated window rationale, report profiles over plausible slopes, and show how much the final *contrast* moves when an entire window is held out. Publish absolute hazards, survival and both raw and anchored ratios. Anchoring exactly eliminates a sustained multiplicative difference on the reconstructed cumulative scale. See paper lines 164–169 and 321–359, supplement lines 292–310, analysis/quiet_windows/summary.csv, and data/Czech/Czech.yaml lines 22–32.

**4. Person-level resampling is a real improvement, but interval calibration is version-specific.** The revised paper discloses that the earlier 189/200 and 502/600 coverage results predate its current weekly equations. A separate paired artifact finds 194/200 covered with the joint fit versus 160/200 on the same datasets with the fixed fit. The mean interval widths are 0.149 and 0.048 respectively, while point-estimate RMSE rises from 0.0136 with the fixed fit to 0.0375 with the joint fit. Wider intervals can give better coverage alongside worse point accuracy. The paired artifact records a different code hash from the current code file. The historical large unequal-frailty experiment accepted all 600 original datasets despite percentile coverage of only 83.7%. These results justify investigating the fitter and its uncertainty, not treating a diagnostic pass as calibration. Repeat the paired experiment on a pinned current release with the actual reporting gates, disjoint windows and wave offsets. Generate independent cohorts and use the known population target. Report acceptance, bias, RMSE, interval width and coverage among accepted comparisons. See paper lines 638–671, validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json lines 2–28, summary.json lines 130–158 and design.json under “common”.

**5. The post-wave calculation is conditionally sound. Its in-wave input can be wrong even with noiseless data.** The new solver correctly conditions on wave-exit survival for fixed baseline and wave exposures. Its test independently integrates the post-wave survival law but takes the wave exposure from the algorithm itself. During the wave, the code still divides accumulated excess by a changing mean-one gamma moment. For example, start with unit-mean exponential frailty and consider a tiny wave with individual hazard proportional to squared frailty after cumulative baseline exposure 1. Survivors then have mean 0.5 and second moment 0.5. The ordinary gamma inverse multiplies the marginal wave increment by 2, yielding the true reference-frailty increment to first order. The code divides it by a further 1.5, hence recovers only two thirds of that increment. The paper now acknowledges the approximation. Its baseline reference also uses start-of-week hazard in place of the exact Gompertz weekly integral introduced for fitting. Given a specified baseline trajectory and externally fixed exponent, solve the joint baseline-plus-wave survival equation for wave exposure at each week, carry this distribution into the tail, and flag incompatible observations. Test all stages against independently generated exposures. Until validated, give wave-off estimates primary status and show the forced 1.18 analysis as sensitivity. Table 8 uses that exponent despite the paper’s conclusion of weak identification. See paper lines 550–555, 570–607, 820 and 923–929, code/KCOR.py lines 1028–1047, 2491–2541 and 2658–2722, and test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py lines 33–55.

**6. Keep control and comparison claims tied to their targets and data versions.** If one simulated cohort has no frailty and the other has gamma variance θ with the same individual baseline, the marginal hazard ratio B/A is 1/(1+θH₀(t)). A non-null Cox summary or RMST difference can reflect a real marginal survival difference. The week-52 KCOR bars in the revised figure quantify event sampling with known variances. They do not test recovery of those variances. Give each comparator its own population benchmark and add an estimated-variance arm. The contamination study adds actual deaths to one arm, so zero treatment effect and equal all-cause mortality are different truths. Its 20 replicates per scenario cannot bound Czech confounding. Finally, some supplementary examples still mix editions. The displayed 1950/dose-0 diagnostic matches four numbers in the v6 negative-control log, including a cumulative-hazard RMSE relabeled as hazard RMSE. The archived Czech primary log for this key shows different values and 41 fit points versus 97. Generate the supplement’s diagnostics and tables from the same pinned run as the paper. See paper lines 698–741, 849–863 and 1266–1272, supplement lines 263–277 and 366–408, test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log lines 2 and 48, and data/Czech/KCOR_summary.log lines 227–230.

The most informative next result would be an independently generated, repeated-sampling evaluation of the exact released estimator, followed by a Czech primary run with the same settings. That would let a reader determine whether a reported ratio reflects a stable inference from observed deaths, an assumed baseline trajectory, or a default applied after the data supplied too little information.
