## Addendum to the saved open review

This is a separate assessment of the specified `m83` edition. **Three earlier objections should be retired for this version:** the fixed-slope identification proposition is now correct, the core weekly inversion is exact, and the manuscript has withdrawn its blanket claim that assumption violations are conservative. Several other changes correct the description of an experiment while leaving its evidential limits in place.

1. **Identification and hazard scaling — the algebraic objection is resolved.** Section 2.4.4 now gives \(q'=\gamma q-\theta q^2\) and both relevant counterexamples. Zero frailty can give a curved cumulative hazard, and positive frailty can give a straight one. It also correctly distinguishes scaling the observed hazard by \(c\), which maps \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\), from scaling the individual baseline, which preserves \(\theta\). Under the first transformation, both reconstructed cumulative hazards scale by \(c\) if the multiplier is common, so their ratio can stay fixed while both fitted variances change. The quiet-window diagnostic now uses the fitted Gompertz curve. **The slope-sensitivity objection remains.** The paper says a prespecified slope range confirmed cancellation, but the cited supplement instructs the analyst to vary \(\gamma\). I found no numerical range, cohort-specific results, joint slope–variance profile, or held-out prediction errors there. The monthly scan refits overlapping windows at the configured slope. 2. **Weekly updates, estimator choice, and offsets — different verdicts.** The Euler objection is resolved for the core fitter. It now predicts the integrated weekly hazard and reconstructs the cumulative hazard at the **start** of the re-entry week, including the preceding gap. The ordinary workbook still defaults to the early fixed-\(k\) estimate, while the Table 8 replay explicitly selects the joint seed. The pooled objective still includes early anchor weeks outside the quiet windows. The exact weekly integration corrects the time discretization of the anchor but leaves the anchor based on *observed, already depleted* hazards. The signed-offset objection also remains. The paper defines a signed difference, while the code clamps a nonpositive gap offset and, on the first pass, returns zero frailty. Even a scientific assumption that waves only increase risk permits a **zero** gap contribution with positive frailty. A grouped-binomial likelihood remains a proposed improvement. The present code uses least squares. 3. **Cox benchmark — the disclosure objection is resolved; the comparison objection remains.** Section 2.11.1 explicitly says KCOR uses the generating variances. The final row still gives Cox HR 0.551 and KCOR 1.024, and all seven new KCOR intervals include one. Those are pointwise, known-variance intervals. Each grid point uses one generated dataset, and resampling keeps its variance fixed. Table 8 implements that scheme with disjoint death-week and survivor categories, reconstructs overlapping subgroups, refits, and excludes the original estimate from the percentiles. The old Poisson pilot still has four 100% rows, one 25.4% row, and a fitted gamma-null target of 1.00944. Section 2.9 now explains why those figures cannot calibrate population coverage. The newer studies also deserve credit for reporting Monte Carlo uncertainty and diagnostic acceptance. For example, the sparse scenario explicitly records zero accepted datasets and leaves conditional coverage undefined. **Calibration of this edition remains open.** The paper dates its studies before the week-boundary change. 5. **Czech provenance and diagnostic gating — Table 8 is traceable; the broader objection remains.** The revised Table 8 has a dedicated replay and saved results. The separate quiet-window scan counts all 78 attempts. Its pass rule checks the status of each individual fit. All 78 passing therefore can coexist with dose-1 variance estimates ranging from effectively zero to 7.70 or 14.51 across placements. That pass count does not establish *stability across placements*. Three distinct production paths matter here: too few deaths returns zero with `success=True`, a nonpositive first-pass offset returns zero with `success=True`, and downstream normalization starts from zero even when parameters are missing or the fit failed. Status flags exist, but they do not consistently suppress a contrast. The supplement’s purported hazard-RMSE example still matches a v6 negative-control **cumulative-hazard** RMSE, and the Czech configuration still gives an outcome-based rationale for choosing 2023. The remedy is a run-specific table of original cohort keys, fit statuses, applied parameters, and reporting decisions. 6. **Wave correction — the post-wave repair is real; the in-wave and external-uncertainty objections remain.** The \(\alpha=1\) identity is appropriate. For \(\alpha\ne1\), the revised tail solves the conditional survival equation given exit baseline and wave exposures. Inside the wave, the code still divides accumulated excess by the current week’s mean-one gamma moment. The paper now calls this an approximation. The synthetic exponent-recovery study generates its expected excess using that same moment, so it can demonstrate recovery under the approximation without testing its accuracy for actual survivors. The bootstrap explicitly holds the exponent, wave window, and external effect scale fixed. Those intervals therefore omit uncertainty in those inputs. Finally, §2.7.4 directs analysts to treat the module as inactive after failed identification, while Table 8 explicitly uses 1.18 and the archived Czech primary log records a nonzero correction. The forced setting is now disclosed; the conflict with the reporting rule remains.
