### 1. Passing the quiet-window checks can leave the scientific conclusion sensitive to the window

The revised proposition is correct. With a known Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), the positive instantaneous cohort hazard satisfies \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\). The paper now correctly explains how a straight cumulative hazard can coexist with positive frailty, distinguishes rescaling individual and observed hazards, and fits week-long hazard increments in both fitting steps. The remaining identification limit is fundamental. For *any* observed cumulative hazard \(H\) and any proposed \(\theta>0\), the baseline \(B_\theta=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) reproduces that same curve. The quiet-window baseline restriction supplies the information that distinguishes these explanations. At fixed observed \(h,h'\), changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). A common slope error can therefore affect low- and high-mortality cohorts differently. ### 3. The diagnostic failure rule and the published intervals describe different procedures

I reproduced all six current Table 8 point estimates from the supplied workbook. The bootstrap correctly preserves subgroup membership and death week and rebuilds risk sets. The problem is how it handles weak fits. In 2022, **four of the nine original subgroup fits** were flagged for insufficient signal, and **999 of 999** resamples had at least one such flag. In 2021 the corresponding figures were zero original fits and **307 of 999** resamples. The fitter can return `success=True` with applied \(\theta=0\) for insufficient signal. The table procedure counts the flags, retains the results, and takes percentiles over all draws. Consequently, these are intervals for a procedure that includes the zero-frailty fallback. The distinction matters for the 2022 pseudo-dose 1 versus 0 result, **1.0462 [0.322, 1.268]**. Inclusion of one in that interval offers little evidence of equivalence. ### 4. The optional wave correction still gives the wrong exposure under its own model

The revision fixes the first-wave-week bookkeeping and acknowledges the in-wave approximation. I would revise one point in the prior review: the **implemented** mean-one gamma moment tends to one as \(\alpha\to1\), so the asserted finite jump does not follow for that implementation. The underlying error persists away from one. The code divides accumulated excess after nonlinear inversion by a proxy for a moment that the model defines for *instantaneous observed* excess. [Wave equations](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:550) · [Implemented moment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1028) · [Applied correction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:2658)

A numerical counterexample needs no sampling noise. Let starting frailty \(Z\) be unit exponential, \(\alpha=2\), cumulative baseline exposure \(B=1\), and true wave exposure \(W=0.1\). Exact model survival is \(E[e^{-Z-0.1Z^2}]=0.4780433\). Ordinary gamma inversion gives \(1/S-1=1.0918607\). Even supplying the **correct** baseline reference of 1, the implemented proxy yields an estimated wave exposure of **0.06215**. The new post-wave test checks the tail conditional on the exposure returned by the in-wave code. It cannot detect this error in the exposure itself. [In-wave and conditional post-wave definitions](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:590) · [Post-wave test construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/test/test_nph_post_window_continuity.py:35)

I would use the paper’s survival law \(L(B,W)=E[e^{-ZB-Z^\alpha W}]\) throughout the wave. With a justified baseline path and specified \(\alpha\), fit nonnegative wave increments to weekly deaths using successive survival ratios, then carry the resulting survivor distribution past exit. Use the same integrated Gompertz increment in the wave reference as in the weekly fitter. For the Czech data, the authors’ own diagnostics declare \(\alpha\) unidentified. ### 5. State exactly what the controls can validate

The point-anchor definition is now consistent in the main text. Its consequence is important: if the two reference-frailty cumulative hazards are proportional by any factor \(r\), unanchored KCOR equals \(r\) and anchored KCOR equals one throughout. A flat age-shift control tests for drift and cannot establish recovery of a sustained level difference. Skipping weeks also changes the population to survivors at the new origin. Age pooling changes the comparison again. The raw dose-0/dose-2 ratio exceeds one in every displayed age band and is **0.3159** in the full population, which includes other ages and different cohort age compositions. Show observed mortality from enrollment, unanchored KCOR, and anchored drift together, with the population and weights specified for each. I would retain **core KCOR as a model-based sensitivity analysis for fixed cohorts**. The defensible scientific result is how a stated frailty model changes an observed mortality comparison, including how much the answer moves under other admissible windows and baselines. The 20-replicate contamination grid likewise supports reporting the first *tested* increment at which the mean anchored ratio exceeded one. It does not bound residual confounding in the Czech population. The highest-priority revision is to validate the exact reporting pipeline against known cumulative targets and show readers where its conclusions change.
