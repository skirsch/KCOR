### 1. The quiet-window study shows why a diagnostic pass is insufficient

The revised Czech scan is the clearest empirical finding. **All 78 fits passed.** Yet for dose 1, changing the placement of the 12-month window moved the estimated frailty variance from effectively zero to **7.70** in the 1940s birth cohort and to **14.51** in the 1950s cohort. I counted **25 of 78** raw estimates at approximately \(10^{-10}\) or below in the supplied rows. The scan defines a pass from solver success, an `ok` status, and a degeneracy check. That rule can accept every individual fit while the estimates across windows vary substantially. A boundary estimate alone also cannot distinguish precise evidence for negligible frailty from a nearly uninformative fit. [Supplement §S5.4, PDF p. 65](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304) · [Pass rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/turns/evaluated-to-current.diff:219) · [All 78 numerical rows](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/turns/evaluated-to-current.diff:284)

This matters because the corrected proposition is **conditional on the baseline**. Given an observed cumulative hazard \(H\), every candidate \(\theta>0\) has a baseline \((e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) that reproduces exactly the same survival. The Gompertz restriction supplies the information that discriminates between them. Moreover, the paper’s own identity \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) implies that, at a given observed \(h,h'\), changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The effect can be appreciable in a low-mortality cohort. The supplement lists the slope as “Fixed implementation value” while the main text claims stability over a prespecified range. **Publish the numerical slope and range, profile \(k,\theta\) jointly, and show the final anchored and unanchored KCOR for each window and slope.** A varying \(\hat\theta\) may have little effect on the final ratio. That is an empirical question the 78 pass flags cannot answer. ### 2. Table 8 needs an explicit rule for insufficient information

The 2022 joint-fit results are **1.0462 [0.322, 1.268]**, **1.0677 [0.335, 1.213]**, and **1.0205 [0.822, 1.208]**. All include one, but the first two intervals accommodate very large departures from one. ### 3. The nonlinear wave correction still has a model error

m83 fixes the first-wave-week boundary and now acknowledges that the in-wave factor is a **mean-one gamma approximation**. That changes one point from my earlier review: this implemented factor can tend to one as \(\alpha\to1\), so the earlier discontinuity argument does not establish a jump in the implementation. The remaining problem is that the paper defines \(F\) as a moment **among survivors** multiplying instantaneous excess hazard, then uses a different moment to divide accumulated excess after nonlinear inversion. The manuscript’s post-wave equation is exact conditional on the supplied exit exposures. It cannot correct an erroneous estimate of those exposures. [Definition of \(F\)](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:551) · [Implemented approximation and post-wave equation, PDF pp. 16–17](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:574)

Two checks make this concrete:

- **Zero-wave check.** The core fit uses the exact weekly Gompertz increment \(G(s+1)-G(s)=ke^{\gamma s}(e^\gamma-1)/\gamma\). The wave reference accumulates \(ke^{\gamma s}\). For \(\gamma>0\), it undercounts baseline every week. Even exact no-wave data can therefore produce positive “wave excess.” Use the same integrated increment in both places. [Core equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:372) · [Wave reference](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:576)
- **Known-wave check.** Take the manuscript’s survival law \(S=E[\exp(-BZ-WZ^2)]\), with unit exponential starting frailty, and consider a small wave after baseline exposure \(B=2\). At wave entry the survivor distribution is exponential with rate 3, so \(d(-\log S)/dW=E[Z^2\mid\mathrm{alive}]=2/9\). Ordinary gamma inversion multiplies this derivative by 3. Thus the inverted excess initially grows by only \(2/3\) per unit of true \(W\). Dividing by the second moment of a mean-one distribution, which is at least one, cannot recover the missing exposure. The new post-wave test takes its wave exposure **from the correction itself** before generating later survival. It tests the conditional post-wave inverse correctly, but leaves in-wave recovery untested. [Survival law](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:598) · [Regression-test construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/turns/evaluated-to-current.diff:1424)

**Fix:** use \(E[\exp(-ZB(t)-Z^\alpha W(t))]\) through wave entry, during the wave, and after exit. With a prespecified baseline path and exponent, estimate nonnegative wave increments from weekly survival and test recovery against independently specified \(B,W\). For the Czech analysis, publish the module-off result as primary. Table 8 fixes \(\alpha=1.18\) even though the Czech identification exercise rejects a reportable exponent and the workflow says to deactivate the module in that circumstance. The fixed-1.18 calculation can be reported as a sensitivity analysis. For publication now, I would present **observed mortality from enrollment, unanchored post-skip KCOR, and anchored drift** side by side. They answer different questions. A constant reference-frailty hazard ratio of \(r\) produces unanchored KCOR \(r\) and anchored KCOR exactly one, however large the level difference. The age-shift example can therefore illustrate stability of drift, while the observed and unanchored curves show what anchoring removed. That narrower presentation would preserve the paper’s useful contribution and make its scientific limits visible in the results themselves. My assessment uses only the m83 combined manuscript, its local sources, and the supplied diff, inspected read-only.
