## Addendum to the saved m83 review

**Major revision remains my assessment.** I checked all nine numbered objections in the named archive against m83. Several specific criticisms should be retired, particularly the incorrect identification proposition, the missing last gap week, the first wave week being absorbed into baseline, and the inaccurate descriptions of the Cox experiment and quiet-window scan. The objections about the target population, late-window fitting, diagnostic fallbacks, and interval calibration still matter. This addendum is separate from the saved open answer. ### 1. Identification, slope cancellation, and the shape diagnostic — partly resolved

**Resolved:** m83 gives the correct identity \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\), explains both the curved zero-frailty case and the straight positive-frailty case, and assesses the inverted curve against cumulative Gompertz shape with its window offset. The old objection to the proposition and the diagnostic reference curve no longer applies. [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354) · [Shape diagnostic](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:466)

**Still applies:** the paper says common Gompertz slope error tends to cancel in ratios and that a prespecified sensitivity analysis confirms stability. The supplement instructs readers to vary \(\gamma\), but I did not find the stated cohort-by-contrast grid or joint \(k,\theta\) profiles there. At a given observed \(h,h'\), a slope error changes the locally inferred \(\theta\) by \(\delta\gamma/h\). I reran the 2022 Table 8 joint point calculation at annual slopes **0.065, 0.085, and 0.105**, changing only that input. Pseudo-dose 2 versus 1 was **1.0122, 1.0205, and 0.9940**; the enrollment had **4, 4, and 6** insufficient-signal cells, respectively. The outer slopes are my illustrative choices. This shows that the fitted contrast can cross unity as the common slope changes, with the diagnostic state changing too. Publish the actual sensitivity grid, including flags. ### 2. Fitted decomposition, transparent failure, and window selection — partly resolved

**Resolved:** the supplement now explicitly says that a disturbance consistent with the fitted trajectory can pass every diagnostic. **Still applies:** the same observed cumulative hazard \(H\) is reproduced by every candidate \(\theta\) paired with \(B_\theta=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H)/\theta\), including \(B_0=H\). The baseline restriction selects the decomposition. A simulation of particular contamination mechanisms cannot establish which decomposition generated Czech mortality. The paper still uses that experiment to support adequacy for the Czech cohorts. **Resolved:** Table 8 no longer rewrites function source. **The late-window objection also still applies.** For enrollment 2022-06, the first quiet week begins **May 30, 2022**, after the configured wave. The seed sets the prior offset to zero and then fixes the estimated scale. Under the paper’s own weekly equation, if a constant prior offset \(A\) is present, \(q(t;k,\theta,A)=q(t;k/(1+\theta A),\theta,0)\). Thus even perfect first-window data can yield a perfect fit with a rescaled \(k\). This matters when that \(k\) is subsequently used as the no-wave baseline. Account for prior exposure, use independent baseline information, or label the seed scale as conditional on zero prior offset. ### 5. Diagnostic reporting and fallback — still applies, including the sparse-death case

Below the quiet-death threshold, the fitter returns \(\theta=0\), `success=True`, `insufficient_deaths=True`, and `insufficient_signal=False`. Table 8 counts `insufficient_signal` and unsuccessful fits. Consequently, its two counters would both miss this fallback if it occurred. The nonpositive-offset and upper-bound branches also substitute zero. The current empirical counts are **307/999** draws with an insufficient-signal fit in 2021 and **999/999** in 2022. Four original 2022 cells flagged; each of the three pooled comparisons uses at least one of them. The current 1-versus-0 interval is **[0.322, 1.268]**. ### 7. A common survival law during and after the wave — the mathematical objection still applies

**Disclosure is improved.** Section 2.7.3 now calls the in-wave calculation an empirical approximation. The original numerical example still holds: with \(\theta=B=1\), the baseline-selected survivors have second moment **0.5**; the code’s new mean-one gamma at propagated scale 0.5 gives **1.5**. The post-wave survival equation is valid conditional on its exit exposures. The revised test uses the program’s fitted wave exposure to generate the tail, so it verifies the conditional inversion without independently validating that exposure. The paper calls the Czech exponent unidentified, while Table 8 requires forced 1.18. Use the same survival integral to estimate in-wave exposure, test against independently generated wave truth, and report the identity analysis as primary.
