**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound when the frailty variance is known and the individual hazard has the assumed multiplicative form. The central problem is deciding how much of an observed mortality curve to attribute to frailty. The paper can estimate that quantity *conditional on a specified baseline model*. Its diagnostics cannot establish that frailty generated the curvature, and several claims about invariance, conservative failure, and empirical validation go beyond what the equations or results support. The manuscript already makes an important distinction by describing KCOR as a non-causal contrast. I would retain that narrower purpose. [Main text, §§1.6 and 2.4.2](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:85)

### 1. The identification proposition and the scale-invariance claim need correction

Let \(m(t)\) be the observed hazard. Under the paper’s exact Gompertz–gamma model with fixed Gompertz slope \(\gamma\), differentiating its hazard equation gives

\[
m'(t)=\gamma m(t)-\theta m(t)^2,
\qquad
\theta=\frac{\gamma m(t)-m'(t)}{m(t)^2}.
\]

This gives a direct proof of parametric identification from an ideal, noise-free trajectory with \(m>0\). It also gives three checks on the manuscript’s interpretation. [Main text, §§2.4.2–2.4.4, especially the proposition on PDF p. 11](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:311)

- **Nonzero curvature is not equivalent to positive frailty.** With \(\theta=0\), a growing Gompertz hazard still gives a curved cumulative hazard. Conversely, with \(\theta k=\gamma>0\), the observed hazard is exactly \(k\), so the observed cumulative hazard is a straight line despite positive frailty.
- **Multiplying the observed hazard by \(c\) changes the fitted frailty variance.** Substituting \(cm\) into the formula gives \(\theta/c\), even if both cohorts use the same \(c\). Equivalently, \((k,\theta)\mapsto(ck,\theta/c)\) reproduces the rescaled observed curve exactly. A common factor can cancel from the final KCOR ratio because both reconstructed baselines scale by \(c\). That is a property of the *ratio*. Multiplying the individual baseline before depletion is a different operation. [Main text, scale-invariance claim](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345)
- **The fixed slope supplies identifying information.** At a given observed level and derivative, changing the assumed \(\gamma\) by \(\delta\) changes the implied \(\theta\) by \(\delta/m\). This matters particularly at low weekly mortality. The SI gives the default slope as “Fixed implementation value” and describes a sensitivity check without giving the numerical slope grid there. [SI, default settings](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:165) · [SI, sensitivity checks](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:292)

There is a deeper limit. For any observed cumulative hazard \(H\) and any \(q>0\), the latent baseline \(B_q=(e^{qH}-1)/q\), paired with gamma variance \(q\), produces exactly \(H\). A homogeneous cohort with baseline \(B_0=H\) also produces it. Thus a time-varying baseline with zero frailty can generate exactly the observations that KCOR fits perfectly as Gompertz plus positive frailty. Every residual and window check computed from those observations will also agree. The discussion recognizes ambiguity of mechanism, but the later assurances that violations produce conservative estimates or visible diagnostic failure do not follow. [Inversion](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Discussion of ambiguity](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:940) · [Conservativeness claims](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1014)

**Fix.** Replace the proposition with the conditional derivation above. Publish the assumed weekly slope, its justification, and profiles of both \(\theta\) and KCOR across plausible slopes and baseline shapes. Include the observationally equivalent zero-frailty example as a stress test. It establishes exactly what a diagnostic pass can mean.

### 2. The fitting algorithm and the displayed inversion disagree

The paper defines each weekly \(h_t=-\log(1-d_t/N_t)\) as an **integrated** hazard. If \(B_t\) is the reconstructed baseline at the start of a week, the exact gamma update is

\[
B_{t+1}-B_t=(1+\theta B_t)\frac{\exp(\theta h_t)-1}{\theta}.
\]

Step 2 instead uses \((1+\theta B_t)h_t\). That is a first-order approximation. For positive \(\theta h_t\) it understates the increment. The approximation feeds the wave offsets and the refit of \(\theta\), while the final plotted estimator calls for the exact exponential inversion. Step 1 likewise compares a weekly integrated observation with a point hazard. The size of the resulting error is an empirical question, readily testable on the supplied examples. [Weekly hazard definition](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Steps 1–4](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Final inversion](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:432)

There is a visible reason to reconcile the calculation. In the empirical workflow figure, dose 2 ends around **0.030** in raw panel A and **0.020** in normalized panel B. For the same input and origin, the stated inversion satisfies \(B_q(H)\ge H\) for every \(H\ge0,\ q\ge0\). The plotted decrease therefore requires some additional change of input, origin, or scale. The caption does not give enough information to reproduce it. [Empirical workflow figure, panels A–C](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/figures/fig_kcor_empirical_intuition.png) · [Caption](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:137)

**Fix.** Fit weekly death probabilities from exact differences in model cumulative hazard and use the same inversion throughout. Publish one worked comparison with weekly deaths, risk sets, skipped weeks, fitted parameters, reconstructed increments, offsets, and final ratio. Regenerate all three panels from that table. For quiet-window checks, compare the reconstructed path with the fitted Gompertz cumulative curve plus its offset. A straight line in calendar time is only an approximation to that target. ### 3. The Czech results expose limitations in both the controls and the diagnostic gate

**The age-shift control tests post-anchor drift.** If two age groups have cumulative hazards \(B_A(t)=cB_B(t)\), their anchored ratio is identically one for *any* \(c\), even without depletion correction. Moreover, using the same vaccination category across ages does not establish that their mortality hazards have the same time course. The SI itself says this construction is expected to have little or weakly identified depletion. In Table 8 the 2022_06 comparisons are **1.0756 [1.055, 1.097]** and **1.0911 [1.070, 1.112]**. Both reported intervals exclude one. Those results deserve an explanation wherever the paper invokes this cohort as evidence of flatness. Show raw, unadjusted-anchored, and KCOR-anchored curves side by side, define a tolerable drift in advance, and resolve the point-anchor versus reference-window-mean definitions. ### 4. The reported 95% intervals lack demonstrated calibration

Resampling contiguous weeks moves deaths between calendar periods that the estimator treats differently. Carrying the original risk sets with those blocks generally breaks the risk-set identity at block boundaries. Rebuilding risk sets restores the identity but changes the death probabilities associated with the resampled counts. Neither operation, by itself, establishes the manuscript’s claimed preservation of martingale covariance. Table 12 leaves **every empirical coverage cell blank**, including the correctly specified gamma null. [Bootstrap procedure](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:635) · [Table 12, PDF p. 55](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

**Fix.** For independent individuals in a fixed cohort with complete follow-up, the aggregated counts support a simple individual-equivalent bootstrap. Within each enrollment stratum, resample the multinomial categories “death in week 1, …, death in week \(T\), survived through \(T\),” then rebuild risk sets. This keeps deaths on their original calendar scale. Refit the entire procedure, including the anchor and diagnostic decision. Report Monte Carlo coverage and pass rates together, using known generating truth, with separate results for boundary, sparse, and misspecified cases. Describe curve-wide uncertainty separately from pointwise intervals.

### 5. The Cox comparison scores different quantities against the same null

In the paper’s simulation, cohort A has no frailty and cohort B has gamma variance \(\theta>0\). Their individual baseline hazards are identical. The paper’s own equation nevertheless gives the **true marginal** hazard ratio \(m_B(t)/m_A(t)=1/(1+\theta B(t))\). It falls below one as survivors accumulate follow-up. A cohort-only Cox fit can therefore report a non-unit summary when the conditional intervention effect is exactly zero. Scoring that result as bias against one mixes estimands. An observed RMST difference can likewise be a correct summary of genuinely different survival distributions. The shared-frailty comparison curve also extrapolates from one attenuation factor instead of fitting the comparator at each grid point, as its caption discloses. [Simulation and null claim](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:691) · [Comparator construction](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:726)

**Fix.** Report the generating marginal survival, marginal hazard ratio, and latent baseline ratio separately. Score each estimator against its own truth. For KCOR, publish replicated bias and interval coverage for \(\hat\theta\) itself as well as for the cumulative ratio. A near-unit ratio can hide offsetting errors in its numerator and denominator. For a temporary positive-control multiplier, calculate the true cumulative ratio at the evaluation week from the integral of the injected baseline. The weekly multiplier alone is insufficient. ### 6. The optional wave correction does not follow from its hazard model

The manuscript models *weekly excess hazard* as multiplied by \(F(t;\alpha)=E[Z^\alpha\mid\text{alive at }t]\), but divides the *whole accumulated positive excess*, after gamma inversion, by the current week’s \(F\). If \(F\) changes over time, division at the endpoint gives a different answer from correcting each increment when it arose. There is a simple internal check at \(\alpha=1\). A wave proportional to \(Z\) already satisfies the core multiplicative model, so exact gamma inversion recovers the combined baseline. Yet \(F(t;1)=E[Z\mid\text{alive at }t]\) is generally below one after depletion; dividing positive excess by it changes the result again. For \(\alpha\ne1\), a wave acting through \(Z^\alpha\) also changes the survivor frailty distribution, which affects subsequent quiet windows. An externally supplied efficacy scale does not resolve either problem. [Wave model](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Correction operator](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module inactive for the Czech analysis, as the authors concluded. For future use, derive the weekly marginal survival probabilities from a joint individual-level baseline-plus-wave model, propagate the survivor distribution, and verify exact reduction to core KCOR at \(\alpha=1\). [Czech identification result](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:894)

The most persuasive next result would be one fully reproducible worked comparison alongside replicated parameter recovery and calibrated intervals.
