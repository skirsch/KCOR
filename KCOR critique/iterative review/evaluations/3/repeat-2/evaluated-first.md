**I would recommend major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound when the frailty model and its parameters are correct. The problem is establishing those parameters from mortality curves, then showing that the estimator actually used has the claimed performance. In this edition, the paper and production code use different fitters, several headline validations bypass the fitting problem, and the Czech example produces reportable-looking intervals even when its own diagnostics flag insufficient signal.

I checked the combined PDF, its Markdown sources, the local code, and the archived results. I reproduced all six revised Table 8 point estimates from the supplied workbook and independently counted coverage from the stored paired intervals. The bootstrap interval endpoints below are from the archived runs. I made no repository changes.

### 1. Mortality curvature alone cannot distinguish frailty depletion from a changing baseline

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any proposed \(\theta>0\), define \(B_\theta(t)=(e^{\theta H(t)}-1)/\theta\). A gamma-frailty population with baseline cumulative hazard \(B_\theta\) reproduces exactly the observed survival. A homogeneous population with baseline \(H\) reproduces it too. The fixed Gompertz slope and the quiet-window assumptions supply the identifying information. A good fit cannot independently verify that the fitted curvature came from depletion. The manuscript recognizes this for a treatment effect that resembles depletion, but its conclusion makes a stronger claim about diagnostics detecting failures. [Gamma identity and inversion, §2.4.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Quiet-window limitation, §5.2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1041) · [Conclusion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1081)

There is also a correctable error **within** the stated model. For the Gompertz–gamma marginal hazard \(h\), differentiation gives \(h'/h=\gamma-\theta h\). At \(\theta=0\), the Gompertz baseline itself produces cumulative-hazard curvature. At \(\theta k=\gamma\), the marginal hazard is constant and cumulative hazard is straight despite positive frailty variance. Thus the proposition’s equivalence between nonzero curvature and positive frailty is false. With known \(\gamma\) and an ideal, correctly specified quiet interval, the differential identity supplies a cleaner identification argument. Likewise, the paper correctly derives that scaling the **observed** hazard by \(c\) changes the fitted parameters to \(ck,\theta/c\). Its abstract should distinguish that result from scaling an **individual** baseline hazard, which preserves frailty variance. [Gompertz specification](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323) · [Scaling and identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Abstract](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:15)

**Fix.** State the estimand as a model-dependent descriptive contrast. Replace the proposition with the conditional identification argument. Show the observed survival curves alongside KCOR, profile uncertainty in \(k,\theta\), and show how the contrast changes over plausible baseline slopes and quiet windows. A flat anchored curve also needs a precise interpretation. If the unanchored ratio is a constant 1.3, anchoring makes it exactly 1 at every time. It measures subsequent relative change and discards that persistent level difference. [Definition of anchored KCOR](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:153)

### 2. The published estimator and the default program differ in consequential ways

The paper jointly fits \(k,\theta\) in the first quiet window. Production fixes \(k\) from the first four post-skip weeks, fits only \(\theta\) in the first quiet window, and includes the early anchor weeks in the pooled fit even when they fall outside the quiet windows. Table 8 obtains the manuscript’s joint seed by dynamically replacing part of the production function. Section 2.9.1 also says that frailty parameters are not estimated by stratum, whereas Table 8 fits nine stratum-by-pseudo-dose cells before pooling their anchored ratios. These choices change what the reported number represents. [Published fitting objective](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Production anchor and fit mask](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1226) · [Table 8 fitter replacement](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/compare.py:27) · [Stratification statement](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:636) · [Actual fitting and pooling](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:80)

The revised weekly **reconstruction** correctly uses `expm1`. The **fitting** objective still compares an integrated weekly observation with a start-of-week instantaneous prediction. With baseline cumulative hazard \(B\) at week start and baseline increment \(b\), the exact predicted observed increment is \(\log[1+\theta b/(1+\theta B)]/\theta\). There is a separate boundary error. The code stores the reconstructed hazard *before* each week, reads it at the last gap week, and applies that offset to the next quiet week. An excess confined to the final gap week is consequently omitted from that offset. [Weekly observation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:246) · [Gap-end index](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1384) · [Reconstruction and offset](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1450) · [Pooled prediction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1534)

**Fix.** Give the manuscript estimator one production entry point used by workbooks, simulations, and bootstrap draws. Keep the fixed-\(k\) fit as an explicitly named sensitivity analysis. Fit weekly deaths against their start-of-week risk sets using the exact interval probabilities. Index all cumulative quantities at week boundaries and test a known shock occurring only in the last gap week.

### 3. The bootstrap evidence shows an actual coverage failure and a precision tradeoff

In the stored one-million-person, unequal-frailty, within-model null, the fixed-\(k\) nominal 95% percentile interval covered truth in **502/600 datasets (83.7%, Wilson interval 80.5–86.4%)**. All 600 original fits passed diagnostics. Diagnostic acceptance therefore did not protect coverage in this setting. [Study design](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/validation/person_bootstrap/design.json:35) · [Coverage, acceptance, and fit failures](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/validation/person_bootstrap/summary.json:130)

The separate **paired** archive gives a more informative fitter comparison than juxtaposing the manuscript’s 189/200 joint-fit result with 502/600 from a different design. On the same 200 paired datasets, I counted **160/200 covered with fixed \(k\)** and **194/200 with the joint fit**. There were 38 joint-only covers and four fixed-only covers. Mean interval width increased from **0.0478 to 0.1488**, and point-estimate RMSE increased from **0.0136 to 0.0375**. The joint fit improved coverage here at a substantial cost in precision. This archive identifies its own code digest, so its result should be tied to that run. [Manuscript account, combined PDF p. 19](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:661) · [Paired design, results, and individual intervals](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/validation/person_bootstrap/paired.json:2)

The generator uses deterministic expected deaths, and “truth” is obtained by running the estimator on a reference realization. The four 100% coverage entries cannot establish repeated-sample calibration. **Fix:** generate independent outer populations with a known population target, resample complete histories within each, and run the exact reporting fitter. Publish unconditional coverage, coverage among accepted analyses, abstention rate, width, and RMSE together. ### 4. Table 8 reproduces, but its 2022 intervals provide little evidence of a successful negative control

The Table 8 bootstrap preserves the overlap between subgroup rows and the total population, resamples death-week or survival outcomes, rebuilds risk sets, and refits. That is a defensible person-bootstrap implementation for these closed-cohort summaries. The problem is the interpretation. For 2022, **four of nine original joint fits flagged insufficient signal**, as did at least one fit in **998 of 999 draws**. The analogous 2021 count was **512 of 999 draws**, despite zero original flags. The implementation retains flagged fallback estimates in its percentiles. Zero unsuccessful optimizations is compatible with all of this because an insufficient-signal branch can return `success=True` and \(\theta=0\). [Overlap-preserving resampling](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:32) · [Fallback behavior](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1486) · [2021 diagnostics](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json:32) · [2022 diagnostics and endpoints](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json:47)

For 2022 pseudo-dose 1 versus 0, the joint estimate is **1.0503 [0.233, 1.299]**, versus **1.0065** with the early fixed-\(k\) fit under the same configuration. An interval that wide includes both 1 and substantial departures from 1. Moreover, pseudo-dose encodes **birth cohort**. Holding vaccination category fixed removes a vaccination contrast, but age groups can genuinely respond differently to a calendar shock. A unit anchored trajectory is therefore an empirical expectation, not a known truth by construction. [Table 8, combined PDF p. 51](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1229) · [Pseudo-dose construction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/test/negative_control/code/generate_negative_control.py:28)

**Fix.** Label Table 8 as an age-shift stress test. Put original and replicate diagnostic counts beside each result. Prespecify whether a fallback defines a reportable estimator or triggers abstention, and validate that whole rule. If the scientific claim is that drift is small, specify a meaningful tolerance and demonstrate precision relative to it. Merely including 1 cannot answer that question. [Manuscript’s stated failure rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:64)

### 6. The optional wave correction has a mathematical problem beyond weak identification of \(\alpha\)

The **post-wave** conditional survival equation is sound given the exit exposures \(B_e,W_e\). The unresolved problem is how the **in-wave** calculation obtains them. Even under baseline-only selection, starting mean-one gamma frailty has survivor distribution with shape \(1/\theta\) and scale \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\). The code passes that updated scale into a **new mean-one gamma** moment calculation, which also changes the shape. For example, at \(\theta=1,B=1,\alpha=2\), the true survivor moment \(E[Z^2\mid\mathrm{alive}]\) is **0.5**. The code’s factor is **1.5**. During a wave, the additional survivor weighting \(e^{-WZ^\alpha}\) generally takes the distribution outside the gamma family altogether. Dividing accumulated positive excess by the endpoint factor does not solve that survival model. [Moment and propagation code](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1028) · [In-wave use](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2653) · [Cumulative correction and conditional tail](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:582)

**Fix.** Use the same survival law \(L(B,W)=E[e^{-ZB-Z^\alpha W}]\) during and after the wave. Given a justified baseline path and external effect scale, solve for wave exposure at successive boundaries and check recovery against known simulated exposures. Make the identity correction primary for the Czech analysis. The manuscript reports that \(\alpha\) failed identification, while the Czech configuration forces **1.18**. As a read-only sensitivity check, setting \(\alpha=1\) moved the 2022 joint 1-versus-0 point from **1.0503 to 1.0517** and left all four original flags. The wave correction deserves repair, but it does not explain the 2022 fitting instability. [Identification result](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:915) · [Forced Czech setting](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/data/Czech/Czech.yaml:14) · [Table 8 correction path](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:89)

I would keep KCOR as a **descriptive sensitivity analysis** alongside observed survival and absolute risks. The most important revision is to make one estimator generate the paper, controls, diagnostics, and intervals, then test it against known population targets with the fitter blinded to truth. Until that succeeds, the Czech results show how mortality contrasts behave under particular normalization assumptions. They cannot establish how much selection was removed or attribute the remaining contrast to vaccination.
