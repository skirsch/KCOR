# Scientific review of KCOR

**Major revision.** The identity behind KCOR is sound. With unit-mean gamma frailty of known variance θ, marginal survival is (S=(1+θB)^{-1/θ}), where B is the cumulative hazard for a person with frailty one. Inverting that relation recovers B. The difficulty is learning θ, deciding what B represents across selected populations, and establishing how the fitted procedure behaves. Several results presented as validation of that procedure were generated using a different estimator or information unavailable in registry data.

**1. Correct the identification claim.** Under the stated continuous-time Gompertz–gamma model, the marginal hazard satisfies (h'=γh-θh^2). At positive h and known γ, the exact population expression is (θ=(γ-d\log h/dt)/h). This supplies the omitted derivation and two counterexamples. A pure Gompertz baseline has curved cumulative hazard even at θ=0. Conversely, θ=γ/k yields constant marginal hazard k, hence a straight cumulative hazard, despite strictly positive frailty variance. Curvature alone therefore cannot distinguish a boundary estimate from weak information. Moreover, if marginal hazard is scaled by c exactly as the paper specifies, the identical-shaped curve has parameters (k'=ck, θ'=θ/c). Common scaling can cancel in a ratio of inverted hazards while changing each fitted variance. Scaling the individual baseline before mixing is a different operation. Revise the abstract and proposition, assess weak identification with a profile over θ and γ, and assess quiet-period residuals against integrated Gompertz time, where the normalized path should be linear. A shared error in γ changes local inferred θ by Δγ/h, which can differ substantially between cohorts. Locations: paper §§2.4.3–2.4.4, lines 323–359, and §2.6.2, line 458.

**2. Limit what a good fit can establish.** For any smooth observed H and any nonnegative candidate θ, choose (B_θ=(e^{θH}-1)/θ), with (B_0=H). Each pair gives exactly the same observed survival. Thus a cohort with no frailty and an intrinsically declining baseline can pass every observed-curve check that a Gompertz cohort with depletion passes if their marginal hazards coincide. More windows or larger cohorts cannot distinguish these explanations. Unit-mean frailty within each cohort also puts its *own* mean underlying risk into its baseline k. If two selected groups have different mean risks with zero intervention effect, their normalized baseline ratio may differ from one. Anchoring cancels a permanent proportional difference, including a genuine effect present at the anchor. Define the unanchored ratio, the post-skip survivor population, and the anchored change in ratio as distinct targets. Report observed risks, absolute differences, age-standardized results and numbers at risk alongside them. For a question about sustained exposure, frozen enrollment groups also require information on subsequent exposure histories. Reserve claims about vaccine harm, benefit or removal of confounding for a design that identifies them. Locations: paper §§2.1.1, 2.2, 2.4.1–2, 2.7–2.8 and 5.2. The paper already acknowledges the general causal limitation; claims of reliably detecting misspecification in SI §S2 go further than the data allow.

**3. Specify and validate one estimator.** The abstract and §2.5 jointly fit k and θ in the nearest quiet window. The registry analysis calls a function that fixes k at the mean of early post-skip hazards divided by (e^{γt}), even outside the quiet window, then fits only θ in that first window. Even under its own model this anchor averages (k/(1+θB(t))). Low event counts or a nonpositive first-pass wave offset can return θ=0 with success set true. These cases are scientifically different from evidence favoring no depletion. Moreover the reconstruction uses a first-order update, whereas the final inversion is exponential. For a weekly observed integrated hazard increment u the exact baseline increment is ((1+θB_{prev})\operatorname{expm1}(θu)/θ). Use one interval model throughout, preferably fitting the weekly binomial counts with exact integrated probabilities. Publish the exact windows, thresholds, reason for each fallback, and a separate reporting eligibility flag. Refit the whole analysis with that implementation. Locations: paper lines 371–428, SI S4.1, and `code/KCOR.py` lines 1223–1237, 1253–1275, 1350–1382, 1449–1495, 6929–6938.

**4. Redo the validations using the same inputs as an analyst.** The Cox table is reproducible as an *oracle* experiment. The driver passes the generating θ directly to KCOR inversion and sets the comparator cohort's θ to zero. The paper explicitly says both are estimated from counts. Further, with common individual baseline and only B heterogeneous, the population marginal hazard ratio B/A is (1/(1+θB_0(t))), which differs from one even when the individual intervention effect is null. Marginal survival and RMST differences are real features of these simulated groups. Compare methods against their respective population targets, include an oracle curve clearly labeled, and add repeated fitted runs with bias, dispersion and failure rates. The empirical age-shift example also lacks a known horizontal target: age groups can respond differently to winter and epidemics. Its cited log identifies v6.0 and a single quiet window. Two of its 2022 comparisons have intervals wholly above one. The quiet-window robustness figure likewise uses the older constant-baseline fit. Redo both, disclose the empirical figure selection rule, and include all attempted cohorts. Locations: paper lines 689–724, 803–811, 1217–1240; `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py` lines 209–235 and 278–285; `test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log` lines 2 and 20–22; `test/quiet_window/code/quiet_window_scan_theta_czech_2021_24.py` lines 77–113 and 242–257; `code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py` lines 120–145 preferentially select an example with reduced curvature discrepancy.

**5. The coverage study cannot calibrate the proposed intervals.** The paper describes resampling contiguous blocks of weeks. The coverage program independently draws Poisson counts with means equal to observed weekly deaths, reconstructs risk sets, and calls a constant-baseline cumulative-hazard fit. Its “true KCOR” is that fitted estimator on one seeded realization. In four scenarios the generator sets deaths to their expected fractional count, so all 500 outer datasets reproduce the same point estimate. The committed 500 × 200 results give 100% coverage in those four scenarios and 25.4% against the single reference draw in the non-gamma scenario. These figures cannot establish sampling coverage. Generate independent integer-valued trial datasets, calculate population truth analytically or by independent high-accuracy integration, and bootstrap individual event-week categories plus survival to the horizon within a fixed cohort (a multinomial draw is sufficient for complete follow-up). Preserve dates and shared subjects where enrolments overlap. Refit the intended pipeline, including the anchor and any data-estimated auxiliary parameters. Report coverage and widths together with gate pass rates, separately for all runs and those reported. Label interval procedures on each figure and workbook. Locations: paper lines 637–662 and 1266–1274; `test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py` lines 76–123 and 210–225; `test/sim_grid/code/generate_sim_grid.py` lines 108–119, 385–395, 850–877 and 919–926; `test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage.csv`.

**6. The contamination threshold is unsupported even in the supplied experiment.** The comorbidity-only zero-contamination cell already has mean anchored KCOR 1.034, with 15/20 estimates above one. At the proposed 66%-of-baseline threshold the corresponding numbers are 1.062 and 13/20. The dynamic-only zero cell averages 1.063. Crossing one in a sample mean over a grid cannot define the onset of sign reversal or bound confounding in Czech cohorts. The injection changes actual death probabilities; it also begins in the week used to normalize the ratio. The experiment therefore combines extra deaths, anchor movement, and error in fitted θ. Use paired random numbers and many more replicates, report uncertainty for the change relative to zero contamination, and evaluate raw, oracle-transformed, fitted and anchored contrasts separately. Put a clinically meaningful error threshold and a false-positive criterion in the protocol. Report numerical evidence for the empirical diagnostic pass claims, particularly the near-zero dose-2 fits in every age band. Locations: paper lines 842–870; `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv` rows 2 and 7–9; `test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py` lines 151–175 and 210–216; SI lines 366–378 and 398–421.

**7. Rederive the optional wave correction before using it.** Under proportional depletion alone, surviving frailty is gamma with original shape (1/θ) and updated scale (s=θ/(1+θB)). Its α moment is (s^α Γ(1/θ+α)/Γ(1/θ)). The code computes s, then treats it as the variance of a *new mean-one gamma* with shape 1/s. For θ=1 and B=1 the actual survivor second moment is 0.5; the implementation returns 1.5. A cumulative hazard containing a term proportional to (z^α) generally also breaks gamma conjugacy during the wave. Finally an instantaneous excess multiplied by a time-varying F does not imply that accumulated excess can be divided by the final F. Derive marginal survival and cause-specific increments from one individual-level model with the survivor distribution updated over the entire path. Verify recovery of the corrected cumulative target against that independent generator, and retain the paper's conclusion that Czech α is unresolved. The core gamma identity does not depend on this optional module. Locations: paper lines 508–530, 542–558 and 568–598; `code/KCOR.py` lines 1028–1047, 2591–2593 and 2627–2635.

I would retain KCOR as a descriptive sensitivity analysis and publish the fitted and oracle results side by side. The most important new result would be the error in the final reported ratio when plausible alternative baseline trends fit the same observations, including how often the method accepts those cases. I checked the supplied source, equations and committed outputs; I did not rerun registry processing or the full simulations.
