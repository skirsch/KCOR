# Follow-up on the original objections

I would close the objections to the main identification formula, the core weekly inversion, disclosure of the oracle Cox comparison, and disclosure of how the empirical illustration was selected. Several qualifications in my original review still apply. The following are the points whose status could be unclear from my current review. Line numbers refer to the current edition.

1. **Hazard scaling and straight-line diagnostics.** The original mathematical objection is resolved in the main text. For known γ the paper now gives θ = (γh − h′)/h², distinguishes individual-level scaling from marginal-hazard scaling, and supplies both counterexamples to the claim that curvature is equivalent to positive frailty. The paper also gives the fitted Gompertz curve as the quiet-window reference. The implementation does retain a descriptive workbook diagnostic that regresses normalized cumulative hazard on ordinary event time and computes maximum departure from that straight line. The SI still associates near-zero θ with effectively linear cumulative hazards. A clearly estimated zero variance under a growing Gompertz baseline can have a curved cumulative hazard. Label the workbook statistic as descriptive or calculate deviations on integrated Gompertz time, and revise the SI note. My concern about common slope error remains: SI S5.3 requests a sensitivity check, while the main text asserts first-order cancellation and stability. Show the γ values and resulting ratios by stratum and horizon. Sources: paper lines 178, 332–357, 464–467; SI lines 51, 299, 411; `code/KCOR.py` lines 7142, 7424, 9502–9532.

2. **What a passing diagnostic means.** My original criticism of the SI’s blanket assurance is resolved. SI line 26 now explicitly allows a model-compatible disturbance to pass every check, and paper line 1050 acknowledges that stable bias need not be conservative. The underlying identification limit persists. The same marginal H can arise from alternative baseline–frailty pairs. In particular, unit-mean frailty is imposed *separately for each cohort*. Different mean underlying risks can therefore reside in the two baseline scales even when an intervention has no effect. A permanent baseline difference yields an unanchored contrast, and dividing by its early value removes a permanent intervention difference as readily as a selection difference. The paper defines both ratios and acknowledges confounding, so the needed change is to keep these interpretations explicit in the displays and claims. Sources: paper lines 153–169, 275–284, 346, 945 and 1048–1054.

3. **Post-enrollment exposure and the population at risk.** This remains a qualification about the question one can answer. Freezing the exposure label is appropriate for describing groups defined at enrollment. It cannot by itself estimate a contrast between sustained exposure histories, because later changes are not encoded in the label. The post-skip trajectory also pertains to people who survived through the skip. I am not recommending that the authors censor switchers in the descriptive analysis. Report the frozen-label and survivor population explicitly and use subsequent exposure histories and a separate identification strategy only if making a sustained-exposure claim. Observed absolute risks, fixed-age-standardized differences and risk-set counts alongside the model-based ratios would make the difference visible. Sources: paper lines 208–220, 235–249, 483–506 and 1052; SI lines 423–437 report raw cumulative hazards for one enrollment.

4. **Estimation details and fallback rules.** The first-order weekly reconstruction criticized in my original review has been replaced: §2.5 and the core code now difference the exact inversion. A binomial likelihood is an optional improvement for unequal weekly precision, not a prerequisite for that algebra. The joint seed fit is also implemented, so it would be inaccurate to say that it is absent. It remains opt-in at the ordinary workbook entry point. Low quiet-window death counts still return zero θ with a successful computation, as can a nonpositive first-pass gap offset. An unsupported fit and evidence for θ = 0 have different interpretations even when their numerical outputs coincide. The current Table 8 reporting problem makes this distinction material. Sources: paper lines 371–405, 422–436; `code/KCOR.py` lines 1126–1143, 1268–1289, 1373–1395, 1460–1467, 1474–1516 and 7006–7015.

5. **Provenance of the figures and control results.** My earlier request to disclose selection of the empirical intuition figure is satisfied. Paper lines 137–139 disclose the restriction to final KCOR 0.85–1.15 and the preference for reduced curvature discrepancy, and identify the earlier monthly slope method. This remains an illustration selected for the behavior shown. The Cox variance is now disclosed as known in the experiment (paper lines 718 and 743). The old observations about two 2022 intervals excluding one apply only to the historical column in Table 8. All six current joint-fit intervals include one. The original log still carries a v6.0 header, while the caption describes a historical v4.3 reproduction; the joint-fit workbook replay is independently identifiable and that historical log should not be used to infer the version of the new analysis. Similarly, the previous criticism that the *current* window scan used the constant-baseline fit no longer applies. That fit is used for historical checking, and the revised estimates explicitly enable the joint Gompertz fit. The current issue is that every scanned fit passes while materially different θ estimates remain possible. Sources: paper lines 820, 1237–1246; SI lines 304–310; `test/negative_control/out/KCOR_summary.log` lines 2, 20–22; `analysis/table8/replay.py` lines 16–25 and `analysis/quiet_windows/scan.py` lines 62–82.

6. **Which intervals have actually been calibrated.** The deterministic outer datasets, one-draw “truth,” older constant-baseline fitter and independent weekly Poisson draws still describe the *historical* pilot. The paper now disavows that coverage evidence. Those defects should not be ascribed to the revised person bootstrap. In Table 8 the code reconstructs mutually exclusive person/outcome cells before sampling and restores their subgroup overlap, a sound fix for those marginal enrollment estimates. Across separate enrollments it seeds each draw by enrollment index. Dependence between estimates would still need to be preserved for a simultaneous band or direct cross-enrollment contrast. My original request to identify uncertainty methods on every display remains partly open. The known-θ Cox figure and Table 8 now name their procedures. Ordinary workbook columns named CI_lower/CI_upper can instead contain clipped analytic log-scale intervals, and the supplied Table 8 percentile calculation is for a reporting date, not the entire age-shift time-series band. Supply per-figure generation provenance before equating those outputs. I did not establish that any particular age-shift band was drawn from the analytic columns. Finally, keeping an external α fixed gives uncertainty conditional on that value. If the inferential target includes estimating α, its estimation or external uncertainty must also be propagated. Sources: paper lines 642–671, 743, 818, 820 and 1237; SI line 335; `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` lines 24–40, 63–72, 93–98 and 162–172; `code/KCOR.py` lines 4473–4482 and 4825–4834.

7. **Contamination and nonlinear waves.** The numerical contamination criticism still applies. The zero-contamination comorbidity cell is already above one on average, and injection begins at the anchor. The fact that the positive doses form an increasing subsequence does not establish onset at 66% of baseline or bound confounding in this population. The nonlinear-wave criticism is partly addressed: the paper explicitly labels the mean-one moment and division of cumulative excess by a current-week factor as approximations, includes the first wave week, and gives a valid conditional post-wave survival inversion if the exit exposures are known. The objection about incorrect **in-wave** survivor moments and unvalidated exposure estimates therefore remains, while an objection to the **conditional post-wave equation itself** would be unwarranted. The α = 1 identity case is also sound. Sources: paper lines 575–607, 851–879; SI line 92; `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv` rows 2, 7–9; `test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py` lines 151–175, 210–216; `code/KCOR.py` lines 1028–1047, 2491–2541, 2624–2627 and 2658–2722.

These judgments come from the supplied manuscript, source and saved outputs. I did not rerun the registry or coverage studies for this follow-up.
