**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound when the frailty variance is known and the model holds. The main problem is how the procedure estimates that variance and decides when to use it. In a read-only check on exact data generated from the manuscript’s own model, I obtained **KCOR 0.7402 when the true ratio was 1**. I also reproduced all six revised Czech negative-control point estimates. The saved results show that the pipeline reported the 2022 comparisons despite flagging insufficient signal in four of the original fits.

### 1. The fitter can turn a correct answer into a false contrast

I generated 90 noiseless weekly increments from the exact Gompertz–gamma equations, using a common baseline \(k=0.002\) per week, \(\gamma=0.085/52\), and cohort frailty variances 4 and 0. I designated weeks 0–29 and 40–89 as quiet. There was no wave, treatment effect, or change during the intervening ten weeks, so the true cumulative baseline ratio was exactly 1. The joint seed recovered the first cohort’s variance as **3.999999999999865**. Its reconstructed gap offset was **−2.50 × 10⁻¹⁶**. The code treats *any* first-pass offset at or below zero as insufficient signal, returns an applied variance of **0** with `success=True`, and thereby produces the week-90 ratio of **0.7402**. A gap with zero excess should be an ordinary case for this method. [Weekly hazard equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1126) · [Offset calculation and zero-variance return](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1474)

**Fix.** Give zero offset its own valid case, with a tolerance scaled to numerical and sampling uncertainty. If wave excess must be nonnegative, fit the offset at its zero boundary while retaining the information about frailty in the quiet weeks. A material model failure should produce an unavailable normalized estimate. Add the noiseless two-window example as a regression test, together with positive and negative gap perturbations.

### 2. The manuscript, ordinary analysis, and reporting gate use different rules

The manuscript specifies a joint seed fit of \(k\) and \(\theta\) in the first quiet window. The ordinary workbook caller leaves `joint_seed=False`, fixing \(k\) from early post-skip weeks. The Table 8 replay explicitly switches on the joint fit. The pooled code also includes the early anchor weeks even when they fall outside the designated quiet windows. This choice matters: for the 2022 pseudo-dose 2 versus 0 comparison, the joint fit gives **1.0677** and the early-rate fit gives **1.0129** on the same inputs. ### 3. Quiet-window fit cannot establish how much curvature came from frailty

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any candidate \(\theta>0\), the baseline \(B_\theta(t)=[\exp\{\theta H(t)\}-1]/\theta\) reproduces the same survival curve. The paper correctly shows what supplies identification within its model: a *known* quiet-window Gompertz slope gives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). This also exposes the sensitivity. At a fixed observed hazard and derivative, changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the locally implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). The same slope error can therefore affect two cohorts differently. Agreement with the fitted curve cannot establish that the assumed slope was right. [Inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Claim of cancellation in ratios](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354)

The Czech scan makes this consequential. **All 78 fits passed**, while dose-1 variance ranged from effectively zero to **7.70** in one birth decade and **14.51** in another. The pass flag checks each fit individually. It cannot certify stability *across* windows. The Czech configuration also says the 2023 period was chosen because it showed the “least vaccine harm.” That comment warrants reconciling the window choice with the manuscript’s claim of prespecification. [Supplementary scan](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304) · [Actual pass calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/quiet_windows/scan.py:70) · [Czech window rationale and dates](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/data/Czech/Czech.yaml:22)

**Fix.** Report the range of **KCOR itself** across plausible slopes and independently justified windows, alongside uncertainty in \(k\) and \(\theta\). Show observed, unanchored, and anchored contrasts together. Anchoring forces equality at the reference week and removes a persistent proportional difference. Each cohort’s mean-one frailty convention also leaves baseline risk differences in its fitted scale. These distinctions matter even for a descriptive application. The approximation matters mathematically as well. Under the manuscript’s nonlinear wave model, survivor frailty is weighted by \(\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)\). The implementation instead divides accumulated wave excess by a mean-one gamma moment for the current week. Its post-wave equation is sound **conditional on the exit exposures**, as the paper says. It inherits any error made estimating those exposures. In a noiseless check with \(\theta=1\), \(\alpha=2\), \(k=0.002\), \(\gamma=0.085/52\), and ten weekly wave increments of 0.02, the routine recovered exit wave exposure **0.1496** from a true **0.2000**, even with the generating parameters supplied. [In-wave approximation and post-wave equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:576) · [Implemented correction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:2658)

**Fix.** Make core KCOR the primary Czech analysis and label forced exponents as sensitivity analyses. If the wave module is retained, use the same survival model during and after the wave. Given a justified baseline path and externally specified exponent, solve \(E[\exp(-ZB_t-Z^\alpha W_t)]=S(t)\) at the week boundaries and test recovery of both \(W_t\) and the final contrast.

### 5. The validation establishes less than the paper claims

- **The main Cox comparison gives KCOR the generating frailty variances.** It verifies the inversion. The two cohorts genuinely have different marginal survival under the simulated conditional treatment null, so a non-null marginal hazard contrast or RMST difference is expected. Benchmark each method against its own population target, and show fitted-variance KCOR alongside the known-variance result. [Simulation methods](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:702) · [Actual inversion in the experiment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:234)
- **The positive control tests direction more directly than magnitude.** The generator multiplies the cohort hazard among survivors inside the effect window and resumes the original hazard afterward. An individual-level intervention would also change who survives that window. Generate effects at the individual frailty level, calculate the resulting *cumulative* target at each horizon, and score the current fitter against it. The separate S7 study does update survivors by frailty, but uses the earlier cumulative-hazard fitter. - **The contamination experiment does not establish a 66% threshold for apparent harm.** In its saved comorbidity-only sweep, *zero* contamination already gave mean anchored KCOR **1.034**, with **15 of 20** estimates above 1. Use paired uncontaminated runs, more replicates, and a prespecified material deviation from the true target. I would retain KCOR as a **model-based sensitivity analysis for fixed cohorts**. The first revision should fix the zero-offset failure, make the ordinary and published estimators identical, and rerun the controls under the stated reporting rule. Until then, a flat anchored curve, an interval containing 1, or a diagnostic pass provides insufficient evidence that the procedure removed selection-induced depletion.
