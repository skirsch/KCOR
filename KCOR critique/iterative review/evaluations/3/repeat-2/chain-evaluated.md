### 4. The identification claim is still stronger than the mathematics

The revised §2.4.4 correctly distinguishes scaling the observed hazard from scaling each individual’s baseline. The abstract and discussion still assert invariance under common observed-hazard rescaling. The proposition also retains the false equivalence between nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature and positive frailty. Under the stated continuous-time Gompertz–gamma model, observed hazard \(m(t)\) obeys

\[
m'(t)=\gamma m(t)-\theta m(t)^2.
\]

With fixed \(\gamma\), this identifies \(\theta=(\gamma m-m')/m^2\) from an ideal positive trajectory. At \(\theta=0\), a growing Gompertz baseline gives nonzero curvature. At \(\theta k=\gamma>0\), the observed hazard is constant despite positive frailty. Rescaling \(m\) by \(c\) gives \(\theta/c\), exactly as the revised methods paragraph now says. More fundamentally, a homogeneous population with time-varying baseline \(m(t)\) produces the identical observed trajectory. Repeating the fit across quiet windows cannot distinguish those mechanisms from mortality alone. ### 5. The post-wave equation is sound conditional on its inputs. The in-wave calculation does not recover those inputs.

This edition correctly handles \(\alpha=1\) by ordinary gamma inversion. It also gives a valid post-wave conditional-survival equation if the exit baseline \(B_e\) and wave exposure \(W_e\) are known. The remaining error is how it obtains \(W_e\): it divides *accumulated* excess on the gamma-inverted scale by the current week’s moment. Even before any nonlinear wave, baseline exposure \(B\) changes a starting gamma law to shape \(1/\theta\) and scale \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\). The code instead uses the propagated scale as a new variance parameter of a **mean-one** gamma law, changing the shape and discarding the survivor mean. [Post-wave derivation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:582) · [Moment and propagation functions](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1028) · [In-wave calculation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2653)

A numerical check shows the consequence without sampling error. Take mean-one exponential frailty, \(\alpha=2\), true exit baseline \(B_e=0.2\), and true wave exposure \(W_e=0.1\). The paper’s own joint survival equation gives survival **0.747746**. Even granting the algorithm the exact baseline, its in-wave rule gives \(W_e=0.078589\). Substituting that estimate back into the same survival equation gives **0.762491**. The post-wave solver therefore starts from an exposure pair that fails to reproduce observed exit survival. [Exposure assignment and survival equation](/C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:590)

**Fix:** with an independently justified baseline path and exponent, fit nonnegative weekly wave increments directly through \(S(t)=E[\exp\{-ZB(t)-Z^\alpha W(t)\}]\). Use that same survivor distribution through wave exit and follow-up. Test recovery of known exposures, agreement with observed survival at the boundary, and continuity as \(\alpha\) approaches one.

**Recommendation:** retain KCOR as a descriptive sensitivity analysis under an explicit frailty and baseline model. Before presenting it as validated depletion adjustment, use one named fitter for the simulations, figures, empirical results, and intervals, and enforce the reporting rule on the Czech example.
