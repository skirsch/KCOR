### 1. A zero wave offset can erase real frailty

I tested the current joint fitter on 104 weeks of exact Gompertz–gamma hazards with \(k=0.002\), \(\gamma=0.085/52\), and true \(\theta=1\). I designated weeks 0–19 and 40–103 as quiet and generated the intervening weeks from exactly the same model. The first fit recovered \(\theta=0.999999999999986\). At re-entry the estimated excess cumulative hazard was \(-6.94\times10^{-17}\), numerically zero. The fitter returned **\(\theta=0\), `insufficient_signal`, `success=True`**. Adding a genuine 0.01 baseline-hazard increment only in week 39 recovered both the increment and \(\theta=1\). The latter check confirms that this edition fixed the previous review’s last-gap-week indexing error. [Weekly prediction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1126) · [Reconstruction and offset comparison](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1459) · [Return of zero frailty](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1496)

A gap with no excess mortality is compatible with any frailty variance. Its sign is therefore an invalid test for frailty. **Allow a zero offset, use a numerical tolerance, and assess uncertainty in the offset separately from uncertainty in \(\theta\).** A materially negative offset can trigger a warning about the assumed wave model. It supplies no justification for replacing a well-estimated positive variance with zero. The noiseless no-wave and final-week-shock examples should both become regression tests. There is a scientific consequence in the 2022 example. Its first quiet window lasts **four weeks and starts after the configured wave**. Step 1 assumes zero prior offset and then freezes \(k\). Even under proportional gamma frailty, if the true prior cumulative excess is \(a>0\), the quiet-window hazard with parameters \(k,\theta,a\) is exactly representable by the zero-offset formula with \(k'=k/(1+\theta a)\) and the same \(\theta\). ### 5. The optional wave correction is approximate in a consequential place

This edition fixes the first modeled wave week and correctly describes the post-wave survival equation as exact **conditional on the supplied exit exposures**. The in-wave calculation still substitutes a mean-one gamma moment for the survivor moment and divides accumulated excess by the current week’s factor. The tail test generates its later survival using the program’s own estimated wave exposure. It verifies the conditional tail calculation while leaving the accuracy of that exposure untested. **What I would retain.** KCOR can serve as a descriptive sensitivity analysis alongside observed survival, absolute mortality risks, and the unanchored contrast. For any observed cumulative hazard \(H\) and proposed \(\theta>0\), the baseline \(B_\theta=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\) reproduces exactly that same survival under gamma frailty. The fixed Gompertz slope and quiet-window assumptions supply the identifying information. The revised proposition now expresses this conditional result correctly. And because anchoring maps any constant ratio, including 1.3, to 1 throughout follow-up, a flat anchored curve establishes only stability relative to its reference. The publishable claim is how mortality contrasts change **under explicit normalization assumptions**. Establishing how much selection was actually removed requires independent evidence.
