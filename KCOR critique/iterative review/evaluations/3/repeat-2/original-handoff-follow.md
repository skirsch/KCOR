## Addendum to the saved open review

**My recommendation remains major revision.** Several earlier objections should be retired for this edition. The manuscript now gives the correct fixed-slope identification result, distinguishes individual from observed hazard scaling, discloses how the illustrative Czech figure was selected, and labels the Cox experiment as using known frailty. The strongest surviving objections concern what the fitted procedure actually reports when information is weak and which results validate that procedure.

### 1. Identification, scaling, and quiet-window diagnostics

**The objection to the mathematical proposition is resolved.** Section 2.4.4 now derives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) and gives both counterexamples from the earlier review. Zero frailty can produce curved cumulative hazard under Gompertz growth. Positive frailty can produce a straight cumulative hazard when \(k\theta=\gamma\). The revised diagnostic also uses the correct reference curve, which is linear in integrated Gompertz time \((e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). The scaling objection is resolved too. Multiplying the observed hazard by \(c\) gives \(k'=ck,\ \theta'=\theta/c\). Under that transformation the inverted cumulative hazard scales by \(c\), so a common factor can cancel exactly in the ratio while changing both fitted variances. [Identification and scaling](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Quiet-window reference curve](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:466)

**The objection to the claimed robustness to \(\gamma\) still applies.** The paper says first-order slope errors tend to cancel and that sensitivity analyses confirm this. Its own formula gives a local change \(\Delta\theta_d=\Delta\gamma/h_d\) for a fixed observed trajectory. The same slope error can therefore move the two cohort estimates by different amounts. I found an instruction in the supplement to perturb \(\gamma\), but no numerical range and resulting cohort fits and ratios there to substantiate the claim. Likewise, §2.6.2 still interprets near-zero \(\hat\theta\) as indicating weak identification. A tightly estimated zero, a flat objective, and a software fallback have different meanings. A profile of the objective and the resulting KCOR across plausible slopes would distinguish them. ### 2. What a good fit and an anchored ratio establish

**The specific objection to SI §S2’s wording is resolved.** It now explicitly says that a disturbance consistent with the fitted trajectory can pass every check. The main text acknowledges this for a treatment effect during a quiet window and explicitly disclaims general confounding adjustment. I would qualify the narrower remaining claim that an empirical age-shift control verifies the absence of artifactual effects. Age groups can respond differently to the same winter or epidemic, so that comparison has no known horizontal population target. [Revised SI §S2](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:24) · [Scope of adjustment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:943) · [Age-control and quiet-window interpretations](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1039)

**The objection about different cohort mean risks still applies.** Suppose underlying susceptibility has mean \(m_d\) in cohort \(d\), and the individual hazard is susceptibility times a common baseline \(\lambda(t)\). Writing susceptibility as \(m_d Z_d\), with \(E[Z_d]=1\) as the paper requires, puts \(m_d\lambda(t)\) into the cohort baseline. Perfect inversion can then give unanchored KCOR \(=m_A/m_B\) under zero intervention effect. Anchoring removes that constant difference, and it also removes a genuine proportional effect already present at the anchor. This is an ordinary consequence of the definition. The paper now distinguishes the two ratios and specifies the post-skip origin. The remaining fix is to state explicitly what each ratio can detect. ### 3. Which estimator runs, and what a zero means

**The old first-order weekly reconstruction objection is resolved for the core.** The fitter uses the integrated weekly gamma hazard, reconstructs with `expm1`, and evaluates the gap offset at entry to the first quiet week. **The single-estimator and fallback objections remain.** Table 8 selects the joint seed. The ordinary workbook uses the default early fixed-\(k\) seed. The final fit also includes early anchor weeks outside the quiet window. A low death count, an upper-bound fit, and excessive residual error can each produce an applied zero with `success=True`. The software records distinct reasons, which deserves credit. The paper nevertheless says it never imposes zero, and pooled results can include these cases. The exact Czech windows are available in YAML and the numerical thresholds in code. What remains necessary is a reporting rule that distinguishes a calculated result from an eligible scientific estimate. ### 5. Bootstrap procedure and coverage

**The historical pilot criticism remains correct, and the revision acknowledges it.** The pilot resamples weekly Poisson deaths and uses a fitted value from one seeded dataset as truth. Its four 100% results and one 25.4% result cannot calibrate the current intervals. The revised methods now describe person resampling, and the ordinary Monte Carlo generator samples complete records with replacement. Table 8’s multinomial event-week implementation preserves subgroup overlap within each enrollment, recomputes pooling weights and the anchor, and excludes the original estimate from percentile calculations. Separate draws by enrollment support separate intervals. A joint comparison across overlapping enrollments would additionally require preserving the same person across those enrollments. ### 7. Survivor moments and the optional wave correction

**The in-wave mathematical objection survives.** Even before a nonlinear wave, proportional depletion leaves gamma shape \(1/\theta\) and scale \(s=\theta/(1+\theta B)\). Its survivor moment is \(s^\alpha\Gamma(1/\theta+\alpha)/\Gamma(1/\theta)\). At \(\theta=B=1,\alpha=2\), that gives **0.5**. The code puts the propagated scale into a new mean-one gamma moment and obtains **1.5**. During a nonlinear wave the survivor density is tilted further by \(\exp(-Wz^\alpha)\). The revision now explicitly calls its in-wave factor and division of cumulative excess by the current factor approximations. Its synthetic exponent-recovery generator itself generates excess using that proxy, so recovery there cannot validate the survivor-moment approximation.
