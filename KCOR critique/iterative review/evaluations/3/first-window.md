# Can the first quiet window recover the baseline rate?

**The exact weekly equation confirms the concern in [blind repetition 1, point 2](repeat-1/blind.md).** If a shock between the rebased origin and the first quiet window leaves a positive cumulative baseline offset \(a\), the quiet weeks alone identify only an effective Gompertz scale \(k_* = k/(1+\theta a)\). A perfect seed fit can therefore underestimate the scale at the rebased origin while recovering the generating \(\theta\).

Write \(G(s;k)=kF(s)\) and \(\Delta F=F(s+1)-F(s)\). The fraction inside the manuscript's \(\log(1+x)\) satisfies

\[
\frac{\theta k\Delta F}{1+\theta a+\theta kF(s)}
=\frac{\theta k_*\Delta F}{1+\theta k_*F(s)}.
\]

This gives equality of every weekly prediction at the same \(\theta\). It requires a common slope, the same post-shock Gompertz increments and an offset constant across the seed window in the gamma model's cumulative **individual baseline hazard**. For \(a=0\) or \(\theta=0\), \(k_*=k\). A nonlinear frailty-dependent shock can change the survivor distribution and requires a separate analysis. The rate is defined at post-skip time zero. [Current manuscript, §§2.4.3–2.5, lines 323–332 and 371–394](../../revised/documentation/preprint/paper.md)

**What the joint fitter does.** Step 1 fits only \(k,\theta\) and supplies zero offset. The implementation reconstructs exposure over all post-skip weeks but reads an offset only at the start of segments after the first. The pooled step fixes \(k\), varies \(\theta\), and includes the early anchor weeks. With one quiet segment there is no estimated offset anywhere. The ordinary fixed-\(k\) default is a different procedure. [Current fitter, lines 1126–1143, 1236–1252, 1373–1403, 1459–1484 and 1544–1564](../../revised/code/KCOR.py)

For a numerical check, I ran the current weekly predictor and joint fitter function bodies using their default fit thresholds. Take \(s=0,\ldots,74\), \(k=.004,\theta=4,\gamma=.085/52\), add .05 to the individual increment in week 8 only, and generate exact weekly marginal hazards from the resulting start states. With quiet weeks 12–40 and four early anchor weeks, the two predicted seed curves differ by at most \(4.34\times10^{-19}\). The fitter gives seed \(k=.003333333333333334,\theta=4\), then final \(\theta=3.9368643\) with status **ok**. This counterexample shows how the pooled value can move. The wave-off inversion depends directly on \(\theta\) and the observed cumulative hazard. The optional no-wave reference also uses \(k\) directly. The seed identity alone establishes neither a direction nor a magnitude of error in a Czech KCOR. The Czech scan uses delayed windows, while the multiwave recovery example supplies the generating rate. [Current paper, §§2.4.2, 2.7.3 and 3.1.1](../../revised/documentation/preprint/paper.md) · [Current supplement, lines 302–308](../../revised/documentation/preprint/supplement.md)

To estimate the origin-scale \(k\), an additional observation can supply the missing scale. At the first quiet boundary \(s_0\), survival measured from the same rebased origin gives \(G(s_0;k)+a=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H_{\rm obs}(s_0))/\theta\) under this model. A recovery test could impose that constraint and report \(k,\theta\) and KCOR separately.
