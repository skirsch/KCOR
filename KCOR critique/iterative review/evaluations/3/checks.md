## Quiet weeks following a nonlinear wave

[Continuing chain 1](repeat-1/chain-current.md) also connected the nonlinear-wave survivor distribution to a later quiet-window fit. With a multiplicative, frailty-proportional quiet baseline after the wave, the observed hazard derivative is the Gompertz slope times the hazard minus the squared hazard times the survivors' squared coefficient of variation. Under proportional gamma selection that coefficient is the original variance parameter. It can change after nonlinear selection. For an initial unit exponential frailty, integrating the first two moments against the survival weight with exponent 2 gave squared coefficients of variation 1.0000 at baseline exposure 1 with zero wave exposure, 0.8876 at baseline exposure 1 with wave exposure 0.2, and 0.9350 at baseline exposure 2 with that same wave exposure. This is an additional consequence of PR #5 item 2 under the specified model. The examples do not estimate the effect on the Czech data.

## First-window offset

The independent [derivation and joint-fitter replay](first-window.md) traces the first-window rate ambiguity back through the previous editions. The [two-window zero-offset test](gap-check.md) checks a separate branch that resets applied frailty.

One blind review derived an exact ambiguity in the published seed equation. Since both the Gompertz cumulative hazard and its weekly increment are linear in the rate $k$, dividing numerator and denominator of the weekly hazard expression by $1+\theta a$ gives $q(s;k,\theta,a)=q(s;k/(1+\theta a),\theta,0)$. Here $a$ is a constant cumulative offset **already present at the start of the first fit window**. A noiseless seed fit that fixes that offset at zero can then recover the effective window rate in place of the original enrollment-scale rate. The equality alone does not imply that frailty changes or that any specified Table 8 endpoint is biased.

For $k=0.002$, $\theta=2$, $a=0.1$, and weekly Gompertz slope $0.085/52$, the effective rate is 0.001666667. Evaluating both sides for 52 consecutive weeks beginning at week 25 gave maximum absolute difference $4.34\times10^{-19}$. The seed code fits zero prior offset and the subsequent fit holds the rate fixed. The historical model also used a zero-offset seed. This is a failure condition that can predate the weekly integration correction.

## Retrospective fits at the Table 8 reporting date

I instrumented the original-sample 2022 fit to check each component. The two-dose subgroup's pseudo-dose 1 and the all-person subgroup's pseudo-dose 1 reached the upper bound near 100. The two-dose subgroup's pseudo-dose 2 and the unvaccinated subgroup's pseudo-dose 0 triggered the first-pass gap rule. All four applied zero. Consequently each of the three original pooled pairwise estimates uses at least one flagged component. The 999/999 replicate count is enrollment-wide; it does not identify which pairs used a fallback in every draw.

[Original handoff, repetition 2](repeat-2/original-handoff.md) compared the published retrospective fit with one using quiet weeks available through the reporting date. An independent replay kept the current workbook, time axis, wave setting, joint fit, anchor and pooling, restricting the quiet mask to dates through December 26, 2022. For 2022 the three estimates were 1.341178, 1.247183 and 0.929916, with two flagged original component fits. The published retrospective counterparts were 1.046204, 1.067703 and 1.020550. For 2021 the restricted estimates were 1.004894, 1.037021 and 1.031970, with no flagged original components. This is a sensitivity comparison of how much follow-up informs the fit. The paper explicitly describes a retrospective method. No interval was calculated for these alternative fits.

## Final fit mask at the same reporting date

[Previous-edition same-context review, repetition 3](repeat-3/evaluated-same.md) isolated the different final fit masks in the paper and code. I reproduced that comparison by changing only the final fit mask in memory, preserving the full retrospective dates, joint seed, reference-week normalization, wave exponent and pooling. Removing the extra anchor weeks **from the fit** leaves the three 2021 points identical. For 2022 the quiet-only points become 1.576495, 1.066180 and 0.676298 (two original fits flagged). The executable quiet-plus-anchor fit gives 1.046204, 1.067703 and 1.020550 (four flagged). The contrast's reference week was identical in both replays. This quantifies the consequence of a previously registered mismatch in PR #5 item 3; it does not choose a replacement estimator.
