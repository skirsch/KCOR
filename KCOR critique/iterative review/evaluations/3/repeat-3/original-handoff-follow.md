## Addendum to the open review

**Major revision remains my assessment.** I would withdraw four specific objections for this edition: the claim that the paper gets hazard rescaling wrong, the claim that the core reconstruction uses Euler steps, the objection to splicing calendar-time blocks in the revised Table 8 bootstrap, and the claim that the post-wave algorithm merely carries a constant offset forward. The earlier review’s eight numbered objections have the following status.

1. **Hazard-scale invariance — resolved for the core model.** The abstract, §1.5, §2.4.4, and SI now distinguish the two operations. Multiplying the *observed* hazard by \(c\) gives \((k,\theta)\mapsto(ck,\theta/c)\). Multiplying the *individual baseline* by \(c\) preserves \(\theta\). Under a common observed-hazard rescaling, both inverted cumulative hazards scale by \(c\), so their ratio is preserved. A sustained cohort-specific baseline multiplier remains inseparable from that cohort’s \(k\). The earlier objection concerned invariance of the fitted **variance** and has been answered. 2. **Identification and residual checks — the mathematical objections are resolved; practical identification remains uncertain.** The paper now gives \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) and both curvature counterexamples. Thus an exactly flat positive hazard identifies \(\theta=\gamma/h\) under the stated fixed-slope model. The revised diagnostic also compares the inverted curve with the fitted cumulative Gompertz curve, allowing a window offset. What remains unsupported is the claim that slope error generally cancels in cohort ratios. The same error \(\delta\gamma\) shifts the locally inferred variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\), which can differ greatly across cohorts. More broadly, every candidate \(\theta\) gives the same observed \(H\) with baseline \(B_\theta=\operatorname{expm1}(\theta H)/\theta\). Diagnostics computed from that \(H\) cannot distinguish those explanations. The revised Czech scan makes the practical issue visible: all 78 fits passed, yet dose-1 estimates ranged from effectively zero to 7.70 or 14.51 depending on birth decade and window. Report profiles and downstream KCOR sensitivity to alternative baselines. 3. **Initial health, anchoring, and the skip — the estimand objection still applies.** Giving each cohort mean-one frailty puts its initial mean risk into its own \(k\). Even with perfect inversion and no treatment effect, two cohorts with different initial health can therefore have an unanchored ratio \(k_A/k_B\). Anchoring removes any proportional level difference present from the start, including a genuine sustained effect. A flat age-shift control tests subsequent drift. The paper now explicitly acknowledges that outcomes confined to the skipped weeks are missed. The remaining reporting need is to identify the survivors at that landmark and show their observed risks, risk differences, and anchored and unanchored contrasts in the same orientation. The SI raw table still runs from enrollment and gives Dose 0/Dose 2 as 0.3159 overall versus 1.28–2.13 in the displayed age bands. In the separate Table 8 pseudo-dose comparison, my same-sample wave-off, \(\theta=0\) calculation already gave anchored 2-versus-0 ratios of 1.0243 and 1.0287. 4. **Fitter, fallback, and weekly arithmetic — two objections remain and one is resolved.** The ordinary workbook still defaults to estimating \(k\) from early observed hazards. Those hazards already contain depletion, so dividing them by unit Gompertz increments can underestimate \(k\) even without noise. Table 8 enables the joint seed explicitly, and the final fit includes anchor weeks outside the quiet windows. The fallback objection also stands. Insufficient deaths or a nonpositive first-pass gap offset can return \(\theta=0\) with `success=True`, and the reporting path uses that value. The clean-gap example in the open review showed why the distinction matters: the seed recovered 4, and a roundoff-sized offset erased it. **The Euler objection is resolved.** The core now predicts integrated weekly hazards and reconstructs them using the exact `expm1` increment, including the zero-variance limit. The remaining repair is to use the same fitter across entry points and distinguish an estimated zero from an uninformative or failed fit. 8. **Wave correction — the in-wave objection survives; the constant-offset tail objection is obsolete.** Under proportional depletion, the survivor gamma has scale \(\theta/(1+\theta B)\), variance \(\theta/(1+\theta B)^2\), and squared coefficient of variation \(\theta\). The code passes the *scale* to a mean-one gamma moment calculation. For a nonlinear wave, survivor weights additionally contain \(\exp(-Wz^\alpha)\), which generally changes the shape and affects later quiet windows. The paper now acknowledges that its in-wave moment is a proxy. It still divides cumulative excess by the current factor and applies a cumulative maximum. Supplying external efficacy cannot identify the survivor distribution or correct that accumulation rule. **After exit**, the revised conditional-survival equation is mathematically appropriate given the exit exposures. Its test verifies recovery of a known later baseline increment conditional on the supplied wave exposure. It leaves the accuracy of the in-wave exposure unresolved. This matters empirically because Table 8 uses forced \(\alpha=1.18\) while the manuscript declares the Czech exponent unidentified. Wave-off results should be primary, with the forced value explicitly labeled as sensitivity analysis.
