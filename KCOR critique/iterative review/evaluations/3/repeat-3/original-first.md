# Independent scientific review of KCOR

**Major revision.** The gamma-mixture survival identity and its inversion for a *given* frailty variance are correct. The paper also makes a valuable distinction between a descriptive contrast and a causal treatment effect, and appropriately declines to report the Czech wave exponent as identified. My concerns concern how the variance is learned, what the resulting ratio means, and whether the evidence actually tests the proposed estimator. The strongest empirical problem is directly verifiable: the headline Cox comparison gives KCOR the simulator's true frailty variance although the paper explicitly says it estimates that variance from the observations.

## Identification and interpretation

1. **The claimed scale invariance is false as written.** Section 2.4.4 explicitly defines scaling as multiplying the *observed* hazard by a constant. Write the model cumulative hazard as H(k,θ) = log(1 + θkG)/θ, with G(t) = (exp(γt)−1)/γ. Then cH(k,θ) = H(ck,θ/c) exactly. Doubling both cohorts' observed hazards halves both inferred variances, even in noiseless data. The two inverted cumulative hazards both double, so their *ratio* is invariant under that common transformation. That is a different statement. A multiplier acting on the *individual baseline* instead changes k to ck with θ unchanged and changes the depletion trajectory. Cohort-specific multipliers of the individual baseline are absorbed by the separately fitted k values under this model. Specify the scale and timing of each effect and correct the abstract, §1.5 and §2.4.4.

2. **There is a narrower, provable identification result.** In a quiet interval satisfying the exact Gompertz individual baseline and proportional gamma model, the marginal hazard h satisfies h′ = γh − θh². With known γ and h>0, θ = (γh−h′)/h² at the population level. Thus θ=0 can produce nonzero curvature in cumulative hazard, and θ>0 with k=γ/θ can produce *zero* curvature. The asserted equivalence in §2.4.4 is wrong. Even an exactly flat hazard identifies θ given the fixed growth rate in this special case. Practically, a slope error δγ shifts the local inferred θ by δγ/h; the effect can be large in low-risk strata. Beyond the baseline restriction, every θ≥0 fits the same observed H exactly with baseline Bθ=(exp(θH)−1)/θ (and B₀=H). One data-generating explanation has no frailty and a changing individual hazard, another has gamma frailty and a different individual hazard. Every diagnostic using only those same observed paths has identical data under the two explanations. Prove the restricted result, quantify practical identification with profiles and plausible alternative baseline paths, and stop treating diagnostic acceptance as evidence that depletion generated the curvature. In the Gompertz model the expected inverted cumulative curve is Gompertz, so residual checks should target that curve or use G(t) as the time axis. See paper §§2.4.2–2.4.4, 2.6.2 and SI §S5.

3. **The raw, inverted and anchored contrasts answer different questions.** Separately normalizing frailty to mean one absorbs each cohort's initial mean risk into k. Two cohorts can have identical variance, no treatment effect, and different baseline scales because one is healthier at entry. Even perfect inversion then returns their baseline-scale ratio. Dividing by the ratio at an anchor forces any sustained proportional difference present from entry to disappear, including a genuine constant effect. The anchored age-shift result can therefore be exactly flat even with no frailty adjustment. Its horizontal shape tests drift under that construction, not recovery of the absolute neutralized contrast. Show observed risks and risk differences, unanchored and anchored ratios with the same orientation, and a θ=0 analysis on the identical samples. Specify that the skip targets survivors at the landmark and excludes early outcome differences. Show age-standardized observed outcomes for comparison, especially given that the SI raw Dose 0/Dose 2 ratio is 0.316 overall but above one in every displayed age band. See paper §§1.6, 2.1.1, 2.7, 3.1.2 and SI §§S5.6, S6.1.2.

## The software and evidence

4. **There are multiple estimators in the evidence presented as one.** Paper §2.5 fits k and θ jointly in the nearest quiet window. In `code/KCOR.py:1224–1240, 1375–1382`, the reference fixes k to the mean of early post-skip observed hazards divided by exp(γt), including bins outside that window, then fits θ alone. Even noise-free, later anchor terms contain the depletion denominator and generally underestimate the individual baseline scale. The same code returns θ=0 with success for insufficient deaths or a nonpositive first-pass offset, then can calculate an identity-path result (`code/KCOR.py:1253–1276, 1460–1508, 7058, 7098–7117`). The text promises to withhold unidentified normalized results. A legitimate negative deviation or mere sampling noise is insufficient reason to substitute homogeneity. Make the scientific estimator, implementational fallbacks and output gate agree. Use a joint count likelihood with exact weekly event probabilities 1−exp[−(H(t+1)−H(t))], or document and validate the actual alternative. The reconstruction update in §2.5 and `code/KCOR.py:1451–1458` is forward Euler. For a weekly integrated hazard u, the exact increment is (1+θB)expm1(θu)/θ. Use the same exact inversion in fitting and reporting, including the θ→0 limit.

5. **The Cox demonstration uses an oracle.** Paper §2.11.1 says both methods have identical information and KCOR does not exploit simulator knowledge. In `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:209–235, 278–285`, cohort A is assigned θ=0 and cohort B is assigned the generating θ. The KCOR figure's plotting function plots a point series without the claimed bootstrap band (`test/sim_grid/code/plot_cox_bias_sim.py:59–85`). Moreover the marginal HR in this design is 1/(1+θB(t)) for B versus A even when the conditional treatment multiplier is one. A cohort-only Cox fit detects genuine differences in the marginal survival distributions. RMST also has a well-defined, generally non-null observed-population target. Evaluate each estimator against its own population truth, distinguish target mismatch from statistical bias, and compare oracle, blind estimated-θ and unadjusted arms over independent replicates. Fit or omit the scaled proxy curve for the shared-frailty comparator (§2.11.1). An additional example has different provenance: the “representative” figure in §2.1 uses a monthly sensitivity pipeline with slope removal h×exp(−bt), and filters for a near-null endpoint and an improvement in its curvature metric. See `code/generate_kcor_empirical_intuition_figure.py:100–145, 310–317`, `code/kcor_mortality_sensitivity.py:30,162` and `code/kcor_mortality.py:355–375`. Regenerate it using the revised algorithm and an outcome-independent cohort rule.

6. **The interval calibration exercise cannot establish repeated-sampling coverage.** The coverage code defines “truth” as KCOR estimated on one reference realization by the very estimator being tested (`test/sim_grid/code/compute_bootstrap_coverage.py:201–226`). Several outer scenarios return deterministic, fractional expected deaths, so changing their seeds does not generate new datasets (`test/sim_grid/code/generate_sim_grid.py:327–395`). The grid also fits a one-window cumulative-hazard model, not the reported Gompertz delta estimator (same file, lines 854–871). The saved 500×200 results record a “true” gamma-null KCOR of 1.00944, four 100% KCOR coverages and a non-gamma coverage of 25.4%; θ coverage in the gamma-null scenario is 35.4% and 18.8% for the two cohorts (`test/sim_grid/out/bootstrap_coverage.csv` and `bootstrap_coverage_theta.csv`). These are implementation clues, not calibration measurements for the proposed method. Paper §2.9.1 resamples calendar-time blocks of (deaths, at-risk), which can create incompatible risk sets at joins and change the identity of quiet/wave weeks. The coverage code instead independently Poisson-resamples weekly counts and rebuilds risk sets (lines 76–121); a reported-output path uses a Nelson–Aalen-style normal approximation on post-anchor increments (`code/KCOR.py:4377–4410`). Specify a single interval procedure per reported result. Generate stochastic outer survival paths, use analytic known targets, resample subjects or aggregate death-week/survivor categories for fixed fully followed cohorts, preserve calendar alignment and shared memberships, and refit the full pipeline. Report unconditional and diagnostic-pass coverage, pass rates, Monte Carlo errors and widths. Until that is done, the nominal 95% labels have no demonstrated calibration here.

7. **The contamination “threshold” is already crossed without contamination.** Section 3.3.2 identifies a 66%-of-baseline constant addition as the first crossing above one. In `test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv:2,7–10`, the uncontaminated comorbidity series has mean 1.034 and 75% of its 20 replicates above one; the 66% cell has mean 1.062 and 65% above one. The uncontaminated dynamic series has mean 1.063. A grid crossing conditional on ignoring the zero cell gives no empirical lower bound on bias in Czech data. Also, a positive addition to the outcome hazard directly changes observed outcome accumulation; the test conflates that direct contribution with distortion of fitted θ. Its anchor is skip+4, whereas production uses skip+4−1, and contamination starts at rebased week 4 (`test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py:209–217, 251–278`, `code/KCOR.py:815`). The direct exposure of the anchor to contamination can even change the direction of the result. Use paired random draws, common anchor, an oracle-θ arm and separate evaluation-only data, report changes from the clean case with uncertainty, and limit the conclusion to the tested perturbations.

8. **The optional wave correction lacks a compatible survival model.** Under the core model with entry θ, the survivor gamma distribution has shape 1/θ and scale θ/(1+θB). Its mean is 1/(1+θB), its variance is θ/(1+θB)², and its squared coefficient of variation stays θ. `propagate_theta` returns the *scale*, while `gamma_moment_alpha` treats that value as the variance of a new mean-one gamma and returns one at α=1 (`code/KCOR.py:1028–1047, 2591–2594`). It therefore does not compute the stated survivor moment. More fundamentally, adding an individual wave hazard proportional to z^α weights survivors by exp(−Bz−Cz^α), which generally breaks gamma conjugacy for α≠1 and feeds back into later quiet windows. Dividing already accumulated excess by the *current* F(t) also rescales past increments and differs from accumulating appropriately corrected weekly increments; cumulative-maximum repair and carrying a terminal offset forward do not derive this transformation. External efficacy can fix a relative scale but cannot cure these issues. Keep the optional module inactive pending an explicit joint model with correctly propagated survivor moments, weekly count predictions and validation from individual-level wave simulations. See paper §§2.7.1–2.7.4, 3.4.2–3.4.4.

I would retain the core inversion as a model-dependent sensitivity analysis. The most informative revision would release one traceable end-to-end estimator with prespecified failure handling, reproduce every headline figure from that estimator, and put empirical risk contrasts and model sensitivity beside the transformed curves. The present evidence does not establish that a residual Czech contrast is protected against selection bias. This review checks the supplied equations, source paths and saved numerical outputs. I did not rerun the full registry or simulation analyses.
