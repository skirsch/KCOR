### 2. The production fitter and the manuscript fitter give materially different inference

Methods specifies a joint first-window fit of \(k\) and \(\theta\). Production fixes \(k\) from the first post-skip weeks, fits only \(\theta\) in the seed window, and includes the early anchor weeks in the final fit mask even when they fall outside the designated quiet windows. Table 8 obtains its joint fit by replacing part of the production function’s source at runtime. The weekly reconstruction is now exact, but the fitting objective still compares an **integrated weekly hazard** with a **point hazard**. There is also a one-bin offset error: the code indexes the last gap week but reads the reconstruction stored *before* that week’s increment. A shock confined to the last gap week is therefore absent from the offset applied to the next quiet window. ### 4. The post-wave equation is sound conditional on exit exposures. The in-wave calculation can supply the wrong exposures

The revision correctly writes survivor probability as \(L(B,W)=E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) and solves a conditional survival equation after wave exit. The remaining error is the estimate fed into that equation. During the wave, the code first applies ordinary gamma inversion, subtracts the reference baseline, and divides accumulated excess by a moment computed at the endpoint. Its moment routine uses a mean-one gamma distribution with a changed variance parameter. Even under baseline-only depletion, the surviving gamma population has a **changed mean and unchanged shape**. A wave proportional to \(Z^\alpha\) changes the survivor law further. [In-wave and post-wave equations](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/documentation/preprint/paper.md:582) · [Moment and propagation routines](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:1028) · [In-wave calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2653)

A noiseless example isolates the error. Take mean-one exponential frailty (\(\theta=1\)), \(\alpha=2\), true baseline exposure \(B=1\), and true wave exposure \(W=0.25\). The model gives survival \(L=\int_0^\infty e^{-2z-0.25z^2}\,dz=0.452677\). Even granting the estimator the exact baseline, ordinary inversion gives \(1/L-1=1.209080\). Its code then uses \(F=1.452677\) and attributes \((1.209080-1)/F=\mathbf{0.143928}\) to the wave. **The true value is 0.25.** Neither sampling noise nor a failed quiet-window fit is involved. [Exit attribution used by the implementation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m42/code/KCOR.py:2702)

**Fix.** Use the joint survival equation *during* the wave as well. Given an independently justified baseline path \(B(t)\) and specified \(\alpha\), infer \(W(t)\) from \(L(B(t),W(t))=S_{\rm obs}(t)\), with nonnegative increments if the assumed wave process requires them. Propagate the resulting survivor law to later weeks. If baseline and wave exposures cannot be separated, report the range under explicit assumptions. The exact post-wave solver is worth keeping.

### 5. Narrow the identification claim to what the data can establish

Section 2.4.4 now correctly distinguishes rescaling observed hazards from rescaling individual baseline hazards. The abstract and discussion still make the broader invariance claim. The proposition also still equates nonzero cumulative-hazard curvature with positive frailty variance. Under the stated quiet-period model, the observable hazard \(h\) satisfies
\[
\frac{d}{dt}\log h(t)=\gamma-\theta h(t).
\]
With known correct \(\gamma\) and ideal data, this identifies \(\theta\) parametrically. But \(\theta=0\) with Gompertz growth already gives curved cumulative hazard, and \(\theta k=\gamma>0\) gives constant observed hazard and a straight cumulative hazard. More fundamentally, a population with no frailty and a common time-varying individual hazard equal to the observed \(h(t)\) produces the same death-count distribution. Every diagnostic based on those counts then has the same distribution. The revised discussion acknowledges this ambiguity more accurately than the proposition and supplement do. **Fix.** State the conditional identification result, give its derivation, and show how the inferred contrast changes over plausible baseline slopes, windows, and frailty distributions. Report observed absolute mortality alongside unanchored and anchored KCOR. That leaves readers with an interpretable scientific result: how much of the observed difference remains under each specified depletion model.

Before accepting claims that the fitted method detects effects or removes selection-induced distortion, I would require regenerated end-to-end exhibits, coverage for the exact released fitter, and a Czech analysis that follows its own diagnostic and wave-use rules. The most informative result in `m42` is the **83.7% coverage despite 600 diagnostic passes**. It directly measures a limitation that the manuscript presently asks its diagnostics to resolve.
