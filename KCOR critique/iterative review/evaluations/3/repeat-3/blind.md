**I would request major revision.** KCOR’s gamma-frailty inversion is mathematically sound when the frailty variance is known and the model holds. The consequential problems are in estimating that variance, deciding when the estimate is usable, and reporting what the resulting ratio means. In this edition, the fitter can discard a correct frailty estimate on noiseless data, and the principal Czech control table reports results for fits that triggered the method’s own insufficient-signal rule. ### 1. A quiet gap can erase genuine frailty

I checked the joint fitter in memory with exact weekly hazards from its own model. I used \(k=0.001\), \(\gamma=0.085/52\), true frailty variance \(\theta=4\), and no wave or sampling noise. Fitting weeks 0–19 gave **3.99999993**. Adding a second, equally valid quiet window at weeks 40–59 changed the applied estimate to **0**. The reconstructed gap offset was \(-2.65\times10^{-11}\), numerically indistinguishable from its correct value of zero. The code treats *any* nonpositive first-pass offset as insufficient frailty signal, returns zero variance, and sets `success=True`. A gap with no excess mortality is entirely compatible with substantial frailty. [Weekly hazard equation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1126) · [Offset decision](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1474) · [Zero-variance return](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1496)

**Fix.** Estimate the gap offset separately from frailty. Allow zero excess while retaining the quiet-week information about \(\theta\). If a materially negative offset violates the chosen wave model, flag that assumption explicitly. A floating-point tolerance fixes this particular example, but sampling noise will also put genuine zero offsets on either side of zero. The two-window example should become a regression test.

### 2. The default program and the manuscript estimate different quantities

The manuscript specifies a joint first-window fit of \(k,\theta\), followed by a refit over the quiet windows. The ordinary workbook call uses the default `joint_seed=False`, which fixes \(k\) from the early post-skip hazards. Even in joint mode, the pooled refit adds early anchor weeks whether or not they are quiet. Table 8 explicitly overrides the default to enable the joint fit. The choice matters: for 2022 pseudo-dose 2 versus 0, the joint estimate is **1.0677** and the fixed-rate estimate is **1.0129** on the same input. [Manuscript fitting objective](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:380) · [Default and fit mask](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1236) · [Workbook call](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:7006) · [Table override](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/compare.py:9) · [Paired estimates](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json:48)

The output also changes the **estimand**. The paper defines the primary KCOR as the ratio of transformed cumulative hazards. The workbook column named `KCOR` divides that ratio by its early value. If one cohort has twice the transformed cumulative hazard at every time, the primary KCOR is 2 and the workbook KCOR is 1 throughout. Skipping early deaths also makes follow-up conditional on surviving to the new origin. An anchored age-shift control near 1 therefore tests stability of *relative growth* after the anchor. It provides little evidence about recovery of an absolute level difference. [Estimand and anchor](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:153) · [Post-skip origin](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:487) · [Workbook calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:4422)

**Fix.** Use one explicit estimator configuration for the paper, workbook, and bootstrap. Export observed survival, the unanchored transformed ratio, and the anchored ratio under distinct names. State the survivor population, anchor, fit weeks, and reporting horizon for each result.

### 3. Table 8 does not satisfy the stated diagnostic gate

I reproduced all six published Table 8 point estimates from the supplied workbook. The underlying resampling preserves subgroup overlap and death week, which is a strength. The problem is what happens **after** resampling. For 2022, four of the nine original subgroup fits had insufficient signal, and **999 of 999** bootstrap draws had at least one such fit. For 2021, **307 of 999** draws did. The code retains those draws in the percentiles. The 2022 interval for pseudo-dose 1 versus 0 is **0.322–1.268**. Its inclusion of 1 is compatible with a wide range of departures from unity. These are intervals for a procedure that includes a zero-frailty fallback, despite the manuscript’s statement that reporting is conditional on diagnostic acceptance. [Reporting rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:95) · [Resampling and fallback](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:63) · [Percentile calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:161) · [Flag counts and intervals](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.json:32)

The ordinary workbook uses yet another interval calculation. It centers the interval on the anchored ratio, computes its standard error from *post-anchor differences* in cumulative hazards, and omits uncertainty from the fitted frailty and random anchor. The repository’s paired simulation illustrates why fitter choice matters: the fixed-rate intervals covered in **160/200** datasets and the joint-fit intervals in **194/200**, with substantially wider joint intervals. Those results do not establish coverage for the final weekly estimator. **Fix.** For the current edition, label the 2022 result as a fallback analysis and withdraw its use as a passed validation. After repairing the fitter, measure how often the complete procedure issues a result, how often issued intervals cover, and how often it issues a misleading result under misspecification. Use the same interval procedure for the same estimand throughout.

### 4. Good fit cannot establish that the curvature came from depletion

For any observed cumulative hazard \(H(t)\) and any proposed \(\theta>0\), the baseline \(B_\theta(t)=[\exp(\theta H(t))-1]/\theta\) reproduces exactly that same observed curve under gamma frailty. The paper correctly narrows identification by fixing a Gompertz slope in quiet periods. Under that restriction, \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\). This also shows the sensitivity directly: holding the observed hazard and its derivative fixed, changing the assumed slope by \(\delta\gamma\) changes the implied variance by \(\delta\gamma/h\). Cohorts with different hazard levels can respond quite differently to the same slope error. [Inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:297) · [Slope-cancellation claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:332) · [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354)

The Czech scan makes the distinction concrete. All **78/78** fits passed, while moving the window took the dose-1 variance from effectively zero to **7.70** in one birth decade and **14.51** in another. The pass rule checks individual fit status and degeneracy. It does not test stability across windows. The Czech configuration also says 2023 was chosen because it showed the least vaccine harm, which qualifies the claim of outcome-independent prespecification. [Supplementary scan](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304) · [Actual pass rule](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/quiet_windows/scan.py:70) · [Window-selection comment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/data/Czech/Czech.yaml:22)

**Fix.** Present KCOR as a sensitivity analysis under specified baseline and frailty assumptions. Show the *final contrast* across plausible slopes, windows, anchors, and frailty distributions so readers can distinguish unstable parameters from unstable conclusions. Select confirmatory windows independently of the contrast and evaluate predictions in held-out periods.

### 5. The optional wave correction enters the principal control despite failing identification

The manuscript concludes that the Czech data do not identify the wave exponent \(\alpha\). The Czech configuration nevertheless forces **1.18**, explicitly labeled a sensitivity override, and the Table 8 script requires it. In my in-memory replay, changing only \(\alpha\) to the identity value of 1 moved the 2021 pseudo-dose 2 versus 0 estimate from **1.0312 to 1.0423**, and the 2022 pseudo-dose 2 versus 1 estimate from **1.0205 to 1.0053**. [Non-identification finding](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:923) · [Czech configuration](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/data/Czech/Czech.yaml:14) · [Required override and calculation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py:49)

There is a separate mathematical limitation. Under a nonlinear wave, survivors are weighted by \(\exp(-zB-z^\alpha W)\). The in-wave code uses a mean-one gamma moment as a proxy and divides *all accumulated excess* by the current week’s factor. The manuscript acknowledges this approximation. Its post-wave conditional-survival equation is sound **given the exit exposures**, but inherits errors in those exposures. There is also a smaller, directly fixable inconsistency: the wave reference uses \(k e^{\gamma t}\) for a weekly increment, while the fitter uses the exact integral \(k e^{\gamma t}\operatorname{expm1}(\gamma)/\gamma\). Even a pure Gompertz baseline can therefore register positive wave excess. [In-wave and post-wave equations](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:576) · [Fitted weekly increment](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:1241) · [Wave implementation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/code/KCOR.py:2658)

**Fix.** Publish the core \(\alpha=1\) analysis first and label forced values as sensitivity analyses. Reuse the exact weekly increment. For a model-based nonlinear correction, fit the weekly survival probabilities using the same \(E[\exp(-ZB-Z^\alpha W)]\) model during and after the wave, with an independently justified baseline and effect scale.

### 6. The controls establish less than the conclusions imply

The headline Cox comparison gives KCOR the **generating frailty variances**. It checks the inversion under its generating model. It cannot measure how well KCOR learns those variances from registry counts. Cox and RMST also have different targets: two cohorts with different frailty distributions can genuinely have different marginal survival under identical individual treatment effects. A non-null marginal summary alone establishes no bias for its own target. The paper also discloses that its shared-frailty comparison curve is interpolated and its broader comparison grid uses an earlier estimator. [Known-variance comparison](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:700) · [Shared-frailty curve](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:737) · [Earlier-estimator grid](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

The quiet-window contamination experiment also cannot establish the claimed empirical bound on Czech confounding. Even at **zero contamination**, the two sets of 20 replicates have mean anchored KCORs of **1.063** and **1.034**, with 70% and 75% above 1. Calling the first positive grid cell with mean above 1 a “sign reversal” gives too much meaning to a noisy crossing of a null value. The generator also adds actual deaths to one cohort, so treatment-effect truth and all-cause outcome truth must be specified separately. [Simulation results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/test/quiet_window_contamination/out/quiet_contamination_agg.csv:2) · [Death generator](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/test/quiet_window_contamination/run_contamination_test.py:151) · [Published interpretation](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:853)

**Fix.** Keep the known-variance exercise as an algebra check. Validate the released fitter on independently sampled cohorts with known targets for each method. Include true effects inside fitting windows, smooth baseline drift, zero-excess gaps, and non-gamma frailty. For contamination, use paired uncontaminated runs, report Monte Carlo uncertainty, and evaluate prespecified materially important errors in both directions.

The publishable contribution is a transparent way to ask **how a cohort comparison changes under a specified depletion model**. The evidence in this edition does not justify treating the transformation as a prerequisite for survival analysis or using a residual Czech contrast to attribute mortality to vaccination.
