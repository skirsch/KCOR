# Addendum to the saved KCOR review

**My recommendation remains major revision.** Three archived objections can be closed for the current edition: the lost last week of a gap, the forced loss of the first week of a wave, and the obsolete post-wave regression test. The revised PDF also corrects the Cox figure and the quiet-window pass-rate presentation. The main unresolved issues are the nonlinear wave calculation, the difference between the advertised and default fitters, and the interpretation of results after diagnostic failures. This addendum leaves the saved open answer intact. ### Identification and interpretation

**The curvature and scale objections are corrected in the formal statements.** Section 2.4.4 gives \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\) and both counterexamples from the archived review. The abstract and SI now distinguish scaling individual baseline hazards, which preserves frailty variance, from scaling observed cohort hazards by \(c\), which changes the fitted variance to \(\theta/c\). Under the latter transformation the reconstructed baseline scales by \(c\), so a common multiplier can cancel in the ratio while changing both variance estimates. The remaining identification limitation is substantive: for any observed \(H\), each candidate \(q\) admits \(B_q=\operatorname{expm1}(qH)/q\), with \(B_0=H\). Distinguishing those explanations requires the quiet-window baseline assumption. **The straightness criterion is corrected in the methods and still inconsistent in the workbook diagnostics.** Section 2.6.2 specifies the fitted cumulative Gompertz curve with its window offset, or linearity in \((e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). Its weekly hazard residual is also correct. The workbook still calculates and exports `max_abs_dev` from a straight-line regression on ordinary elapsed years. That number measures a different departure. Label it as a descriptive time trend or calculate deviations from the fitted offset-Gompertz curve. The general claim that diagnostics reveal every violation has also largely been repaired: §2.6.2 and SI §S2 explicitly allow undetected misspecification. The assurance in §2.14 that diagnostics “will fail” for unsuitable designs should be qualified consistently. **The age-shift and skip objections still matter.** Giving two age groups the same vaccination category removes an intervention contrast by construction. It gives no guarantee that the groups have parallel age-specific mortality trends. Anchoring can also make any constant reference-hazard ratio appear exactly 1. Separately, zeroing the skipped increments while retaining the surviving risk set makes the subsequent observed cumulative hazard \(-\log[S(t)/S(t_{\rm skip})]\). The paper describes this rebasing and acknowledges that an acute effect in the skipped weeks is lost. I would label the resulting estimates explicitly as conditional on survival to the first accumulating week and show absolute mortality from original enrollment, unanchored KCOR, and anchored KCOR together.
