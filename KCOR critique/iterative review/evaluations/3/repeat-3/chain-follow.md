## Addendum to the open review of `m83`

The preceding open review stands as a separate answer. I checked the earlier objections against the current manuscript and code. Two findings deserve particular attention: **a shock before the first quiet window still aliases with the fitted baseline rate**, and **the configured Czech collapse objective cannot produce the reported interior optimum for \(\alpha\)**.

### Identification and interpretation

1. **“Frailty is identified by nonzero curvature, and common hazard scaling leaves its variance unchanged.” Resolved as a mathematical objection.** The revised proposition correctly gives \(\theta=(\gamma h-h')/h^2\) for a positive instantaneous hazard under a known Gompertz slope. It gives both counterexamples to the old curvature claim. It also correctly distinguishes multiplying the observed cohort hazard, which changes the fitted variance to \(\theta/c\), from multiplying the individual baseline hazard, which preserves frailty variance. The quiet-window diagnostic now uses the fitted cumulative Gompertz shape. [Scaling and proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345) · [Revised shape diagnostic](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m83/documentation/preprint/paper.md:466)

2. **“A common error in the Gompertz slope need not cancel in KCOR.” Still applies.** The paper still claims first-order cancellation and says a sensitivity analysis confirms stability. Its own proposition implies that, at a fixed observed \(h,h'\), changing \(\gamma\) changes the locally implied \(\theta\) by \(\Delta\gamma/h\). Cohorts at different hazard levels respond differently. I also checked the `2022_06` Table 8 joint fit with the wave correction off. Using annual slopes **0.065, 0.085, and 0.105**, the anchored pseudo-dose 2 versus 1 ratios were **1.00131, 1.00527, and 0.99399**. The number of flagged component fits was **4, 4, and 6**. These were reviewer-selected sensitivity values around the configured 0.085. The contrast stays close to one, but even its direction and fit status are sensitive here. Publish the slope grid, contrasts, and flags behind the manuscript’s claim. 3. **“The seed fit can absorb a wave that precedes the first quiet window.” Still applies, independently of the repaired gap index.** The first-window objective sets the prior offset to zero and subsequently freezes \(k\). Under the paper’s weekly equation, a true accumulated pre-window excess \(a\) gives exactly the same quiet-window predictions as a zero-offset model with \(k'=k/(1+\theta a)\) and the same \(\theta\). Even perfect residuals cannot distinguish those baseline rates. The code adds offsets only at entry to the *second and later* quiet segments. This matters for `2022_06`: enrollment falls inside the configured wave, and its first subsequent quiet window starts in week `2022-22`. Include pre-window history in the seed model or show sensitivity to the opening offset. 4. **“Diagnostics cannot establish that curvature came from selection or that error is conservative.” The limitation remains; the blanket guarantees have been corrected.** A homogeneous population with individual hazard equal to the observed cohort hazard produces the same death-count distribution as the fitted frailty population. `m83` now acknowledges that such a disturbance can pass every check, that quiet-window overlap can escape detection, and that crossover can change the contrast in either direction. The objection still applies to the stronger claim that the tested contamination grid bounds the explanation for the actual Czech cohorts. A grid yielding mean ratios from **0.782 to 1.194** measures behavior under those generating mechanisms; it does not measure how much contamination occurred in the registry. 5. **“Mean frailty, the stabilization skip, and anchoring change the estimand.” Still applies. ### Validation and comparators

6. **“The Cox and RMST comparisons use the wrong null for their own estimands.” Still applies to the comparative claims.** The paper now correctly states that the treatment hazard ratio *at fixed frailty* is one. But in its simulation the marginal hazard ratio B/A is \(1/(1+\theta_B H_0(t))\). A cohort-only Cox fit therefore sees a genuine marginal difference, and unequal observed survival gives genuinely different restricted mean survival times. Table 11 still calls this “depletion bias.” The shared-frailty Cox curve is also still an extrapolation: the plotting code multiplies each ordinary Cox deviation by the same factor derived from **0.87 and 0.94**. The caption discloses that construction. It supplies no grid-wide performance evidence for shared-frailty Cox. Score each method against its own population target and fit each displayed comparator. 11. **“Agreement between the pairwise and collapse estimates independently supports \(\alpha\).” Still applies, with a stronger implementation finding.** For common residuals and equal weights, the sum of squared pairwise differences within a week is proportional to the sum of squared deviations from their mean. Actual weights differ, so the two objectives need not coincide. More importantly, the configured primary mode is `reference_anchored`. In that mode `neutralize_excess` returns the original excess and the calculated factor separately. The collapse objective **discards the factor** and calculates dispersion of the unchanged excess. Its value is therefore constant in \(\alpha\). I checked the function on a three-cohort input: it returned **4.1676955992** at \(\alpha=1,\ 1.185,\ 1.3\). The reported interior collapse minimum at **1.185** needs a different documented configuration or corrected code and regenerated output. The manuscript’s overall conclusion that Czech \(\alpha\) is unidentified remains appropriate. The distinct \(\alpha=1\) identity objection is resolved; the in-wave approximation for \(\alpha\ne1\) remains and is now disclosed.
