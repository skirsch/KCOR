**I would request major revision.** KCOR has a sound mathematical core. Given a known mean-one gamma frailty distribution with variance \(\theta\), the manuscript correctly inverts an observed cumulative hazard \(H\) to obtain \(T_\theta(H)=(e^{\theta H}-1)/\theta\). The consequential question is how the analyst knows which \(\theta\) to use. The paper overstates what mortality curves can establish about that choice, its weekly fitting algorithm differs from its stated inversion, and its optional wave correction fails a test case specified by the authors themselves. I would retain KCOR as a **model-dependent descriptive sensitivity analysis**. The evidence in this edition does not establish reliable removal of selection bias. I reviewed the 78-page combined manuscript, its Markdown sources, and local figure artifacts under `documentation/preprint`, read-only. The findings below concern the published equations and evidence. I did not inspect implementation files or rerun simulations outside the specified inputs.

### 1. The identification proposition needs a different proof and a narrower claim

Let \(h=H'_{\rm obs}\). In a quiet period with frailty-neutral baseline hazard \(ke^{\gamma t}\), differentiating the paper’s own equations gives
\[
\frac{h'}h=\gamma-\theta h.
\]
With a known correct \(\gamma\), an ideal noiseless hazard can therefore identify \(\theta=(\gamma-h'/h)/h\). This is a genuine positive result. It also exposes two errors in the manuscript. First, observed cumulative-hazard curvature is **not equivalent** to positive frailty variance. With \(\theta=0\), a growing Gompertz baseline already produces curvature. With \(\gamma>0\) and \(\theta k=\gamma\), depletion exactly offsets baseline growth, \(h=k\), and the observed cumulative hazard is straight despite positive frailty variance. Second, an error in \(\gamma\) changes the locally implied \(\theta\) by \(\Delta\gamma/h\). The same slope error can matter quite differently in cohorts with different mortality levels. [Gompertz assumption and cancellation claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:323) · [Identification proposition](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:354)

The claim of invariance to multiplying the **observed** hazard by a common constant is also false as written. Write \(G_\gamma(t)=(e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\). Then
\[
c\,\frac{\log(1+\theta kG_\gamma)}{\theta}
=\frac{\log(1+(\theta/c)(ck)G_\gamma)}{\theta/c}.
\]
The rescaled curve fits perfectly with \(k'=ck\) and \(\theta'=\theta/c\). This holds even if both cohorts receive exactly the same multiplier. Their *ratio* can remain invariant because both reconstructed cumulative hazards scale by \(c\). Their fitted variances change. Multiplying the **individual baseline hazard before depletion** is a different operation. There it is \(k\) that changes while the frailty distribution can stay fixed. [Scaling claim, §2.4.4](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:345)

**Fix.** Prove the conditional result above, distinguish the two levels of scaling, and publish profiles for \(\theta\) and KCOR over a numerical range of \(\gamma\). Assess the fitted cumulative trajectory against \(G_\gamma(t)\) plus the allowed offset. Straightness in calendar time is a justified approximation only over a range where Gompertz growth is negligible. The supplement currently gives the slope as “Fixed implementation value” and instructs the reader to check sensitivity. [Linearity diagnostic](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:454) · [Supplementary defaults](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:169) · [Sensitivity description](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:292)

### 2. A clean diagnostic pass cannot establish that the fitted curvature came from selection

There is an exact counterexample. Generate an observed curve \(H(t)\) from the paper’s Gompertz–gamma model with positive frailty variance. Now construct a second population in which everyone has the *same* time-varying hazard \(H'(t)\). The two populations have identical survival and identical weekly death-count distributions for the same starting cohort size. Every quiet-window residual, window perturbation, and KCOR diagnostic computed from those counts has the same distribution. Yet the first population has depletion and the second has none. This matters even for a descriptive paper because it expressly claims that violations lead to conservative behavior or visible failure. Its own contamination experiment reports mean anchored values of **0.782** and **1.194** on opposite sides of its stated null benchmark. [Diagnostic guarantee](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:24) · [Conservativeness claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1023) · [Contamination results](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:860)

**Fix.** Describe a pass as compatibility with the chosen model. Separate three targets throughout the paper: observed mortality among cohort members, reconstructed cumulative hazard for a unit-frailty individual, and the anchored change in the ratio of two such reconstructions. Setting mean frailty to one *within each cohort* lets baseline health-level differences enter \(k_d\). Skipping early deaths conditions the analysis on survival to the new origin. Dividing by an early ratio then removes any constant level contrast, including a genuine one. These choices can all be useful, but each changes what the number answers. Show observed absolute mortality, unanchored KCOR, and anchored KCOR together, with one explicit anchor definition. The manuscript currently specifies both division by a single time point and division by a window mean. [Frailty scale](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:275) · [Survivor landmark](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:479) · [Point anchor](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:164) · [Window-mean anchor](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:621)

### 3. The weekly estimator and the final inversion use different mathematics

The paper defines a weekly integrated hazard \(q=-\log(1-d/N)\). If \(B\) is the reconstructed cumulative hazard at the **start** of that week, the exact gamma update is
\[
\Delta B=(1+\theta B)\frac{e^{\theta q}-1}{\theta}.
\]
Step 2 instead uses \((1+\theta B)q\). That is a first-order approximation, systematically smaller for \(\theta q>0\). Thus even with perfect inputs and the true \(\theta\), the trajectory used to calculate wave offsets can disagree with the final closed-form reconstruction. Step 1 also fits a point hazard to a weekly integrated hazard. And if a wave precedes the *first* quiet window, the seed fit omits its cumulative offset before freezing \(\hat k\) for all subsequent refits. [Weekly input](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:241) · [Seed and recursion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:371) · [Fixed-scale refit](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:414)

**Fix.** Use the exact interval increment throughout and estimate the quiet-window parameters jointly with the relevant prior history. Verify recovery on noiseless weekly data with a wave *before* the first fitting window. There is also a direct figure check. For \(\theta\ge0\), \(T_\theta(H)\ge H\). At the right edge of Figure 1, the dose-2 curve appears to fall from about **0.030** in the raw panel to **0.020** in the normalized panel. That cannot result from the stated inversion on the same input and origin. If the panels use different origins or preprocessing, label them and plot the actual input to inversion. Otherwise the figure or calculation needs correction. [Figure 1 and caption, PDF p. 6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/figures/fig_kcor_empirical_intuition.png) · [Final inversion](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:432)

### 4. The optional wave correction fails its own \(\alpha=1\) test

The paper says the extension is effectively inactive at \(\alpha=1\). This is a decisive test because the individual wave hazard is then proportional to the same frailty \(z\) as the baseline hazard. Core gamma inversion already recovers their combined unit-frailty cumulative hazard. But the proposed second step divides its positive wave excess by \(F(t;1)=E[z\mid\text{alive at }t]\). That mean is below one after depletion when \(\theta>0\). The purportedly inactive correction therefore **increases an already correctly reconstructed excess**. More generally, dividing accumulated excess by today’s \(F(t)\) does not implement a correction to each earlier hazard increment. For \(\alpha\ne1\), survival through a wave also selects on \(z^\alpha\), so a survivor distribution derived under ordinary gamma depletion cannot simply be carried through the wave. [Inactive-at-one claim](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:508) · [Hazard-level model](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:536) · [Cumulative correction](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:568)

**Fix.** Keep the module off for substantive reporting while deriving the survivor distribution and interval death probabilities under the joint baseline-plus-wave model. Require exact recovery in the no-wave and \(\alpha=1\) cases. The authors correctly withhold a Czech estimate of \(\alpha\). Agreement between the two reported optima provides little independent reassurance: with the same cohorts and equal weights, the sum of squared pairwise differences of cohort residuals is proportional to their cross-cohort variance. The two objectives can prefer the same value by construction. [Pairwise and variance objectives](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:102) · [Czech non-identification finding](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:902)

### 6. The uncertainty intervals and Czech diagnostic gate need validation

Resampling contiguous blocks of deaths and risk sets moves observations between calendar periods that have different expected hazards. Carrying the original risk sets across spliced blocks generally breaks the stated risk-set identity. Recomputing risk sets restores the arithmetic but does not restore the original event law or align the waves. The paper claims preservation of martingale covariance without establishing it. It also calls the intervals calibrated while **all five empirical coverage cells are blank** in the combined PDF. [Bootstrap algorithm and calibration language](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:635) · [Table 12, PDF p. 55](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:1266)

There is a related empirical reporting problem. The Czech gate marks every age band as passing. Several dose-2 variances are around \(10^{-17}\), which the paper elsewhere treats as a weak-identification signal. A boundary estimate **can** be informative if the data tightly exclude appreciable positive values. A yes/no table cannot distinguish that case from a nearly flat objective. The pooling description also needs reconciliation: the bootstrap section says parameters are never fitted separately by stratum, while the supplement reports independent age-band and all-age fits. The distinction matters here. The raw dose-0/dose-2 cumulative hazard ratio exceeds one in every listed age band but is **0.3159** in the full population. [Gate, Table S6](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:366) · [Fitted variances, Table S7](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:398) · [Raw contrasts, Table S8](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/supplement.md:423) · [Pooling specification](C:/Users/jasmi/AppData/Local/Temp/kcor-s3-8d29/m17/documentation/preprint/paper.md:631)

**Fix.** For the stated closed cohorts, resample people within the defined strata or, when only counts are available, resample the mutually exclusive death-week and end-of-follow-up survivor counts jointly under an explicit sampling model. Preserve calendar time and refit the entire analysis. Publish unconditional coverage, the gate pass rate, and coverage among passes against analytic simulation truth. For each Czech fit, give the event counts, actual windows, numerical slope, objective profile or one-sided bound, and the change in KCOR over the supported parameter range. Specify the age weights and order of pooling, inversion, and anchoring.

The first revision I would want to see is an end-to-end recovery table for the **unanchored core estimator**, using exact weekly probabilities, known parameters, and prespecified departures from the model. It should show when KCOR improves on the raw contrast, when it worsens it, and how often the diagnostics distinguish those cases. That would give readers a defensible basis for deciding what weight to place on a normalized mortality curve.
