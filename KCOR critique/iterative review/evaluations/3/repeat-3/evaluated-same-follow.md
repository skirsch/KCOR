## Separate addendum to the saved `m83` review

**Major revision remains my recommendation, but several objections in the archived review should be retired.** In `m83`, the weekly fit uses the correct integrated hazard, the gap offset includes the last gap week, the first wave week can contribute excess, and the post-wave regression test checks the correct conditional equation. The revised quiet-window scan also counts its denominator correctly, and the Cox figure now has genuine bootstrap intervals. The remaining objections concern what the wave calculation estimates, which fitter produced the empirical results, and how much validation those results supply. ### Identification and interpretation

- **Curvature, scaling, and linearity — the specific mathematical objections are resolved.** Section 2.4.4 now gives \(h'=\gamma h-\theta h^2\), including both counterexamples to equating curvature with frailty. It distinguishes multiplying *observed* hazards by \(c\), which sends \((k,\theta)\) to \((ck,\theta/c)\), from multiplying the individual baseline, which preserves frailty variance. Section 2.6.2 checks the fitted curve against Gompertz cumulative time \((e^{\gamma t}-1)/\gamma\) and defines weekly-model residuals. Those are the appropriate checks under the stated model. - **Identification from unrestricted mortality curves — the limitation remains, and the revision largely acknowledges it.** For any observed \(H\), each candidate \(q\geq0\) gives a baseline \(B_q=(e^{qH}-1)/q\) producing exactly that survival, with \(B_0=H\). No residual check can distinguish those explanations without restrictions on the baseline. The SI now explicitly concedes that a disturbance can pass every diagnostic. The wording in §3.3.2 that its contamination experiment empirically bounds confounding in Czech cohorts still goes too far. It tests specified constant and exponentially decaying disturbances in one cohort over one window. Report the bound for those scenarios.
