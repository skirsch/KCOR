# Round 11 targeted resolution review of m152 and m156

> Please examine KCOR closely. What is wrong, if anything, and how would you fix it?

**All ten round 10 objections are resolved in their stated scope.** Two related numerical discrepancies remain in the dedicated Table 8 calculator. It can report a large finite contrast after a required group has exhausted its risk set, and it can lose a valid pooled contrast when one group has no early deaths. Both were reproduced with modified, internally consistent versions of the published input. The ordinary workbook gives the intended result in each case. The published Table 8 histories do not exhibit either discrepancy.

The proportional inversion remains sound under the stated model. For mean-one gamma frailty of variance θ and cumulative reference exposure B, marginal survival is (1 + θB) raised to the power −1/θ. Inverting the observed cumulative hazard recovers B, with the ordinary cumulative hazard as the limit at θ = 0. This algebra leaves the adequacy of the fitted baseline, the gamma law and the descriptive cohort comparison as empirical or design assumptions.

## Related findings and concrete fixes

1. **High for affected dedicated-replay results. A required, exhausted group contributes a fabricated finite endpoint.** In the 2021-24 disjoint cells, move every remaining 1940 dose-2 death and survivor after zero-based week 29 into the death cell for week 29. Rebuild the overlapping total from those disjoint groups. At the publication date the ordinary workbook returned unavailable for 2/0 and 2/1. The dedicated `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py::estimate` returned **73.342353 and 72.173637**, respectively, with zero unsuccessful group fits. Both routes returned 1.016193 for the unaffected 1/0. The dedicated route computes deaths divided by opening risk plus epsilon, caps the death fraction at 0.999, integrates through exhaustion and pools all three rows. The arithmetic already existed in m152 for explicit zeros. The reader correction makes equivalent omitted suffixes reach that same arithmetic. Use the ordinary support and endpoint rules in the dedicated calculation and compare its result directly to the published-date pooled row, including unavailable cells.
2. **Moderate for the dedicated replay on sparse histories. Zero early deaths in one stratum invalidate every pooled pair.** Starting afresh from the 2021-24 cells, transfer the first four weeks' 1940 deaths, for each dose, to terminal survivors in the same disjoint group and reconstruct the inclusive counts. Every stratum remains at risk and every group fit succeeded. The ordinary workbook assigned 1940 weight 0.117092 and returned 0.938236, 0.912340 and 0.972400 for pairs 1/0, 2/0 and 2/1. The dedicated replay returned three NaNs. Its `nanmean` has no positive rate in the affected stratum. Its `workbook_points` checker separately reconstructs the same deficient weights and reads age rows instead of the workbook's actual pooled row. Share the Section 2.8 fallback and compare with the ordinary pooled row. This divergence predates m156. The revision supplied the intended specification.
3. **Moderate for person-resampling input integrity in the dedicated reader. Fractional people are silently truncated.** In the 10.1 fixture, add 0.5 to every opening-risk observation of 1940 dose-0 females and to its inclusive counterpart. This preserves changes in the risk sets through time. The workbook bootstrap rejected the fractional cells. The dedicated reader returned an integer cell array exactly equal to the original fixture because it applied `to_numpy(dtype=int)` before checking person counts. The cast existed in m152. Add the integer validation before conversion and reuse the same disjoint-cell checks as the workbook. Neither published enrollment contains fractional people.
4. **Low to moderate for provenance. The caption and reproduction notes identify superseded runs as current.** Section 3's discussion and SI S10 identify the refreshed primary and preceding-seed forced-alpha results correctly. The Table 8 caption still calls the sensitivity current and cites `analysis/ceiling500/table8/identified_only-table8.json` for the primary. The caption's primary archive differs from the refreshed file in 43 enrollment/draw triplets by at least 10⁻⁸, with a maximum difference of 0.001324 for 2022-06 draw 612, pair 2/0. The SI S6 source note still cites `ceiling500/s6-all-window.json`. The analysis README names both older primary paths and recommends an older archive directory as the output for a new replay. Revise those pointers to `analysis/seed_ceiling/table8-primary/` and `analysis/seed_ceiling/s6/`, label the full forced-alpha run by its preceding seed implementation, and give a fresh reproduction output directory. These descriptions became stale after m154. The displayed intervals agree at reported precision.
5. **Low for audit completeness. A bounded original seed removes its re-entry from the diagnostic table.** In the exact θ = 550 case, the fitter knows the gap re-enters at week 70. It returns `gap_first_pass=None`. The workbook's `gap_diagnostic` returns cohort status `not_assessed` and zero detail rows for that cohort, even if a resampled offset exists at week 70. With an assessable original point and a bounded resample, the same routine retains an explicit unassessed row. Preserve gap locations separately from the availability of the signed value and report an unassessed row with its reason for each known re-entry. The bounded-point route became routine with m154's corrected seed status. The current published 54-row diagnostic has no missing point locations.
