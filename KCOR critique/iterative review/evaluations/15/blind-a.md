# Independent review of candidate m173

**Review question.** Examine KCOR closely. What, if anything, is wrong that matters to the validity of the method or its results, and how would you fix it? Distinguish substantive flaws from minor corrections, and do not propose unnecessary redesigns.

The proportional gamma inversion is correct for mean-one gamma frailty under multiplicative exposure. For known positive Gompertz slope, the instantaneous equation in §2.4.4 follows by differentiating the marginal hazard. The assumed baseline, the gap history and the post-skip reference population give that result its meaning. I found a reproducible workbook population error that can change both the endpoint and its interval. I found no evidence that it changes the published Table 8 or S6 results. Those results have separate limitations on interpretation.

## Substantive implementation finding — wrong all-ages population in a declared partial extract

**Material within the supported partial-input mode.** A partial sheet can contain a displayed older birth stratum and an independently supplied born-1960-or-later total (code −60), without a supplied all-ages total (−2). The ordinary workbook then creates an “all ages” cohort from the displayed birth strata. It excludes even the people the younger inclusive total proves are present. The bootstrap can attach a complete interval to this narrower population.

I ran the ordinary workbook entry point on the candidate’s 20-week 2023_01 fixture with two birth strata (1940 and 1960), 20,000 enrollees per age/dose, and doses 0 and 1. To distinguish the populations, I multiplied dose-1 deaths in the younger stratum by four from 2023-03-06 onward and recomputed every opening risk set. The full disjoint file produced an anchored all-ages endpoint of **2.103526** (unanchored **2.426016**). I replaced the 1960 rows with their identical independently supplied −60 counts, retained the 1940 rows, and declared partial scope. The returned all-ages estimate became **1.003326** (unanchored **1.193147**), exactly the 1940-only result. The separately displayed −60 endpoint was **2.922934**. In the written workbook the spurious −2 estimate had normalization status “ok” and all three requested draws produced its interval **[0.849748, 1.264642]**, exactly the displayed 1940 interval. Three draws establish the reporting-path error only.

The cause is the sheet-wide presence test at `code/KCOR.py:6449` and source selection at `code/KCOR.py:4296`. The latter uses positive birth labels and unknown birth year if it sees no explicit −2. Neither operation determines whether those groups cover the target population in partial mode. The bootstrap exclusion in `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:294` tracks unsupported **supplied** aggregate/dose keys, missing this newly synthesized −2.

**Fix.** Resolve coverage for each aggregate and dose before fitting, and carry the same eligibility into resampling. In partial mode, withhold an unsupplied all-ages point and interval unless a complete, non-overlapping partition of its population is established. Continue to report supported individual and supplied-total points. In exhaustive mode, reconstruct a missing dose-specific inclusive total from its complete components. I also confirmed that supplying just the valid dose-1 −2 rows to the exhaustive fixture removed the all-ages dose pair altogether, despite complete disjoint data for both doses. The exhaustive case loses an available result. The partial case publishes a result for the wrong people.

**Related validation defect.** Partial reconciliation checks each supplied total against the visible disjoint rows separately (`code/fixed_cohort_history.py:28–79`). With a visible 1940 cohort of 20,000 per dose, an all-ages total of 40,000 and a born-1960-or-later total of **100,000** per dose, all three internally death-only, the validator returned no invalid totals. The ordinary estimator reported both incompatible aggregate points as “ok”. Withholding intervals did not prevent the invalid points. Check that the all-ages history accommodates the born-1960-or-later total plus displayed people known to lie outside it, including feasibility of the residual death/survival history. Where conflicting inputs do not identify the erroneous source, flag the affected aggregate conclusions. Unknown birth year needs separate treatment from known pre-1960 births.

## Scientific interpretation and numerical checks

- **The Czech age-shift controls are an audit of behavior.** Direct extraction of `analysis/finite_reference/table8-primary/identified_only-draws.npz`, excluding draw zero from percentiles, reproduced the six primary endpoints (1.01619, 1.04560, 1.02894 and 1.00695, 1.01642, 1.00940) and their displayed intervals. All six include one. The construction has no known normalized target, and every pooled primary comparison had a contributing residual flag in the point and all 999 draws (SI S10). Historical outcome-informed window preference further limits validation. The specific §3.1.2 sentence asserting the negative control *evaluates ... under model-consistent conditions* should say those conditions would be needed for a flatness prediction. Data and diagnostics do not establish them. The later description of these figures as descriptive audit is appropriate.
- **The chosen Gompertz slope can matter in the empirical application.** I independently refitted the 149 source weeks, the two Czech doses and each SI S6 age group under the stated weeks, skip, reference, four fitting anchors and module-off model. At the specified annual slope 0.085 I recovered all seven printed endpoints to rounding. For the three slopes 0.065, 0.085, 0.105, the all-ages points were **1.16909, 1.13555, 1.12740**. At ages 60–69 they were **1.33311, 1.29504, 1.25774**, and at ages 90–99 **1.04110, 1.01216, 0.98378**. At ages 40–49 the endpoint barely moved (1.50743–1.50754) despite appreciable movement in both fitted variances. These are point sensitivities. The oldest group already has a primary interval including one. The 0.4% statement in §2.4.3 is explicitly a single short example and is not arithmetically contradicted. Reporting the actual empirical sensitivity alongside it would prevent extrapolation and would implement the check described in SI S5.3. A new baseline family is unnecessary.
- **Retrospective eligibility limits the Czech S6 population.** The supplied eligibility audit records 363,033 people who would otherwise qualify but had their first disqualifying product in the enrollment week or later, of whom 354,604 had no earlier recorded dose among their first four. Future product receipt requires survival to receipt. Depletion inversion cannot reconstruct people excluded by that rule. S6.1.1 discloses this, and its actual analysis remains a descriptive contrast within the restricted population. Any claim about cohorts defined using only enrollment-time eligibility would need that distinct analysis. The exclusion count alone cannot give the sign of its effect.
- **Coverage and recovery support bounded claims.** The information statement is model conditional. The gamma-null noiseless S9 output recovers variances 1.0 and 0.3 and anchored ratio 1 to numerical precision. In the S9 lognormal expected-count example the fitted first variance is 0.8724, while the endpoint is 0.99918. A stable ratio does not validate the frailty attribution. The manuscript identifies the limitations of the earlier coverage studies, the Table 9 benefit gap violation, and the absence of an identified empirical nonlinear exponent. I found no reason to redesign the core model or recast these audits as causal evidence.

## Minor correction and scope

Box 2 and §2.7.1 describe the optional NPH procedure in a sequential shorthand that can suggest first fitting an ordinary proportional gamma model and then correcting its output. The operative §§2.5 and 2.7.3 specify refitting and inversion with the same nonlinear survivor law at a supplied exponent. Use that wording consistently. The module is inactive for the primary Czech results, so this clarification does not change them.

I inspected the manuscript and supplement, the ordinary fitter, survivor and bootstrap paths, source counts, and archived numerical outputs. My empirical checks comprised point refits, existing-draw percentile checks, and small workbook reproductions. I did not rerun a 999-draw experiment. The failure above was reproduced on controlled inputs, not inferred from a discrepancy in a published cohort.
