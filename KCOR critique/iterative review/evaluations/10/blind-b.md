# Independent review of KCOR

The proportional gamma inversion is correct for its stated model. With mean-one gamma frailty, survival is $S=(1+\theta H_0)^{-1/\theta}$, so $H_0=\operatorname{expm1}(-\theta\log S)/\theta$. A known Gompertz slope supplies additional identifying structure in a quiet interval. The registry comparisons still depend on that structure, and the paper appropriately withholds causal attribution. I found two reproducible errors in the diagnostics that govern whether readers may interpret a result. I would correct those before relying on the diagnostic status of a new cohort.

## 1. High severity for interpretation. The gap test can reject an exactly correct proportional model

Section 2.5 uses the first fitting pass to test whether a gap supplied negative excess exposure. The pooled fit allows frailty variance up to 500. The seed fit in `code/KCOR.py` at lines 1421–1423 bounds that same parameter at 100. The signed gap statistic at line 1526 uses the seed, before the full fit can recover. Once this statistic determines whether an otherwise accepted contrast may be interpreted, the seed ceiling affects a scientific decision.

I checked 120 weeks with $k=0.002$, annual Gompertz slope 0.085, $\theta=200$, four fitting anchors and quiet weeks 0–19 and 70–119. Every week, including the intervening gap, follows the same baseline. The true excess is exactly zero. With expected weekly deaths, the final fit recovered $k=0.002$ and $\theta=200$, but the first-pass excess at week 70 was −0.0534085. In a sample of 10 million enrollment histories with NumPy seed 423, the final fit returned $k=0.0019930$ and $\theta=199.229$. The reported excess was −0.053239. Its pointwise percentile interval from 49 person-bootstrap draws was [−0.054033, −0.052203], and the production diagnostic returned `violation`. The exact-count result establishes the mechanism independently of how many draws were used.

Use the configured variance ceiling in the seed fit and report when the seed used for the diagnostic is constrained or unusable. Add a regression check with true variance between 100 and 500 and zero gap exposure. The existing protective-gap test uses variance 4 and cannot detect this error. I also substituted the configured ceiling in memory for the 18 original Table 8 subgroup fits. None of their point gap statistics changed by more than 0.0001. I did not re-estimate the 999-draw intervals, so their response to this fix remains unverified. The demonstration establishes a failure of the general screen, not a reversal of a published Czech result.

## 2. Moderate severity. The optional wave fit reports a proportional initializer's gap as its own

In `code/kcor_survivor.py`, `make_fitter` obtains its diagnostics dictionary from a proportional fit with marked-wave weeks removed. The final nonlinear fit replaces the gap exposures in that dictionary but retains the initializer's `gap_first_pass`. The ordinary workbook exports this field, and `code/bootstrap_reporting.py` uses it to assign the gap-assumption status. The missing weeks in the initializer can be the very weeks explicitly modeled as a wave in the final analysis.

In a 45-week exact example, I marked weeks 10–19 as a wave, designated every other week quiet and used $k=0.002$, $\theta=4$, $\alpha=1.18$ and weekly wave exposure 0.006. The nonlinear fit recovered the generating parameters to numerical precision. Its `nonlinear_gap_exposure` was correctly empty. Yet `gap_first_pass` contained a purported gap at week 20, with signed excess 0.0701172. No ordinary gap existed. The workbook can consequently present an assessment for the wrong mechanism.

Clear the proportional initializer's first-pass gap diagnostics on the nonlinear path. When there are no unmarked gaps, report `no_gaps`. When unmarked gaps exist, report the screen as unavailable unless it uses the selected nonlinear survivor history. Keep marked-wave attribution and deficit counts separate. The manuscript explicitly describes its 54 Table 8 gap intervals as proportional. This finding concerns the general workbook when the optional module is enabled.

## 3. Moderate severity for reproducibility. Define exactly what the pooled weights represent

Section 2.8 says the workbook retains a weighted geometric mean. The Table 8 caption identifies the three overlapping groups but does not give the weights needed to reconstruct that statistic. In `code/KCOR.py` at lines 4656–4690 and `analysis/table8/table8_bootstrap.py` at lines 91–93, the calculation takes the **unweighted arithmetic mean of positive dose-week mortality rates** in the first four enrollment weeks and multiplies it by the stratum's total person-weeks. These four weeks include the stabilization skip. The ordinary workbook also substitutes the smallest positive rate across groups when a stratum has no positive early rates. The code calls the result an expected-deaths weight. A mortality rate estimated from the pooled people would instead use the sum of deaths divided by the sum of the risk sets, with zero-death weeks contributing exposure.

For example, two dose cells with 1 death in 10 and 0 deaths in 990 produce weight 100 by the implemented positive-rate mean and weight 1 from the pooled mortality rate. For the actual 2022-06 Table 8 enrollment, the implemented normalized weights in subgroup order 1940, 1950, 1960 were 0.17343, 0.17560 and 0.65096. Total first-four-week deaths gave 0.16244, 0.17864 and 0.65893. The distinction therefore applies to the actual control.

If the existing empirical weight is intended, publish its formula, time origin, zero-rate rule and the two actual sets of weights. Describe the pooled ratio as a descriptive summary of the three specified overlapping groups. If burden-proportional weighting is intended, use the pooled rate and reproduce the affected point estimates and intervals. The choice changes the target and deserves an explicit decision.

## 4. Minor scientific wording corrections

Section 2.6.2 calls near-zero fitted variance an indicator of weak identification. A cohort with zero frailty variance can have a sharply estimated upper bound when there are enough events over a sufficiently long interval. Section 2.9 already distinguishes information from a point estimate. Use a precision measure or parameter-sensitivity result for the identification judgment and report boundary location separately.

Section 2.14 says that in unsuitable designs the required diagnostics “will fail.” A homogeneous cohort with individual hazard $k e^{\gamma t}/[1+\theta G(t;k)]$ has the same observable survival as the fitted gamma–Gompertz cohort, despite having zero frailty variance. No residual, split-window or bootstrap check based on these observations can distinguish those two mechanisms. The limitations and supplement already allow for observationally equivalent disturbances. Qualify the guarantee in §2.14 accordingly.

These findings call for targeted changes to the fitting diagnostics and reporting. They leave the algebra of the proportional normalization intact. The Czech age-shift intervals, the exploratory residual flags and the uncertain intervention scale support the paper's existing decision to present those analyses as descriptive.
