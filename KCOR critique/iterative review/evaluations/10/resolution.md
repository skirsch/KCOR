# Round 10 independent resolution review of KCOR, m142 to m152

> Please examine KCOR closely. What is wrong, if anything, and how would you fix it?

**Thirteen of the fifteen substantive round 9 objections are resolved within their stated scope. Two are partly resolved.** The missing positive-risk sex history is now detected, but two resampling paths still reject a legitimate representation of complete exhaustion. The supplement now interprets a near-zero variance estimate correctly, but the main text still treats it as evidence of weak identification. The separately retained Czech eligibility rule warrants a narrow qualification to the cohort-construction text. Its effect on the estimated contrast has not been measured.

The proportional gamma inversion remains mathematically consistent. For mean-one gamma frailty with relative variance θ, observed cumulative hazard is log(1 + θB)/θ at cumulative reference exposure B. Applying the inverse recovers B, including the continuous limit at θ = 0. This identity concerns a specified frailty law and reference. It does not determine whether the quiet-window fit describes a particular cohort or whether a registry contrast has a causal interpretation.

## A boundary estimate does not measure available information

For Gompertz baseline hazard k exp(γt), the observed cumulative hazard has H″(0) = k(γ − kθ). At zero variance and positive γ the curvature is positive. With θ = γ/k the observed cumulative hazard is linear despite positive frailty. Main §2.4 already gives this identity.

Information can also differ between studies having exactly the same population value θ = 0. For 80 weeks of binomial risk-set counts, fixed γ = 0.085/52 and k = 0.002, I differentiated the weekly death probabilities at zero from the right and profiled the unknown k using the expected information matrix. The per-enrollee information for θ was 0.00038195. Independent replication multiplies the information while leaving θ equal to zero. Boundary-aware inference is still needed.

Replace the inference at `documentation/preprint/paper.md:426` and `:932` with the SI distinction. A near-zero estimate means little fitted frailty-induced depletion. Precision depends on how strongly the observed history distinguishes small positive alternatives. In §2.6.1 at line 407, “little fitted frailty-induced depletion” would also be more precise than “negligible detectable depletion curvature.”

## 9.16. Retrospective Czech population eligibility

Section 2.2 already says that eligibility using later information can induce selection. Section 3.1.2 clearly distinguishes the archived Table 8 population from the product-restricted S6 application. SI S6 and the stored eligibility audit agree that 363,033 people were excluded on the basis of a product in or after the enrollment week, with no prior disqualifying product. Of those, 354,604 had no prior recorded dose among the first four. These counts do not establish the sign or size of any effect on KCOR.

The broader statement in §2.2 that fixed cohorts avoid immortal-time artifacts still invites a stronger reading than the design supports. A precise replacement would be “Assigning exposure at enrollment prevents later exposure from determining the initial exposure group. Eligibility based on later records can still select participants by post-enrollment survival, as in the Czech S6 illustration.” This leaves the intended retrospective population intact.
