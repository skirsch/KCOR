# Independent review of KCOR

**Verdict.** The proportional gamma inversion is correct for the stated starting frailty law. The fixed-slope identification proposition is also correct under its stated assumptions. These give KCOR a defensible descriptive estimand. I found reproducible errors in deciding whether a gap violates the working model, in locating those gaps, and in which people contribute to some bootstrap intervals. Two statements in the paper also draw stronger conclusions than the model supports. I would correct these before relying on the diagnostic labels or distributing the workbook for general use.

## 1. A historical frailty ceiling can make the primary diagnostic reject the exact model

**High priority.** Section 2.5 treats a wholly negative bootstrap interval for the signed first-pass gap offset as grounds to withhold interpretation. The final proportional fit admits frailty up to the configured ceiling of 500. Its first-window fit still hard-codes 100. That fit supplies the frailty and rate used to compute every first-pass offset. The final optimizer can recover the correct parameters while the diagnostic confidently rejects a gap that followed the baseline exactly. See `code/KCOR.py` around lines 1421, 1524 and 1576, and `code/bootstrap_reporting.py::gap_diagnostic`.

I generated 100 weeks of exact proportional gamma–Gompertz hazards with initial weekly rate 0.0006, slope 0.085/52 and frailty 200. The designated fit windows were weeks 0–17 and 65–99, with zero excess exposure throughout. The full fit returned rate 0.0006 and frailty 200. The seed returned 100. The signed week-65 offset was −0.011288 on the first pass and within 10⁻¹⁵ of zero at the final fit. With ten million enrollment histories sampled from the same law, the point frailty was 200.49, and a small check using 49 person-bootstrap draws produced a signed interval of [−0.011314, −0.010806], labeled `violation`. The noiseless calculation establishes the systematic error independently of this bootstrap illustration.

**Correction.** Pass the configured ceiling to the seed optimizer, constrain its initial value accordingly, and record whether the seed itself hit a bound. Treat a first-pass screen based on a bound-truncated seed as unresolved unless a fit over the intended range confirms the deficit. Include a true variance between 100 and 500 in the gap-diagnostic regression test. Recheck the reported gap flags with the corrected seed. Whether any Czech result changes requires a matched replay.

## 2. The proportional fitter inserts offsets where there was no gap between fitted weeks

**Moderate priority.** The objective includes the union of quiet weeks and early fitting anchors, but the code builds its re-entry indices from quiet-only segments. When a quiet window overlaps the anchors or begins immediately after them, the fitter introduces a persistent excess-exposure offset within uninterrupted baseline-model follow-up. That extra offset allows ordinary early sampling error to change later fitted hazards. It can also generate a signed diagnostic for a gap of length zero. See `code/KCOR.py` around lines 1235, 1332, 1448 and 1575.

In a 55-week, 100,000-person gamma cohort with four anchor weeks, I compared declaring all weeks quiet with declaring weeks 4–54 quiet. Both calls fitted exactly the same 55 observations. The first returned no offset and frailty 4.184287. The second inserted an offset of 0.0000614 at week 4 and returned frailty 4.181767. Both had sufficient designated quiet deaths. The small numerical difference in this example identifies the mechanism. It does not measure its largest possible impact.

**Correction.** Retain quiet-only rows for the seed and quiet-death eligibility checks. Derive offset transitions from contiguous segments of the actual modeled baseline-history mask, inserting an offset only after one or more unmodeled weeks. Test adjacent and overlapping quiet/anchor windows as well as a genuine multiweek gap.

## 3. With a marked nonlinear wave, the workbook tests gaps from a different survivor model

**Moderate priority for the optional module.** The nonlinear fitter starts with the proportional fit's diagnostic dictionary. It replaces the fitted parameters and final gap residuals, but leaves `gap_first_pass` inherited from that initializer. The workbook interprets the inherited values as the gap-assumption screen for the fitted cohort. In particular, the provisional fit regards marked wave weeks as a proportional gap even when the final model explicitly reconstructs every one of those weeks using the nonlinear survival law. A wave can also affect the survivor distribution entering a later, genuine gap. See `code/kcor_survivor.py::make_fitter`, `Profile.predict` and `code/bootstrap_reporting.py::annotate_gap_diagnostic`.

I generated exact weekly survival with rate 0.002, slope 0.085/52, frailty 4, exponent 1.8 and wave increment 0.009 in weeks 12–27. Every other week was quiet. The nonlinear fit recovered rate 0.002 and frailty 4, with no proportional gaps and no deficit weeks. Its forwarded first-pass diagnostic nonetheless reported a proportional gap offset of +0.221537 at week 28. This would be labeled a screened gap although the nonlinear model had no such gap. The paper correctly limits its published 54-gap result to the primary proportional Table 8 run.

**Correction.** Generate signed gap diagnostics from the sequential survivor law for the actual unmarked gaps. Until that diagnostic has been implemented and checked, report an explicit unassessed status for nonlinear fits with unmarked gaps and no-gaps status when the modeled history is complete. Keep marked-wave deficit counts separate.

## 4. Bootstrap workers can silently use a different age grouping from the point estimate

**High priority for affected workbook users.** The ordinary bootstrap transfers several analysis settings to spawned workers but omits `AGE_RANGE`. On Windows the workers import the default ten-year grouping even when the caller selected five-year groups. Five-year grouping is an actual project use case in `analysis/s6/replay.py`. Reindexing matches the label 1940 in both results, so a ten-year interval can be attached to a five-year estimate with a complete valid-draw count. See `code/bootstrap_reporting.py::attach`, `init_worker`, and the age bucketing in `code/KCOR.py` around line 6391.

I ran a two-draw workbook smoke test on Windows with source birth groups 1940 and 1945, `K.AGE_RANGE=5`, and one worker. Both groups had finite point estimates. Group 1940 was assigned two valid draws and an interval, although the worker combined both groups under that label. Group 1945 had two missing draws and an unavailable interval. Two draws suffice to verify group membership and output keys. The S6 publication uses a dedicated interval generator, so this test does not establish an error in its published intervals.

**Correction.** Include the effective age-group setting in the worker configuration and verify the fitted group definitions as well as output keys. Add a spawned-process regression using 1940 and 1945 with different mortality histories.

## 5. Correct two scientific interpretation rules

- **A near-zero estimate does not establish weak identification.** Section 2.6.2 lists near-zero frailty itself as an indicator that the parameter is weakly identified. For the exact weekly model, let G₀ and G₁ be the cumulative exposures at a week's boundaries. At zero frailty the derivative of the integrated hazard with respect to frailty is −(G₁² − G₀²)/2, while its derivative with respect to log baseline rate is G₁ − G₀. The derivative ratio varies over time. Multiple informative weeks and increasing cohort size can therefore distinguish zero from scientifically meaningful positive variances with high precision. The one-sided parameter boundary changes interval calibration but does not by itself remove information. Revise the rule to distinguish a constrained optimum from a poorly resolved positive range. SI S6 already makes this distinction in discussing the empirical estimates.
- **Depletion need not exaggerate the vaccinated–unvaccinated difference.** Section 4.3 draws a systematic direction of distortion from greater depletion in the unvaccinated group. Suppose the reference-frailty cumulative hazards are 0.05 for vaccinated and 0.10 for unvaccinated, with respective frailty variances 0 and 5. The marginal cumulative hazards are 0.05 and log(1.5)/5 = 0.08109. The observed vaccinated/unvaccinated ratio is 0.617 against a reference-frailty ratio of 0.5. These groups look closer together after depletion. Give the direction conditional on both cohort hazards and variances, or state simply that the contrast can change in either direction.

## Consequences for the Czech example

The paper already provides material reasons to treat this example as descriptive. SI S6 reports six residual-screen flags among 14 fits. The S6 eligibility rule looks forward across the first four recorded doses. For the 2021-24 enrollment, the paper identifies 363,033 otherwise eligible people whose disqualifying product occurred during the enrollment week or later, with none previously. Freezing the cohort label cannot undo that eligibility conditioning. If S6 is to support a claim about a population identifiable at enrollment, rerun with eligibility determined from information available then and compare the same contrasts. The supplied counts alone determine neither the size nor the direction of any change. The age-shift control used an earlier extract and should be kept distinct.

I inspected the frozen manuscript, supplement, estimator, bootstrap, empirical and simulation replay code, and relevant tests. Numerical checks above are targeted constructions. I did not rerun the national analysis or the full simulation studies.
