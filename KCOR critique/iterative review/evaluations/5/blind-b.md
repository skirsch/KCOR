# KCOR scientific and implementation assessment

KCOR’s proportional gamma inversion is correct conditional on the fitted frailty variance. If the mean-one starting frailty is gamma and individual cumulative hazard is (zB), the marginal survival is (1 + θB)^(−1/θ). Inverting it gives the cumulative hazard for a person at reference frailty one. That person is defined relative to their own cohort. Two observed survival curves alone cannot establish a common frailty distribution, exchangeability, a mortality risk ratio, or a treatment effect. The manuscript explicitly restricts its target to a descriptive contrast (paper §§1.6, 2.1.1, 2.4.2). An arbitrary nonnegative variance paired with its corresponding inverted baseline reproduces the observed survival exactly. Identification of variance comes from the restrictions on the quiet-period baseline and its slope. Good fit cannot test alternative explanations that give the same observed hazards.

## Problems that can change analyses

1. **Enrollment eligibility can depend on a future vaccination.** The registry converter checks the manufacturer of any of the first four doses and drops the person before processing the individual enrollment dates (`code/KCOR_CMR.py:470–492`). It subsequently freezes the dose state at enrollment (`code/KCOR_CMR.py:689–705`). Two unvaccinated people eligible on the same Monday can therefore enter different risk sets solely because one survives and receives a non-mRNA dose months later. This violates the enrollment-only construction in `documentation/preprint/paper.md:208–220`; fixed group membership after this exclusion cannot undo the selection. Apply the product rule to doses received by the specified enrollment boundary, then retain the person's follow-up. Document and quantify any deliberately future-defined restriction as a separate population. The filter also inspects only four product columns while dose assignment can use six dates (converter lines 448–454, 713–736). The code establishes the route for bias, not its magnitude in the archived tables.

2. **An undefined anchor silently becomes 1.** The workbook's ordinary per-age and all-ages paths pick the reference by row position, use row zero for short series, and set an invalid or zero reference ratio to 1 (`code/KCOR.py:4422–4437, 5412–5427`). They still label the result KCOR. For example, zero numerator deaths at the reference and positive cumulative hazards later make the defined anchored endpoint mathematically undefined, yet the workbook will return the later unanchored ratio. Bootstrap draws with sparse early mortality can silently switch estimands, because `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:143–156` tests the resulting values for finiteness. Select the actual reference date, report raw and anchored estimates in distinctly named columns, propagate an invalid anchor, and report the number of undefined draws. This also aligns the workbook with the distinction between primary and optional anchored contrasts in `documentation/preprint/paper.md:153–169`.

3. **The default bootstrap rejects standard output of the project's own converter.** `code/KCOR_CMR.py:497–510, 939–951, 1619–1648` defines F, M and O, emits rows by DCCI, and emits known overlapping totals for years −2 and −60 with sex O. `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:45–55` accepts only F and M, and treats two DCCI rows for the same age, sex, dose and week as duplicate histories. The first such error makes intervals unavailable for *every* requested sheet (`bootstrap_reporting.py:130–133`). The inability to handle undocumented overlap is reasonable. Here the overlap and the disjoint components are specified in the producing code. Resample the disjoint histories once, preserving relevant strata, and rebuild the totals. Validate availability per enrollment. Maintain an explicit layout for the separate Table 8 age-shift construction. This finding concerns the ordinary default and does not invalidate intervals computed by the dedicated Table 8 procedure.

4. **The bootstrap sampling design differs across its descriptions and implementations.** The manuscript specifies sampling the pooled enrollment population and allowing cohort sizes to vary (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:566–585`). Ordinary disjoint mode draws a separate fixed-size multinomial for each birth-year/dose group (`code/bootstrap_reporting.py:47–62, 75–84`). Table 8 pools the disjoint cells into one multinomial (`bootstrap_reporting.py:64–69`). Both sampling schemes can be defensible for different inference targets. They give different distributions of composition-dependent pooling weights. State the intended conditioning and use it consistently in the estimator, simulations and documentation. The displayed risk-set equation at paper line 571 needs a nondeath-exit term for the supported histories with exits.

5. **The marked-wave fitter borrows eligibility and diagnostics from a different fit.** Its base fit counts deaths in all designated quiet weeks (`code/KCOR.py:1261–1275`). Its nonlinear fit excludes marked wave weeks but checks the seed's death flag and inherits its total, quiet-bin count and weak-identifiability flag (`code/kcor_survivor.py:249–263, 288–301`). With weeks 0–23, four anchors, quiet weeks 4–23, marked wave 4–19, and one death per week except ten per marked week, only four eligible quiet deaths remain. Using exact weekly Gompertz–gamma hazards with (k=.001,θ=2,γ=.085/52) and supplied alpha one, the production fit returned `status=ok`, `total_quiet_deaths=164` and `n_quiet_bins=20`. The configured minimum is 30. Compute each eligibility statistic and flag on the final fit masks. A poor or unavailable initialization should be handled independently from whether the target fit has sufficient information.

6. **The all-ages reporting path discards the nonlinear correction.** The ordinary calculation applies weekly conditional-survival inversion and stores corrected hazard increments for each age/dose (`code/KCOR.py:8092–8099, 8127–8176`). The all-ages reporting branch selects year −2, zeros its hazard and cumulative-hazard columns, and recomputes plain gamma inversion from raw hazard (`code/KCOR.py:5150–5162, 5226–5252`). Under an active nonlinear wave and positive fitted variance, its reported ratio can therefore refer to a different method than the per-age rows and the intermediate trajectory for the same aggregate. In the Monte Carlo branch the parameter lookup at line 5186 also omits the draw identifier included at line 8118. Reuse the already computed trajectory for the matching group and draw. This concerns active-wave analyses and should not be imputed to the primary Czech module-off result.

7. **Fit diagnostics do not always describe the fitted model.** In the proportional fit the exported gap offsets are saved before the least-squares update and its normal one-iteration exit (`code/KCOR.py:1503–1524, 1547–1559, 1602–1607`). Final offsets are rebuilt for prediction, but are not copied into the exported diagnostics (lines 1611–1628, 1761–1768). A 24-week numerical check with (k=.001,θ=2,γ=.085/52), exact model increments, quiet weeks 0–7 and 12–23, first four increments multiplied by 1.15 and weeks 8–11 multiplied by 3 returned offset 0.01277145. Evaluating the documented offset at the fitted parameters gave 0.00834640. Recompute signed and applied offsets and their flags at the returned optimum. In addition, the reported post-normalization shape diagnostic fits one straight line in elapsed years to the cumulative values across windows (`code/KCOR.py:7139–7145, 9534–9547`), while the manuscript specifies Gompertz time with window offsets (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:409–426`). Use the correct fitted trajectory and compute residual diagnostics separately within windows, including nonlinear survivor history when active.

## Scientific and statistical interpretation

- **There is no general first-order cancellation of a common Gompertz slope error.** Paper lines 332–334 assert empirical slope sensitivity and a tendency for cancellation. The supplement lists a fixed slope and recommends a check (lines 174, 299), without presenting the claimed range or corresponding endpoint values there. For two cohorts with constant quiet hazards (c_A,c_B), the exact fitted parameters for any chosen positive slope (g) are (k_d=c_d,θ_d=g/c_d). After 20 quiet weeks, let A's observed hazard double for 20 weeks and B's remain constant. The week-40 ratio anchored at week 20 is `expm1(60*g)/expm1(40*g)`, with expansion (1.5+15g+O(g^2)). The perturbation is common to both cohorts. Annual slopes 0.065, 0.085 and 0.105 give 1.5191, 1.5251 and 1.5311. The point is the surviving first-order term, not that this example has large bias. Publish a prespecified gamma sweep for the analyses at issue and delimit any cancellation theorem to its actual assumptions.

- **A common wave intensity cancels at a given instant conditional on survivor composition.** It does not generally cancel from the history that created that composition. With no baseline exposure, external scale one and alpha one, the surviving frailty mean after cumulative wave exposure (W) is (1/(1+θ_d W)). The instantaneous excess ratio depends on (W) whenever the two variances differ, even if the current wave intensities are identical. Qualify the abstract's invariance statement (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:15, 473–481, 517–527`). Propagate the prior wave through the survivor law when evaluating any amplification objective. External calibration and model fit are still required. The manuscript appropriately reports the Czech exponent as unidentified (lines 830–852).

- **The Cox comparison uses different targets and an oracle input.** In the synthetic example with common individual baseline and differing frailty variances, the marginal hazards really differ among survivors. A non-null Cox summary is therefore evidence of a distinction between marginal survivor hazards and a conditional treatment-null benchmark. Calling it a spurious marginal contrast or estimator bias conflates the targets. In the plotted KCOR intervals the generating variances were supplied (paper lines 616–627, 660–674). The shared-frailty trend on the grid is explicitly interpolated from a separate calculation. Retain the illustration of different estimands, label the reference quantities point by point, and use fitted KCOR plus a genuinely fitted comparator to claim performance on a common target.

- **The registry pseudo-control has no known numerical null.** Comparing different ages within the same vaccination category removes the deliberately assigned treatment contrast. It does not force baseline changes, wave effects, or their interaction with age to be proportional over time. The manuscript acknowledges this and the outcome-linked rationale for the historical window (paper line 749), but elsewhere calls the design evidence directly checking for no artifactual cumulative effects under strong selection-induced curvature (line 966), even though lines 735–739 say it was not a strong latent-depletion stress test. Treat it as a descriptive check. Use an independently specified design with a known contrast to assess calibration. In the current sampled nonlinear main design, 90/100 nominal 95% intervals covered the target, and the stress-design fitted mean was 1.04019 with target one (supplement line 459). These are useful quantified limitations. Neither the observed coverage nor intervals crossing one establish equivalence or broadly calibrated inference.

## Additional implementation and reproducibility issues

- An all-zero early anchor mean aborts the joint fit before checking later quiet information (`code/KCOR.py:1242–1264`). A 60-week example with 50 later quiet weeks each assigned 15 deaths returns `k_anchor_too_small` when the first four hazards are zero. Initialize from available positive quiet information. The diagnostic should distinguish a missing ratio anchor from the separate question of whether the joint model can be fitted.
- Weekly death fractions are pre-clipped to 0.999 on the ordinary age-fitting path (`code/KCOR.py:6975–6978`), contradicting the universal exactness claim at paper lines 249–254. A risk of 0.9999 has integrated hazard 9.21034, while clipping gives 6.90776. Handle an exhausted risk set explicitly and validate invalid counts. The additionally documented horizon-wide inversion cap (paper line 405, `code/KCOR.py:1011–1025`) also changes *earlier* otherwise representable values when later follow-up is appended. At fixed theta 90, (H=.01) gives 0.01621781 alone and 0.01229562 if (H=2) is appended. Use stable logarithms for extreme ratios and flag true range failures locally. These are edge cases, with no evidence here that they affected Table 8.
- The secondary analytic interval uses the variance of post-anchor increments divided by squared increments (`code/KCOR.py:4452–4483`), although the point estimator divides ratios of *full* cumulative hazards. Anchor uncertainty and its covariance with later totals are missing. Clipping the upper limit to ten times the point estimate further compromises confidence-level interpretation. The columns are correctly separated from the primary bootstrap. If retained, derive a delta-method interval for the actual anchored functional or label the existing columns as heuristic bounds.
- `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:453` says `make paper` regenerates all artifacts. The target builds paper PDF/TeX and then attempts to upload to a configured private host (`Makefile:419–422`). Its build depends on auxiliary files including `header.tex` and a CSL that are absent from this source edition (`Makefile:430–451`). Provide a self-contained local build target, separate publication, and list analysis replay commands for the scientific outputs.

The numerical checks above used the supplied implementation and small constructed arrays. I did not rerun the full simulation grid or infer changes to specific historical numerical results from code-path defects alone.
