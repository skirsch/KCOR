# Independent review of KCOR

KCOR has a defensible, limited use as a descriptive transformation of fixed-cohort survival data. Under a mean-one gamma frailty model, the marginal survival transform and its inverse are correct. With known positive Gompertz slope and proportional exposure history, the instantaneous hazard satisfies the differential equation in the identification proposition. Neither identity identifies the decomposition from aggregate data when the baseline time course is free. Several ordinary reporting and validation paths still give stronger or different answers than the paper describes. I would prioritize the reportability rules, the estimand exposed in the workbook, and the evidence for sensitivity to the baseline assumption.

## 1. The public fitter can accept a nonlinear analysis with zero usable quiet weeks

This is a reproducible implementation error. In a short in-memory call to `fit_theta0_gompertz`, I set weekly times to 0 through 29, hazards to `.001 * exp(.0015*t)`, the quiet mask and marked wave both to weeks 8 through 15 inclusive, early anchors to four weeks, gamma to .0015, alpha to 1.18, and deaths to ten per week. The result was `success=True, status=ok, n_fit=4, total_quiet_deaths=80, identifiability_flag=False`. All four fitted points were early anchors. Every one of the 80 nominal quiet deaths lay inside the excluded wave. The paper requires adequate quiet data and explicitly removes marked-wave rows from fitting.

The nonlinear fitter first runs the proportional fitter on the full quiet mask. It then counts eligible rows using the union of quiet and anchor rows minus wave rows and accepts three. It carries the proportional fit's quiet deaths and weak-identification flag into the updated diagnostic dictionary. A sufficiently long excluded wave can make a four-point fit pass a six-point check. Independently, the proportional fitter requires a positive rate estimate from the early anchors before it even tries the quiet-window fit, so an all-zero early count can veto informative later quiet observations. Recompute quiet-only counts, eligible anchor counts, span and weak-identification criteria in the actual candidate model. Permit a positive quiet-window initialization when the early estimate is zero. Add both boundary cases to the public fitter tests. The demonstration establishes a possible false pass, not that the six published negative-control endpoints had this configuration. (`code/kcor_survivor.py:247–263, 288–301`; `code/KCOR.py:1242–1274, 1643, 10225–10248`; `documentation/preprint/paper.md:371–383, 697`.)

## 2. Routine output silently changes the primary estimand

The paper defines primary KCOR as the ratio of two inverted cumulative hazards. An anchored ratio divides that primary ratio by an earlier value. In the normal per-age workbook route the code computes both quantities internally but always writes the latter as `KCOR`. If the reference ratio is zero or undefined it substitutes 1.0. Therefore the same output column can contain a ratio-of-ratios for one group and the raw ratio for another, with no corresponding change of label. For a constant baseline hazard multiplier of two, exact proportional inversion gives a primary ratio of two throughout and an anchored ratio of one throughout. This is an interpretation-changing difference. The pooled row takes a weighted geometric mean of available stratum-specific anchored ratios, renormalizing among available strata. It is another estimand, distinct from a ratio of direct age-standardized cumulative hazards. The primary unpooled ratio can be recovered from exported hazard columns where available.

Export both `KCOR_unanchored` and `KCOR_anchored`, their own intervals, and the reference date, value and status. Return a missing anchored result if its reference is undefined. Label the pooled result as a weighted geometric mean and publish its weights and included strata. Correctly labeled anchored applications, including an empirical pseudo-control intended to examine drift, are legitimate. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:149–171, 539–550, 562`; `code/KCOR.py:4423–4437, 4532–4540, 4722–4726, 4747–4779`.)

## 3. A good fit does not establish the baseline assumption or enforce the promised failure policy

For any differentiable observed cumulative hazard H and candidate variance theta, the proportional baseline cumulative hazard can be chosen as `expm1(theta*H)/theta`, recovering exactly the same observed survival. The paper recognizes this and its fixed-slope identification proposition is sound. But its next substantive claim, that a common misspecification of the Gompertz slope tends to cancel to first order in ratios and that the supplement confirms robustness, needs more support. In an exactly flat quiet period with h=.001 for both groups, the proposition gives theta=gamma/.001. Let a subsequent period leave observed cumulative hazards .10 and .05. Both groups have the very same quiet hazard, reference ratio and slope misspecification. The resulting ratio is `1 + exp(.05*theta)`, whose first derivative in gamma is positive. Gamma .001, .0015 and .002 gives ratios 2.0513, 2.0779 and 2.1052, respectively. This example disproves a general first-order cancellation; it does not quantify the Czech effect. The supplement specifies a fixed slope and recommends varying it, but gives no perturbation grid, range or resulting estimates in support of the cited claim. Report that grid with both variances, anchored and unanchored endpoints, and gate decisions. Examine alternatives with seasonality or stronger data-derived baseline restrictions. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:321–359, 388–394`; `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:174, 292–300`.)

The manuscript repeatedly says failed analyses terminate without reporting normalized contrasts. The ordinary code defaults failed fits to theta=0, applies identity inversion, and can still report a numeric contrast and even a flagged percentile interval. Retaining a fallback throughout bootstrap draws avoids conditioning on successful draws and is reasonable for diagnostic output. A consumer needs a separate reportable estimate and status, distinct from the diagnostic fallback. For the claimed cumulative-shape check, the workbook diagnostic regresses the uninformed ordinary gamma-inverted H on straight calendar time across the fit set, even in the optional nonlinear case. Its largest deviation from that line is not the departure from a Gompertz baseline with window offsets described in the methods. Compute observed-versus-model residuals using the active survivor law and actual window histories. Report per-window results and leave-window-out sensitivity. With default relative RMSE threshold 100,000 and early/late zero counts treated via division by the observed increment plus 1e-12, a descriptive residual screen has no calibrated general identification interpretation. The 78 overlapping passed fits in the application establish algorithmic acceptance only; for one subgroup the fitted range still reaches from essentially zero to 1.56. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:95–98, 204, 407–426, 564, 904`; `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:63–70, 304–310`; `code/KCOR.py:1630–1648, 2221–2223, 7139–7145, 8114–8128, 9534–9547`; `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:148–157`.)

## 4. The exponent localization evidence does not validate the full survivor-law model

The optional correction uses the survival integral with kernel exp(-Z B - Z^alpha W). Its amplification moment is the integral of Z^alpha against this tilted distribution, divided by its normalizing integral. Even before nonlinear wave exposure, at W=0, the moment scales by (1+theta B)^(-alpha) relative to the starting gamma moment. In particular, at alpha=1, E[Z given survival]=1/(1+theta B), generally below one. The supplied historical exponent estimator computes the moment of a mean-one gamma with a propagated scalar parameter and returns exactly one at alpha=1 for every history. Its synthetic generator creates amplification with the same helper. Successful recovery under that construction checks the surrogate's internal calibration. It does not establish recovery of alpha from the stated joint survival process. Cancellation of the *current* common intensity in a ratio also does not remove the dependence of the survivor distribution on *past* wave intensity.

Separate historical/exploratory exponent-validation claims from evidence for the documented forward law. For an operational externally scaled estimator, propagate each candidate's conditional survivor moments from its B,W path, define observed excess against the corresponding conditional baseline, include uncertainty in the external effect scale in a sensitivity analysis, and test against independently generated individual-level or exact-survival data. The Czech inference that alpha is unidentified is appropriate. The primary configuration leaves the module off, so the problem with localization does not by itself invalidate the reported primary endpoints. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:464–483, 495–513, 517–527, 824–832, 850–852`; `test/alpha/code/estimate_alpha.py:282–299, 435–446, 638–642, 1131–1132`; `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:14–20`.)

## 5. Ordinary and documented bootstrap designs differ, and current coverage evidence is limited

The step-by-step text says to sample the entire enrollment population with replacement and permits cohort counts to change. The ordinary disjoint implementation draws a separate multinomial of fixed size for each birth-year/dose combination. Sex counts can change inside those cells, but neither birth-year/dose stratum size nor total dose-cohort size can change. The special age-shift reconstruction, in contrast, does use one multinomial across disjoint components, rebuilding overlapping groups. Either conditional or unconditional resampling can be appropriate, but these procedures target different sampling distributions, particularly when weights depend on empirical composition. Specify which population is random, implement that policy for each design and expose it in the output. The death-only identity in the prose also requires a subtraction for exits when working with nondeath losses as supported by the ordinary code. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:562–585`; `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:30–47, 56–83`.)

The updated matched-wave experiment observed 90/100 coverage for nominal 95% intervals (reported interval for coverage 82.4%–95.1%). The stress experiment observed 48/50 but its mean endpoint, 1.0402, had Monte Carlo interval [1.0179, 1.0625] for truth one. The implementations and scenarios in the two older no-wave coverage studies differ; the manuscript already states this. These data neither demonstrate calibrated 95% inference across the intended uses nor establish a general failure of 95% coverage. Rerun the exact default public estimator on independent samples across risk levels, theta=0, weak curvature, model misspecification and alternative anchor dates; report coverage conditional on reportability and the fraction reportable, alongside unconditional fallback performance. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:587–600`; `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:457–459`; `analysis/survivor_validation/main-full-results.json`, `stress-results.json`.)

## 6. The controls answer narrower questions than some summaries imply

The Cox comparison uses the true simulated frailty variance for inversion. The cohorts have different hazard trajectories among survivors by construction, despite identical hazards at fixed frailty. A non-unit common Cox coefficient under nonproportional hazards is a mismatch with the fixed-frailty treatment contrast. It is not in itself bias for the observed marginal comparison. Similarly, differing marginal RMSTs under those cohorts reflect actual differences in marginal survival. The methods and figure caption disclose oracle theta, but the “spurious” language and “RMST inherits depletion bias” summary can still mislead. Present true marginal survival and the fixed-frailty target alongside fitted and oracle KCOR. The pseudo-doses in the Czech controls encode actual age differences, so exact equal anchored trajectories do not follow from absence of a pseudo-dose intervention. The paper correctly acknowledges their unknown exact targets, the exploratory Czech quiet-period selection and that intervals crossing one do not establish equivalence. Keep these qualifications with the negative-control and general diagnostic claims. (`documentation/preprint/paper.md:641–672, 731–749, 1191–1197`; `test/sim_grid/code/generate_cox_bias_sim.py:125–153, 220–235`; `test/negative_control/code/generate_negative_control.py:51–71`; `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:22–33`.)

## 7. Several supplied reproduction paths need repair

The supplement says that the 78-window scan used forced alpha 1.18 and the saved rows say “nonlinear survivor fit.” Its replay calls the fitter without an explicit exponent or wave, relying on the dataset default. That default is now identified-only with no supplied exponent. A clean invocation follows the proportional branch. Set the reproduction mode explicitly, assert the selected law, and produce separate primary and sensitivity outputs. Likewise the default combined-PDF make target requires a JSON bibliography, CSL and header that are absent from the supplied preprint directory; it contains a BibTeX file instead. The recommended top-level paper target additionally invokes a private SCP destination. Supply the declared dependencies or adjust the build, make local compilation independent of publication, and add a source-to-result manifest for numerical studies provided as outputs. (`analysis/README.md:3–5`; `analysis/quiet_windows/scan.py:70–81`; `analysis/quiet_windows/windows.csv:2`; `documentation/preprint/supplement.md:304–310, 447–453`; `data/Czech/Czech.yaml:14–19`; `code/KCOR.py:10231–10247`; `Makefile:14–20, 419–430, 566`.)
