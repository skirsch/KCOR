# Resolution review of candidate m102

**Assessment.** The original zero-row false success, fitted jump at a specified exponent of one, cumulative offset double counting, and two-row first-window rejection have concrete corrections. Two related eligibility problems survive in the public fitter. The wave fit can count deaths outside its final estimation sample. A zero-death early anchor can also veto a later window with hundreds of deaths. I would correct the first and settle the intended eligibility rule for the second before presenting an unqualified all-window estimator. Neither example measures a change in the published Czech contrasts.

I inspected the frozen m102 source and its saved outputs, exercised two small fixed-cohort examples through the public fitting function, and recomputed Table 8 percentiles from the saved draws. I used the earlier objection records for historical counterexamples and expensive validation runs. I did not rerun those simulations, generate new 999-draw workbooks, or verify the historical commit independently. Source locations below are relative to m102. Earlier investigation files are under `KCOR critique/objections/<ID>/` in the review repository.

### Two direct eligibility checks

Both examples start with 15,000 people, follow 40 weeks with deaths as the only exits, and calculate the weekly hazard as `-log1p(-deaths/risk_at_week_start)`. They call `KCOR.fit_theta0_gompertz` with times 0 through 39, two anchor weeks, slope 0.002, and the actual weekly death counts. The configured quiet-death minimum was 30.

1. Mark every week as quiet and weeks 5 through 18 inclusive as a wave. Give each wave week four deaths and each other week one. Only 26 deaths are eligible. For specified alpha 1 and 1.18 the fitter returned `ok`, `n_fit=26`, `total_quiet_deaths=82`, `n_quiet_bins=40` and `insufficient_deaths=False`. Fitted variances were 11.44245 and 10.32288. All counts and risk sets are internally consistent. The relevant discrepancy is the eligibility decision, irrespective of the statistical meaning of those variances.
2. Give weeks 0 and 1 no deaths and every later week ten. Set the sole quiet window to weeks 9 through 39 and use the module-off route. There are 310 quiet deaths. The public fitter returned two missing parameters, `insufficient_data` and `k_anchor_too_small`. This establishes an early veto. It does not establish that every sample with zero early deaths has enough information for a precise estimate.

**Decision.** Prioritize the verified quiet-death gate mismatch. Clarify or remove the zero-early-anchor veto and compare affected subgroup flags and endpoints after any change. A full default-path workbook replay would close a software-verification gap. Further calibrated simulations, prospectively justified window choices, or independent controls would be needed for stronger scientific inference. The existing evidence supports a conditional retrospective comparison and does not establish a causal intervention effect.
