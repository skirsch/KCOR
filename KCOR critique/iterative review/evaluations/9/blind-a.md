# Independent review of KCOR

The proportional gamma inversion is mathematically sound under its assumptions. If a cohort starts with mean-one gamma frailty of variance θ and all subsequent individual exposures are proportional to frailty, then survival is (1 + θH₀)^(−1/θ). Its inverse and the fixed-Gompertz-slope identification equation in §2.4 follow. The more consequential issues concern who is in the risk set, which populations are included in an estimate, and whether the starting survivor law is plausible. I would retain the core as a conditional descriptive method and make the following corrections.

## 1. High for ordinary-workbook use — unrecorded losses pass as a valid primary cohort

Sections 2.2–2.3 require a fixed death-only cohort with no nonadministrative censoring. The source validator in `code/fixed_cohort_history.py::invalid_components` checks for *increases* in risk after accounting for deaths. Any decrease is allowed. The bootstrap in `code/bootstrap_reporting.py::histories` infers a nondeath exit for every missing person. These counts cannot distinguish planned, independent censoring from a registry transfer, a dropped class of people, or a denominator error.

I tested the ordinary workbook path on the supplied 56-week two-age, two-dose fixture in `test/test_source_component_history.py`. The original groups have matching mortality. I subtracted 12,000 from each of the two sex-specific risk sets in just the 1950 dose-1 group from 4 October 2021 onward and kept every death unchanged. Source validation returned no errors, bootstrap history reconstruction accepted the sheet, and the 1950 endpoint moved from 1 to **1.45793**, with fit and normalization both OK. No exit column or sensitivity-mode setting was needed. This demonstrates a defect in the input contract. It does not establish a denominator error in the published Czech series.

**Correction.** Enforce Y(t+1) = Y(t) − D(t) for the primary death-only specification. Keep a declared sensitivity/input mode for planned nondeath exits, including the explicitly documented synthetic rounding losses in Table 9. Report exit counts by cohort and mark which estimand the workbook actually used. Apply the same rule before point estimation and resampling.

## 2. High for interpretation of the Czech S6 comparison — membership uses future survival

The S6 application excludes a person's record when any of the first four recorded products, including products received after enrollment, is outside the permitted list. A person must survive to receive a later product. Among otherwise eligible people, 363,033 exclusions were triggered only in the enrollment week or later, and 354,604 of those people had no earlier dose among the first four. This selection therefore particularly concerns people who would initially enter dose 0. Keeping membership fixed *after* this retrospective exclusion does not cure the problem. Neither the direction nor the magnitude of endpoint distortion follows from the counts. The supplement discloses the issue, but the main text's broad claim that the fixed design avoids immortal-time artifacts needs this qualification. See §2.2, SI S6.1.1, `code/KCOR_CMR.py` and `analysis/eligibility/counts.json`.

**Correction.** For an enrollment-defined comparison, apply product eligibility using information available at enrollment and repeat the S6 estimates alongside the historical definition. Until then, identify the future-history restriction in the main worked example and keep S6 explicitly descriptive of that retrospectively selected population. Table 8 uses a different extract and this particular objection does not transfer to it.

## 3. Moderate for the forced-α sensitivity — resetting frailty during an ongoing nonlinear wave

The Czech wave is marked from 2021-41 to 2022-12. The 2022-06 enrollment in Table 8 starts accumulating in 2022-08, inside that wave. The code clips the wave to post-skip weeks and initializes its accumulated wave exposure at zero (`code/KCOR.py::fit_theta0_gompertz`, `code/kcor_survivor.py::Profile.predict` and `make_correction`). This defines a fresh gamma approximation among people at the new origin. It is not, in general, the survivor law implied by the same nonlinear mechanism that operated before that origin.

Specifically, starting with gamma density f(z), survival through pre-origin exposures B and W changes it to a density proportional to f(z) exp(−Bz−Wz^α). A positive W and α = 1.18 introduce a term in z^1.18 that rescaling frailty to mean one cannot put back into the gamma family. As an illustration, quadrature with starting θ = 4, B = 0.01, W = 0.10 and α = 1.18 gives skewness 3.76205 among survivors. A gamma with the same standardized variance would have skewness 3.88602. These are illustrative exposures, not estimates from the registry. The paper correctly conditions the calculation on a gamma law *at the origin*. What is missing is the specific consequence for a reported sensitivity case.

**Correction.** Label these 2022-06 sensitivity estimates as assuming a fresh gamma distribution mid-wave and state that earlier nonlinear selection cannot be recovered from this cohort's post-origin counts alone. Where the purpose is to model continuation of the same wave, use the survivor distribution carried from an observed pre-wave origin, or withhold that continuation claim for affected enrollments. This issue does not invalidate the module-off column.

## 4. Moderate for sparse pooled results — the bootstrap can compare different sets of ages

The pooled workbook result uses the geometric mean over ages with a usable reference and positive weight. An age with an undefined anchor is dropped and the remaining weights are renormalized (`code/KCOR.py::build_kcor_rows`, around lines 4750–4860). Section 2.8 mentions “available strata.” The unresolved inference problem is that availability can change *across draws*. A draw may return a finite pool from fewer strata than the point estimate, and `bootstrap_reporting.attach` counts that draw as valid. Its undefined-anchor counter recognizes a pool with **no** usable reference, not one that lost a contributor.

I reproduced the reporting behavior by setting the first six weeks' deaths to zero in both sex components of the 1950 dose-1 group of the same fixture and rebuilding valid death-only risk sets. The 1950 anchored contrast was unavailable, while the pooled row reported **1.00000**, normalization OK, and no abnormal-fit flag. The only contributing age was 1940. In a group with one anchor-period death, a person bootstrap has probability approximately e^(−1) of drawing no copy of that death. A complete set of finite pooled draws can therefore mask frequent changes of population.

**Correction.** For inference on a designated age pool, record its contributors and require the same members in every draw. Mark the fixed-pool interval unavailable and count draws losing a contributor. If the available-age statistic is intentionally retained, report its contributors, retained fraction of total weight, and frequency of composition changes separately. This example does not show that published Table 8 lost strata.

## 5. Moderate for workbook uncertainty — the legacy interval uses a different contrast

For one group the anchored log-growth contribution is log H(t) − log H(a). The count-based calculation in `code/KCOR.py::build_kcor_rows` divides the variance of post-anchor hazard increments by **[H(t)−H(a)]²**. That is the denominator for log post-anchor *increment*. Even with θ fixed at zero and independent hazard increments, writing H(t) = A + J with A = H(a), the actual first-order variance has terms Var(J)/(A+J)² and [1/(A+J) − 1/A]² Var(A). The code further caps its reported upper bound at ten times the estimate. Labeling these columns analytical does not resolve the mismatch. Separately, `bootstrap_reporting.attach` requires the legacy lower bound to be present to emit the anchored percentile interval. At the valid reference week all defined draws equal one, yet this extra gate suppresses the available [1,1] result because legacy bounds were blanked.

**Correction.** Remove the legacy analytical bounds from inferential reporting, or compute the covariance-aware uncertainty of the actual log contrast and document any conditional-on-fit approximation. Derive the percentile interval's availability solely from its own point and draws. Give the unanchored primary interval its own status, since it can exist even when the anchored interval is unavailable.

The S9 simulation is pertinent evidence about the anchored endpoint for its specific rates, window and frailty laws. It cannot establish that a fitted θ represents biological frailty in the Czech comparisons. Indeed, 63 of 78 fits in the Czech placement scan triggered the residual screen despite numerical acceptance. Likewise, §2.7.2 explicitly says weekly identification of α under the full survivor law remains unvalidated. I would change the abstract's claim that the module “localizes” the exponent to say it proposes an approximate localization procedure and implements inversion for a supplied exponent. These distinctions preserve the formal result without turning successful optimization or a null control into a test of the latent mechanism.

I reviewed the frozen manuscript, supplement, method and relevant archived analyses. The two ordinary-workbook checks above used small temporary fixtures. I did not regenerate the large registry or simulation studies.
