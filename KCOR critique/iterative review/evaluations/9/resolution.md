# Round 9 independent resolution review of KCOR, m135 to m142

> Please examine KCOR closely. What is wrong, if anything, and how would you fix it?

Nine of the thirteen round 8 objections are resolved within their stated scope. Four are partly resolved. The remaining implementation problems concern a positive-risk sex disappearing in the overlapping control, loss of the original sheet calendar in a sensitivity sweep, and reuse of Table 8 results after changing the reference week. The last wording problem is the supplement's association of a small fitted frailty variance with low curvature or weak identification. A separate Table 8 verification command also fails on this edition. The observed failures concern altered input or settings and a stale verifier. The published pooled points, stored percentile arithmetic, and sensitivity figure checked out on their intended inputs.

The proportional gamma inversion is mathematically consistent. If the reference cumulative hazard is B and mean-one gamma frailty has variance θ, the marginal cumulative hazard is log(1 + θB)/θ. Inverting that expression recovers B, with the continuous limit at θ = 0. The interpretation depends on the frailty distribution, baseline model and cohort-specific mean used as the reference. It does not establish an intervention effect from the Czech contrasts.

### Distinguish small fitted variance from weak information

At θ = 0, the cumulative hazard is k[exp(γt) − 1]/γ. More generally, the observed cumulative-hazard curvature at the origin is H′′(0) = k(γ − kθ). With a positive slope and rate, curvature is positive at zero variance. Exact linearity occurs at the positive variance θ = γ/k. Proximity to the nonnegative parameter boundary therefore establishes neither the observed curvature nor the precision with which positive alternatives can be excluded. Keep the corrected note at `supplement.md:413` and revise the diagnostic bullet at line 420 to say that a near-zero estimate implies little fitted frailty-induced depletion, with uncertainty assessed from the information in the observed histories and boundary-aware diagnostics.

**Related age-pattern explanation.** SI S6.1.2 at line 396 suggests that stronger ongoing depletion at older ages can compress dispersion and thereby yield smaller fitted θ. In the exact proportional gamma model, survival tilts the gamma rate. Its shape is unchanged. The absolute survivor variance falls together with the mean, but the variance after scaling to mean one remains θ. Higher cumulative exposure alone cannot explain a lower population value of that parameter under this law. Sampling uncertainty, different entry distributions, or model departures could yield an age pattern in the estimates. The observed age pattern can be reported without attributing it to a change in relative variance caused by gamma depletion. This qualification is distinct from the corrected post-skip null.
