## Remaining and related issues

1. **Weekly equality can be counted as positive exposure in the new identity branch.** I generated 52 exact baseline increments using a weekly rate of 0.01 and growth 0.085/52, accumulated them, and marked indices 1 through 50. With either zero frailty and exponent 1.18 or frailty 0.5 and exponent 1, the entire marked interval has no wave. Both runs reported 50 marked weeks, zero strict deficits, **24** zero-excess weeks and **26** positive-exposure weeks. Total putative wave exposure was 3.07 × 10⁻¹⁶. In `code/kcor_survivor.py` the identity branch differences cumulative values and counts every positive residual after subtraction from the planned increment. A rounding-sized residual can flip this discrete classification. Classify equality with a scale-aware rounding tolerance and use the classification consistently for wave attribution and counts. Include a long exact-equality fixture and small genuine positive and negative perturbations. This is a residual defect in the new m163 diagnostic. The cumulative contrast and the originally reported strict-deficit example are correct.

2. **The m162 birth-year change also admits impossible source ages.** A 20-week ordinary disjoint sheet starting 2023-05-08 contained 1950 birth cohorts in doses 0 and 1, each starting with 20,000 people and 20 deaths per week. The final inclusive point was 1.000000. Adding a source group labeled **2099** with the same opening risk, 20 weekly deaths in dose 0, and 20 then 90 deaths per week in dose 1 changed that point to 1.981297. The workbook reported both normalization and anchor status as OK. Death-only accounting was exact. The older blanket 2020 cutoff removed this group along with legitimate post-2020 births. Check literal raw birth years against the actual enrollment date before aggregation, flag invalid input in both representations, and preserve explicit synthetic layouts and sentinel codes. This affects malformed ordinary inputs. The published Czech eligible birth years do not trigger it.

3. **A genuine zero observed before a valid reference is dropped by the pooled curve.** In an ordinary 2021-24 workbook with valid positive references, I set the first accumulating week's numerator deaths to zero in the 1940 group and used 20 weekly deaths in its denominator. All other dose-weeks had 20 deaths. The direct 1940 ratio that week was zero with an OK anchor. In a one-stratum workbook the pooled result was unavailable. Adding a 1950 group with direct ratio 1 produced a pooled ratio of **1**, retaining only that group and weight fraction 0.500022. The geometric mean extended continuously to nonnegative observations would be zero. The implementation predated m167 and requires a strictly positive within-age value for log pooling. Specify the intended domain. If a genuine zero belongs to the pooled population, retain the contributor and return zero when its weight is positive. If only positive ratios define this statistic, identify that restriction and the excluded zero in the manuscript and output. This affects early curves on sparse histories. It does not affect the positive reporting-date controls in 12.8.

4. **The separate contamination figure still calls the last eligible estimate an asymptote.** In main §3.3.2 the results paragraph, figure caption and the embedded panel title use “asymptote.” The corresponding historical generator sets `N_WEEKS = 130` and `kcor_asymptote_value` returns the last finite anchored value with supported denominator, searching backward from the final week. The code estimates no infinite-follow-up limit. Label the statistic as the mean last eligible anchored endpoint over 130 weeks in the manuscript and the actual graphic. The historical replicate values can be retained. This wording existed in m161 and concerns a different experiment from 12.3.

It did not perform new full primary, forced-alpha, S6 or S9 refits.
