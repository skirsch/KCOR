# Independent review of frozen candidate m183

## Conclusion

I found errors in the separately invoked Monte Carlo workbook workflow that can change which people enter a reported contrast and can produce a confidence interval for an unsupported reporting date. The underlying proportional gamma inversion and joint fit worked on a deterministic example with two intervening shocks. I found no evidence that the Monte Carlo defects changed the paper’s primary Table 8 results, which use a separate archived person-bootstrap analysis. I did not rerun the 999 draws.

## Substantive corrections

1. **The Monte Carlo converter discards explicitly selected birth years outside 1930–1960.** The master sample uses `MC_YOB_START` and `MC_YOB_END` (`code/KCOR_CMR.py`, lines 637–650). The weekly output grid applies an additional hard-coded 1930–1960 restriction at line 928. Both inclusive populations are calculated from that grid at lines 1365–1384. I executed the frozen `process_enrollment_data` function body with a four-person enrollment master spanning the requested 1930–1970 range. Opening counts by birth year were 1940 = 1, 1960 = 1 and 1970 = 2. The only death was in the 1970 group. The weekly output retained two people and zero deaths. An internal reconciliation of the inclusive total against the emitted ages cannot detect the omission because both exclude the same people. Use the selected enrollment population to construct the grid and check the opening counts and subsequent deaths against the master. The default intentional restriction to 1930–1960 can stay. Carry the selected bounds into the aggregate labels or associated scope fields. With defaults, the emitted `-60` population contains births in 1960 alone, although the summary labels it “born 1960+.” A reader of the standalone result should be able to distinguish a restricted sampling population from an unrestricted aggregate.

2. **The Monte Carlo summary can combine different follow-up dates and silently drop failed draws.** `code/KCOR.py` selects the closest available date separately in each iteration at lines 8318–8368. `create_mc_summary` then discards nonfinite results before calculating percentiles and basic bootstrap intervals. The date and the requested draw count disappear from the per-population statistical summary. In a written workbook test, the target for enrollment 2023_01 was January 1, 2024. The point trajectory ended May 15, 2023 with all-ages KCOR 1.715611. A copy of the same underlying history truncated on March 27 ended at 1.347434. A second draw failed component risk accounting. The log reported a point of 1.7156, a percentile 95% CI of [1.3474, 1.3474], a basic 95% CI of [2.0838, 2.0838], and only two iterations. No history reached the target. This is possible with producer-generated draws because the producer derives the calendar from each sample’s vaccination and death date extremes (`code/KCOR_CMR.py`, lines 784–805). Set the reporting week once. Keep the study observation calendar fixed across person resamples when that calendar is known. Require the same dated point, population and all intended draws for a full interval. Show counts for unavailable dates, changed population and failed draws. In particular, changing the available-age population should render the point-population interval incomplete even if a separate descriptive group is displayed. A latest-observed summary is defensible if separately labeled with the actual common week. The ordinary workbook’s time series is outside this defect. Its separate summary paths also use closest available dates (lines 8527–8543 and 9599–9605) but display the selected date.

3. **Changing the Monte Carlo sampling population can reuse old results.** The stamp in `code/Makefile` at lines 115–126 is named from enrollment and iteration count only. It records the age range and aggregate setting when first created, but never checks those fields afterward. In a scratch copy of the actual Makefile with completed defaults, both `make -n monte_carlo MC_YOB_END=1970` and `make -n monte_carlo MC_INCLUDE_AGGREGATES=0` returned “Nothing to be done.” Use a tracked configuration prerequisite whose contents are checked each run and whose timestamp changes when the effective request changes. Verify switching from one range to another and back. Otherwise the requested changed population may never reach the converter even after fixing its grid.

4. **An empty dose group can abort generation of all comparisons in a draw.** The MC producer's enrollment pivot assigns `max_dose+1` sequential column names without first reindexing to the expected dose values (`code/KCOR_CMR.py`, lines 998–1008). With valid enrollment doses 0 and 1 present and `max_dose=2`, the frozen converter raised `ValueError: Length mismatch: Expected axis has 3 elements, new values have 4 elements`. This can happen in small strata, in narrow date/age selections or by chance on resampling. Reindex the pivot to all configured doses with zero counts before renaming, as the ordinary path already does. Preserve the unaffected 1-versus-0 comparison. Empty comparison groups should retain their usual unavailable status.

## Narrow correction

`build_kcor_ns_rows` still uses `EPS=1e-12` to disallow positive denominator cumulative hazards and small positive reference ratios. In a deterministic fractional-count workbook with numerator deaths of `1e-13` per week early in follow-up and an opening population of 20,000, the ordinary corrected anchored ratio was exactly 1 at its reference, with `normalization_status=ok` and a positive fitted reference ratio of `4.686228e-15`. The raw comparator was blank at that same reference although its ratio is finite, strictly positive and must anchor to 1. The same cutoff is present for individual and ordinary aggregate raw comparators (`code/KCOR.py`, lines 5772–5780 and 5850–5860). Use the existing finite-ratio helper consistently. This is a narrow issue for expected-count inputs at extreme scale. I have no evidence of an effect on ordinary-sized Czech counts.

## Checks and interpretation

For 100 exact integrated weekly hazards, I set Gompertz growth to 0.0017/week, baseline rate to 0.0015/week, frailty variance to 6, and proportional gap excesses to 0.010 and 0.012. The ordinary fitter returned 0.0015000000000000007 and 5.9999999999999964, both offsets to floating-point precision, and a maximum cumulative inversion error of 8.4e-17. This exercises the estimator under its own model. Identifying separate individual-level selection and intervention effects from the same aggregate curve still requires assumptions. The paper describes that limitation, declares the forced exponent a sensitivity value, discloses historical outcome-informed window preferences and reports discrepancies in the empirical residual screens. Those limitations qualify external interpretation but provide no basis for replacing the descriptive estimand, the optional sensitivity calculation, or the conditional person bootstrap. The concrete corrections above address specific reporting and implementation failures.
