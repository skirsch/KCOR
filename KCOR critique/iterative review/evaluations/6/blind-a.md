# Independent scientific review of KCOR

## Overall assessment

The core gamma inversion is mathematically sound under its stated model. If survival is `S=(1+θB)^(-1/θ)`, then `B=expm1(θHobs)/θ`. The known-slope identification argument in §2.4.4 is also correct for the instantaneous hazard under proportional survivor history. These facts support a model-conditional descriptive analysis. They do not supply an empirical test that the fitted curvature came from frailty. The paper recognizes this distinction in several places. The most consequential problems I found concern how the Czech cohorts are selected, what the published diagnostic pass counts establish, and which estimands the validation exercises actually test.

## 1. Czech enrollment eligibility uses future vaccinations

The Czech count builder removes people who ever received a non-mRNA product among their first four recorded doses, before assigning exposure at each enrollment. The filter reads the manufacturer's code without checking its dose date (`code/KCOR_CMR.py:470–495`). The actual enrollment freeze then uses only doses before the enrollment Monday (`code/KCOR_CMR.py:689–746`). Two people unvaccinated at enrollment can be treated differently solely because one survives to receive a particular product months later. The newly excluded people must have survived to that dose. Freezing labels cannot remove this selection. In §2.2 the paper acknowledges post-enrollment eligibility in general terms, but readers are not told that the Czech builder actually uses it. “All ages (full population)” in SI §S6 also needs the underlying age eligibility: the ordinary builder retains birth-band labels 1920 through 2005 and unknowns (`code/KCOR_CMR.py:1605`).

I checked the supplied `data/Czech/records_100k.csv`, using the implementation's infection-row and birth-band restrictions, registry death week, and manufacturer dictionary. Of 85,600 rows remaining alive at the start of 2021-24 before applying the manufacturer rule, 7,091 had a disqualifying first-to-fourth product. For 3,002 the disqualifying product was first observed in the enrollment week or later, and 2,935 of these had no vaccination before the enrollment Monday. These are counts in the supplied 100,000-row file, not national estimates. They establish that the mechanism occurs. The effect on KCOR and its direction require an actual rerun.

**Fix.** Define manufacturer eligibility using the information available immediately before each enrollment and retain subsequent recipients in their original cohorts. Compare counts, estimated frailties and anchored contrasts to the published construction. If a retrospectively defined population is scientifically intended, state its exact rule and retain it as a separately labeled analysis. Trace the filter version for the cached inputs to each published registry table.

## 2. The Czech diagnostic pass count omits a large observable discrepancy

SI §S5.4 reports 78 of 78 fits passing the pipeline diagnostics, and SI §S6.1 describes all age strata meeting all prespecified checks. The scan's actual acceptance statement in `analysis/quiet_windows/scan.py` is `success and status == "ok" and not degenerate`. It doesn't test the residual flag, normalized cumulative shape, or between-placement stability. The fit exports a separate binomial residual screen in `code/KCOR.py:_sampling_diagnostics`. I replayed the published forced-1.18 scan from its frozen input, retaining that screen from each ordinary call. Of the 78 nominal passes, **63** had at least one fitted week with doubled binomial tail probability below the paper's own 0.05/m threshold. By birth decade and doses 0, 1, 2, the counts were **13, 1, 13** of 13 for the 1940s and **10, 13, 13** for the 1950s. The smallest tail was about 7.8 × 10⁻¹⁵. Fitted variance ranged from 1.80 to 3.49 for 1940s dose 0 and 5.46 to 8.47 for 1950s dose 0. The windows overlap. These flags are exploratory, and a flag is not an automatic rejection or evidence about a vaccine effect. They are relevant evidence against describing all these fits as diagnostically clean. The scan and §S6 application are different analyses and the exact counts should not be transferred from one to the other.

**Fix.** Preserve separate labels for numerical fit acceptance and statistical adequacy. Publish the residual-screen counts, affected weeks, shape residuals and placement sensitivity alongside the 78 numerical passes. Supply the actual gate evidence for each §S6 age row, including the estimator and input used. Limit the “all checks passed” claim to criteria actually evaluated. There is no need to reset a fitted variance because a single week was flagged.

## 3. The age-shift controls have an unknown target, and both captions report absent uncertainty

The mapping in `test/negative_control/code/generate_negative_control.py` compares different actual age groups within an exposure category. It removes a literal vaccine assignment contrast. It does not make ages respond proportionally to epidemics, seasonality or changes in care. Even under perfect frailty inversion, the anchored ratio can move if normalized baseline hazards have different time courses. Main §3.1.2 acknowledges the unknown exact target near Table 8 but earlier calls this a pseudo-null by construction and describes flatness as expected. The construction alone does not entail flatness. Both `documentation/preprint/figures/fig2_neg_control_10yr_age_diff.png` and `fig3_neg_control_20yr_age_diff.png` show a single line with no displayed interval. Their captions explicitly say 95% bootstrap uncertainty bands are shown. The separate pointwise intervals at the Table 8 reporting date cannot supply uncertainty for an entire plotted time course.

**Fix.** Describe these as age-shift comparisons with flatness conditional on proportional underlying normalized trajectories. Retain the known-target synthetic null. For each historical plot, identify its enrollment, estimator, reference week and series. Add the claimed pointwise bootstrap bands using the corresponding data and estimator, or correct the captions. SI §S5.6 also calls 1930, 1940 and 1950 “single birth-year cohorts.” The Czech file contains labels such as 1940–1944, `birthBandYears: 5` is explicit in `data/Czech/Czech.yaml`, and the builder extracts the first four digits. Label those curves as five-year birth bands unless a separate single-year input can be demonstrated.

## 4. Anchoring does not isolate post-reference hazard differences

The Figure 3.1.2 caption says division by the reference ratio displays “post-anchor divergence only.” The defined ratio of cumulative ratios retains both pre-anchor totals. For example, let the normalized cumulative hazards at the anchor be 0.02 and 0.01. Add exactly 0.01 to **each** after the anchor. The anchored KCOR falls from 1 to (0.03/0.02)/(0.02/0.01) = 0.75 despite identical subsequent integrated hazards. This is an interpretation error, not a bug in calculating the defined ratio.

**Fix.** Describe the plot as change in the cumulative ratio relative to its reference value. A question specifically about post-reference outcomes requires differences of cumulative hazards from the anchor before forming a contrast. Give the actual reference date or accumulated-week count as appropriate. The current implementation with skip 2 and four accumulating reference weeks uses raw index 5, whereas “week 4 following enrollment” suggests a different reference.

## 5. The Cox and RMST comparisons conflate differences of estimand with estimator bias

In §2.11 the cohorts have the same conditional individual hazard at fixed frailty but different frailty distributions. Their marginal survival functions are genuinely different. RMST, the area under each survival curve, can therefore differ at the population level. Calling this “RMST inherits depletion bias” in the comparison tables is incorrect for RMST's own target. A treatment-only Cox fit under these nonproportional hazards likewise estimates a follow-up-dependent summary of the marginal hazard contrast. Its departure from one is meaningful evidence against interpreting it as a fixed-frailty treatment effect. It is not an estimate of bias for its own marginal summary. The KCOR grid uses the generating frailties supplied to the inversion, as §2.11.1 acknowledges.

In addition, the shared-frailty curve is algebraically manufactured from the standard Cox curve with a constant (0.94−1)/(0.87−1) attenuation (`test/sim_grid/code/plot_estimator_bias_vs_theta.py`). The caption does disclose this approximation. It cannot support any empirical inference about expected attenuation across frailty levels. I found no supplied fitting script for the actual shared-frailty HRs 0.94, 1.19 and 0.82 in the joint-frailty comparison table. The named S7 generator computes KCOR and its saved diagnostic CSV reports KCOR fit parameters.

**Fix.** Label these as estimand contrasts, give the population marginal targets where discussing estimator bias, and separate known-θ inversion from fitting θ. Supply the shared-frailty model, cluster definition, code and results for the three table entries or withdraw those comparisons. Remove the extrapolated line as evidence of comparative performance.

## 6. The optional exponent's instantaneous identity needs an interval-scale counterpart

Sections 2.7.1–2.7.2 correctly cancel common intensity at an instant conditional on the current survivor law. Weekly integrated excesses need not have the ratio of the week-entry moments used to motivate identification. For example, take an isolated wave with no baseline exposure that week, equal external scale, α=1, unit starting mean and θ=2 versus θ=0. At entry both moments are one. A common integrated wave exposure of 0.1 gives observed integrated hazards log(1.2)/2 and 0.1, a ratio of 0.912. This is within the working survivor model. It arises because the at-risk frailty composition changes inside the bin. Also, a difference from a no-wave reference curve can contain a difference in the baseline hazard among the selected survivors, in addition to the wave component. The historical Czech subtraction from dose zero is specifically identified in §3.4.2; it is not an independent measurement of that component.

**Fix.** State explicitly whether the weekly localization objectives approximate the instantaneous relationship and quantify that approximation where they are used. An exact option is to fit shared weekly wave exposure with the specified external cohort multiplier using differences in log survival at the bin boundaries, then validate recovery from weekly death counts. When citing agreement of pairwise and collapse objectives as corroboration, note that the two criteria use the same log residuals. With equal weights at a fixed week, the sum of squared pair differences is exactly the number of cohorts times the centered sum of squares. Weighting and exclusions can change the pooled optimum, but agreement does not provide independent identification. The Czech conclusion that α is unidentified can stand. This issue concerns localization, not inversion at a supplied α.

## Scope of the remaining uncertainty

The paper already discloses the dependence on a fixed Gompertz slope, the restricted frailty law, historical window selection, the lack of a causal estimand, and the inconclusive α fit. These are material conditions on interpretation, not counterexamples to the core inversion. Bootstrap coverage for earlier or oracle fits cannot establish coverage for the current operational estimator. SI §S8 itself reports 90/100 coverage in one nonlinear design and a mean of 1.040 against truth 1 in a different stress design with 48/50 coverage. The latter illustrates how good coverage and point-estimate bias can coexist. I would retain these results with their actual design and uncertainty, without claiming universal calibration or a general ability to detect departures from the model.
