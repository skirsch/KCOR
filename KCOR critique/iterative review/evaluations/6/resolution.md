## Remaining ordinary-workbook defects

1. **All-ages fits and trajectories can refer to different populations.** The all-ages fit at `code/KCOR.py:7171–7184` builds counts from positive birth years. When an all-ages input row is present, the hazard stage at line 7707 retains it, and normalization applies the fit to that row at lines 7998–8037. The validated converter total at `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:65,129` includes unknown birth year −1. I read the frozen Czech registry workbook directly. Across doses at the enrollment week, the −1 stratum contributed 1,587,167 of 9,904,933 people in 2021_24 (16.0%) and 1,583,360 of 9,835,139 in 2022_06 (16.1%). The aggregate hazard and its fitted parameters therefore cover different populations in these inputs, even in primary mode. These counts establish the size of the population mismatch. Its impact on a KCOR estimate was not measured. Choose the actual population for −2 and use identical counts for fitting, normalization and bootstrap reconstruction. Exercise a fixture with known- and unknown-year strata with different hazards. No endpoint change has been established for Table 8.
2. **Flagged-draw and point-flag counts can implicate unrelated ages.** In `code/bootstrap_reporting.py:155–173`, the flag is ORed across every output sharing enrollment, dose pair and date and assigned to every nonpositive birth-year row. I called the current function with one flagged 1930 age, one clean 1970 age, and clean −60 and −2 direct fits. It labeled both aggregates flagged. The 1930 stratum cannot contribute to the born-1960-or-later group. The direct aggregate fit also has its own diagnostic status. The defect affects interval status and flag counts, without changing the endpoints or percentiles in that function. Use each direct fit's own status and propagate flags to pooled rows only from actual contributing strata with positive weights.
3. **Impact of the Czech product rule remains unknown.** An earlier enrollee must survive until a later product to become retrospectively excludable. Cohort assignment at baseline does not remove that selection mechanism. The direction and size depend on actual dates, products and outcomes. Evaluate the alternative eligibility definitions on source records before interpreting an application-level contrast.

## Prior objections and limits of inference

- **1.1, 2.7, 3.2, 4.3, 4.7.** In the proportional model, a given observed cumulative hazard H admits the alternative baseline expm1(theta H)/theta for each admissible theta. Recovery of theta therefore relies on a baseline family and assumptions about selection before and during the quiet period. Misspecified baseline growth, quiet-window contamination, omitted nonproportional history and wave allocation remain sources of bias. The current common-amplitude qualification and optional sensitivity mode narrow the claims. They do not identify arbitrary past shocks or the exponent.
- **1.3, 1.4, 4.6.** Both stored Table 8 arrays in `analysis/table8` have two enrollments, one original point vector, 999 resamples and three comparisons. Independently recomputing the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles from draws 1–999 matched both JSON files exactly. All twelve intervals contain 1 and the stored flags sum to zero. These archived draws predate m117. Earlier selected-draw matches and checks of current sorting and arithmetic are weaker than a new end-to-end replay of all draws. Table 8 is a compatibility check on an age-shift control, not evidence of a guaranteed numerical null, a causal effect, or nominal coverage of every ordinary-workbook interval. Earlier studies used different fitters or data-generating designs. Round 5 records 90/100 coverage for its nonlinear design and a stress-case mean of 1.0402 at truth one.
- **4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 4.5.** The current zero-row and anchor-only tests fail explicitly. The alpha-continuity fixture recovers theta 2.0000000000 at alpha 1 and 2.0000000081 at 1.0000001. Final-fit gap export and the later quiet-window initializer also survive the focused regressions. These are positive regression results for the earlier exact failures.
- **4.9.** Numerical preservation of the valid inversion prefix has direct current evidence. Applying theta zero after other fit failures and retaining flagged draws defines a fallback estimator. Its confidence intervals and fit status need interpretation conditional on the chosen policy. The threshold, analytical comparison intervals, and replacement policy have not acquired general validation through these repairs.

For the next narrow verification, prioritize an unknown-age aggregate fixture and flag attribution by contributor. A full latest-version replay and coverage study would answer separate questions of empirical reproducibility and calibration.
