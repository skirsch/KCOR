# Root causes and links between objections

## 2.7 and 2.8 · KCOR above 1 with zero added hazard

The simulated cohorts have equal baseline hazards, so the target is 1. With zero added hazard, the original fitter gave mean KCOR 1.050 [1.034, 1.066] using the fifth-week reference and 1.052 [1.038, 1.066] using the fourth. The paired change was +0.002 [−0.007, +0.011]. These are pointwise 95% Monte Carlo intervals. The reference-week discrepancy in 2.8 explains attenuation when added hazard starts in the fifth week. The upward shift under the null in 2.7 persists. [Paired anchor comparison](../objections/2.8/README.md#iteration-3-paired-comparison-of-reference-weeks)

In the joint-fit diagnostic on the same datasets with zero added hazard, the largest shift came from estimating the lower-frailty cohort’s variance. The sampled early reference also contributed. How much of the original fitter’s 1.050 comes specifically from estimating the lower-frailty cohort’s variance remains open. [Controlled substitutions](../objections/2.7/README.md#why-the-zero-contamination-mean-exceeded-one)

**Link to 1.3 coverage stress.** With 50,000 people pooled across two age strata and a four-week first quiet window, the joint-fitter median interval width was 213.056 [1.480, 532.287], versus 0.319 [0.284, 0.353] for the fixed-rate fitter. One joint-fit dataset had KCOR 812.675 [0.848, 744.878] with a generating target of 1. A dose-1 cohort with true variance 0.5 was fitted at 91.84. This occurred without an insufficient-signal flag in that fit. [Matched coverage stress](../objections/1.3/multiwindow-coverage/README.md#additional-fallback-stress).

**Numerical mechanism verified.** In the extreme dataset, four first-window counts of 33, 25, 16 and 24 led the joint seed to variance 92.064. Reconstructing the gap at that value made the inferred cumulative excess 378.968, allowing the pooled refit to converge near the same erroneous seed. Replacing only the variance of the offending cohort with its generating 0.5 moved pooled KCOR from 812.675 [0.848, 744.878] to 0.997 [0.354, 1.193]. The single-window tests show that even with the true baseline rate supplied, weak frailty signal remains difficult to recover. Residual rescaling leaves the large fitting errors essentially intact. In the empirical 2022 seed fits the local log-rate/variance correlation is 0.997–0.998. [Full numerical investigation](../objections/2.7/low-frailty/README.md).

The [multiwindow information calculation](../objections/2.7/low-frailty/README.md#information-from-all-quiet-windows-and-uncertain-gap-offsets) measures much stronger information under the proportional gamma working model when quiet weeks and observed gap survival are used together, allowing uncertainty in gap offsets. In the current sequential fitter, perturbing each Table 8 week and refitting attributes 87–89% of the local propagated variance of the three 2021 unvaccinated fits to their first quiet window. For two unstable 2022 fits the local share exceeds 99.9%. This links the weak seed to the uncertainty of the final multiwindow estimate.

## 3.2 · Mortality before the first quiet window

**Link to 2.7.** In the 200 sampled short-window nonlinear null datasets, supplying the denominator's true low variance reduced median absolute error for both fits. Supplying the numerator's higher variance reduced it more. This links frailty estimation to the instability observed in the simulation. The cause of the empirical 2022 interval widths remains open. [Matched substitutions and width precision](../objections/3.2/experiment.md#root-cause-check-on-the-two-fitted-frailty-variances)

The joint seed fixes pre-window excess at zero. A quiet-window rate can therefore absorb selection that occurred earlier in modeled follow-up. The additive-model example reproduces this ambiguity with noiseless data and known frailty. The [standalone 3.2 experiment](../objections/3.2/experiment.md) tests how far observed survival at entry helps. Table 8 in 2022 has a delayed first window, and 2021 supplies a control with zero delay. The experiment measures sensitivity of the empirical estimates and intervals. True Czech baseline and wave contributions are unknown.

This mechanism can occur with substantial frailty and just one quiet window. The later gap reset in [3.1](../objections/3.1/README.md) and the low-variance recovery study in [2.7](../objections/2.7/README.md) have separate evidence. The trial leaves the [1.2 wave attribution](../objections/1.2/README.md) in place and tests it at a specified exponent. Selection during a nonlinear wave can change the shape of the survivor distribution, which is outside the additive entry identity.

## 3.1 · A gap estimate resets frailty

**Confirmed cause.** The gap offset estimates excess cumulative hazard between quiet windows. Frailty variance describes how much individual risk varies within a cohort. Zero excess in a gap is compatible with positive frailty. In iteration 2, on the first pass the fitter used any nonpositive incremental gap estimate to set the applied frailty variance to zero and exit before the pooled refit. The five noiseless cases isolate this decision. [Objection 3.1](../objections/3.1/README.md) · [Five matched fits](evaluations/3/gap-check.md)

### Isolated trial of the proposed correction

On the frozen Series 3 current edition (m83), an in-memory trial removed only the first-pass early return inside `if iteration_neg_preclamp`. This lets the existing nonnegative offset calculation and pooled frailty refit run, retaining the signed offset diagnostic and other fit checks. The trial used the five histories in [gap_check.py](evaluations/3/gap_check.py) and draw zero (original counts) of the Table 8 replay with joint fitting.

All five shock-free fits recovered variance 4 within 0.00000002 and returned status `ok`. In the week-90 example with equal baseline hazards the anchored endpoint moved from 0.752054 to 0.999995 (true value 1).

The approved correction was applied in iteration 3. The [verification record](../objections/3.1/verification.md) gives every Table 8 point estimate with its interval and compares the widths on the same draws. The frozen iteration 2 edition is available as a [checkpoint](checkpoints/iteration-2/).

## Update: shared-rate fitting and statistical resolution (2026-11-24)

The isolated [all-window joint fitter study](../objections/2.7/low-frailty/all-window-joint/README.md) confirms an additional numerical mechanism: freezing the seed rate and treating reconstructed offsets as fixed inside the objective can preserve an incorrect first-window solution, and even profiling the offsets can leave a local optimizer branch. Jointly fitting the common rate and variance, recomputing offsets for candidate parameters, and checking a second starting value corrected the pathological dataset (KCOR 812.675 to 1.016) and reduced the 2022 Table 8 interval widths by 73–90%. Stress coverage was 48/50 with median interval width 0.315 versus 50/50 and 213.056 for the current joint-seed fit. The [exact multiwindow Fisher analysis](../objections/2.7/low-frailty/theoretical-limits/README.md) gives a synthetic pooled KCOR benchmark [0.877,1.140] and clarifies why low frailty itself remains imprecise even when KCOR is reasonably stable. The contamination experiment's zero-added-hazard mean above one remains to be retested with the approved candidate; 3.2 remains distinct. No experimental method has been adopted.

The subsequent [matched contamination replay](../objections/2.7/all-window-contamination/README.md) establishes that this original single-window design is limited by statistical information even with the all-window fitter. At 50k the new mean null KCOR is 1.0494 [1.0305,1.0684], versus 1.0643 [1.0387,1.0899] for the previous joint fit. At 5M it is 1.0013 [0.9993,1.0033]. The clipped-normal approximation to the nonnegative estimate predicts the observed low-frailty boundary frequency and mean closely. Known-truth substitutions separately identify the sampled anchor. No new fitter or experiment has been adopted.

The broader [1.3 coverage follow-up](../objections/1.3/all-window-coverage/README.md) confirms that candidate coverage can be reasonable even with finite-sample null bias. Original 50k-per-cohort single-window null coverage is 187/200, mean interval width 0.549; the 5M case is 192/200, mean width 0.054. In the exact-no-wave multiwindow model candidate coverage is 97/100, but with the existing approximate nonlinear wave it is 90/100. The latter remains a separate link to 1.2.

### 3.2 follow-up: pre-entry exposure and fitting-anchor contamination

[Matched study](../objections/3.2/all-window-entry/README.md) isolates two mechanisms distinct from numerical optimizer trapping and low-frailty sampling bias: failure to carry a preceding proportional shock into the first quiet window, and fitting early anchor weeks as quiet when they contain shock mortality. Entry-aware fitting exactly restores known proportional exposure. Quiet-only residual diagnostic restores a noiseless proportional early-pulse ratio from 1.118 to 1 and a nonlinear partial-wave null from 1.144 to 0.995, but harms precision when anchors are clean. The KCOR normalization anchor itself was not changed.

### 1.2 / 3.2: nonlinear selection and fitted frailty

The [combined exact weekly wave trial](../objections/1.2/all-window-weekly-survival/README.md) isolates a deterministic fitting-model error. Under nonlinear selection the quiet-window proportional gamma objective yields theta above truth even on expected death counts. Exact weekly inversion with these fitted parameters gives 0.988967 at true KCOR 1; supplying generating theta alone recovers 0.999943, supplying generating k alone yields 0.989023. The approximate correction has an opposite error with true inputs, partly canceling the fit error. Coverage stays unchanged in both sampled nonlinear experiments. The finding is distinct from weak-information boundary bias.

The [nonlinear survivor-law fit experiment](../objections/3.2/nonlinear-survivor-fit/README.md) verifies the 1.2/3.2 model-consistency root cause: fitting quiet predictions from S(B,W) rather than the proportional gamma working law recovers generating frailty and KCOR exactly without sampling. Sampled main mean improves 0.98982 to 1.00093; coverage unchanged 90/100. This mechanism is distinct from low-information boundary bias and early anchor contamination. Correctly modeling survivor selection does not create information in a broad wave window and cannot identify unlabeled nonlinear exposure.


### 1.4: nonlinear exponent identification

[Historical pooled replay](../objections/1.4/alpha-pooled-replay/README.md) distinguishes frailty-model consistency from information about alpha. Despite 10.36 million overlapping enrollment memberships, the historical positive-excess objective retains 62 rows from eight cohorts; the raw excess contrast also lacks the specified external effect scale. Correcting survivor moments does not identify alpha: both optima move to the boundary, including after flagged-cohort exclusion. This is a different information limitation from weak low-frailty curvature, although both can affect the fitted inputs.
